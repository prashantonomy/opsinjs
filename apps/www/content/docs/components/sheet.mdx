The bottom sheet is the defining interaction of a modern consumer health app, and it is also
where accessibility most often quietly fails: gestures with no equivalent, focus that never
arrives, and a save button under the keyboard. Each of those three has an answer in this
component, and each answer is named in [Accessibility](#accessibility) as either checked by a
gate or argued from the code.

It is a client component, and that is a requirement rather than an optimisation. It holds
which detent it is resting at, it mounts a portal, it moves focus, and it listens for a drag.
None of those has a CSS answer, which is the test [Surface](./surface.mdx) passes and this one
does not.

## Preview

The default sheet: one content-sized detent, dismissible, modal. Open it and look for the two
things that are easiest to leave out. The close control is in the header before you go looking
for it, and the action is pinned below the content rather than sitting at the end of it.

The sheet portals to the end of the document rather than into the frame below, so what it
covers here is this whole page. That is the real behaviour and not an artefact of the preview:
`Drawer.Portal` renders through to `body` wherever it is mounted, and everything outside it is
given `aria-hidden` for as long as the sheet is open.

The distinction from [Dialog](./dialog.mdx) is not size, it is obligation. A sheet is a place
the reader chose to go and can leave at any moment. A dialog is a question that must be
answered. Products that use a sheet for the second case end up with readers dismissing
questions by accident.

At 390 the two now read apart on sight as well as in obligation. A sheet docks to an
edge, spans the full width there and carries a drag handle the reader may pull to
dismiss, while a dialog floats inset from all four edges with rounded corners and shows
a visible Close beside its glyph. The silhouette follows the obligation: the surface a
reader chose and may leave no longer wears the shape of the question a dialog makes them
answer, so a reader who learns the docked panel with a handle means a place they can
leave keeps that reading on the phone where they most need it. The test between the two
is still obligation rather than size, and it reads the same whichever page the reader
opened first.

`dismissible={false}` is not a way to turn a sheet into a dialog, and the component will not
let it become one. The scrim, the escape key and a downward drag are the three ambient routes,
and `dismissible={false}` closes all three. It never removes the close control, because a
modal surface with no way out is a trap. The one surface that legitimately has no "went away"
outcome is `Dialog severity="alert"`. The component holds that promise by naming the three
routes it cancels rather than the one it spares, so a close arriving by a route it does not
recognise closes the sheet rather than being swallowed. It was written the other way round
once, and for as long as it was, `dismissible={false}` produced a modal sheet with focus
trapped inside it and no exit at all.

**A sheet is not a privacy boundary**, and nothing about being the top surface makes what it
holds less visible to the room.
[On-screen privacy](../health/on-screen-privacy.mdx) names `sheet` in its `implements` list,
and this is the whole of what the component does about it: nothing, deliberately, in both
directions. Masking a sensitive value belongs to the value rather than to the container,
because only the thing that knows it is a reading knows whether it is one this reader asked to
keep hidden. Rule 3 of that page, that a masked value occupies the same space as the real one,
is also a promise a container cannot make on its content's behalf. Covering the window before
the operating system takes its app-switcher snapshot belongs to the host app, because a React
component is never told the app is being backgrounded. A sheet holding something a reader
would not want read over their shoulder needs both of those from somewhere else.

**Scrim** dims and blocks the content behind when the sheet is modal. **Grabber** signals that
the sheet can be dragged, and is never the only way to move it. **Header** names the sheet and
holds the close control. **Content** scrolls internally once it exceeds the detent.
**Footer** pins the primary action above the keyboard and the safe-area inset.

`Sheet.Content` and `Sheet.Description` are the two exported parts, and each passes the narrow
test that [ADR 0011](../project/decisions/0011-anatomy-parts-are-data-slots.mdx) sets: the
consumer supplies the content, and no prop could carry it. A sheet may hold things that do not
scroll beside the things that do, so `Sheet.Content` cannot be a prop; and only the caller
knows which paragraph inside it is the one the reader must weigh, so the description cannot be a
prop either. Everything else is a `data-slot`. That is enough to style and not enough to take
apart. The footer arrives as a prop for the reason in reverse: a prop can carry it, and the
pinning is the point.

The container's width is the one thing that follows the viewport. On a phone it fills the
width, which is the case the file was built for. Above the `sm` breakpoint the width is capped
at the comfortable reading measure and the viewport's own `justify-center` centres it, so
consent text never runs to roughly 120 characters a line and a decision control never spreads
into a bar with its label marooned in the middle. The cap is a default and not a lock: a
product that wants the sheet wider or narrower on a large display passes a `max-w` through
`className`, which lands on the container and wins over the default. On an expanded layout a
sheet is often the wrong surface to reach for in the first place.
[Responsive modes](../foundations/space/responsive-modes.mdx) says the sheet stops being a
temporary overlay and becomes a permanent pane there, and a pane is a Surface rather than a
modal.

Nesting a `Sheet` inside a `Sheet` is a composition error. Two stacked modal surfaces produce
a focus order nobody can predict and an escape key with two plausible meanings. Base UI's
drawer supports nesting deliberately, so the primitive cannot report the mistake; the component
counts its own depth through a context and warns in development.

### Why `content` is the only detent that cannot be combined

Base UI's snap points are fractions of the viewport, pixel values or rem strings, and there is
no content-sized one. Every snap point it resolves is clamped to the popup's own measured
height, with the offset computed as `popupHeight − clampedHeight`. So a sheet that can reach
`full` has to be as tall as the viewport, and once it is, its natural height *is* the viewport:
there is no content height left to snap back to.

`content` is therefore implemented as the absence of a snap point rather than the presence of
one. The popup takes its own height, up to the full viewport, and the browser lays it out.
There is no measurement in JavaScript, nothing frozen at first open, and nothing to go stale
when the content changes. Passing it alongside `half` or `full` drops it with a development
warning rather than approximating it.

Being as tall as its tallest snap point creates a problem the component has to solve, because a
two-detent sheet is forced to the full viewport height and then slid down by the active stop,
which Base UI publishes as `--drawer-snap-point-offset`. Left alone, that means the lower part
of the full-height box, and the footer with it, sits below the bottom edge of the screen at any
detent under the top one. The component reserves that offset back as bottom padding on the
popup rather than as a height. With `box-sizing: border-box` the laid-out column shrinks to
exactly the region on screen, so the header, the scrolling region and the footer are measured
against the part of the sheet a reader can see rather than against the whole popup: the footer
lands on the visible bottom edge, and the scrolling region gets a bounded height that lets it
reach its last row. It stays a padding on purpose. A height on the popup would change the
measurement every offset is derived from, so the height and the offset would chase each other
on every detent change.

## States

Sheet is interactive, and unlike a component that only formats a value, it owns a state
machine of its own: whether it is open, which detent it is resting at, and whether a drag is
in flight. Two of its own controls take focus. `Sheet.Close` and `Sheet.Grabber` both draw the
outset focus ring, and `Sheet.Close` additionally takes a hover treatment of a muted
background; `Sheet.Grabber` takes none, so a pointer resting on it before a press gives no
visual cue beyond the ring that arrives once it is focused. Neither control is ever disabled,
because a modal surface whose exit or whose drag control could be switched off is a trap by
definition, and neither carries a pressed style of its own beyond the browser's default active
outline. The popup itself, `Sheet.Container`, can also take focus, not from a Tab press but
programmatically, the moment a dismiss the product refused needs somewhere to put the reader
back; it draws the same ring turned inward, because an outset ring on an edge this close to the
popup's own clipped top corner loses its top line. `Sheet.Content` takes that same inward ring
for the identical reason, on the one edge-to-edge region in the file.

Two further states belong to the sheet as a whole rather than to one control, and Base UI
stamps both onto the container as data attributes rather than as anything this file writes by
hand: open or closed, which the `open` prop drives directly, and dragging, present for the span
of a swipe and read off the same custom property the resting transform composes. See
[Data attributes](#data-attributes) for the full set.

The five canonical data states from [Data states](../foundations/data-states.mdx) describe a
reading, and Sheet holds no reading of its own to be in one. Each row says what this container
actually does.

| Data state | What Sheet renders |
| --- | --- |
| Error | Nothing of its own. Sheet runs no validation and marks nothing invalid; a control gone wrong inside it is that control's state to show, most often [Field](./field.mdx)'s, inside `Sheet.Content`. |
| Loading | Nothing. Opening and closing are synchronous state changes with no request in between, and a sheet whose content is still arriving renders whatever the caller puts in `Sheet.Content`, typically a [Skeleton](./skeleton.mdx). |
| Empty | The header, the grabber and the footer render exactly as they would with content. A sheet with nothing but a title inside `Sheet.Content` is a legitimate, if unusual, sheet rather than an empty state this component has an opinion about. |
| Partial | Not applicable at the level of the container. Sheet has no notion of a body that is part answered and part not; a form that captures three fields and shows one still blank is the form's own concern, not this container's. |
| Stale | Not applicable. Staleness belongs to a reading ageing on a display surface, which [RelativeTime](./relative-time.mdx) is the component for; Sheet holds no reading and no timestamp of its own. |

## Content guidelines

Title the sheet with what the reader is doing there, as a noun phrase or a short imperative.
The primary action says what it does, as *Save reading* and *Choose unit* do, and it is never
*Done*, which tells a reader nothing about what happens to what they typed.

Keep sheets to one task. A sheet with its own tabs is a screen that has not admitted it yet.

Sheet ships two words of its own and no more: the close control reads **Close**, and the
grabber's accessible name is an instruction, *Make the sheet full screen* or *Make the sheet
half the screen*, for whichever detent the next press moves to. Neither can be replaced by a
prop today, which is a real gap
for any product that is not in English and is an open question on this page. The scrolling
region names itself with the title you passed, so it adds no third string and needs no
translating. Every other word inside a sheet is the product's, and therefore the product's to
check against [Plain-English A to Z](../content/plain-english-a-z.mdx).

A sheet that asks the reader to decide something puts **both answers in the footer at equal
weight**. `Sheet.Close` is an exit and not a *no*: it is a text control in the
header set at the callout step in the full foreground role, one step under body and two under
the footer action's headline, rather than at the provenance footnote in a muted grey it used to
take, so declining is never harder to find than accepting, and letting it stand in for
declining makes declining harder, which is what the safety checklist's consent item forbids. This matters most for
[ConsentSheet](./consent-sheet.mdx), the component that extends this one. The rule is about
the footer's contents rather than about the base, which cannot see what the two controls mean.

## Motion

Sheet is the component `spring-sheet` was tuned for, and its container consumes both halves of
that token pair directly: `--opsin-ease-spring-sheet` for the curve and
`--opsin-duration-spring-sheet` for the settle time, applied together as a
`transition-transform`, or, once there is more than one detent, as a two-property transition of
transform and padding-bottom so the reserved offset settles on the same schedule as the slide.
The spring is overdamped, reaching its resting position without passing it, because a sheet
that bounces at the top of its travel reads as a dropped object rather than a surface arriving.
`tokens/motion.json` records its settle time as 483 milliseconds, measured from the spring's
own stiffness, damping and mass rather than chosen by eye.

<MotionDemo easing="--opsin-ease-spring-sheet" duration="--opsin-duration-spring-sheet" />

Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` the pair collapses to its own fallback: the easing
becomes `linear` and the duration becomes 120 milliseconds, which `tokens/motion.json`
documents as "Cross-fade in place. A sheet that appears with no travel still needs to be seen
to appear." The component reads that instruction rather than dropping the transition outright.
`REDUCED_MOTION_CROSSFADE` in the source switches the transitioned property from `transform` to
`opacity` and pins both the starting and the ending transform to the sheet's own resting
position, so the popup is already at its detent for the whole of the transition and travels no
distance while still visibly fading in and out. The duration and the easing are left entirely
to the token, which is the division of labour this file keeps: a component may only answer for
the one thing a token cannot know, and that is distance.

The scrim's fade is a second transition, and it is left alone rather than answered here. It
runs on `--opsin-duration-base` paired with `--opsin-ease-standard`, and the two collapse under
reduced motion through two different files: `app/product.css` reduces `--opsin-duration-base`
to 1 millisecond for every consumer of it, and `app/tokens.generated.css` reduces
`--opsin-ease-standard` to `linear`. A fade has no distance to remove, so there is nothing
further for this component to add.

No health value is ever animated on first paint by anything in this file. Sheet renders no
measurement of its own; it holds whatever the caller passes into `Sheet.Content`, and a reading
a product wants to count up or sweep into place on arrival is the caller's animation to add or
withhold, never something Sheet does to a number that has already arrived.

## Accessibility

Some of this is checked by a gate and the rest is an argument. They are separated below because
`beta` promises that accessibility is claimed only where it has been checked, with the gaps
listed rather than left out.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y` on every pull request and every push to `main`:**

- **Every colour is a token, and every token resolves under the product theme**, apart from the
  system `Canvas` the scrim paints under `forced-colors: active`, which is a system keyword on
  purpose because a token cannot survive a forced palette. The static rig
  fails the build on a hex literal, an `oklch()`, a Tailwind palette colour or a numbered ramp
  step anywhere in `registry/**`, and separately on the chrome-only roles and radii that exist
  in `app/globals.css` and not in `app/product.css`. The sheet paints five roles of its own,
  and everything else is Surface's material. The five roles are the foreground the close
  control's label takes, the muted surface it takes on hover, the muted foreground on the
  grabber's bar, the border role on the footer's rule, and the ring role on every focus outline. The ring is declared in the
  component rather than inherited from `app/product.css` on purpose: that stylesheet does not
  travel with the file into a consumer's project, and a control whose focus ring depends on a
  stylesheet it was not installed with is a control that loses it silently.
- **No `px` font size, and no banned word** in copy or in an identifier. Unlike most components
  in the system this one ships two reader-facing strings, so the copy half of that gate is
  doing real work here rather than guarding a future edit.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **A modal sheet is a dialogue.** Base UI gives the popup the dialog role, points its
  `aria-labelledby` at `Sheet.Title`, moves focus into it on open, traps focus while it is open,
  and returns focus to whatever opened it on close. When the caller renders a
  `Sheet.Description`, the popup also carries `aria-describedby` pointing at that paragraph, so
  the purpose or scope a reader must weigh is announced with the dialog and not merely on screen
  beside it. When the caller renders none, the popup carries no `aria-describedby` at all, which
  is deliberate: a description that points at no text, or at text invented to fill the
  attribute, is worse than the honest absence of one. The page behind is hidden and unreachable,
  and it is not `inert`: the primitive marks everything outside the popup `aria-hidden`, the
  scrim covers the viewport so a pointer never reaches the page, and Tab is held inside by the
  focus trap. None of the three sets the `inert` attribute, and the difference is observable,
  because focus moved programmatically from behind the sheet takes and keeps focus on a control
  the accessibility tree has just been told is not there. Closing that hole is a change to the
  component rather than to this page, so build on this sentence rather than on the word
  *inert*. `aria-modal` is not set on the popup either, which is the same position Dialog takes:
  what contains the reader is the `aria-hidden`, the scrim and the focus trap, and not an
  attribute on the popup.
- **Escape closes a dismissible sheet, and the product is told which route a close came by.**
  `onOpenChange` receives `close-control`, `scrim`, `escape`, `drag` or `other`, which is what
  makes "ask before discarding unsaved input" possible: hold `open` where it is, show the
  question, and close when the reader answers. The five are mapped from Base UI's own nine
  reason strings, and the mapping is typed against that union rather than against `string`, so
  a name that cannot match is a compile error rather than a close silently reclassified as
  `other`. With `dismissible={false}` the escape key, the platform's close watcher, the scrim
  and a drag are all cancelled by name, because Base UI's own flag covers the pointer only.
  Nothing else is cancelled, so a route the component does not recognise closes the sheet. A
  cancelled scrim press prevents the default on `pointerdown` so the press never takes focus off
  the sheet onto the document, and the sheet then puts focus back on the container, so the title
  is re-announced rather than the reader being left on the page behind.
- **Every gesture has a control.** Drag-to-dismiss has the close button, which no prop removes
  and no configuration cancels. Drag-between-detents has the grabber, which becomes a real
  `<button>` the moment there is more than one detent; its accessible name is an instruction for
  what the next press does, *Make the sheet full screen* or *Make the sheet half the screen*, so
  a reader who cannot see the sheet learns what the control will do. Because the only two detents
  it moves between are half and full, naming the destination also says which one the sheet is at
  now. It reports no disclosure state, because a half-screen sheet is neither open nor shut and
  a word for one would only mislead. With one detent there is nothing to move between and the bar
  is decorative and `aria-hidden`.
  See
  [Target size and motor](../accessibility/target-size-and-motor.mdx).
- **Content scrolls inside the sheet, it is reachable from the keyboard, and scroll chaining is
  prevented.** `Sheet.Content` is Base UI's `Drawer.Content`, and the drawer's pointer handling
  treats a touch that begins inside it as a scroll rather than a swipe; `overscroll-contain`
  stops the page behind from moving when that scroll reaches its end. It also carries
  `tabIndex={0}`, `role="group"` and the sheet's own title as its name, because a scrolling
  region whose contents are not focusable is a keyboard stop only where the browser decides to
  make one. Chrome and Edge do so from 127, and neither Safari nor Firefox does. A list nobody
  can reach with a keyboard is not a scrolling region, it is lost content. The cost is one
  extra tab stop in a sheet whose content already fits. Without `Sheet.Content` nothing scrolls
  and every flick is a drag, which is why its absence raises a development warning.
- **A non-modal sheet does not take focus.** `modal={false}` traps nothing and makes nothing
  inert. Because Base UI resolves initial focus without consulting that flag, it also has to
  decline the focus move explicitly, which it does. Focus is moved on appearance only into a
  modal surface, which is the only case the
  [focus doctrine](../accessibility/keyboard-and-focus.mdx) allows it. Everything else about
  the non-modal path is unaddressed; see the list below.
- **The primary action is not in the scrolling region.** The footer sits outside it, the
  viewport carries Base UI's software-keyboard inset as padding so the sheet shrinks rather than
  being covered, and the safe-area insets are padding *inside* the material so the sheet still
  reaches the edge of the display while its content clears the home indicator. It also stays
  inside the viewport at a sub-full detent, not only at `full`, because the detent's offset is
  reserved as padding inside the popup, so a half-height sheet lays its footer out on the
  visible bottom edge rather than below the screen. See
  [Layout](../foundations/layout.mdx).
- **`prefers-reduced-motion` removes the distance, not the transition, and the drag still
  works.** Under that query the sheet crossfades in place at its detent instead of travelling
  the height of the screen: the transitioned property becomes opacity, and the entering and
  leaving transforms become the resting one. The duration and the easing are left to the token
  layer, which already collapses the sheet spring to 120ms and flat under the same query. What
  a token cannot know is the distance, and the distance is the only thing the component
  overrides. An earlier version of this component removed the transition outright and argued
  the case in a comment; it was wrong, and the page that settles it names `sheet` by name.
  [Motion in health UI](../health/motion-in-health-ui.mdx) asks for exactly this crossfade and
  calls the removal its Don't, because reduced motion means less movement rather than less
  orientation, and a surface that teleports onto the screen is harder to follow than one that
  arrives quickly. The scrim is left alone: its fade has no distance to remove, and the token
  layer collapses `--opsin-duration-base` for it. See also
  [Reduced motion](../accessibility/reduced-motion.mdx).
- **At 200% text the sheet takes the height it needs, and the title wraps rather than being
  clipped.** Every measurement in it is a rem or a fraction of the viewport, the container is
  capped at the viewport rather than at a pixel height, and the header and footer sit outside
  the scrolling region so they cannot overlap the content however tall it becomes. Horizontally
  the popup is `overflow-hidden` for the sake of its top corner, which means a header that
  cannot shrink loses text with no scrollbar, so the title carries `min-w-0 break-words` to
  defeat the `min-width: auto` a flex item takes by default. Ordinary prose was never the
  problem; a single long token at 200% was, whether a compound word or a medication name.
- **The close control's name is visible.** The glyph beside it is decorative and
  `aria-hidden`; the word carries the meaning, which is what makes the control reachable by
  voice and legible to somebody who has never met that glyph.

**Not yet checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- The 44×44 hit area on the grabber, the close control, the scrolling region and the footer's
  actions, and the separation between adjacent targets. They are stated in the component as
  `--opsin-target-minimum` and `--opsin-target-separation`, and measured by nobody. The reason
  is not that the nightly rig has no target here. It has one, because every built registry
  entry is a target and `sheet` is built. The reason is that the rig loads the component's
  `/view` route and measures what is on it, and every control this claim is about is inside a
  portal that is closed when that page loads, so it reaches nothing. Making it measurable means
  opening the sheet before the measurement, which nothing does yet.
- The pair the grabber makes with the close control. With more than one detent the grabber is a
  44pt band and the header below it starts one `--opsin-target-separation` away, which is the
  floor and no more. A miss on Close therefore used to land on a full-width grabber and resize
  the sheet. The grabber is now only as wide as it needs to be and centred, which puts it
  nowhere near the close control at the end of that row, but "nowhere near" is a reading of the
  source and not a measurement.
- Contrast for the close control's foreground and for the grabber's bar against the sheet
  rung's material, each as one pairing. The `<ContrastReport>` below now carries the measured
  token pairs the sheet draws its colour from, and because the sheet is one of the two
  translucent rungs, its material is measured as a composite over both the darkest and the
  lightest backdrop a product can sit it on rather than against its opaque fallback, so the
  material against what is behind it is a measured figure now. The grabber's bar is painted in
  the muted foreground role rather than the border role, because with more than one detent that
  bar is the visible boundary of a control and not a divider, and the muted foreground was
  chosen from the published ramp as the nearest role that clears the 3:1 non-text floor in both
  themes, not measured in a browser. What the table does not isolate is either of those
  foregrounds over the composited material, so both are still read from the source and the
  measurement is still owed.
- The grabber's own focus ring, which can still be clipped. `Sheet.Content` had this exact
  problem and it is fixed: the popup is `overflow-hidden` for the sake of its top corner, so an
  outset focus ring on an element that sits against that clipped edge has its outer line cut
  away rather than drawn, and the content region turns its ring inward so the whole outline
  paints inside the clip. The grabber sits flush against the same clipped top edge and still
  takes the outset ring every other control in the file uses, so the top line of its focus ring
  is cut where the content region's no longer is. The two rings share one root cause, and the
  fix is the same inward offset rather than dropping the clip the top corner radius depends on.
  Turning the grabber's ring inward is a change to the component and not to this page, so until
  it lands a keyboard or switch reader can focus the grabber and be shown a focus indicator that
  is missing its top line. This is read off the source and measured by nobody.
- The software-keyboard behaviour. It needs a device with a software keyboard and a visual
  viewport, and it has not been exercised on one.
- Scroll chaining on iOS Safari specifically, where `overscroll-behavior` has historically been
  the least reliable.
- What a screen reader actually says when the grabber is pressed. The design has an answer
  now, but nobody has heard it. The name is an instruction for the next press, the button reports
  no disclosure state, and it mounts no live region, all on purpose. So the only confirmation after a press is
  the name changing on the control the reader is still focused on, from *Make the sheet full
  screen* to *Make the sheet half the screen* or back. Whether that re-read is enough, and
  whether a reader expects the new height announced as well, is exactly the sort of thing that
  has to be listened to rather than reasoned about. Nothing announces the new height today.
- Everything about `modal={false}` other than the focus move. A non-modal sheet is fixed to the
  bottom edge while the page behind stays focusable, which is the shape WCAG 2.4.11 is about;
  nothing here has been checked against it, and the escape key's behaviour on that path is
  Base UI's rather than this component's.
- `forced-colors: active`. Two things are answered and the rest is not. The panel edge is
  Surface's, and Surface draws it as an `outline` that forced colours keeps and recolours to
  `CanvasText` rather than as a box-shadow it would strip, so the modal keeps a boundary. The
  scrim paints an opaque system `Canvas` background once the palette replaces its tint, so the
  page behind is hidden rather than showing through an undimmed modal. Everything else under
  forced colours is unchecked: whether the focus ring and the grabber's bar hold their
  contrast, whether the fields a form sheet hosts keep a visible invalid edge, and above all
  whether any of it reads right in a real Windows High Contrast session rather than an emulator.
- `prefers-contrast: more`. Under an increased-contrast setting Surface swaps the sheet rung's
  tint for its opaque fallback, drops the backdrop blur and widens the edge to the emphasis
  width, so the sheet loses its translucency rather than gaining a hand-written rule in this
  component. Nothing in `sheet.tsx` answers the query itself, and nobody has opened the page
  under a real increased-contrast setting.
- Anything at all in a browser. Every claim in the section above it is argued from this
  component's source and from Base UI's; the type checker, the linter and the static
  accessibility rig pass, and none of the three renders anything.

component. Sheet asserts no clinical level, paints no category band and renders no measurement
of its own; it is a container, and the two colour axes and the value a reader typed belong to
whatever the caller puts inside it.

## CSS variables

<CssVariablesTable name="sheet" />

Sheet exposes no custom property of its own for a consumer to override, which is why the table
above renders empty. What the source reads instead falls into two groups, and neither is a
component-scoped variable. Base UI writes and reads its own custom properties on the popup for
the resting transform, `--drawer-snap-point-offset`, `--drawer-swipe-movement-y` and

reaching for a variable that is not here to reach for.

## API reference

### Sheet

<PropsTable name="SheetProps" />

`detents` takes the three members of [Detent](../reference/api/Detent.mdx), in order, and the
first is where the sheet opens. It opens there every time, including after a reader has dragged
the last one somewhere else. `half` and `full` are fractions of the viewport and combine
freely; `content` is supported only on its own, for the reason under [Anatomy](#anatomy).

`onOpenChange` takes a second argument the original specification did not declare. It is not
decoration: the accessibility contract on this page requires that a sheet holding unsaved input
asks first, *by the same route as a background tap*, and a product cannot do that unless it is
told which route was taken. A one-argument handler is still assignable to the two-argument
type, so `onOpenChange={setOpen}` keeps working unchanged.

`open` and `onOpenChange` live here rather than on the components that extend Sheet. The
specifications for [LogSheet](./log-sheet.mdx) and [ConsentSheet](./consent-sheet.mdx) both say
they extend Sheet and neither declares either prop, which as written leaves both of them
unopenable. A modal surface owns its own visibility, and a component that extends this one
inherits both props unchanged.

`footer` is a prop and not a compound part, and `className` lands on the container rather than
on the root, because the root renders no element at all. There is no `as`, no `render`, no
`trigger` prop and no `Sheet.Trigger`: a sheet is opened by setting `open`, which is the only
shape that survives a product opening one from a notification, a deep link or a timer.

There is deliberately no way to reach Base UI's third modal state, `'trap-focus'`. Two states
are what this API models, because the page behind is either reachable or it is not. A third
that traps focus while leaving the page scrollable is a surface nobody on this project has
written guidance for.

### Sheet.Content

<PropsTable name="SheetContentProps" />

`className` merges, and the region's own classes win where they conflict. Two of them should not
be overridden by anything: it is the element Base UI identifies as the scroll container, and it
contains its own overscroll. Replace either and a flick inside the sheet starts dismissing it.

### Sheet.Description

<PropsTable name="SheetDescriptionProps" />

Render it inside `Sheet.Content`, as the demo above does, and the popup gains an
`aria-describedby` pointing at it, so the purpose or scope a reader must weigh is announced with
the dialog. There is at most one, and when the caller renders none the popup carries no
`aria-describedby` at all, which is the honest absence the Accessibility section describes.

Bundle cost is measured from a real build, and no build has measured this component yet.
