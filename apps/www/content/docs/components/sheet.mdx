The bottom sheet is the defining interaction of a modern consumer health app, and it is also
where accessibility most often quietly fails: gestures with no equivalent, focus that never
arrives, and a save button under the keyboard. Each of those three has an answer in this
component, and each answer is named in [Accessibility](#accessibility) as either checked by a
gate or argued from the code.

It is a client component, and that is a requirement rather than an optimisation. It holds
which detent it is resting at, it mounts a portal, it moves focus, and it listens for a drag.
None of those has a CSS answer, which is the test [Surface](./surface.mdx) passes and this one
does not.

## Preview

The default sheet: one content-sized detent, dismissible, modal. Open it and look for the two
things that are easiest to leave out — the close control is in the header before you go looking
for it, and the action is pinned below the content rather than sitting at the end of it.

The sheet portals to the end of the document rather than into the frame below, so what it
covers here is this whole page. That is the real behaviour and not an artefact of the preview:
`Drawer.Portal` renders through to `body` wherever it is mounted, and everything outside it is
given `aria-hidden` for as long as the sheet is open.

The distinction from [Dialog](./dialog.mdx) is not size, it is obligation. A sheet is a place
the reader chose to go and can leave at any moment. A dialog is a question that must be
answered. Products that use a sheet for the second case end up with readers dismissing
questions by accident.

`dismissible={false}` is not a way to turn a sheet into a dialog, and the component will not
let it become one. It closes the three ambient routes — the scrim, the escape key and a
downward drag — and it never removes the close control, because a modal surface with no way
out is a trap. The one surface that legitimately has no "went away" outcome is
`Dialog severity="alert"`. The component holds that promise by naming the three routes it
cancels rather than the one it spares, so a close arriving by a route it does not recognise
closes the sheet rather than being swallowed. It was written the other way round once, and for
as long as it was, `dismissible={false}` produced a modal sheet with focus trapped inside it
and no exit at all.

**A sheet is not a privacy boundary**, and nothing about being the top surface makes what it
holds less visible to the room.
[On-screen privacy](../health/on-screen-privacy.mdx) names `sheet` in its `implements` list,
and this is the whole of what the component does about it: nothing, deliberately, in both
directions. Masking a sensitive value belongs to the value rather than to the container,
because only the thing that knows it is a reading knows whether it is one this reader asked to
keep hidden — and rule 3 of that page, that a masked value occupies the same space as the real
one, is a promise a container cannot make on its content's behalf. Covering the window before
the operating system takes its app-switcher snapshot belongs to the host app, because a React
component is never told the app is being backgrounded. A sheet holding something a reader
would not want read over their shoulder needs both of those from somewhere else.

**Scrim** dims and blocks the content behind when the sheet is modal. **Grabber** signals that
the sheet can be dragged, and is never the only way to move it. **Header** names the sheet and
holds the close control. **Content** scrolls internally once it exceeds the detent.
**Footer** pins the primary action above the keyboard and the safe-area inset.

`Sheet.Content` is the only exported part, and it passes the narrow test that
[ADR 0011](../project/decisions/0011-anatomy-parts-are-data-slots.mdx) sets: the consumer
supplies its content, and a sheet may hold things that do not scroll beside the things that do,
so no prop could carry it. Everything else is a `data-slot` — enough to style, not enough to
take apart. The footer arrives as a prop for the same reason in reverse: a prop can carry it,
and the pinning is the point.

Nesting a `Sheet` inside a `Sheet` is a composition error. Two stacked modal surfaces produce
a focus order nobody can predict and an escape key with two plausible meanings. Base UI's
drawer supports nesting deliberately, so the primitive cannot report the mistake; the component
counts its own depth through a context and warns in development.

### Why `content` is the only detent that cannot be combined

Base UI's snap points are fractions of the viewport, pixel values or rem strings — there is no
content-sized one — and every snap point it resolves is clamped to the popup's own measured
height, with the offset computed as `popupHeight − clampedHeight`. So a sheet that can reach
`full` has to be as tall as the viewport, and once it is, its natural height *is* the viewport:
there is no content height left to snap back to.

`content` is therefore implemented as the absence of a snap point rather than the presence of
one. The popup takes its own height, up to the full viewport, and the browser lays it out — no
measurement in JavaScript, nothing frozen at first open, and nothing to go stale when the
content changes. Passing it alongside `half` or `full` drops it with a development warning
rather than approximating it.

## Content guidelines

Title the sheet with what the reader is doing there, as a noun phrase or a short imperative.
The primary action says what it does — *Save reading*, *Choose unit* — and never *Done*, which
tells a reader nothing about what happens to what they typed.

Keep sheets to one task. A sheet with its own tabs is a screen that has not admitted it yet.

Sheet ships two words of its own and no more: the close control reads **Close**, and the
grabber's accessible name is *Sheet height: half the screen* or the equivalent for whichever
detent the sheet is resting at. Neither can be replaced by a prop today, which is a real gap
for any product that is not in English and is an open question on this page. The scrolling
region names itself with the title you passed, so it adds no third string and needs no
translating. Every other word inside a sheet is the product's, and therefore the product's to
check against [Plain English A–Z](../content/plain-english-a-z.mdx).

A sheet that asks the reader to decide something puts **both answers in the footer at equal
weight**. `Sheet.Close` is an exit and not a *no*: it is a footnote-sized text control in the
header, and letting it stand in for declining makes declining harder than accepting, which is
what the safety checklist's consent item forbids. This matters most for the component that
extends this one — see [ConsentSheet](./consent-sheet.mdx) — and it is a rule about the
footer's contents rather than about the base, which cannot see what the two controls mean.

## Accessibility

Some of this is checked by a gate and the rest is an argument. They are separated below because
`alpha` promises that accessibility is claimed only where it has been checked, with the gaps
listed rather than left out.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y` on every pull request and every push to `main`:**

- **Every colour is a token, and every token resolves under the product theme.** The static rig
  fails the build on a hex literal, an `oklch()`, a Tailwind palette colour or a numbered ramp
  step anywhere in `registry/**`, and separately on the chrome-only roles and radii that exist
  in `app/globals.css` and not in `app/product.css`. The sheet paints four roles of its own —
  the muted foreground on the close control, the plain foreground it takes on hover, the border
  role on the grabber's bar and the footer's rule, and the ring role on every focus outline —
  and everything else is Surface's material. The ring is declared in the component rather than
  inherited from `app/product.css` on purpose: that stylesheet does not travel with the file
  into a consumer's project, and a control whose focus ring depends on a stylesheet it was not
  installed with is a control that loses it silently.
- **No `px` font size, and no banned word** in copy or in an identifier. Unlike most components
  in the system this one ships two reader-facing strings, so the copy half of that gate is
  doing real work here rather than guarding a future edit.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **A modal sheet is a dialogue.** Base UI gives the popup the dialog role, points its
  `aria-labelledby` at `Sheet.Title`, moves focus into it on open, traps focus while it is open,
  and returns focus to whatever opened it on close. The page behind is made genuinely inert
  rather than merely dimmed, so assistive technology cannot reach it either — the scrim's
  dimming is the visible half of something that is also true in the accessibility tree.
- **Escape closes a dismissible sheet, and the product is told which route a close came by.**
  `onOpenChange` receives `close-control`, `scrim`, `escape`, `drag` or `other`, which is what
  makes "ask before discarding unsaved input" possible: hold `open` where it is, show the
  question, and close when the reader answers. The five are mapped from Base UI's own nine
  reason strings, and the mapping is typed against that union rather than against `string`, so
  a name that cannot match is a compile error rather than a close silently reclassified as
  `other`. With `dismissible={false}` the escape key, the platform's close watcher, the scrim
  and a drag are all cancelled by name — Base UI's own flag covers the pointer only — and
  nothing else is, so a route the component does not recognise closes the sheet.
- **Every gesture has a control.** Drag-to-dismiss has the close button, which no prop removes
  and no configuration cancels. Drag-between-detents has the grabber, which becomes a real
  `<button>` the moment there is more than one detent; its accessible name states the height
  the sheet is resting at and `aria-expanded` reports the change, so a reader who cannot see
  the sheet can find out where it is rather than only what the next press would do. With one
  detent there is nothing to move between and the bar is decorative and `aria-hidden`. See
  [Target size and motor](../accessibility/target-size-and-motor.mdx).
- **Content scrolls inside the sheet, it is reachable from the keyboard, and scroll chaining is
  prevented.** `Sheet.Content` is Base UI's `Drawer.Content`, and the drawer's pointer handling
  treats a touch that begins inside it as a scroll rather than a swipe; `overscroll-contain`
  stops the page behind from moving when that scroll reaches its end. It also carries
  `tabIndex={0}`, `role="group"` and the sheet's own title as its name, because a scrolling
  region whose contents are not focusable is a keyboard stop only where the browser decides to
  make one — Chrome and Edge from 127, and neither Safari nor Firefox — and a list nobody can
  reach with a keyboard is not a scrolling region, it is lost content. The cost is one extra
  tab stop in a sheet whose content already fits. Without `Sheet.Content` nothing scrolls and
  every flick is a drag, which is why its absence raises a development warning.
- **A non-modal sheet does not take focus.** `modal={false}` traps nothing, makes nothing
  inert, and — because Base UI resolves initial focus without consulting that flag — has to
  decline the focus move explicitly, which it does. Focus is moved on appearance only into a
  modal surface, which is the only case the
  [focus doctrine](../accessibility/keyboard-and-focus.mdx) allows it. Everything else about
  the non-modal path is unaddressed; see the list below.
- **The primary action is not in the scrolling region.** The footer sits outside it, the
  viewport carries Base UI's software-keyboard inset as padding so the sheet shrinks rather than
  being covered, and the safe-area insets are padding *inside* the material so the sheet still
  reaches the edge of the display while its content clears the home indicator. See
  [Layout](../foundations/layout.mdx).
- **`prefers-reduced-motion` removes the distance, not the transition, and the drag still
  works.** Under that query the sheet crossfades in place at its detent instead of travelling
  the height of the screen: the transitioned property becomes opacity, and the entering and
  leaving transforms become the resting one. The duration and the easing are left to the token
  layer, which already collapses the sheet spring to 120ms and flat under the same query — what
  a token cannot know is the distance, and the distance is the only thing the component
  overrides. An earlier version of this component removed the transition outright and argued
  the case in a comment; it was wrong, and the page that settles it names `sheet` by name.
  [Motion in health UI](../health/motion-in-health-ui.mdx) asks for exactly this crossfade and
  calls the removal its Don't, because reduced motion means less movement rather than less
  orientation, and a surface that teleports onto the screen is harder to follow than one that
  arrives quickly. The scrim is left alone: its fade has no distance to remove, and the token
  layer collapses `--opsin-duration-base` for it. See also
  [Reduced motion](../accessibility/reduced-motion.mdx).
- **At 200% text the sheet takes the height it needs, and the title wraps rather than being
  clipped.** Every measurement in it is a rem or a fraction of the viewport, the container is
  capped at the viewport rather than at a pixel height, and the header and footer sit outside
  the scrolling region so they cannot overlap the content however tall it becomes. Horizontally
  the popup is `overflow-hidden` for the sake of its top corner, which means a header that
  cannot shrink loses text with no scrollbar, so the title carries `min-w-0 break-words` to
  defeat the `min-width: auto` a flex item takes by default. Ordinary prose was never the
  problem; a single long token — a compound word, a medication name — at 200% was.
- **The close control's name is visible.** The glyph beside it is decorative and
  `aria-hidden`; the word carries the meaning, which is what makes the control reachable by
  voice and legible to somebody who has never met that glyph.

**Not yet checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- The 44×44 hit area on the grabber, the close control, the scrolling region and the footer's
  actions, and the separation between adjacent targets. They are stated in the component as
  `--opsin-target-minimum` and `--opsin-target-separation`, and measured by nobody. The reason
  is not that the nightly rig has no target here — it has one, because every built registry
  entry is a target and `sheet` is built. The reason is that the rig loads the component's
  `/view` route and measures what is on it, and every control this claim is about is inside a
  portal that is closed when that page loads, so it reaches nothing. Making it measurable means
  opening the sheet before the measurement, which nothing does yet.
- The pair the grabber makes with the close control. With more than one detent the grabber is a
  44pt band and the header below it starts one `--opsin-target-separation` away, which is the
  floor and no more — so a miss on Close used to land on a full-width grabber and resize the
  sheet. The grabber is now only as wide as it needs to be and centred, which puts it nowhere
  near the close control at the end of that row, but "nowhere near" is a reading of the source
  and not a measurement.
- Contrast for the close control's muted foreground against the sheet rung's material, and for
  the material against whatever the product puts behind it. `<ContrastReport>` below takes its
  rows as a prop and nothing wires the measured file into it yet.
- The software-keyboard behaviour. It needs a device with a software keyboard and a visual
  viewport, and it has not been exercised on one.
- Scroll chaining on iOS Safari specifically, where `overscroll-behavior` has historically been
  the least reliable.
- What a screen reader actually says when the grabber is pressed. The design has an answer now
  — the name states the current height and `aria-expanded` changes with it, which is a state
  change the platform announces without the component mounting a live region, and it mounts
  none deliberately — but nobody has heard it. Whether *expanded* and *collapsed* are useful
  words for *full* and *half* is exactly the sort of thing that has to be listened to rather
  than reasoned about.
- Everything about `modal={false}` other than the focus move. A non-modal sheet is fixed to the
  bottom edge while the page behind stays focusable, which is the shape WCAG 2.4.11 is about;
  nothing here has been checked against it, and the escape key's behaviour on that path is
  Base UI's rather than this component's.
- `forced-colors: active`. Nothing in opsinjs answers it yet.
- Anything at all in a browser. Every claim in the section above it is argued from this
  component's source and from Base UI's; the type checker, the linter and the static
  accessibility rig pass, and none of the three renders anything.

## API reference

### Sheet

<PropsTable name="SheetProps" />

`detents` takes the three members of [Detent](../reference/api/Detent.mdx), in order, and the
first is where the sheet opens — every time, including after a reader has dragged the last one
somewhere else. `half` and `full` are fractions of the viewport and combine freely; `content`
is supported only on its own, for the reason under [Anatomy](#anatomy).

`onOpenChange` takes a second argument the original specification did not declare. It is not
decoration: the accessibility contract on this page requires that a sheet holding unsaved input
asks first, *by the same route as a background tap*, and a product cannot do that unless it is
told which route was taken. A one-argument handler is still assignable to the two-argument
type, so `onOpenChange={setOpen}` keeps working unchanged.

`open` and `onOpenChange` live here rather than on the components that extend Sheet. The
specifications for [LogSheet](./log-sheet.mdx) and [ConsentSheet](./consent-sheet.mdx) both say
they extend Sheet and neither declares either prop, which as written leaves both of them
unopenable. A modal surface owns its own visibility, and a component that extends this one
inherits both props unchanged.

`footer` is a prop and not a compound part, and `className` lands on the container rather than
on the root, because the root renders no element at all. There is no `as`, no `render`, no
`trigger` prop and no `Sheet.Trigger`: a sheet is opened by setting `open`, which is the only
shape that survives a product opening one from a notification, a deep link or a timer.

There is deliberately no way to reach Base UI's third modal state, `'trap-focus'`. Two states
are what this API models — the page behind is either reachable or it is not — and a third that
traps focus while leaving the page scrollable is a surface nobody on this project has written
guidance for.

### Sheet.Content

<PropsTable name="SheetContentProps" />

`className` merges, and the region's own classes win where they conflict. Two of them should not
be overridden by anything: it is the element Base UI identifies as the scroll container, and it
contains its own overscroll. Replace either and a flick inside the sheet starts dismissing it.
