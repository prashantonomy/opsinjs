ResultCard is the composition the whole health-data-display family exists to serve. It is
one result, complete: the measurement, its context, its meaning and its consequence, in the
order a reader needs them rather than the order a database returns them.

It is also the component that proves the system composes. It formats no number, draws no
level, positions no tick and decides nothing about when a reading is old — every one of
those is delegated to a component that owns it, and what is left here is the arrangement.
If this card needed a rule of its own, the rule would be missing from somewhere else.

## Preview

One card, whole: title, time, level, reading, the comparison, the paragraph and two next
steps. The interval is fictional and cites *Example data — not a reference range*, which is
the only source an opsinjs example may name.

Open it under the product theme and turn the text size up. Two claims are meant to be
checked rather than believed: that the header wraps and the pill drops beneath the title
instead of the title being clipped, and that in greyscale the level is still legible from
its word and its glyph rather than from its colour.

```tsx
import { EXAMPLE_SOURCE } from "@/lib/opsinjs"
```

```tsx
<ResultCard
  title="Example measurement"
  value={14}
  unit="mg/dL"
  precision={0}
  measuredAt="2026-03-14T08:12:00+00:00"
  now="2026-03-14T11:12:00+00:00"
  range={{ low: 10, high: 20, source: EXAMPLE_SOURCE }}
  status="steady"
  category="labs"
  meaning="Two or three short sentences, in the second person, written by the product."
/>
```

The last line of the *use* list is the one worth reading twice. A ResultCard renders the
number at display size, the interval it is compared against and the level somebody assigned
to it, which is exactly the payload
[Notifications and off-screen alerts](../health/notifications-and-off-screen-alerts.mdx)
keeps out of a notification body: no result values, no diagnoses, no medication names, no
condition names on a surface that appears on a lock screen in front of whoever is in the
room. Put the card on the screen the notification resolves to, behind authentication, and
let the notification say only what it is about.

The line between ResultCard and [MetricTile](./metric-tile.mdx) is the amount of room the
result gets to explain itself. A tile is a number you glance at on the way somewhere else;
a card is a number you stop and read. If your layout cannot give the result three lines of
plain English, it is a tile.

The line between ResultCard and [Card](./card.mdx) is the contract. A Card is a box with a
material and some padding, and what goes in it is yours. A ResultCard has a fixed internal
order, refuses to derive a level, refuses to tint its own surface and says out loud when
the explanation is missing — none of which a Card can do for you, and all of which is the
reason to reach for it.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** That a specific measurement was taken, at a specific time, in a
specific unit, and — where the product has supplied them — where it sits against a stated
reference range and which clinical status the product has assigned to it. Every one of
those is a separate claim and each may be absent independently. An absent one renders as an
absence: no interval means no bar, no level means no pill, no explanation means a sentence
saying there is none.

**What it must never be read as.** A diagnosis, a summary of the reader's health, or advice
from a clinician. The card may carry a next step, but the next step is the product's, and
the card must make its origin obvious — *your clinic asks you to repeat this test in three
months* is a different sentence from *repeat this test in three months*, and the difference
is who is speaking. That attribution lives in the words the product writes, in `meaning`
and in an action's label; the component has no `attribution` prop and cannot supply one for
you.

**Which colour axis it may use.** Both, on different elements, and that is what makes this
card the worked example of [the two colour axes](../health/two-colour-axes.mdx). The
**status** axis reaches exactly one element: the [StatusPill](./status-pill.mdx) in the
header. The **category** axis reaches exactly one element: the title. The card's own surface
stays neutral at every level, and there is no prop that tints it — a card whose entire
background turns amber converts a single measurement into an emotional event, and a screen
with four of them is unreadable. See [Alarm fatigue](../health/alarm-fatigue.mdx).

A card given both a `category` and a `status` is the correct shape rather than the
collision, which is why this component does **not** call `axisConflict()` on its own props.
That function answers a question about one element, and calling it on a props object would
report OPSIN-0001 for every correctly built card in the system.

**Who owns the thresholds.** The consuming product. ResultCard has no clinical rules, no
default ranges, no status derivation and no staleness boundary. There is no comparison
anywhere in the file between a reading and a bound: turning *outside the range* into *needs
attention* requires a clinician or a validated rule, and it belongs to whoever owns the
range. Most people sit outside at least one reference interval at any moment.

**What the card cannot tell you about its own number.** `provenance` is free text. There is
no provenance class on it and none anywhere in opsinjs yet, so this card cannot tell a
laboratory result from a wrist estimate or from something the reader typed — and
[Data provenance and device accuracy](../health/data-provenance-and-device-accuracy.mdx)
bounds what an interface may assert by exactly that class: a device-estimated or
self-reported value may not carry a clinical status on its own. The card renders whatever
`status` you pass, so that rule is yours to keep rather than one the component can enforce.
Say the class in the words you put in `provenance` — *from your cuff*, *estimated by your
watch*, *you entered this* — because that string is currently the only place it can live.

**A result that may distress, and the `urgent` level.** `status` accepts `urgent` and this
card does nothing special with it: it renders a pill and it stays a card the reader chose to
open. [Delivering difficult results](../health/delivering-difficult-results.mdx) asks for
more than the card can supply on its own — the reader is told something is coming before it
is shown, they choose the moment to open it, the plain summary comes before the numbers, and
the surface ends in a route to a person rather than on the figure. The last thing this
component renders is the provenance footnote, so if the result may distress, the route to a
person is an `action` you attach and the announcement is a step you build around the card.
An `urgent` level that must interrupt belongs in an [AlertBanner](./alert-banner.mdx), which
is the only component in the system that announces assertively.

**Privacy is not handled here.** [On-screen privacy](../health/on-screen-privacy.mdx) names
this card as an implementing surface and it has no masking affordance: no `masked` prop, no
fixed-width mask, nothing that hides the label where the label is the disclosure, and
nothing that obscures the card when the app is backgrounded. It renders the number at
display size in the position the eye lands on first, which is the exposure that page opens
with. A product that needs masking builds it around the card today. This is a gap, and it is
listed as one below rather than left to be discovered.

**Staleness is part of the meaning, and the boundary is yours.** `staleAfterHours` is passed
straight through to [RelativeTime](./relative-time.mdx), which applies it. Omit it and there
is no staleness treatment at all — the honest output when nobody has said what *old* means
for this measurement. Supply it and the timestamp past the boundary carries the words *may
be out of date* as text, muted as a scanning aid rather than as the message. The words are
hedged deliberately: the component knows the reading is older than a boundary somebody else
set, and it does not know that the reading is wrong. See
[Uncertainty, staleness and missing data](../health/uncertainty-and-staleness.mdx).

No preview on this page shows that state, and none can. A number reaching `staleAfterHours`
in a shipped example is a staleness default, and those are forbidden for any metric in any
population — including in example data, and including behind a constant whose name says it
belongs to the example. So the treatment is described here and demonstrated nowhere, which
is a real cost stated rather than hidden.

Age lowers what a reading is worth; it does not raise the alarm. A value the app cannot
vouch for must not drive an `attention` or an `urgent` level: if you are not sure the number
is current, you are not entitled to alarm somebody with it. The response to a stale reading
is an action that gets a fresh one.

<SafetyCallout severity="attention" evidence="opinion">
A result the reader has never seen before should not arrive with no way to understand it.
When `meaning` is absent this card says so — *we do not have an explanation for this result
yet* — rather than rendering nothing, and the product should attach an action that leads
somewhere that does explain it. Silence reads as *this is fine*, and silence is the default
no one chose.
</SafetyCallout>

The card is a composition rather than a monolith because real products need to drop parts:
a print view has no actions, a notification body has no chart, an embedded card inside a
timeline has no footnote. Each part is independently omittable and the card's meaning
degrades predictably as they go. Two parts do not omit: the title, which is the card's own
name, and the meaning, which renders its absence in words rather than vanishing.

The parts are identified by `data-slot` and are styleable from outside; none of them is an
export, and there is no dot-notation API. Content arrives as a prop, so there is nothing a
compound export would let you place that a prop does not.

Nesting a second `StatusPill` anywhere inside the card, or placing a `RangeBar` above
`ResultCard.Reading`, is a composition error: the reading is the subject and the bar is its
context, and reversing them changes what the card is about. The embedded bar is given no
`status` for the same reason — a bar with a level draws its own word and glyph, and the
card would then show two objects for one assertion.

## Examples

### A reading made of two numbers

The specification typed `value` as `number | string | null` so a pair could be passed as
`"128/78"`, while its own part tree routed every reading through [Value](./value.mdx),
whose value is a number or nothing. The string form is gone: a compound reading is a
`segments` array, and each part is a real `Value`. On screen the unit is printed once after
the pair; read aloud, every part is named and every number keeps its own spoken unit.

<ComponentPreview name="result-card-a-compound-reading" kind="example" align="start" />

### The smallest card, and the sentence it will not leave out

A number, a unit and a time, and nothing else — no interval, no level, no explanation, no
next step. That is still a ResultCard, on one condition: a card with no explanation says
so. Notice what is *not* drawn. No bar, because no interval was supplied. No pill, because
no level was assigned. No footnote, because nobody said where the reading came from.

<ComponentPreview name="result-card-no-explanation-supplied" kind="example" align="start" />

### The same result at two ages

Two cards, identical in every clinical respect and differing only in when they were
measured. They carry the same level, and that is the example rather than an oversight: age
lowers what a reading is worth and does not raise the alarm, so a card the product cannot
vouch for must not be escalated on its age alone. What tells the reader the difference is
the timestamp, in words and with the absolute date beside it, on screen and in the
accessibility tree and on paper.

Neither card shows the staleness treatment, and no example here can. Supplying
`staleAfterHours` in a shipped example means shipping a staleness boundary, which is
forbidden for any metric in any population — so the state is described in *Clinical meaning*
above and demonstrated nowhere. Pass your own boundary and the timestamp past it carries the
words *may be out of date*, muted as a scanning aid rather than as the message, because an
opacity-only treatment disappears under increased contrast, in greyscale and on paper.

## Content guidelines

The title is the everyday name of the test with the clinical name available as a
[Term](./term.mdx). The meaning paragraph is two or three short sentences: what the test
looks at, what this result means in context, and what usually happens next. Write it in the
second person, in the present tense, and without hedging so heavily that it says nothing.

Actions are imperative and specific. *Book a repeat test* is an action; *learn more* is a
link pretending to be one. Attribute the instruction inside the label or the paragraph — the
component cannot tell whether a clinician or a rule produced the next step, and the reader
cannot either unless you say.

The one string this component owns is the sentence it renders when `meaning` is absent, and
it is deliberately the smallest honest thing that can go there: it names what is missing and
says nothing at all about what the reading means.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"This test looks at how your kidneys have been working over the past few months. Your
    result is slightly below the usual range. Your clinic has asked you to repeat it in
    three months."** Names the test, places the result, attributes the next step.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **"Your eGFR is abnormal. You should see a doctor."** An unexplained acronym, a banned
    word, and advice with no author. The reader cannot tell whether a person looked at this
    or a rule fired.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"Measured 4 months ago · may be out of date."** Staleness stated as a fact about the
    data, in words, with the absolute date beside it.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **"4 months ago" in grey 12px type under the number.** Technically present, practically
    invisible, and the reader acts on a number that is out of date.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

## Accessibility

Some of this is enforced by a gate on every pull request and every push to `main`. The rest
is argued, and it is labelled as such: `alpha` promises that accessibility is claimed only
where it has been checked, with the gaps listed rather than left out. Nothing here has been
through a review with a disabled user, and `a11yDate` is therefore still empty.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y` on every pull request and every push to `main`:**

- **No element takes colour from both axes.** The gate reads every JSX element in the file
  and fails on one carrying both `data-category` and `data-status`, or writing a category
  colour and a status colour into the same opening tag. On this card the two are on sibling
  elements — the title and the pill — which is the arrangement the rule exists to produce
  rather than an exception to it.
- **This file paints no status colour and stamps no `data-status`.** The level reaches the
  screen only through the embedded [StatusPill](./status-pill.mdx), which reads its word from
  `CLINICAL_STATUS_META` and its glyph from the same table. No status word is written as a
  literal anywhere here; the gate fails the build on one that is.
- **No shipped threshold, and no unowned interval.** A name matching *stale*, *threshold*,
  *cutoff*, *limit*, *minimum*, *maximum*, *expiry*, *band* or *plausib* given a numeric
  literal is an error anywhere under `registry/`, including by way of a named constant; so
  is an object literal with two numeric bounds and no `source` beside them. There is no
  staleness boundary, no interval and no plausibility bound in this component **or in any of
  its examples**. An earlier example supplied one under an `EXAMPLE_`-prefixed name; the
  prefix does not reach a staleness boundary, because renaming a number does not change what
  it decides, and the number is gone rather than renamed. Every interval on this page is a
  two-bound literal citing `EXAMPLE_SOURCE`, which is the one attribution an opsinjs example
  may carry and the only form the gate accepts.
- **No type size in pixels and no raw colour literal**, so the card scales with the reader's
  own text size and takes every colour from the two axes and the bridged neutral roles.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **The card is one region with a name.** The root is an `<article>` — exposed to a screen
  reader's rotor whether or not it is named, which a `<div>` is not — with `aria-label` set
  from the title. The name is the title alone and not the title plus the reading; see the
  gap listed below.
- **Reading order matches visual order**: title, time, level, reading, comparison, meaning,
  actions, footnote. Nothing is re-ordered by CSS against the DOM, and the header's wrap at
  large text sizes moves the pill down the page rather than moving it in the tree.
- **The level is announced with its word.** The pill renders the word from
  `CLINICAL_STATUS_META`, a glyph that is one of four distinct shapes, and the colour, in
  that order of reliance — which is what the measured CVD audit in `tokens/color.json`
  requires. It is given the title as its subject, so a listener hears *Needs attention,
  Example measurement* rather than a level floating free. See
  [Health glyphs](../foundations/iconography/health-glyphs.mdx).
- **Units are spoken, not spelled.** Every number goes through [Value](./value.mdx), which
  substitutes the unit's spoken form from `tokens/units.json`. On a compound reading the
  visible symbol is printed once, after the last part, and the unit is left in the
  accessibility tree on every part — so a listener hears it after each number and a reader
  sees it once. The suppression is `sr-only` and not `display: none`, which is the whole of
  the guarantee: `Value` marks the visible symbol `aria-hidden` only when it found a spoken
  form for it, and leaves it announced when the table has no entry, so hiding it outright
  would turn every part but the last into a bare number for exactly the units nobody has
  taught the system to pronounce. It is also suppressed only when the last part will
  actually print a symbol — a compound reading whose final part is `null` renders each part's
  own unit instead, because `Value`'s absence form carries none.
- **Actions are real links and real buttons**, each carrying the 44pt floor on both axes
  from `--opsin-target-minimum`, with `--opsin-target-separation` between them. The floor is
  set in the component rather than inherited from the product stylesheet, because that
  stylesheet does not travel with the file. The recommended one is bordered and on a larger
  target and the alternative is neither, so the difference survives greyscale and a
  black-and-white printout — it is not carried by hue. It is not carried by a fill either,
  which the page used to claim: the recommended link's surface is `bg-card`, the card's own
  ground, so on this card it paints nothing. The boundary and the size are the difference.
- **The card reflows at 200% text.** The header wraps, the reading wraps, the meaning is
  held to a comfortable measure rather than a fixed width, and nothing is truncated: there
  is no ellipsis and no fixed height anywhere in the file. Reasoned from the source; nobody
  has looked at it.
- **Nothing animates**, on first paint or afterwards. No count on the number, no growth on
  the bar, no entrance on the card.
- **The card mounts no live region.** A result whose level changes while it is on screen is
  a change the surrounding region should announce, politely; a card that mounted its own
  would speak on every render and would speak twice wherever the caller had already done the
  right thing. Only `urgent` is assertive, and only through
  [AlertBanner](./alert-banner.mdx). See [Screen readers](../accessibility/screen-readers.mdx).

**Not checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- **The accessible name is the title, not the title and the reading.** The specification asks
  for a name shaped as the measurement's title followed by its reading and unit. Building
  that string means formatting the number here, which this component refuses to do, or
  pointing `aria-labelledby` at the title and the reading, which needs ids a server component
  cannot mint — `useId` is a hook and would put every card in the system into the client
  bundle. The reading is the next thing announced after the name; nobody has listened to
  whether that is enough.
- **Every contrast pair.** `scripts/check-contrast.mts` has not been run for this component,
  which is why the report below is empty rather than reassuring. The category ink on the
  title is the pair to check first: `tokens/color.json` tunes an `ink` role against its own
  `-surface`, and here it is painted on `bg-card` instead, which is a different pairing from
  the one the token contract guarantees.
- **Print.** The card grows a real border rather than relying on a material's inset shadow,
  which is the boundary a printer keeps — but opsinjs has no print stylesheet, nothing here
  has been put on paper, and the specification's requirement that a printed card carry the
  range as an outline is inherited from [RangeBar](./range-bar.mdx) rather than tested.
- **How often the composite says the same thing.** This was previously described as one
  repetition and it is larger than that. On the card in the preview above, the measurement's
  name is announced six times: as the `<article>`'s label, as the heading, as the pill's
  subject, as the bar's visually-hidden label, and twice more as the opening subject of the
  bar's `aria-label` and of its visible summary sentence. Those last two are the same
  twenty-six-word sentence, back to back, with nothing between them, so a listener who has
  heard the graphic named has no way to tell that the paragraph after it is not new. The
  reading itself is spoken three times and the measurement date twice, once by the header and
  once by the bar's footnote. The bar owns the label-and-summary pair and says so on its own
  page; this card adds the article's name over the identical heading, and the second date.
  Nobody has listened to any of it, and it should be measured before it is changed: the two
  candidates worth testing are naming the article by `aria-labelledby` on the heading, which
  is the same `useId` problem listed above, and not passing `measuredAt` to the embedded bar.
- **No masking affordance.** The card has no `masked` prop, no fixed-width mask and no
  behaviour on backgrounding, and it renders the number at display size in the position the
  eye lands on first. [On-screen privacy](../health/on-screen-privacy.mdx) names this
  component, and none of what that page asks for is implemented here or anywhere in opsinjs.
- **Provenance has no class.** `provenance` is a free-text string. Nothing in the card, or in
  the substrate behind it, distinguishes a clinically measured value from a device-estimated
  or self-reported one, so the rule that an estimated value may not carry a clinical status
  on its own cannot be checked from anything this component knows.
- **`measuredAt` has no absence form.** It is required and non-nullable, so a card given
  `value={null}` still states an instant at which the missing reading was measured. Every
  other claim on the card degrades to a stated absence and this one does not.
  [RelativeTime](./relative-time.mdx) has no absence form either, which is where one would
  belong; inventing a sentence here would put a second copy of that rule in this file.
- **`prefers-contrast` and `forced-colors`**, which are not implemented anywhere in opsinjs,
  so no component can currently make a claim about either.

## API reference

<PropsTable name="ResultCardProps" />

Four props are additions to the interface this page proposed while it was unbuilt, and each
is named here rather than slipped in.

`now` is required, and it is the largest of the four. `ResultCard.Time` is a
[RelativeTime](./relative-time.mdx), which refuses to read the clock itself: a component
that read it would read it once per card rather than once per screen, and a page of results
would disagree with itself across a minute boundary. Read it once where the screen is
rendered and pass the same value to every card on it.

`precision` and `locale` exist because
[Numbers, units and precision](../health/numbers-units-precision.mdx) governs this page and
every number on the card goes through [Value](./value.mdx), which takes both. Without them a
card could not state the decimal places its measurement was reported to, and the bar's two
boundary labels would be shown to a different precision from the reading they bound.
`locale` reaches every number and every date, so one card cannot show two conventions.

`titleLevel` exists because the title is a real heading and the level belongs to the page
rather than to the component. Appearance does not follow it.

`value` is `number | null` and is **not** `number | string | null`, which is the one place
this implementation contradicts its own specification. The proposed type allowed a string so
a pair could be passed as `"128/78"`, while the part tree routed every reading through
`Value`, whose value is a number or nothing. A string cannot be rounded to the measurement's
precision, cannot be shaped for a locale, cannot have its unit spoken and cannot be
published as a machine-readable datum — every one of which is something the rest of this
system has already promised about numbers. The compound reading is `segments` instead. An
ADR records the change.

`ResultSegment` and `ResultAction` are exported alongside the props interface. The generator
emits a table only for interfaces named `<Pascal>Props`, so these are the shipped
declarations, copied from `registry/bases/base/result-card.tsx`:

```ts
export interface ResultSegment {
  /** What this part of the reading is, in the reader's language. Announced, not drawn. */
  label: string
  /** This part's number. `null` means this part is missing, and is never rendered as 0. */
  value: number | null
}

export interface ResultAction {
  /** Imperative, specific, and honest about who is asking. */
  label: string
  /** Where it goes. Preferred over `onSelect` wherever the step has an address. */
  href?: string
  /** What it does, where there is nowhere to go. */
  onSelect?: () => void
  /** At most one per card. Rendered in the fuller treatment, and expected first. */
  recommended?: boolean
}
```

Several constraints are stated in prose and enforced at runtime rather than in the type,
because this file ships as source into JavaScript projects where a type is advice: at most
two actions, at most one of them recommended, one reading rather than both a `value` and a
`segments` array, a `titleLevel` inside 2–6, and a non-blank `label` on every segment. Each
is reported once per distinct offender in development and each renders the honest fallback —
the third action is dropped, the first recommendation is the one that is drawn, the compound
reading wins, the heading falls back to `h3` rather than emitting an `h7` that is not a
heading at all, and an unlabelled segment is dropped rather than announced as a bare number.
A blank `title` is the one case with no fallback: it warns, and the card renders no heading
element rather than an empty one. None of them is an `OPSIN-` code: those
describe mistakes a consumer makes with the clinical API, they are a versioned contract
generated from `tokens/errors.json`, and a component may not mint one. The two mistakes that
do have codes are reported through them — an unknown `category` is OPSIN-0010, and a range
with no `source` is OPSIN-0004, raised by the bar rather than by the card.

There is no `attribution` prop, no `disclaimer` prop and no `density` prop. The first two
are copy the product writes; the third is a decision of the screen the card is on, and it
belongs in `className`.
