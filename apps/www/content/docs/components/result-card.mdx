ResultCard is the composition the whole health-data-display family exists to serve. It is
one result, complete: the measurement, its context, its meaning and its consequence, in the
order a reader needs them rather than the order a database returns them.

The line between ResultCard and [MetricTile](./metric-tile.mdx) is the amount of room the
result gets to explain itself. A tile is a number you glance at on the way somewhere else;
a card is a number you stop and read. If your layout cannot give the result three lines of
plain English, it is a tile.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** That a specific measurement was taken, at a specific time, in a
specific unit, and — where the product has supplied them — where it sits against a stated
reference range and which clinical status the product has assigned to it. Every one of
those is a separate claim and each may be absent independently.

**What it must never be read as.** A diagnosis, a summary of the reader's health, or advice
from a clinician. The card may carry a next step, but the next step is the product's, and
the card must make its origin obvious — *your clinic asks you to repeat this test in three
months* is a different sentence from *repeat this test in three months*, and the difference
is who is speaking.

**Which colour axis it may use.** The **status** axis, on the pill and on nothing else. The
card's own surface stays neutral at every status. A card whose entire background turns amber
converts a single measurement into an emotional event, and a screen with four such cards is
unreadable — see [Alarm fatigue](../health/alarm-fatigue.mdx). The **category** axis may
colour the heading icon so a reader can find the heart results among the sleep results, and
may do nothing else.

**Who owns the thresholds.** The consuming product. ResultCard has no clinical rules, no
default ranges and no status derivation. It renders what it is given and refuses to guess
what it is not.

**Staleness is part of the meaning.** A result carries the time it was measured, and past a
product-defined threshold the card must say so rather than only showing a relative time in
small type. A four-month-old blood pressure reading is not a current blood pressure reading,
and the card is the last place that distinction can still be made — see
[Uncertainty, staleness and missing data](../health/uncertainty-and-staleness.mdx).

<SafetyCallout severity="attention" evidence="opinion">
A result the reader has never seen before should not arrive with no way to understand it.
If the product cannot supply plain-English meaning for a result, the honest card says so —
*we do not have an explanation for this test* — and links to somewhere that does. Silence
reads as *this is fine*, and silence is the default no one chose.
</SafetyCallout>

## Anatomy

<Anatomy name="result-card" />

<CompositionTree name="result-card" />

The card is a composition rather than a monolith because real products need to drop parts:
a print view has no actions, a notification body has no chart, an embedded card inside a
timeline has no footnote. Each part is independently omittable and the card's meaning
degrades predictably as they go.

**Header** carries what was measured and when, plus the status pill. **Reading** is the
number itself at display size with its unit — the largest thing on the card, because it is
what the reader came for. **Range** is the embedded [RangeBar](./range-bar.mdx), present
only when a reference range exists. **Meaning** is the plain-English paragraph, the part
that most result UIs omit and the part that decides whether the card is useful. **Actions**
are the next steps, at most two. **Footnote** carries provenance: who measured it, with
what, and where the range came from.

Proposed part hierarchy. This is the specification the implementation must match.

```text
ResultCard                     root; surface stays neutral at every status
├── ResultCard.Header
│   ├── ResultCard.Title       what was measured, in the reader's words
│   ├── ResultCard.Time        <RelativeTime> with an absolute fallback
│   └── StatusPill             the only status-coloured element on the card
├── ResultCard.Reading
│   └── Value                  number + unit at display size
├── RangeBar                   optional; omitted entirely when no range exists
├── ResultCard.Meaning         plain-English paragraph; may contain <Term>
├── ResultCard.Actions         at most two; the first is the recommended one
└── ResultCard.Footnote        provenance, and the not-medical-advice note by rule
```

Nesting a second `StatusPill` anywhere inside the card, or placing a `RangeBar` above
`ResultCard.Reading`, is a composition error: the reading is the subject and the bar is its
context, and reversing them changes what the card is about.

```ts
// code against. It will change without a deprecation cycle.

import type { ClinicalStatus, HealthCategory } from "opsinjs"
import type { ReferenceRange } from "opsinjs"

export interface ResultAction {
  /** Imperative, specific, and honest about who is asking. */
  label: string
  href?: string
  onSelect?: () => void
  /** Exactly one action per card may be `recommended`. */
  recommended?: boolean
}

export interface ResultCardProps {
  /** What was measured, in the reader's language. */
  title: string
  value: number | string | null
  unit?: string
  /** ISO timestamp of measurement, not of retrieval. */
  measuredAt: string
  /** Omit when no reference range is available. Never defaulted. */
  range?: ReferenceRange
  /** Assigned by the product. Never derived from `value` and `range`. */
  status?: ClinicalStatus
  /** Affects the header icon only. Never the card surface. */
  category?: HealthCategory
  /**
   * The plain-English paragraph. When absent the card renders an explicit
   * "no explanation available" state rather than nothing.
   */
  meaning?: React.ReactNode
  /** At most two. More than two is a screen, not a card. */
  actions?: ResultAction[]
  /** Who measured it, with what device or assay, and where the range came from. */
  provenance?: string
  /**
   * Hours after which the card shows its stale treatment. The product owns this
   * number because it is clinical, not visual.
   */
  staleAfterHours?: number
}
```

## Content guidelines

The title is the everyday name of the test with the clinical name available as a
[Term](./term.mdx). The meaning paragraph is two or three short sentences: what the test
looks at, what this result means in context, and what usually happens next. Write it in the
second person, in the present tense, and without hedging so heavily that it says nothing.

Actions are imperative and specific. *Book a repeat test* is an action; *learn more* is a
link pretending to be one.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"This test looks at how your kidneys have been working over the past few months. Your
    result is slightly below the usual range. Your clinic has asked you to repeat it in
    three months."** Names the test, places the result, attributes the next step.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **"Your eGFR is abnormal. You should see a doctor."** An unexplained acronym, a banned
    word, and advice with no author. The reader cannot tell whether a person looked at this
    or a rule fired.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"Measured 4 months ago. This may not reflect your current reading."** Staleness stated
    as a fact with a consequence.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **"4 months ago" in grey 12px type under the number.** Technically present, practically
    invisible, and the reader acts on a number that is out of date.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

## Accessibility requirements

Requirements for the implementation, not results.

- **The card is one region with an accessible name** taken from its title and reading, so a
  screen-reader user scanning by region hears *HbA1c, 44 millimoles per mole* rather than
  *group*.
- **Reading order matches visual order**: title, time, status, reading, range, meaning,
  actions, footnote. This is the order a reader needs and it must not be re-arranged by CSS
  into something the DOM contradicts.
- **The status pill is announced with its word, not its colour.** See
  [Screen readers](../accessibility/screen-readers.mdx) for the status-change announcement
  contract, which applies when a card's status changes while it is on screen.
- **Units are spoken, not symbolic.** `mmol/mol` renders visually as a symbol and is
  announced in full. See
  [Numbers, units and precision](../health/numbers-units-precision.mdx).
- **Actions are real buttons or links** with a 44pt minimum target and 8pt separation, and
  the recommended one is not distinguished by colour alone. See
  [Target size and motor](../accessibility/target-size-and-motor.mdx).
- **The card reflows at 200% text** to a single column with the reading above the bar, and
  never truncates the meaning paragraph behind a *more* control at large text sizes.
- **Print** produces the whole card with no colour: title, reading, range as an outline,
  meaning, provenance. Cards are printed and taken to appointments.
