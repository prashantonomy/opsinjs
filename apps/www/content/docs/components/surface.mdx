## Status

`surface` is implemented and installable. It is **alpha**: it works, the material it
renders comes entirely from measured tokens, the API may change in any release without a
deprecation cycle, and it is not for a production health surface yet. What keeps it at
alpha is the last list in [Accessibility](#accessibility), and it is stated there rather
than summarised here because most of it is a measurement nobody has taken — including the
one for the translucent material this component exists to make safe.

Surface is the least visible component in the system and the one most likely to be
mistaken for decoration. It is the mechanism that makes translucency safe: without a
guaranteed floor, a blurred panel is a readability bug waiting for the wrong wallpaper —
and the wallpaper is whatever the reader happens to be scrolling past.

It is a server component, and that is a requirement rather than an optimisation. Every
state it has is a user preference or an engine capability, each is answered by a media or
support query, and reading any of them in JavaScript would mean rendering one material on
the server and swapping to another after hydration. A flash of the wrong depth is a
cosmetic bug on a marketing page and a legibility bug on the surface holding somebody's
result.

## Preview

The whole ladder over a deliberately hostile backdrop: the page's darkest and lightest

The last entry is a performance limit, not a style preference. Composited translucent
layers are expensive on mid-range Android hardware, and the fourth one is where scrolling
starts to stutter on exactly the devices most likely to belong to the people this system is
for. Surface does not count them, and the [Status](#status) section says why. See
[Performance budget](../foundations/materials/performance-budget.mdx).

### The rung names, and the two that mean a different depth

This page used to name the six rungs `base`, `raised`, `panel`, `overlay`, `sheet`,
`chrome`. Those names are retired. The rung names are the token names —
[ADR 0014](../project/decisions/0014-material-rung-names.mdx) settles it — because they are
the ones a stylesheet resolves and a drift gate checks, and choosing a prose set instead
would have meant renaming six generated custom properties in order to change a word.

Translate by **job**, never by position on the ladder. Three of the retired names are also
current names, and two of those moved:

| Retired name | What it meant | Rung now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `base` | the page itself | `canvas` |
| `raised` | a bounded block of content | `card` |
| `panel` | lifted above the page, not covering it | `raised` |
| `overlay` | covering the page, which stays recognisable | `sheet` |
| `sheet` | a dismissible bottom sheet | `sheet` |
| `chrome` | a pinned toolbar or tab bar | `overlay` |

Read the fourth and sixth rows twice. `overlay` on this ladder is chrome that content
scrolls *beneath*; the panel that covers the page is `sheet`. A positional translation puts
a dismissible sheet on the chrome rung and a tab bar on the sheet rung, and both of those
compile, render and look almost right. A search-and-replace across an older design file is
not a safe migration for the three shared names — only `base`, `panel` and `chrome` are
unambiguous, and those are the three the component can name a replacement for when it warns
in development. The rows above are that warning's source of truth; if the two ever disagree,
the warning is the bug.

For choosing between rungs at all, the decision table on
[Choosing a layer](../foundations/materials/choosing-a-layer.mdx) asks the questions in the
order that resolves them fastest, and [The material ladder](../foundations/materials/the-ladder.mdx)
carries the live specimen.

**Backdrop** is the blur and saturation applied to whatever is behind. **Scrim** is the
layer that guarantees the contrast floor for content on top — the part that makes the whole
thing safe, and the part most hand-rolled glass effects omit. **Edge** is the hairline that
separates the surface from what is behind it, which is what stops a translucent panel
dissolving into a busy backdrop. **Content** is everything the surface holds.

`Surface.Scrim` cannot be removed by any prop, and it is not merely undocumented — there is
no prop, and no token value, that takes it below the floor. Its opacity is
`max(tint-alpha, minimum-scrim-opacity)`, so a tint lightened for a design review still
composites at the published minimum. A rung whose scrim is disabled is not a rung, and the
contrast guarantee is the only reason to use this component rather than a `div` with a
`backdrop-filter`.

Two parts are absent rather than neutralised, and the difference is the point.
`app/tokens.generated.css` already collapses a rung's blur to `0px` under
`prefers-reduced-transparency`, so the Backdrop's filter becomes an identity — but the
element is still a backdrop root, and the browser still snapshots what is behind it on a
rung that has asked not to be composited at all. Removing the element is the half a
stylesheet cannot do, and it is why this component exists rather than a class. The Edge is
absent on `canvas` and `scrim` for a different reason: both of those rungs publish the
keyword `none` as their border, which is invalid inside a `box-shadow` and makes the whole
declaration fall back to `none`. The CSS engine reaches the right answer, and no table of
which rungs have an edge has to be kept up to date by hand.

There is one shape of Surface that has no Scrim and no Edge either, and the tree says so
rather than rounding it off: a rung outside the six is refused, and the refusal renders a
root and a Content and nothing else. That is not a fifth material with the layers switched off — it
is the component declining to draw a material it has no tokens for, which is why the
no-Backdrop-without-a-Scrim guarantee below is not weakened by it.

The parts are `data-slot` attributes, not exports. There is no `Surface.Backdrop` to
import, no way to render a Content without its Scrim, and no `data-rung` attribute — the
data-attribute vocabulary is closed at four, and the rung is legible from the resolved
`--opsin-material-*` values in devtools. Surface also sets no corner of its own: shape is
the composing component's decision, and every layer inside inherits whatever radius the
caller applies.

## Content guidelines

Surface holds content; it generates none, and it ships no default copy at all. The
guideline that matters is about what you put on it: text over a translucent surface is the
one place in this system where a readability failure is invisible in review, because it
depends on what happened to be behind it in the designer's screenshot.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **Text and controls on a surface whose rung was chosen for the content's weight**, so
    the scrim holds the floor over any backdrop.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **Small grey caption text on a heavily blurred surface over a photograph**, checked once
    against one photograph. It passed that day and fails for every reader with a different
    background.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **One translucent surface at a time**, with cards and panels beneath it opaque.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **A translucent sheet over a translucent panel over a translucent header.** Three layers
    of blur that individually look considered and together look like fog.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **A reading on `card`, or on `raised` while it is being acted on.**
    `tokens/material.json` sets the ceiling — never a health value on a translucent rung
    above `raised` — and this page recommends the two rungs below it, which is tighter
    than the rule rather than a restatement of it.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **A number on `sheet` or `overlay`.** A figure a person is trying to read must not sit
    over a moving backdrop, however well the scrim holds — the value stays legible and the
    reader still has to work to read it.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

## Accessibility

Some of this is checked by a gate on every commit, some is measured nightly, and some is an
argument. They are separated below because `alpha` promises that accessibility is claimed
only where it has been checked, with the gaps listed rather than left out.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y` on every commit:**

- **Every colour is a token.** The static rig fails the build on a hex literal, an
  `oklch()`, a Tailwind palette colour or a numbered ramp step anywhere in `registry/**`.
  A neutral ramp step is a warning on the commit gate rather than an error, and fails only
  under `--strict`, which the nightly job runs — so that one item is measured nightly, not
  on every commit. Every value this component paints is a `--opsin-material-*` custom
  property or one of the eleven bridged surface roles, so a rung can be re-tuned or
  re-measured without touching the component.
- **Nothing here resolves to nothing under the product theme.** The same gate fails on the
  chrome-only roles and radii that exist in `app/globals.css` and not in `app/product.css`
  — the defect that looks correct in review because the preview renders inside the docs.
- **No `px` font size, and no banned word** in copy or in an identifier. Surface renders no
  copy of its own, so this is a guard against a future edit rather than a finding.

**Measured, and re-verified nightly rather than on every commit:**

- **The contrast floor of each rung's opaque fallback.** `scripts/check-contrast.mts`
  measures body text against every rung's *opaque fallback* in both themes, and records
  APCA Lc and WCAG 2.2 into `lib/generated/contrast.json` under the `materials` scope. A
  regression fails the nightly job. It is not in `pnpm check`, so a change made and merged
  in a day is measured that night rather than at review time. Read the scope carefully:
  the opaque fallback is the material as it renders once translucency is gone, so what
  this measures is the degraded path. The translucent path — the tint composited at the
  published floor over an arbitrary backdrop, which is the case the scrim exists for — is
  not measured by anything, and is in the last list on this page rather than in this one.
  The job covers the five content rungs in both themes; `scrim` is excluded, because
  nothing is written on a dimmer.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **The surface is not announced.** It has no role and no name, and it contributes no
  named node to the accessibility tree — the root and the content are generic `div`s a
  screen reader passes straight through, and the three decorative layers are empty and
  `aria-hidden`. It is also not focusable and adds no tab stop, which is what makes it
  safe to nest.
- **`prefers-reduced-transparency` produces a defined opaque fallback for every rung**, and
  the layering stays legible through the shadow and the edge rather than through blur. It
  is not a global switch that flattens the interface into one plane: the token layer swaps
  four properties and deliberately leaves the border, the shadow and the geometry alone.
  See [Reduced transparency](../accessibility/reduced-transparency.mdx).
- **Where `backdrop-filter` is unavailable, the same fallback renders**, tested for both the
  plain and the `-webkit-` spelling because the system's tested floor is Safari 16.4 and
  Safari carried only the prefixed form until 18. The degraded path is therefore the one
  real readers exercise. See [Browser support](../start/browser-support.mdx).
- **`prefers-contrast: more` takes the rung to its opaque fallback and widens the edge.**
  Dropping the translucency is the fourth of the four things
  [Increased contrast](../accessibility/increased-contrast.mdx) specifies, and it is
  answered in full here, in this component's own utilities rather than in the token layer
  where that page says the answer belongs. The second thing — hairline dividers at low
  alpha becoming *solid at full token colour* — is **not** answered here. The edge moves
  from the hairline width to the emphasis width and its colour does not change, so a rung
  whose border token is published at a low alpha keeps a low-alpha edge under increased
  contrast, only wider. Choosing a solid colour for it would mean this component
  overruling the ladder, and would give `canvas` and `scrim` an edge they are specified
  not to have; whether the widened low-alpha edge clears the non-text contrast floor has
  not been measured. That page's "today, nothing" therefore remains true of the token
  layer, which is what it describes.
- **No text is ever placed directly over an unscrimmed backdrop.** There is no code path
  that renders a Backdrop without a Scrim: the Backdrop exists only inside the branch where
  the rung resolves, and the Scrim in that branch is unconditional. The refusal path for an
  unknown rung renders neither. This is a guarantee about the four layers Surface owns and
  not about what a caller puts in them — text baked into an image, or an icon carrying
  meaning, is the caller's to get right.

**Not yet checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- **The translucent case, which is the one the component exists for.** Every published
  materials figure is text against a rung's opaque fallback. What a reader on a modern
  engine with no preferences set actually sees is the tint composited at the published
  floor over whatever is behind it, and no job measures that. Measuring it means compositing
  each translucent rung's tint at its floor over the darkest and the lightest tone the
  product theme can produce and evaluating both APCA and WCAG on the result — and it means
  deriving `minScrimOpacity` from that measurement rather than publishing it alongside one.
  Until then the floor for a translucent surface is argued from the fallback, not measured
  on the surface that renders.
- **The colour half of increased contrast.** The edge widens under `prefers-contrast: more`
  and keeps its published alpha, and nobody has measured whether the result clears the
  non-text floor. The rule that low-alpha dividers become solid at full token colour is
  the ladder's to answer, not this component's.
- **The large-text floor.** `tokens/material.json` publishes one minimum scrim opacity per
  rung and no second, thinner one for large text, so `contentWeight="large"` takes the body
  floor. Who measures a large-text floor, and against what, is open — and this component
  may not answer it by choosing a number.
- **Whether `prefers-contrast: more` belongs here at all.** It is answered in this
  component before it is answered in the token layer, which is where
  [Increased contrast](../accessibility/increased-contrast.mdx) says the answer belongs. If
  the degradation moves into `tokens/material.json`, what happens to a component that has
  already shipped its own is unsettled.
- The rendered contrast figures are not on this page. `<ContrastReport>` takes its rows as a
  prop and nothing wires `lib/generated/contrast.json` into it yet, and retyping measured
  numbers into MDX would produce a page that quietly stops being true. This page carries no
  `<ContrastReport>` for the same reason: given no rows it prints that the report has not
  been generated, and that is false — it has been, and this page cannot reach it. Run
  `pnpm run contrast` and read the `materials` scope.
- Behaviour under `forced-colors: active`. Nothing in opsinjs answers it, and a material
  ladder is precisely what a forced-colours environment replaces.
- The composited-surface budget. Three translucent rungs at once is the designed worst case
  and nothing counts them; a fourth is a screen that needs redesigning rather than tuning.
  Surface declines to count them on purpose: a module-scoped counter in a server component
  is shared between requests, so it would report one reader's screen to another. Review is
  the only thing enforcing the budget today.
- Whether the scrim's floor holds at `[data-text-size="200"]`. The floor is a colour
  relationship and does not move with type size, but nobody has looked at a 200% sheet over
  a hostile backdrop and said so. The one thing that does not scale with type is the edge:
  the hairline and emphasis widths are fixed pixel values, so the material thins out
  relative to its content at 200%.

## API reference

<PropsTable name="SurfaceProps" />

`rung` is required because there is no sensible default depth, and a component that guessed
would place a surface at the wrong height silently rather than loudly. `MaterialRung` is
declared once, in the shared substrate that ships with every registry item, and its six
members are documented at [MaterialRung](../reference/api/MaterialRung.mdx).

There is no `blur` prop, no `opacity` prop and no `tint` prop. Free-form values are how a
ladder becomes a gradient of ad-hoc guesses, and how the contrast guarantee stops being
computable. There is no radius prop either, and no polymorphic `as`: a component that needs
a different element wraps a Surface rather than becoming one, which keeps the four layers'
painting order out of the caller's hands.

`className` is the exception, and it is worth being blunt about what it opens. It is merged
onto the root unrestricted, so it is the one prop that can put colour on a Surface, and the
[two colour axes](../health/two-colour-axes.mdx) rule applies to it in full: a Surface may
take a category tint or sit under a status, never both on the same element. A
category-tinted Surface renders its status as a StatusPill inside it rather than as a tint
on the root. Surface is the primitive under every tinted container in
[Category identity](../health/category-identity.mdx), which makes it the easiest place in
the system to write the violation by accident — and this component cannot detect it, because
merging a class is all it does with what it is handed.

`contentWeight` is the one prop that promises more than it currently delivers, and it is
worth saying plainly. `tokens/material.json` publishes one minimum scrim opacity per rung,
measured against that rung's opaque fallback. There is no second, thinner floor for large
text anywhere in the token source, and this component may not invent one — a number that
decides whether a person can read their own result is measured or it does not exist. So
`"large"` is accepted, is recorded as a promise about the content, and takes the body floor
until a large-text floor is published. A surface that is more readable than it needs to be
is not a defect; the other direction is. A weight that is neither — which the type
forbids and a JavaScript consumer can still pass — takes the body floor and warns, rather
than being looked up and thrown on: a mistake in a styling prop must not be able to take a
surface holding somebody's readings off the screen.

A rung outside the six is refused rather than approximated. In development the component
warns once per distinct offending value, names the rung the retired vocabulary meant where
it can, and renders the children with no material at all. It does not fall back to `card`: a plausible-looking material at
the wrong depth is the failure the whole renaming exists to prevent, and it is invisible.
The children are still rendered, because Surface is a wrapper and dropping it would take a
reader's own data off the screen in order to report a styling mistake.
