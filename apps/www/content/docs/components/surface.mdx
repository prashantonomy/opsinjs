What is still open is the last list in [Accessibility](#accessibility),
and it is stated there rather than summarised here because most of it is a measurement nobody
has taken. That includes the one for the translucent material this component exists
to make safe.

Surface is the least visible component in the system and the one most likely to be
mistaken for decoration. It is the mechanism that makes translucency safe: without a
guaranteed floor, a blurred panel is a readability bug waiting for the wrong wallpaper.
The wallpaper is whatever the reader happens to be scrolling past.

It is a server component, and that is a requirement rather than an optimisation. Every
state it has is a user preference or an engine capability, each is answered by a media or
support query, and reading any of them in JavaScript would mean rendering one material on
the server and swapping to another after hydration. A flash of the wrong depth is a
cosmetic bug on a marketing page and a legibility bug on the surface holding somebody's
result.

## Preview

The whole ladder in two strips, because the six rungs do not make the same claim and one
backdrop cannot show both. The three opaque rungs sit on the page colour they are designed
for, where a Card is told from the page by its hairline and a Raised by its shadow, both of
which would vanish over a busy backdrop. The three translucent rungs keep the deliberately
hostile tiles, because readability over anything is their only claim and it is worth nothing
demonstrated over a flat grey. The tiles are arranged so the page's darkest tone and its
lightest pass under every translucent rung at every width, not only on a phone, because a
material that holds its floor only over a tasteful photograph has not been tested. The sixth
rung, `scrim`, is shown doing its real job. That job is dimming a page with content on a rung
above it, never with text on the scrim itself.

The last entry is a performance limit, not a style preference. Composited translucent
layers are expensive on mid-range Android hardware, and the fourth one is where scrolling
starts to stutter on exactly the devices most likely to belong to the people this system is
for. Surface does not count them, and the [Status](#status) section says why. See
[Performance budget](../foundations/materials/performance-budget.mdx).

### The rung names, and the two that mean a different depth

This page used to name the six rungs `base`, `raised`, `panel`, `overlay`, `sheet`,
`chrome`. Those names are retired. The rung names are the token names, because they
are the ones a stylesheet resolves and a drift gate checks, and choosing a prose set
instead would have meant renaming six generated custom properties in order to change
a word. [ADR 0014](../project/decisions/0014-material-rung-names.mdx) settles it.

Translate by **job**, never by position on the ladder. Three of the retired names are also
current names, and two of those moved:

| Retired name | What it meant | Rung now |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `base` | the page itself | `canvas` |
| `raised` | a bounded block of content | `card` |
| `panel` | lifted above the page, not covering it | `raised` |
| `overlay` | covering the page, which stays recognisable | `sheet` |
| `sheet` | a dismissible bottom sheet | `sheet` |
| `chrome` | a pinned toolbar or tab bar | `overlay` |

Read the fourth and sixth rows twice. `overlay` on this ladder is chrome that content
scrolls *beneath*; the panel that covers the page is `sheet`. A positional translation
puts a dismissible sheet on the chrome rung and a tab bar on the sheet rung, and both of
those compile, render and look almost right. A search-and-replace across an older design
file is not a safe migration for the three shared names. Only `base`, `panel` and `chrome`
are unambiguous, and those are the three the component can name a replacement for when it
warns in development. The rows above are that warning's source of truth; if the two ever
disagree, the warning is the bug.

One thing the names do not tell you is that in the light theme the three opaque rungs share
a fill. `canvas`, `card` and `raised` all resolve to `--opsin-neutral-0`, so the lower ladder
does not lift by colour there. `card` is told from `canvas` by a hairline and `raised` by a
shadow, and the ladder lifts by fill only in the dark theme, where the three take
`--opsin-neutral-950`, `--opsin-neutral-900` and `--opsin-neutral-800`. So in the light theme
the choice between `canvas` and `card` is a choice about a boundary, not about a colour.

For choosing between rungs at all, the decision table on
[Choosing a layer](../foundations/materials/choosing-a-layer.mdx) asks the questions in the
order that resolves them fastest, and [The material ladder](../foundations/materials/the-ladder.mdx)
carries the live specimen.

**Backdrop** is the blur and saturation applied to whatever is behind. **Scrim** is the
layer that guarantees the contrast floor for content on top. It is the part that makes the
whole thing safe, and the part most hand-rolled glass effects omit. **Edge** is the
hairline that separates the surface from what is behind it, which is what stops a
translucent panel dissolving into a busy backdrop. **Content** is everything the
surface holds.

`Surface.Scrim` cannot be removed by any prop, and it is not merely undocumented. There is
no prop, and no token value, that takes it below the floor. Its opacity is
`max(tint-alpha, minimum-scrim-opacity)`, so a tint lightened for a design review still
composites at the published minimum. A rung whose scrim is disabled is not a rung, and the
contrast guarantee is the only reason to use this component rather than a `div` with a
`backdrop-filter`.

Two parts are absent rather than neutralised, and the difference is the point.
`app/tokens.generated.css` already collapses a rung's blur to `0px` under
`prefers-reduced-transparency`, so the Backdrop's filter becomes an identity. The element
is nevertheless still a backdrop root, and the browser still snapshots what is behind it
on a rung that has asked not to be composited at all. Removing the element is the half a
stylesheet cannot do, and it is why this component exists rather than a class. The Edge is
absent on `canvas` and `scrim` for a different reason: both of those rungs publish the
keyword `none` as their border. Substituted into the `outline` shorthand that keyword is
invalid at computed-value time, so every longhand takes its unset value and `outline-style`,
whose initial value is `none`, draws nothing. The CSS engine reaches the right answer, and no
table of which rungs have an edge has to be kept up to date by hand.

There is one shape of Surface that has no Scrim and no Edge either, and the tree says so
rather than rounding it off: a rung outside the six is refused, and the refusal renders a
root and a Content and nothing else. That is not a fifth material with the layers switched
off. It is the component declining to draw a material it has no tokens for, which is why
the no-Backdrop-without-a-Scrim guarantee below is not weakened by it.

The parts are `data-slot` attributes, not exports. There is no `Surface.Backdrop` to
import, no way to render a Content without its Scrim, and no `data-rung` attribute. The
data-attribute vocabulary is closed at four, and the rung is legible from the resolved
`--opsin-material-*` values in devtools. Surface also sets no corner of its own: shape is
the composing component's decision, and every layer inside inherits whatever radius the
caller applies.

## Examples

### Chrome that content scrolls beneath

The `overlay` rung doing the job it is named for, which is the one most often given to the
wrong component. The panel opens already scrolled, so a dark row is sitting under the pinned
header in the frame the page opens on, and the blur and the scrim are visibly holding the
heading legible over it before you touch anything. A header at a scroll position of zero has
only the container's own background behind it, which is why the resting frame is scrolled
rather than left at the top: at the top the depth reads from the drop shadow alone and the
material has nothing to work over. Scroll the panel and a light row and a dark row pass under
the header in turn, and the heading stays readable over both.

### The degraded path, without changing a setting

Reduce Transparency, `prefers-contrast: more` and a missing `backdrop-filter` all end at
the same surface, deliberately, so that the fallback is exercised daily by real readers
rather than only by an old engine. `opaque` is the fourth way in and the one a reviewer can
look at. Note what does not change: the border, the shadow and the geometry stay, because
they are what carries the layering once the translucency is gone.

Print is a separate path to the same opaque fallback, not an exception folded into the
other three. The theme layer carries every rung to its opaque tint on paper, and the edge,
drawn as an outline and not a shadow, prints with it, so the boundary survives. The fill is
the part that does not follow reliably, because it depends on whether the reader has
background graphics switched on. So read the panel below as the fallback on a screen, and not
as a picture of a printout. That the boundary survives is read from the outline behaviour and
the theme's print block rather than from paper: nobody has run the site to a printer or under
an operating system's print theme, so like the forced-colours edge it is argued rather than
measured.

## States

Surface has no interaction states of its own. It renders no role, takes no focus and adds
no tab stop, so it has no hover, focus, pressed or disabled variant to design for: anything
a reader can act on lives inside the children `Surface.Content` holds, and each child
carries its own states on its own terms. A caller reaching for a hover ring or a pressed
fill on Surface itself is reaching for a prop this component does not have and will not
gain, because a Surface that reacted to pointer events would claim an interactivity its
four static layers do not back.

Surface also takes no data of its own: it renders whatever `rung` names and whatever
children a caller hands it, so none of the five canonical data states changes what the
four layers paint. The table says so plainly rather than describing a behaviour the source
does not have.

| Data state | What Surface renders |
| --- | --- |
| Error | The children a caller passes, unchanged. Surface carries no error copy and does not switch to a different rung when a reading does not arrive; a composing component that wants the error to look different picks that appearance itself and passes it as content. |
| Loading | The same delegation. A skeleton placeholder sits inside a Surface as a child rather than Surface switching into a loading appearance of its own. See [Skeleton](./skeleton.mdx). |
| Empty | The same. An empty-state message is copy the composing component writes; Surface has no empty variant to render instead of it. |
| Partial | The same. Surface never reads what its children contain, so it has no notion of a reading being partial; a composing component marks that in the children rather than through a prop here. |
| Stale | The same. A staleness indicator, a word, an icon or a timestamp, is content, which is what `Surface.Content` exists to hold rather than a state Surface tracks itself. |

## Content guidelines

Surface holds content; it generates none, and it ships no default copy at all. The
guideline that matters is about what you put on it: text over a translucent surface is the
one place in this system where a readability failure is invisible in review, because it
depends on what happened to be behind it in the designer's screenshot.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **Text and controls on a surface whose rung was chosen for the content's weight**, so

  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>

    background.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **One translucent surface at a time**, with cards and panels beneath it opaque.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **A translucent sheet over a translucent panel over a translucent header.** Three layers
    of blur that individually look considered and together look like fog.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **A reading on `card`, or on `raised` while it is being acted on.**
    `tokens/material.json` sets the ceiling, and the ceiling is that a health value

    rungs below it, which is tighter than the rule rather than a restatement of it.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>

## Motion

Surface declares no motion. There is no `transition`, no `animation` and no reference to
any `--opsin-ease-*` or `--opsin-duration-*` token anywhere in the source, so there is
nothing here for `prefers-reduced-motion` to reduce: reducing zero motion is still zero
motion. The four layers mount in their resolved state and stay there for the life of the
surface; a rung change is a new `rung` prop, which is a full re-render rather than a
transition between two materials.

That is easy to conflate with `prefers-reduced-transparency`, which this component does
handle, but the two preferences answer different questions and Surface's handling of one
says nothing about the other. Reduced transparency swaps four token values and removes
`Surface.Backdrop` from the render entirely, covered in [Accessibility](#accessibility);
reduced motion is a request about movement over time, and Surface has none to hold back.
A caller who wants a rung change to be perceptible rather than instant supplies that
transition on the composing component, not on Surface, because animating the material
itself would put a moving blur behind exactly the content this component exists to keep
legible.

## Accessibility

Some of this is checked by a gate on every commit, some is measured nightly, and some is an
argument. They are separated below because a component page claims accessibility only where it has been checked, with the gaps listed rather than left out.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y` on every commit:**

- **Every colour is a token.** The static rig fails the build on a hex literal, an
  `oklch()`, a Tailwind palette colour or a numbered ramp step anywhere in `registry/**`.
  A neutral ramp step is a warning on the commit gate rather than an error, and fails only
  under `--strict`, which the nightly job runs. That one item is therefore measured
  nightly, not on every commit. Every value this component paints is a
  `--opsin-material-*` custom property or one of the eleven bridged surface roles, so a
  rung can be re-tuned or re-measured without touching the component.
- **Nothing here resolves to nothing under the product theme.** The same gate fails on the
  chrome-only roles and radii that exist in `app/globals.css` and not in
  `app/product.css`. That is the defect that looks correct in review because the
  preview renders inside the docs.
- **No `px` font size, and no banned word** in copy or in an identifier. Surface renders no
  copy of its own, so this is a guard against a future edit rather than a finding.

**Measured, and re-verified nightly rather than on every commit:**

- **The contrast floor of each content rung.** `scripts/check-contrast.mts` measures body
  text against every content rung in both themes and records APCA Lc and WCAG 2.2 into
  `lib/generated/contrast.json` under the `materials` scope. For the opaque rungs it measures
  the opaque fallback directly, the material as it renders once translucency is gone. For the
  two translucent rungs, `sheet` and `overlay`, it composites the tint at its floor over the
  darkest and the lightest backdrop the product theme can produce and measures body text
  against both, which is what `tokens/material.json` records as `worstCaseComposite`. A
  regression fails the nightly job. It is not in `pnpm check`, so a change made and merged in
  a day is measured that night rather than at review time. The committed
  `lib/generated/contrast.json` predates that pipeline change, so every materials row this
  page renders is still text against a rung's opaque fallback. The job covers the five content
  rungs in both themes; `scrim` is excluded, because nothing is written on a dimmer.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **The surface is not announced.** It has no role and no name, and it contributes no
  named node to the accessibility tree. The root and the content are generic `div`s a
  screen reader passes straight through, and the three decorative layers are empty and
  `aria-hidden`. It is also not focusable and adds no tab stop, which is what makes it
  safe to nest.
- **`prefers-reduced-transparency` produces a defined opaque fallback for every rung**, and
  the layering stays legible through the shadow and the edge rather than through blur. It
  is not a global switch that flattens the interface into one plane: the token layer swaps
  four properties and deliberately leaves the border, the shadow and the geometry alone.
  See [Reduced transparency](../accessibility/reduced-transparency.mdx).
- **Where `backdrop-filter` is unavailable, the same fallback renders**, tested for both
  the plain and the `-webkit-` spelling because the system's tested floor is Safari 16.4
  and Safari carried only the prefixed form until 18. The degraded path is therefore the
  one real readers exercise. See [Browser support](../start/browser-support.mdx).
- **`prefers-contrast: more` takes the rung to its opaque fallback and widens the edge.**
  Dropping the translucency is the fourth of the four things
  [Increased contrast](../accessibility/increased-contrast.mdx) specifies, and it is
  answered in full here, in this component's own utilities rather than in the token layer
  where that page says the answer belongs. The second thing is hairline dividers at low
  alpha becoming *solid at full token colour*, and it is **not** answered here. The edge
  moves from the hairline width to the emphasis width and its colour does not change, so a
  rung whose border token is published at a low alpha keeps a low-alpha edge under
  increased contrast, only wider. Choosing a solid colour for it would mean this component
  overruling the ladder, and would give `canvas` and `scrim` an edge they are specified
  not to have; whether the widened low-alpha edge clears the non-text contrast floor has
  not been measured. That page's "today, nothing" therefore remains true of the token
  layer, which is what it describes.
- **`forced-colors: active` keeps the edge and drops the material.** The edge is drawn with
  an outline at a negative offset rather than an inset box-shadow, and a forced-colours
  palette keeps outlines and recolours them to `CanvasText` while it forces every
  `box-shadow` to `none`, so the four rungs that publish a border keep a visible boundary
  and `canvas` and `scrim` keep none. The backdrop is removed from the render, alongside its
  other five removal conditions. What does not survive is the scrim's dimming, because the
  palette replaces the rung's tint with `Canvas`. This is read from the CSS Color Adjust
  property list rather than from a capture, and the capture is in the last list below.
- **No text is ever placed directly over an unscrimmed backdrop.** There is no code path
  that renders a Backdrop without a Scrim: the Backdrop exists only inside the branch where
  the rung resolves, and the Scrim in that branch is unconditional. The refusal path for an
  unknown rung renders neither. This is a guarantee about the four layers Surface owns and
  not about what a caller puts in them. Text baked into an image, or an icon carrying
  meaning, is the caller's to get right.

**Not yet checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- **The translucent case as published, not as it is now measured.** The pipeline has been
  taught to composite each translucent rung's tint at its floor over the darkest and the
  lightest tone the product theme can produce and to evaluate both APCA and WCAG on the
  result, which is the case the scrim exists for. What is still open is that the committed
  `lib/generated/contrast.json` predates that change, so every published materials figure on
  this page is still text against a rung's opaque fallback rather than against the composite.
  Open too is that `minScrimOpacity` is published alongside that measurement rather than
  derived from it, so the floor a translucent surface runs to is asserted rather than read
  back off the composited result.
- **The colour half of increased contrast.** The edge widens under `prefers-contrast: more`
  and keeps its published alpha, and nobody has measured whether the result clears the
  non-text floor. The rule that low-alpha dividers become solid at full token colour is
  the ladder's to answer, not this component's.
- **The large-text floor.** `tokens/material.json` publishes one minimum scrim opacity per
  rung and no second, thinner one for large text. Who measures a large-text floor, and
  against what, is open. The component carries no weight prop for it, because a prop that
  records an intention nothing acts on is a knob rather than a promise. This component may
  not answer it by choosing a number.
- **Whether `prefers-contrast: more` belongs here at all.** It is answered in this
  component before it is answered in the token layer, which is where
  [Increased contrast](../accessibility/increased-contrast.mdx) says the answer belongs. If
  the degradation moves into `tokens/material.json`, what happens to a component that has
  already shipped its own is unsettled.
- **Whether the edge clears any contrast floor under `forced-colors: active`.** The edge is
  drawn as an outline, so the mode keeps it and recolours it to `CanvasText` on the four
  rungs that publish a border, while `canvas` and `scrim` keep none and the backdrop is not
  rendered. That the boundary survives is a mechanism, not a measurement. A synthetic
  forced-colours theme does not let anyone check whether the recoloured edge meets a non-text
  contrast floor, so this is argued in the same way the `contrast-more:` edge-width escalation
  above it is, and neither has been measured. The scrim's dimming does not survive, because a
  forced-colours palette replaces the rung's tint with `Canvas`, and nobody has opened the
  site under a real Windows contrast theme to confirm any of it.
- The composited-surface budget. Three translucent rungs at once is the designed worst case
  and nothing counts them; a fourth is a screen that needs redesigning rather than tuning.
  Surface declines to count them on purpose: a module-scoped counter in a server component
  is shared between requests, so it would report one reader's screen to another. Review is
  the only thing enforcing the budget today.
- Whether the scrim's floor holds at `[data-text-size="200"]`. The floor is a colour
  relationship and does not move with type size, but nobody has looked at a 200% sheet over
  a hostile backdrop and said so. The one thing that does not scale with type is the edge:
  the hairline and emphasis widths are fixed pixel values, so the material thins out
  relative to its content at 200%.
- **The dark-theme tint alpha for `sheet` and `overlay`, which never renders.** `minScrimOpacity`
  is published once per rung while `tintAlpha` is published per theme, and the component takes
  each theme to `max(tintAlpha, minScrimOpacity)`. The dark `tintAlpha` for `sheet` (0.90) and
  for `overlay` (0.70) sits below the single floor (0.93 and 0.74), so the floor wins and the
  authored dark alpha does not render. The direction is safe, because a more opaque surface is
  a more readable one. The harm is to whoever tunes the dark materials next, who will change a
  number that does nothing and see no result. Two ways out, and the choice belongs to the token
  owner rather than to this component: publish `minScrimOpacity` per theme so the dark floor can
  be lowered, or set the dark `tintAlpha` equal to the floor so the token says what renders.

## CSS variables

<CssVariablesTable name="surface" />

Surface sets seven custom properties on its own root element in an inline `style` object
(`--opsinjs-surface-tint`, `-opaque`, `-alpha`, `-blur`, `-saturation`, `-edge` and
`-edge-width`), so that a single reduced-transparency, increased-contrast or
unsupported-`backdrop-filter` rule can override one declaration instead of branching in
JavaScript. None of them is offered as a public theming surface: the source states
directly that they are internal plumbing rather than a versioned API, and restyling
belongs on the `data-slot` parts or on the semantic `--opsin-material-*` token roles listed
under [Tokens](#tokens) instead. The table above has no rows because there is no override
point to document, not because the component paints with nothing of its own.

## Tokens

<TokenTable component="surface" />

These rows are generated by `scripts/build-tokens.mts` from `tokensUsedBy`, not listed by
hand, so a rung that starts reading a further token shows up here the next time the
generator runs rather than waiting for someone to notice the page is stale. Surface reads
only from the `materials` scope: each rung's tint, tint alpha, minimum scrim opacity,
blur, saturation, border and shadow tokens, plus two structural roles,
`--opsin-border-hairline` for the edge's resting width and `--opsin-border-emphasis` for
its increased-contrast width. It never reads a category token and a status token onto the
same element, because Surface carries neither axis itself; a caller who wants a category
tint applies it through `className`, and [the two colour axes](../health/two-colour-axes.mdx)
rule is what keeps that from becoming a status colour on the same layer.

## API reference

<PropsTable name="SurfaceProps" />

`rung` is required because there is no sensible default depth, and a component that guessed
would place a surface at the wrong height silently rather than loudly. `MaterialRung` is
declared once, in the shared substrate that ships with every registry item, and its six
members are documented at [MaterialRung](../reference/api/MaterialRung.mdx).

There is no `blur` prop, no `opacity` prop and no `tint` prop. Free-form values are how a
ladder becomes a gradient of ad-hoc guesses, and how the contrast guarantee stops being
computable. There is no radius prop either, and no polymorphic `as`: a component that needs
a different element wraps a Surface rather than becoming one, which keeps the four layers'
painting order out of the caller's hands.

`className` is the exception, and it is worth being blunt about what it opens. It is
merged onto the root unrestricted, so it is the one prop that can put colour on a Surface,
and the [two colour axes](../health/two-colour-axes.mdx) rule applies to it in full: a
Surface may take a category tint or sit under a status, never both on the same element. A
category-tinted Surface renders its status as a StatusPill inside it rather than as a tint
on the root. Surface is the primitive under every tinted container in
[Category identity](../health/category-identity.mdx), which makes it the easiest place in
the system to write the violation by accident. This component cannot detect it, because
merging a class is all it does with what it is handed.

There is no `contentWeight` prop. `tokens/material.json` publishes one minimum scrim
opacity per rung, measured against that rung's opaque fallback, and there is no second,
thinner floor for large text anywhere in the token source. The prop that once fed the
scrim resolved to the same expression for both of its values, so it recorded an intention
nothing acted on. It returns only when a large-text floor is measured and published in
`tokens/material.json`, in the commit that ships the token, so that the prop and its effect
arrive together. A number that decides whether a person can read their own result is
measured or it does not exist.

A rung outside the six is refused rather than approximated. In development the component
warns once per distinct offending value, names the rung the retired vocabulary meant where
it can, and renders the children with no material at all. It does not fall back to `card`: a plausible-looking material at
the wrong depth is the failure the whole renaming exists to prevent, and it is invisible.
The children are still rendered, because Surface is a wrapper and dropping it would take a
reader's own data off the screen in order to report a styling mistake.
