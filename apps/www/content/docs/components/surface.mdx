Surface is the least visible component in the system and the one most likely to be
misunderstood as decoration. It is the mechanism that makes translucency safe: without a
guaranteed floor, a blurred panel is a readability bug waiting for the wrong wallpaper.

The last entry is a performance limit, not a style preference. Composited translucent layers
are expensive on mid-range Android hardware, and the fourth one is where scrolling starts to
stutter on exactly the devices most likely to belong to the people this system is for. See
[Performance budget](../foundations/materials/performance-budget.mdx).

## Anatomy

<Anatomy name="surface" />

<CompositionTree name="surface" />

**Backdrop** is the blur and saturation applied to whatever is behind. **Scrim** is the
computed layer that guarantees the contrast floor for content on top — the part that makes
the whole thing safe, and the part most hand-rolled glass effects omit. **Content** is
everything the surface holds. **Edge** is the hairline that separates the surface from what
is behind it, which is what stops a translucent panel dissolving into a busy backdrop.

Proposed part hierarchy. This is the specification the implementation must match.

```text
Surface                        root; declares a rung of the material ladder
├── Surface.Backdrop           blur + saturation; absent under reduced transparency
├── Surface.Scrim              computed to guarantee the floor; never optional
├── Surface.Edge               hairline separation from the backdrop
└── Surface.Content            everything the surface holds
```

`Surface.Scrim` cannot be removed by any prop. A rung whose scrim is disabled is not a rung,
and the contrast guarantee is the only reason to use this component rather than a `div` with
a `backdrop-filter`.

## Proposed API

<NotBuiltYet name="surface" />

```ts
// PROPOSED. Not implemented. A specification for review, not an API to generate
// code against. It will change without a deprecation cycle.

/**
 * The six rungs of the material ladder, ordered from most recessive to most
 * prominent. Named for their job, not for their blur radius, so a rung can be
 * retuned without renaming every use of it.
 */
export type MaterialRung =
  | "base"
  | "raised"
  | "panel"
  | "overlay"
  | "sheet"
  | "chrome"

export interface SurfaceProps {
  /** Which rung. Required: there is no sensible default depth. */
  rung: MaterialRung
  /**
   * Whether content on this surface must clear the floor for body text or only
   * for large text. Drives the computed scrim.
   */
  contentWeight?: "body" | "large"
  /** Renders opaque regardless of user preference — for print and export paths. */
  opaque?: boolean
  children: React.ReactNode
}
```

There is no `blur` prop, no `opacity` prop and no `tint` prop. Free-form values are how a
ladder becomes a gradient of ad-hoc guesses, and how the contrast guarantee stops being
computable.

## Content guidelines

Surface holds content; it does not generate any. The guideline that matters is about what you
put on it: text over a translucent surface is the one place in this system where a
readability failure is invisible in review, because it depends on what happened to be behind
it in the designer's screenshot.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **Text and controls on a surface whose rung was chosen for the content's weight**, so the
    computed scrim guarantees the floor over any backdrop.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **Small grey caption text on a heavily blurred surface over a photograph**, checked once
    against one photograph. It passed that day and fails for every reader with a different
    background.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **One translucent surface at a time**, with cards and panels beneath it opaque.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **A translucent sheet over a translucent panel over a translucent header.** Three layers
    of blur that individually look considered and together look like fog.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

## Accessibility requirements

Requirements for the implementation, not results.

- **The contrast floor is guaranteed, not hoped for.** Every rung's scrim is computed so that
  content on it clears the published floor over any backdrop, and the result is measured in
  CI — see [The contrast floor](../foundations/materials/the-contrast-floor.mdx).
- **`prefers-reduced-transparency` produces a defined opaque fallback for every rung**, with
  layering still legible through elevation and edges rather than through blur. Not a global
  switch that flattens the interface into one plane — see
  [Reduced transparency](../accessibility/reduced-transparency.mdx).
- **`prefers-contrast: more` strengthens edges and scrims** rather than only darkening text.
- **The surface is not announced.** It is a presentational layer with no role, no name and no
  place in the accessibility tree; its content is the content.
- **Where `backdrop-filter` is unsupported, the opaque fallback renders**, and it is the same
  fallback used under reduced transparency, so the degraded path is the one that gets tested.
  See [Browser support](../start/browser-support.mdx).
- **No text is ever placed directly over an unscrimmed backdrop**, including text inside
  images and icons that carry meaning.
