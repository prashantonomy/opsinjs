Term is small, unglamorous and probably the component with the highest ratio of benefit to
effort in the whole system. Most health interfaces are readable except for four words per
screen, and those four words are why people stop reading.

The first exclusion is the one that matters. Term is not a licence to keep the jargon. The
question is always *does this reader have to learn this word?* — and the answer is yes only
when the word will reach them from somewhere the product does not control. Everywhere else,
say it plainly and skip the component.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** That this word has this everyday meaning, in this context, according to
the product's glossary. It is a translation claim, and it is the product's claim, not a
clinician's.

**What it must never be read as.** A definition of the reader's condition, or advice.
*Chronic means long-lasting* is a translation; *chronic means you will have this for life*
is a prognosis, and the glossary is not the place for one. Definitions describe words.

**Which colour axis it may use.** Neither. A term is typographic — a dotted underline or an
equivalent mark that does not rely on colour to be visible. Tinting jargon by category makes
words look like statuses, and tinting them by status is worse.

**The glossary is the product's, and it is data.** Definitions live in
`tokens/glossary.json`, are written in plain English by the product, and are versioned and
reviewable like any other content. They are never generated at runtime, never fetched from a
third party, and never copied from a national health service — the NHS A-to-Z is Crown
copyright and is not reusable, however tempting the shortcut. Cite sources; never paste
them. See [Plain-English A–Z](../content/plain-english-a-z.mdx).

<ClinicalNote evidence="opinion">
The definition should be readable by someone who has not finished secondary school, because
a large share of adults in any population find health information hard to use. A definition
that needs a second definition has failed, and *idiopathic: of unknown aetiology* is the
canonical example of failing.
</ClinicalNote>

## Anatomy

<Anatomy name="term" />

<CompositionTree name="term" />

**Trigger** is the word as it appears in the sentence, marked so a reader knows an
explanation exists. **Definition** is the plain-English text. **Expansion** is the full form
of an abbreviation, which is a distinct thing from a definition and often both are needed.
The component has three presentations of the same content — inline, disclosure and print —
and the content is identical in all three.

Proposed part hierarchy. This is the specification the implementation must match.

```text
Term                           root; resolves an id against the glossary
├── Term.Trigger               the word in the sentence; underlined, not coloured
└── Term.Definition            plain English; always in the accessibility tree
    └── Term.Expansion         the full form, when the term is an abbreviation
```

`Term.Definition` is never removed from the accessibility tree by a presentation choice. A
disclosure hides it visually; it does not hide it from a screen reader before it is opened,
because a reader using speech has no way to know something is there to open.

```ts
// PROPOSED. Not implemented. A specification for review, not an API to generate
// code against. It will change without a deprecation cycle.

export interface TermProps {
  /**
   * The glossary key. Resolved against tokens/glossary.json at build time, so
   * an unknown term is a build error rather than a blank tooltip in production.
   */
  id: string
  /**
   * The word as it should read in this sentence, when it differs from the
   * glossary's headword — plural, tense, or capitalisation.
   */
  children?: React.ReactNode
  /**
   * How the definition is presented. `inline` places it in parentheses after
   * the word; `disclosure` puts it behind a control. `auto` chooses by
   * available width. There is no `hover` value, on purpose.
   */
  present?: "auto" | "inline" | "disclosure"
  /**
   * Show the definition only the first time the term appears on a surface.
   * Repetition is noise; absence is a barrier. This is the compromise.
   */
  once?: boolean
}
```

There is deliberately no way to supply a definition inline. Definitions live in the glossary
so that they are consistent across every screen, reviewable in one place, translatable, and
countable — a product can ask *how many words are we asking our readers to learn?* and get
an answer.

## Content guidelines

Definitions are one sentence, under about twenty words, in the second person where it helps.
Define the word, not the condition. Do not use a second piece of jargon inside a definition.
Do not hedge to the point of vacuity.

Expansions are the full form and nothing else: *HbA1c — haemoglobin A1c* is an expansion,
and *a measure of your average blood sugar over the last two to three months* is the
definition. Most readers need both, in that order.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"HbA1c (haemoglobin A1c) — a blood test that shows your average blood sugar level over
    the past two to three months."** Expansion, then plain definition, then stop.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **"HbA1c — glycated haemoglobin, a marker of glycaemic control."** Every word after the
    dash needs its own definition. This is a definition written for a colleague.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"Positive — the test found what it was looking for. For some tests that is good news
    and for others it is not."** Names the mismatch with the everyday meaning, which is the
    reason this term needs an entry at all.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **"Positive — an affirmative result."** Restates the word in slightly longer words and
    leaves the reader exactly where they started.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

## Accessibility requirements

Requirements for the implementation, not results.

- **The definition is available without hover and without pointer precision.** Hover-only
  definitions do not exist on touch, do not exist for keyboard users, and are unreliable for
  anyone with a tremor. This is why `present` has no `hover` value.
- **The trigger is not marked by colour alone.** An underline or equivalent non-colour mark
  distinguishes it, so it survives grayscale and increased contrast.
- **When the definition is behind a disclosure, the disclosure is a button** with an
  accessible name naming the term (*what does HbA1c mean?*), correct expanded state, and a
  44pt target.
- **The word is never split from its definition in the reading order.** A screen-reader user
  reaching the term hears, or can reach, the definition without leaving the sentence.
- **Speech pronunciation is considered**: abbreviations that must be spelt out are marked so
  they are not read as words. `SpO2` is not *spoh-two*.
- **At 200% text an inline definition reflows** onto as many lines as it needs and never
  becomes a horizontally scrolling strip.
- **Print** expands every term inline, once per term per page. A printed page has no
  disclosures, and the definition is the part the reader takes to the appointment.
