RangeBar is the component the rest of opsinjs is arranged around, and it is the one to
read first if you are deciding whether this system is worth building on. It is also the
most dangerous, because the distance between *here is your number against its range* and
*here is a verdict about your health* is one careless colour choice wide.

The distinction that matters most is the first one. A reference range is not a property of
a measurement; it is a property of a measurement *and a population and a laboratory*. If
you cannot say whose range it is, RangeBar is the wrong component, because drawing a band
on a line is a much stronger claim than printing two numbers in small type. Use
[Value](./value.mdx) and say plainly that no range is available.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** RangeBar asserts exactly one thing: *this measurement, expressed in
this unit, sits at this position relative to this reference range, which came from this
source*. It asserts nothing about cause, nothing about trend, and nothing about what
happens next.

**What it must never be read as.** It is not a diagnosis, not a risk score, and not a
verdict. A value outside the range is a value outside the range; it is not "bad", it is not
"abnormal", and it is emphatically not "normal" when it is inside — that word is banned
throughout opsinjs and [Reference ranges and normal](../health/reference-ranges.mdx)
explains why. Most people fall outside at least one reference range at any given time, and
a UI that treats *outside* as *wrong* generates far more anxiety than information.

**Which colour axis it may use.** The **status** axis only, and only when the consuming
product has supplied a status. RangeBar may never derive a status from position on its own
bar: *outside the range* and *needs attention* are different claims, and only a clinician
or a validated rule can convert one into the other. It may never use the **category** axis
to fill the bar — a heart-red bar and an attention-red bar side by side make the two axes
indistinguishable, which is the single failure mode
[The two colour axes](../health/two-colour-axes.mdx) exists to prevent. Category colour may
appear on the label or an icon beside the bar, never inside it.

**Who owns the thresholds.** The consuming product, always. RangeBar takes a range and a
status as inputs and has no table of its own; there is no built-in set of reference ranges
in opsinjs and there will never be one. Shipping thresholds inside a component library
would mean shipping clinical decisions to every product that installs it, silently, with no
review and no version story.

**Colour is never the only carrier.** Position on the bar, the tick and its numeric label,
and the text summary all carry the same information independently, so the component
survives grayscale, all three common forms of colour-vision deficiency, and a monochrome
print-out. See [Colour independence](../accessibility/colour-independence.mdx).

<SafetyCallout severity="high" evidence="opinion">
If the reference range is unknown, RangeBar must render the value with an explicit *no
reference range available* state and no band at all. It must never fall back to a default
range, a population average, or a range inferred from the person's own history. A plausible
band is worse than no band, because the reader cannot tell the difference.
</SafetyCallout>

<ResearchNote evidence="mixed" date="2026-09-02">
The design bet — that a value drawn against its range on a line is understood more reliably
by non-specialists than the same value printed beside its range, or flagged H and L in a
table — rests on the published literature on presenting laboratory results to patients. Two
anchors worth reading before you argue with this page: the 2024 systematic review of
result-presentation formats in JMIR (doi:10.2196/53993), and the 2018 study of patient
portal result presentation in BMC Medical Informatics and Decision Making
(doi:10.1186/s12911-018-0589-7). Both are reviews of a body of work rather than a single
trial, and both are the right starting point for checking this claim rather than taking it
from us.

What is **evidence** here is the broad direction: visual, positional formats support
comprehension better than bare numbers for readers with low numeracy. What is **opinion**,
and marked as such, is every specific decision on this page — the refusal to derive status
from position, the treatment of an unknown range as a first-class state, the choice to put
the numeric value adjacent to the tick rather than in a legend. Those are our judgements.
Nobody has run a trial on them.
</ResearchNote>

## Anatomy

<Anatomy name="range-bar" />

<CompositionTree name="range-bar" />

The bar is a compound component because every part is optional in some real screen and
mandatory in others: a dashboard needs the track and the tick and nothing else, a results
page needs all of it, and a print view needs the summary but not the track. Composition
makes those the same component rather than three props.

**Track** is the full drawn extent, which is deliberately wider than the reference range on
both sides so that a value outside the range still has somewhere to be drawn. **Band** is
the reference range itself. **Tick** is the measurement. **Marks** are the band boundaries
with their numeric labels — the two numbers a reader needs in order to check the picture
against the text. **Summary** is the sentence that carries the whole component's meaning
when the graphic is unavailable, and it is not optional: it is what a screen reader reads
and what a printed page shows.

Proposed part hierarchy. This is the specification the implementation must match, not a
generated tree; `<CompositionTree>` takes over once the component is in the registry.

```text
RangeBar                       root; owns value, range, unit, status, no defaults
├── RangeBar.Label             what was measured, e.g. "HbA1c"
├── RangeBar.Track             the drawn extent; wider than the band on both sides
│   ├── RangeBar.Band          the reference range, drawn once; never status-coloured
│   ├── RangeBar.Mark          band boundary + its numeric label (low)
│   ├── RangeBar.Mark          band boundary + its numeric label (high)
│   └── RangeBar.Tick          the measurement; the only part that may carry status
│       └── RangeBar.Value     the number and its unit, adjacent to the tick
├── RangeBar.Summary           the sentence form; mandatory, never visually hidden by default
└── RangeBar.Footnote          range provenance: whose range, from which source, when
```

`RangeBar.Tick` is the only part permitted to carry status colour. Nesting anything else
inside `RangeBar.Band`, or placing a `RangeBar.Tick` outside a `RangeBar.Track`, is a
composition error the implementation should refuse in development with a named error code.

## Proposed API

<NotBuiltYet name="range-bar" />

```ts
// PROPOSED. Not implemented. This is a specification for review, not an API to
// generate code against. It will change without a deprecation cycle.

import type { ClinicalStatus } from "opsinjs"

/** An open- or closed-ended reference range, in the same unit as the value. */
export interface ReferenceRange {
  /** Lower bound. Omit for an open-ended range such as "under 5.7". */
  low?: number
  /** Upper bound. Omit for an open-ended range such as "over 40". */
  high?: number
  /**
   * Whose range this is and where it came from — a laboratory, a guideline, a
   * device manufacturer. Required: a band with no provenance is an assertion
   * with no author.
   */
  source: string
  /** ISO date the range was captured or last confirmed. */
  asOf?: string
}

export interface RangeBarProps {
  /** What was measured, in the reader's language. Not an internal code. */
  label: string
  /**
   * The measurement. `null` is a first-class state meaning "we have no value",
   * distinct from `0`, and renders the empty form rather than a tick at zero.
   */
  value: number | null
  /** Unit symbol as it should be displayed, e.g. "mmol/L". */
  unit: string
  /**
   * The reference range. Omit it entirely when none is available: the component
   * renders without a band and says so. It never substitutes a default.
   */
  range?: ReferenceRange
  /**
   * Clinical status, supplied by the product. RangeBar never derives this from
   * position within the range. Omit when no status has been assigned.
   */
  status?: ClinicalStatus
  /**
   * Health category, used only for the label and icon treatment. It may not
   * colour the track, the band or the tick.
   */
  category?: string
  /** Significant figures for display. Defaults to the precision of `value`. */
  precision?: number
  /** When the measurement was taken, for the staleness treatment. */
  measuredAt?: string
  /**
   * The sentence form. Supply it when the automatic sentence would be wrong for
   * your unit or your reader; the component always renders one.
   */
  summary?: string
}
```

`ClinicalStatus` is the four-level union shared by every status-bearing component in the
system — see [Clinical status semantics](../health/clinical-status-semantics.mdx). It is
deliberately not a `string`: a component that accepts an arbitrary status string accepts
`"critical"`, `"abnormal"` and `"bad"` on day one, and the vocabulary is gone.

## Content guidelines

The label is what was measured, in the reader's words. Prefer the everyday name and give
the clinical one as a [Term](./term.mdx) if the reader will meet it on a letter from a
clinic. The summary sentence states position, never verdict, and always includes both the
value and the range so the sentence stands alone when it is read out of context — in a
screen reader, in a notification, in a printed summary.

Keep the summary under about twenty words. It is read aloud, and a long sentence is a
sentence somebody interrupts.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"HbA1c 44 mmol/mol. The usual range for this test is 20 to 41 mmol/mol, so this is a
    little above it."** States the value, states the range, states the position, stops.
    Leaves the interpretation to the person who is qualified to give it.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **"HbA1c 44 mmol/mol — abnormal. Your blood sugar control is poor."** Two failures. It
    converts a position into a verdict the component has no basis for, and it converts a
    verdict into a judgement about the reader. Neither is RangeBar's to make.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"We do not have a reference range for this test, so we cannot show where this result
    sits."** Says what is missing and why the picture is not there. The reader learns
    something true.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **A bar drawn with a typical adult range and a small asterisk.** The asterisk is not
    read. The band is. Once a band is on screen the reader has already formed a judgement
    about a range that may not be theirs.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

Words banned in and around this component: **normal**, **abnormal**, **good**, **bad**,
**poor**, **failed**, **passed**. The first two are the ones people reach for hardest, and
[Reference ranges and normal](../health/reference-ranges.mdx) is the argument for why
*within the usual range* is both kinder and more accurate.

## Accessibility requirements

These are requirements for the implementation, not results. Nothing has been measured
because nothing has been built.

- **The graphic is never the only carrier.** `RangeBar.Summary` is present in the
  accessibility tree at every size, and states the value, the unit, the range and the
  position in one sentence. A reader who cannot see the bar loses nothing.
- **The bar is not an interactive slider.** It is a static graphic and must not be
  focusable, must not be announced as a `slider`, and must not appear in the tab order.
  `role="img"` with an accessible name from the summary is the intended pattern; the
  implementation must justify anything else against
  [Screen readers](../accessibility/screen-readers.mdx).
- **Status survives grayscale and all three common forms of colour-vision deficiency.**
  Position, the numeric label and the summary each carry the information independently.
  This is verified by the generated CVD audit, not asserted.
- **Contrast.** Every token pair the component uses must clear the published floor in both
  themes: the tick against the band, the tick against the track, the band against the page,
  and every text pair. Measured by `scripts/check-contrast.mts`; a regression fails the
  build. See [Contrast and APCA](../foundations/colour/contrast-and-apca.mdx).
- **Text at 200% must not clip the value or the boundary labels.** At the largest Dynamic
  Type sizes the component is expected to reflow to a stacked form — summary above, track
  below, boundary labels beneath their marks — rather than shrink its text. See
  [Text resizing and zoom](../accessibility/text-resizing-and-zoom.mdx).
- **Motion is decorative here and always optional.** If the tick animates into position, it
  is a single position transition that resolves in under a fifth of a second and is removed
  entirely under `prefers-reduced-motion`, with the tick appearing at its final position.
  Urgency is never conveyed by motion — see
  [Motion in health UI](../health/motion-in-health-ui.mdx).
- **Print.** The component must render usefully with no colour and no background: the band
  as an outline, the tick as a solid mark, the summary as text. See
  [Print and export](../accessibility/print-and-export.mdx).
