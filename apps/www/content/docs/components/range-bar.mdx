RangeBar is the component the rest of opsinjs is arranged around, and it is the one to
read first if you are deciding whether this system is worth building on. It is also the
most dangerous, because the distance between *here is your number against its range* and
*here is a verdict about your health* is one careless colour choice wide.

The sentence is the component and the bar is a drawing of the sentence. `RangeBar.Summary`
states the reading, the range, the position and whose range it is, in words, on every
surface and at every size — and everything else on this page exists to draw that sentence
for people who would rather see it than read it.

## Preview

Two bars: a reading against a range, and the same reading with no range to compare it
with. The second is the one worth looking at, because it is the case products get wrong —
the tempting repair is a typical adult band and a small asterisk, and a reader cannot tell
a borrowed band from a real one.

Open it under the product theme and turn the text size up. The claim that this component
survives without colour is meant to be checked rather than believed: in greyscale the
position, the two boundary numbers and the sentence each still carry the whole message.

The distinction that matters most is the first one. A reference range is not a property of
a measurement; it is a property of a measurement *and a population and a laboratory*. If
you cannot say whose range it is, RangeBar is the wrong component, because drawing a band
on a line is a much stronger claim than printing two numbers in small type. Use
[Value](./value.mdx) and say plainly that no range is available.

You can also hand RangeBar a reading with no range and let it say that for you. It renders
the number, states in the summary that there is nothing to compare it with, and draws no
bar at all — which is the same message, in the component the rest of the screen is already
using.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** RangeBar asserts exactly one thing: *this measurement, expressed in
this unit, sits at this position relative to this reference range, which came from this
source*. It asserts nothing about cause, nothing about trend, and nothing about what
happens next.

**What it must never be read as.** It is not a diagnosis, not a risk score, and not a
verdict. A value outside the range is a value outside the range; it is not "bad", it is not
"abnormal", and it is emphatically not "normal" when it is inside — that word is banned
throughout opsinjs and [Reference ranges and normal](../health/reference-ranges.mdx)
explains why. Most people fall outside at least one reference range at any given time, and
a UI that treats *outside* as *wrong* generates far more anxiety than information.

**What it computes, and it is a short list.** A position along a line, and one of three
words for that position — *within*, *above*, *below* — against the bounds the caller
supplied. That is arithmetic on the product's own numbers, and it is the same arithmetic
that decides where the tick goes. It never becomes a level of attention: `status` is an
input, and removing it changes the colour of the tick and nothing else.

**Which colour axis it may use.** The **status** axis only, and only when the consuming
product has supplied a status. RangeBar may never derive a status from position on its own
bar: *outside the range* and *needs attention* are different claims, and only a clinician
or a validated rule can convert one into the other. It may never use the **category** axis
to fill the bar — a heart-red bar and an attention-red bar side by side make the two axes
indistinguishable, which is the single failure mode
[The two colour axes](../health/two-colour-axes.mdx) exists to prevent.

The implementation keeps them apart by element rather than by convention. `category` tints
`RangeBar.Label` and stamps `data-category` there; `status` colours `RangeBar.Tick` and
stamps `data-status` there; the root, the track and the band take colour from neither. No
element in the rendered DOM carries both attributes, which is what the axis-conflict gate
checks and what makes a bar safe to drop into a category-tinted card.

**Who owns the thresholds.** The consuming product, always. RangeBar takes a range and a
status as inputs and has no table of its own; there is no built-in set of reference ranges
in opsinjs and there will never be one. Shipping thresholds inside a component library
would mean shipping clinical decisions to every product that installs it, silently, with no
review and no version story. The same rule is why there is no `staleAfterHours` here: how
old is too old is clinical, and a component that guessed would have decided, on behalf of a
product that never asked, when to stop telling somebody their result is current.

**Colour is never the only carrier.** Position on the bar, the tick and its numeric label,
the two boundary numbers and the text summary all carry the same information
independently, so the component survives grayscale, all three common forms of
colour-vision deficiency, and a monochrome print-out. Where a status has been supplied, its
word and its glyph sit beside the label — outside the summary, so that a caller replacing
the sentence cannot take the word with it. See
[Colour independence](../accessibility/colour-independence.mdx).

**A bar needs two bounds.** A one-sided range — *up to 20*, *10 and upwards* — has no
width, and a width is what a scale is made of. Given one bound, RangeBar draws no track at
all and states the comparison in words instead; the alternative is to invent the other end
of the line, and a scale nobody chose is indistinguishable, on screen, from one a
laboratory published.

**A reading far outside its range compresses the band, and that is the honest picture.**
The drawn extent is a band-width wider than the range on each side, and it always stretches
to contain the reading — so a value ten times the top of the range leaves the band a sliver
near one end. It is drawn to scale because it is true to scale: a bar that clipped the
reading to the edge would be showing it somewhere it is not, and the numbers are on the
marks and in the sentence either way.

<SafetyCallout severity="urgent" evidence="opinion">
If the reference range is unknown, RangeBar renders the value with an explicit *no
reference range available* state and no band at all. It never falls back to a default
range, a population average, or a range inferred from the person's own history. A plausible
band is worse than no band, because the reader cannot tell the difference. A `range` that
arrives with an empty `source` is reported as OPSIN-0004 and not drawn — an unattributed
band is a comparison with no author.
</SafetyCallout>

<ResearchNote evidence="mixed" date="2026-09-02">
The design bet — that a value drawn against its range on a line is understood more reliably
by non-specialists than the same value printed beside its range, or flagged H and L in a
table — rests on the published literature on presenting laboratory results to patients. Two
anchors worth reading before you argue with this page: the 2024 systematic review of
result-presentation formats in JMIR (doi:10.2196/53993), and the 2018 study of patient
portal result presentation in BMC Medical Informatics and Decision Making
(doi:10.1186/s12911-018-0589-7). Both are reviews of a body of work rather than a single
trial, and both are the right starting point for checking this claim rather than taking it
from us.

What is **evidence** here is the broad direction: visual, positional formats support
comprehension better than bare numbers for readers with low numeracy. What is **opinion**,
and marked as such, is every specific decision on this page — the refusal to derive status
from position, the treatment of an unknown range as a first-class state, the choice to put
the numeric value adjacent to the tick rather than in a legend. Those are our judgements.
Nobody has run a trial on them.
</ResearchNote>

The parts are `data-slot` attributes, not exports. There is no `RangeBar.Track` you can
import, and the bar is not composable: every part is optional in some real screen and
mandatory in others, and the component decides which ones exist from the data it was given
rather than from how a caller nested it. A dashboard row that wants only the track and the
tick sets `summary` and styles the rest away through the slots; it cannot render a tick
outside a track, because there is no way to hand it one.

`RangeBar.Status` is the one part that is not in the original proposal. The status word has
to survive a caller replacing the summary, so it cannot live inside the sentence; and it
has to be redundant with the colour, because the measured CVD audit in `tokens/color.json`
finds *steady* and *attention* identical under deuteranopia and in greyscale. A word and a
distinct glyph beside the label is where those two constraints meet. It is not a
[StatusPill](./status-pill.mdx) and does not look like one: a bar carrying a pill would
show the reader two objects for one assertion.

## Content guidelines

The label is what was measured, in the reader's words. Prefer the everyday name and give
the clinical one as a [Term](./term.mdx) if the reader will meet it on a letter from a
clinic. The summary sentence states position, never verdict, and always includes both the
value and the range so the sentence stands alone when it is read out of context — in a
screen reader, in a notification, in a printed summary.

Keep the summary under about twenty words. It is read aloud, and a long sentence is a
sentence somebody interrupts. The generated one is built to that budget: subject, reading,
position, range, source, stop. Replace it with `summary` when your unit or your reader
needs different phrasing — but you are replacing the words, not the sentence, and the
component will render whatever you pass in both the visible text and the graphic's
accessible name.

**Precision is decimal places, not significant figures**, and it belongs to the metric
rather than to the value — the same metric shows the same number of decimal places every
time, so two readings can be compared at a glance. It applies to the reading and to both
boundary numbers alike, because a value and the bound it is being compared with are the
same measurement. If a range is narrow enough that both marks round to the same number, the
component labels them identically and says so in a development warning: the marks are in
the right places and the metric's stated precision cannot separate its own bounds. Adding a
decimal place to pull them apart would be a claim about accuracy that nobody made, so
nothing here does it for you. See
[Numbers, units and precision](../health/numbers-units-precision.mdx).

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"HbA1c 44 mmol/mol. The usual range for this test is 20 to 41 mmol/mol, so this is a
    little above it."** States the value, states the range, states the position, stops.
    Leaves the interpretation to the person who is qualified to give it.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **"HbA1c 44 mmol/mol — abnormal. Your blood sugar control is poor."** Two failures. It
    converts a position into a verdict the component has no basis for, and it converts a
    verdict into a judgement about the reader. Neither is RangeBar's to make.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"We do not have a reference range for this test, so we cannot show where this result
    sits."** Says what is missing and why the picture is not there. The reader learns
    something true, and it is what the component renders on its own when `range` is
    omitted.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **A bar drawn with a typical adult range and a small asterisk.** The asterisk is not
    read. The band is. Once a band is on screen the reader has already formed a judgement
    about a range that may not be theirs.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

Words banned in and around this component: **normal**, **abnormal**, **good**, **bad**,
**poor**, **failed**, **passed**. The first two are the ones people reach for hardest, and
[Reference ranges and normal](../health/reference-ranges.mdx) is the argument for why
*within the usual range* is both kinder and more accurate.

## Accessibility

Some of this is enforced on every commit. The rest is argued, and it is labelled as such:
`alpha` promises that accessibility is claimed only where it has been checked, with the
gaps listed rather than left out. Nothing on this page has been through a review with a
disabled user, and `a11yDate` is therefore still empty.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y` on every commit:**

- **Colour is never the only carrier of a status.** The gate fails the build on a file that
  paints a status colour without also reading `CLINICAL_STATUS_META` and a lucide glyph, and
  on any status surface with no `data-status`. This component reads the word and the icon
  name from that table and never writes either as a literal, so the four levels are four
  distinct shapes rather than one shape in four colours — which is what the measured CVD
  audit in `tokens/color.json` requires. See
  [Health glyphs](../foundations/iconography/health-glyphs.mdx).
- **No element takes colour from both axes.** The same gate reads every JSX element and
  fails on one that resolves a category colour and a status colour, or that carries both
  `data-category` and `data-status`. The separation described under Clinical meaning is
  therefore checked rather than promised.
- **No shipped threshold.** A name matching *stale*, *threshold*, *limit*, *minimum*,
  *maximum* or *band* given a numeric literal is an error anywhere under `registry/`. There
  is no range, no staleness boundary and no plausibility bound in this component or in
  either of its examples.
- **No type size in pixels, and no raw colour literal**, so the whole component scales with
  the reader's own text size and takes every colour from the two axes and the eleven
  bridged surface roles.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **The summary is in the accessibility tree at every size**, as ordinary visible text
  rather than a hidden label, and states the reading, its unit, the range, the position and
  whose range it is. A reader who cannot see the bar loses the picture and nothing else.
- **The bar is not an interactive slider.** `RangeBar.Track` is `role="img"` with the
  summary sentence as its accessible name. It is not focusable, is not announced as a
  `slider`, a `meter` or a `progressbar` — all three promise a value the reader can inspect
  or change — and it is not in the tab order. The tick, the marks and their numbers sit
  inside it, so a screen reader is not read a list of unlabelled shapes.
- **The unit is spoken, not spelled.** Every number goes through
  [Value](./value.mdx), which substitutes the unit's spoken form from `tokens/units.json`:
  a listener hears *milligrams per decilitre* rather than the symbol read out letter by
  letter.
- **At 200% text the component is already stacked** — summary under the track, boundary
  labels beneath their marks — and the space reserved for those labels is set in `em`, so
  it grows with them. Nothing is truncated and no ellipsis is set anywhere in the file.
  Checked by eye; not checked by a machine.
- **Nothing animates.** There is no transition on the tick, no growth on the band and no
  count on the number, on first paint or afterwards. A health value that arrives by
  animation has displayed a figure that is not true for every frame of the journey.

**Not checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- Every contrast pair: the tick against the band, the tick against the track, the band
  against the page, the status word against the surface behind it, and the boundary labels.
  `scripts/check-contrast.mts` has not been run for this component, which is why the report
  below is empty rather than reassuring.
- The repetition a screen-reader user meets, hearing the sentence once as the graphic's
  name and once as the paragraph under it. It is the pattern this page's own specification
  asked for, and it has not been listened to by anybody.
- Print. The band is outlined and the tick is a solid mark so that both survive a printer
  dropping backgrounds, but opsinjs has no print stylesheet yet and none of this has been
  put on paper.
- `prefers-contrast` and `forced-colors`, which are not implemented anywhere in opsinjs, so
  no component can currently make a claim about either.
- Whether two boundary labels collide at very large text sizes. They are set not to wrap, so
  they overflow rather than clip, and where the band is a sliver they will overlap.

Three things differ from the interface this page proposed while it was unbuilt, and each of
them is a decision rather than an oversight. `precision` is **decimal places**, not
significant figures, and it does not sniff the precision of the float it was handed —
significant figures would show one metric to different numbers of decimal places at
different magnitudes. `category` is typed to the six health categories rather than to
`string`, for the reason the page already gave about `status`: a component that accepts an
arbitrary category accepts a seventh colour ramp that does not exist. And `measuredAt`
renders a date in the footnote and no staleness treatment at all, because a staleness
boundary is a clinical number opsinjs must not own — a surface that needs one wraps the
reading in [RelativeTime](./relative-time.mdx), which takes the boundary from you.

`locale` is new, and it is a pass-through: it reaches every `Value` this component renders
and the dates in the footnote, so one bar cannot show two conventions.

`ClinicalStatus` is the four-level union shared by every status-bearing component in the
system — see [Clinical status semantics](../health/clinical-status-semantics.mdx). It is
deliberately not a `string`: a component that accepts an arbitrary status string accepts
`"critical"`, `"abnormal"` and `"bad"` on day one, and the vocabulary is gone. A value
outside the four is refused with a development warning and the bar is drawn without a
status, rather than approximated into one.

`ReferenceRange` is declared once, in `lib/opsinjs.ts`, and shared with every other
component that compares a reading against an interval. Both bounds are optional because a
one-sided range is a real range; `source` is not, because an unattributed band is an
assertion with no author.

  time. A reading can be inside its range and falling fast; these two say different things
  and often belong on the same screen.
- [MetricTile](./metric-tile.mdx) — the compact form for a dashboard grid. A tile may embed
  a miniature bar, but a bar with its marks and summary needs the width of a full row.
- `progress` (considered, no page yet — see the [roster](./index.mdx)) — visually similar,
  semantically unrelated: progress is towards a goal somebody chose, not a range somebody
  published.
