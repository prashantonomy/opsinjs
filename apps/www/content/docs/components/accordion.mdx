Accordion is a stack of sections that each expand and collapse, so a long page of headings can be scanned first and read second. Its home is a set of related passages a reader wants an index of before they commit to any one of them, and the case it was built for is a list of questions and answers where the headers are the map and the panels are the detail.

The rule that shapes every use of this component comes before any design decision. A collapsed panel is hidden when the screen loads, so nothing a reader must not miss may live inside one. A safety message, an urgent instruction, a warning about a dose, or a step a reader has to take belongs in a [Card](./card.mdx) or a [Callout](./callout.mdx) that stays open on the surface, never in a panel a reader may never open. The accordion is for content a reader chooses to reach, not for content they must meet whether they reach for it or not.

The accordion is built on Base UI's Accordion primitive, which is where the open-state management, the `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls` wiring and the header markup come from, so installing it pulls in `@base-ui/react`. The chevron is a single glyph from `lucide-react`, which is the second dependency.

import { Accordion } from "@/components/ui/accordion"

  ]}

## When to use it

<WhenToUse
  use={[
    "Giving a reader an index of related sections they can scan first and open one at a time, such as a list of questions and answers where the headers are the map.",
    "Folding away detail that a reader wants on demand but not on arrival, such as the small print under a summary, as long as none of it is content the reader must not miss.",
    "Fitting several independent groups of settings onto one small screen, opening more than one at a time with multiple set, where a wall of always-open panels would run past the fold.",
  ]}
  avoid={[
    { case: "The content is a safety message, an urgent instruction or anything a reader must not miss. A collapsed panel is hidden by default, so a reader may never open it and never meet the message.", instead: "callout" },
    { case: "The content must always be visible on the surface, not behind a header a reader has to press to reveal it.", instead: "card" },
    { case: "Choosing a header should swap the whole view for a different one that occupies the same space, rather than reveal detail below the header in place.", instead: "tabs" },
  ]}
/>

The first exclusion is the one that matters, and it is not a matter of taste. The measured harm is simple: a reader who never opens a panel never reads what is inside it, so any content that a reader has to meet cannot live in a section that starts closed. When you find yourself reaching for an accordion to tuck away a warning because the screen is busy, the busy screen is the problem to solve, and the warning belongs in a [Callout](./callout.mdx) that stays open. The accordion earns its place only for detail a reader is free to leave folded.

The third exclusion draws the line against [Tabs](./tabs.mdx). Both organise a set of sections, but a tab swaps the whole panel for another that occupies the same space, while an accordion reveals its panel in place, below its header, and can show several at once. If choosing a header replaces what fills the region, it is tabs; if it opens detail underneath the header while the other headers stay put, it is this component.

The parts above are identified by `data-slot` and are styleable from outside; none of them is an export, and there is no way to render a panel without its header. Inside the trigger sit two more slots the tree does not nest: `data-slot="accordion-title"` on the header words, and the chevron glyph, which carries no `data-slot` because it is `aria-hidden` decoration whose open state the trigger's own `aria-expanded` already tells. There is deliberately no `Accordion.Indicator`, because the chevron turning is the section's own state made visible, not a mark to be placed separately.

## Content guidelines

Write headers as a scannable index, not as sentences. A reader reads every header before opening any panel, so each one is a short, parallel label a reader can weigh against its neighbours: a question, a noun phrase, or a setting name, in sentence case and without terminal punctuation. Keep the panel content to the detail the header promises, and do not let a panel grow so long that the reader loses the header it belongs to.

Never fold content a reader must meet into a panel that starts closed. If a section carries a warning, a safety instruction or a step the reader has to take, it does not belong in an accordion at all, because the reader may never open it. Move that content to a [Callout](./callout.mdx) or a [Card](./card.mdx) that stays open on the surface, and keep the accordion for detail the reader is free to leave folded.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"When can I reach the team?"** A header written as the question the reader

## Accessibility

Some of this is enforced by a gate on every pull request and every push to `main`, and the rest is argued and labelled as such. At `alpha`, accessibility is claimed only where it has been checked, with the gaps listed rather than left out. Nothing here has been through a review with a disabled user.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y`:**

- **No status colour, no colour literal, no ramp step.** The accordion draws only the neutral chrome roles, so it carries no `data-status`, reads no clinical vocabulary, and cannot mix the two axes. The gate reads every element and would fail the build on a colour drawn from either axis or on a raw colour value, and there is neither here.
- **A 44pt target and no px type size.** Each trigger floors its pressable region at `--opsin-target-minimum` in rem, and its text is the `headline` semantic step, so both the hit area and the label grow with the reader's text size rather than pinning at a device pixel.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **Each header is its own tab stop, and the panel is wired to it.** Base UI renders the header as an `<h3>` with a `<button>` inside, sets `aria-expanded` on the trigger and `aria-controls` to its panel, and toggles the panel with Enter or Space. The open state a reader relies on is `aria-expanded`, not the chevron, which is `aria-hidden`.
- **The chevron rotation respects reduced motion for free.** It turns over `--opsin-duration-fast`, which the product stylesheet collapses to an instant inside its own `prefers-reduced-motion` block, so a reader who asked for less motion gets an instant turn with no branch of its own.
- **A closed section costs nothing.** Base UI unmounts a closed panel, so its content is out of the tab order and out of the accessibility tree until the section is opened, and a reader never tabs into hidden content.

**Not checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- Every contrast pair: the header ink and the chevron against the card surface, and the section hairlines and root border against the same surface. `scripts/check-contrast.mts` has not been run for this component, which is why the report below is empty rather than reassuring.
- What the accordion draws under `forced-colors: active`, and whether the open section still reads as open when the platform substitutes its own colours. None of this has been put in front of a machine or a high contrast user.

## API reference

<PropsTable name="AccordionProps" />

`items` is the whole content of the accordion: each entry is a `value`, a header `title`, the panel `content` and an optional `disabled`. `multiple` decides whether opening a section leaves the others open, and it defaults to `false` so a small screen shows one panel at a time. `value` with `onValueChange` makes the accordion controlled, while `defaultValue` alone lets it manage its own open state; passing `value` without `onValueChange` fixes the accordion open exactly as given, which is occasionally what a static layout wants and usually a mistake.

## Related

<RelatedComponents name="accordion" />

**Easily confused with**

- [Tabs](./tabs.mdx) swap the whole panel for another that occupies the same space, while an accordion reveals its panel in place and can show several at once. If choosing a header replaces what fills the region, it is tabs.
- [Card](./card.mdx) holds content that stays open on the surface. When a section must always be visible, it is a card, not a panel a reader has to press a header to reveal.
- [Callout](./callout.mdx) sets a short piece of information apart where a reader cannot miss it. Anything a reader must meet, a warning or an instruction, belongs in a callout rather than folded into a collapsed accordion panel.

**Used by**

- No pattern, recipe or screen composes it yet. It is a neutral organiser that a product wraps around its own sections, and wiring it into a page is a product's to make rather than something the corpus ships today.
