## Examples

### One primary per surface

One filled button answers *what should I do here?*. Everything else is secondary
or quiet, and the row is spaced by `--opsin-target-separation`.

<ComponentPreview name="button-one-primary-per-surface" kind="example" align="start" />

### Busy, at the foot of a form

`busy` keeps the label, blocks activation and shows the indicator in the icon's
seat. `busyLabel="Saving"` gives the state a word, and `fullWidth` stops the control
growing when that word appears. The submit control says `type="submit"`, because
the default here is `button`.

## Content guidelines

A label is a verb that names its own outcome, in sentence case, with no full stop:
*Save reading*, *Delete this reading*. Never *OK*, *Submit*, *Yes* or *Click here*,
because each sends the reader back up the page to reconstruct the question.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"Save reading"** names the outcome and survives being read out of context.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **"Submit"** on a screen with three of them tells a screen reader user nothing.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

## Accessibility

**Checked by `pnpm run check:a11y` on every commit.** Every colour is a role
token, no type size is in `px`, and no banned word appears anywhere.

**Argued from the source, not measured.**

- A real `button` element, so Return, Space and the role come from the platform.
  `render` and `nativeButton` are pinned, so nothing can change the tag.
- The visible label is the accessible name and the icon is `aria-hidden`. A label
  that renders no text raises a development warning.
- `busy` sets `aria-busy` and `aria-disabled`, never the native `disabled`, so the
  control keeps its tab stop and its name.
- The focus ring is an outline outside the box, so it never moves layout. An
  ancestor that clips will clip it.

**Not checked by any gate.** Contrast for every variant in both themes, the 44 by
44 target and the 8pt separation, whether `destructive` reads as different from
`secondary`, `forced-colors` beyond `primary`, and whether a screen reader speaks
the busy state.

## API reference

<PropsTable name="ButtonProps" />

`type` defaults to `button`, not `submit`, so a form's submit control has to say
`type="submit"`. There is no `disabled` convenience prop; the native attribute is
the whole of it, and passing `busy` and `disabled` together raises a development
warning. `className` and `style` are merged caller last and must never resolve a
`--opsin-status-*` or `--opsin-category-*` role, because a Button carries no
clinical axis.
