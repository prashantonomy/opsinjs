Stepper shows a reader where they are in a fixed, ordered sequence of steps. It marks each step as complete, current or upcoming, and it does one job and no other: it is a read-only picture of progress, not a control the reader operates.

That restraint is deliberate. The sequence itself lives in the product, which decides how many steps there are and which one the reader is on. This component reflects that decision. It has no next, no back and no click target, because a stepper that steers the flow invites a form to put several questions on one screen, and a health form reads best as [one question per page](../patterns/forms/question-pages.mdx).

## When to use it

    { case: "The items are really separate views the reader switches between at will, rather than a sequence they progress through in order.", instead: "tabs" },

The exclusion that matters is the first, and it is the reason this component was left off the roster for so long. A bar of steps across the top of a screen quietly tells a designer that a form may put several questions on one page, and a health form reads best as [one question per page](../patterns/forms/question-pages.mdx), because a reader answering one thing at a time makes fewer mistakes and a screen reader user is never lost among a dozen inputs. Use the stepper to reassure a reader who is already moving one question at a time, and do not use it to justify moving faster.

The second exclusion is about direction. [Tabs](./tabs.mdx) let a reader jump between views in any order and change nothing by choosing one. A stepper shows an ordered sequence with a before and an after, and it changes nothing at all: it only reports. If the reader picks freely, it is tabs; if the reader moves through in order, it is this indicator.

The parts above are identified by `data-slot` and are styleable from outside; none of them is an export, and there is no way to render a marker without its list. There is deliberately no `Stepper.Indicator`, no click handler and no `href`: the component reports a position and never moves the reader, because moving the reader is the flow's job and not the indicator's.

## Content guidelines

Keep the labels short, parallel and in sentence case: a word or two each, the same part of speech, so the sequence reads as one set. Name what happens at each step from the reader's side, "Your details" rather than "Collect user data", and let the labels carry no terminal punctuation. Order the steps the way the reader moves through them, because the list order is the order drawn, and a stepper whose order does not match the flow is worse than none.

Write a description only when the label leaves a real question open, and keep it to a clarifying phrase. A stepper reports position, so a description that reads as an instruction ("Enter your postcode now") promises an action the reader cannot take here and belongs on the step's own page instead.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"Your details" then "Your preferences" then "Review"** Three short, parallel labels in the order the reader moves through them, so the sequence reads at a glance and the current step is obvious.

  <DoDont.Dont>
    **"Step 1: fill in everything about yourself" then "Prefs" then "Now check it all and press submit"** Three different lengths and three different grammars, one of them an instruction the indicator cannot carry out, so the set stops reading as a sequence and starts reading as a form.

## Accessibility

The stepper is an ordered list. The root is an `<ol>` and each step is an `<li>`, so a screen reader announces a list of a known length and reads the steps in order, which is the position information the component exists to give. The current step carries `aria-current="step"`, the assistive-technology signal for the item in a set that represents the reader's current position.

The state of each step is never carried by colour alone. A completed step draws a solid marker with a check glyph and an sr-only "Completed"; the current step draws a heavier ring and carries both `aria-current="step"` and an sr-only "Current step"; an upcoming step draws a faint ring in muted ink and an sr-only "Upcoming". So a reader in greyscale, a reader with a colour vision deficiency and a reader listening all learn the same three states, from shape, from the words, and from position in the list. The check glyph is `aria-hidden`, because the word "Completed" beside it already carries the meaning and announcing the glyph as well would say it twice.

The component holds no tab stop, because it is read-only and has nothing to operate. This is by design: it reports where the reader is and never moves them, so there is no button, no link and no focusable mark. A reader steps straight past it to the interactive content of the current step, which is where the flow's own controls live.

What is not yet checked is listed rather than left out, and it matches the open questions at the top of this page. No contrast pair has been measured for the marker fills against the page or for the muted description ink; nobody has listened to the component in a screen reader, so whether `aria-current` and the sr-only "Current step" announce the current step twice is unverified; and there is no `forced-colors` or `prefers-contrast: more` rule, so a reader in either mode meets whatever the browser substitutes for the neutral fills.

## API reference

<PropsTable name="StepperProps" />

`current` is a 0-based index into `steps` and makes this an indicator with no state of its own: the product passes the step the reader is on and the component draws it. A value outside the range, or a non-integer, is truncated and clamped to the nearest real step and raises a development warning, because a progress indicator that renders nothing helps nobody and a step the product did not choose would misreport the reader's position.

**Easily confused with**

- [Progress](./progress.mdx) shows one continuous measure of how far through a single task the reader is. A stepper names discrete, ordered steps and marks which one is current, so reach for it when the steps have names and for progress when only the fraction matters.
- [Tabs](./tabs.mdx) let a reader switch between views in any order and change nothing by choosing one. A stepper shows an ordered sequence with a before and an after, and it changes nothing at all because it only reports.
- [Field](./field.mdx) is where a question actually lives. A stepper sits beside the fields of the current step and reports position; it never collects an answer.

**Used by**

- No pattern, recipe or screen composes it yet. It is an indicator a product places beside the current step's content, and wiring it to a real flow is a product's to make rather than something the corpus ships today.
