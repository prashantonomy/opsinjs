A history is a list of things that happened, and each thing needs three facts side by side:
what it was, when it was recorded, and, where a product has made a judgement, how much
attention it needs. TimelineEntry places one of those events on a vertical rail. It is
deliberately one entry and not the timeline, because a timeline is a layout every product
builds differently, and a component that owned the list would own a data model with it.

So the product keeps the list. It supplies the `ol` or `ul`, decides what an entry is and
in what order they run, and drops a TimelineEntry into each row. The entry draws the neutral
rail and marker, writes the recorded time the way people say it, and shows any status the
product assigned on a pill that keeps its own word and glyph. The entry itself stays out of
both colour axes.

## Usage

```tsx
import { TimelineEntry } from "@/components/ui/timeline-entry"
```

```tsx
<ol>
  <TimelineEntry
    when="2026-03-14T08:12:00+00:00"
    now={new Date().toISOString()}
    title="Repeat prescription issued"
    status="watch"
    isLast
  >
    Issued by the example clinic.
  </TimelineEntry>
</ol>
```

The first exclusion is the one that matters. If you find yourself wanting the component to
hold the list, sort it, group it by day or fetch it, you want a layout the product builds
and this entry is a piece of it rather than a replacement for it. Lay out your own list, put
one TimelineEntry in each row, and set `isLast` on the final one so the rail does not trail
past it.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** That the named event was recorded at the given instant, and nothing
more. It does not assert that the event was clinically significant, that the record of the
history is complete, or that the time is still fresh. A gap between two entries is not
evidence that nothing happened in it, and this component makes no claim either way.

**What it must never be read as.** A diagnosis, a verdict, or a guarantee that the history
in front of the reader is complete or current. Where the product supplies a status, the
status says how much attention that one event needs, in the product's own words on a pill;
the entry adds nothing to it. TimelineEntry never triages, never diagnoses and never
advises, and any words in the title or the body are the product's, not the system's.

**Which colour axis it may use.** Neither, on the entry itself. The rail and the marker are
neutral chrome, a muted fill and a hairline, and they take colour from neither the category
axis nor the status axis. Where the product supplies a `status` it is delegated to a nested
StatusPill, which carries the status axis with a word, a distinct glyph and a colour
together, exactly as it does everywhere else in the system. The entry around it stays
neutral, so a status colour never lands on the record of an event where a reader could not
tell it from a category tint. See [The two colour axes](../health/two-colour-axes.mdx).

**Who owns the thresholds and the words.** The consuming product, always. TimelineEntry
owns no thresholds, no staleness boundary and no event text: the product supplies the
title, the body, the status and the instant, and the component draws them. It shares that
discipline with the two pages that govern it. [Uncertainty, staleness and missing
data](../health/uncertainty-and-staleness.mdx) is why the entry never guesses whether a
recorded time is now too old to trust, and [Trends and change](../health/trends-and-change.mdx)
is why one entry is one event rather than a shape that implies a direction the product never
claimed.

The parts are `data-slot` names, not exports. There is no `TimelineEntry.Rail` to import and
the entry is not composable: which parts exist is decided by the props, so a status pill
appears only when a status was given, a body only when there were children, and the
connector only when the entry is not the last. The connector line inside the rail has no
`data-slot` of its own; it is drawn or not drawn from `isLast` and styled through the rail.

The root is a list item, and it wants a list around it. Place your TimelineEntry elements
inside your own `ol` or `ul` so a screen reader announces the history as a list of so many
items. The component supplies the item and the product supplies the list, which is the same
split that keeps a data model out of opsinjs.

## Content guidelines

Name the event in the title as something that happened: `Repeat prescription issued`, `Blood
test booked`, `Appointment attended`. A title that is a bare noun leaves the reader to guess
whether the thing was done, is due, or was cancelled, and the recorded time cannot answer
that on its own.

Let the status say only what the product's own rule says, and let it stay on the pill. If
you feel the pull to colour the whole row so a serious event stands out, that pull is the
bug: a tinted row is a status carried by colour alone, and a reader cannot tell it from a
category tint or read it in greyscale.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"Repeat prescription issued" with the recorded time beneath it and, where the product assigned one, a status on a pill beside the title.** Names the event, dates it, and keeps any verdict on the pill where it carries a word and a glyph.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **The whole entry tinted amber to signal the event needs attention.** It paints a status colour onto the record of an event, leaves a reader unable to tell it from a category tint, and drops the word and glyph a status needs to survive greyscale.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>

## Accessibility

Some of this is enforced by a gate on every pull request, and the rest is argued from the
source and labelled as such. Alpha promises only that accessibility is claimed where it has
been checked, with the gaps listed rather than left out, and nothing here has been through a
review with a disabled user.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured.** The rail and the marker
carry `aria-hidden`, so the drawing is out of the accessibility tree and a screen reader
meets the recorded time, the title, any status word and the body in reading order, with
nothing said twice. The recorded time is the system RelativeTime, so the exact date and time
are always in the accessibility tree even when the phrase alone is on screen. The status,
where there is one, rides a StatusPill that carries its own word and glyph, so the level
survives greyscale and does not depend on the pill's colour. Nothing in the entry is
focusable, because nothing in it is a control.

**The list is the product's to provide.** The root is a list item, and it announces as one
item of a list only when a product places it inside an `ol` or `ul`. A lone entry outside a
list is still readable but loses the count, so the page asks you to wrap your entries as the
examples do.

**Not checked at all, and listed rather than omitted.** No contrast pair has been put in
front of a machine: the muted marker and the hairline connector are painted on whatever
ground the host provides, and that pairing is unmeasured. Marker alignment against the time
at 200 percent text has not been checked, and no screen reader has met the component.

## API reference

`when`, `now` and `title` are required. `when` is ISO 8601 with an offset, the same
contract RelativeTime enforces on its `at`, and `now` is the reference instant, taken as a
prop because this component never reads the clock. `now` accepts a Date, a number or a
string for the caller's convenience and is normalised to an ISO string before it reaches
RelativeTime. `status` is optional and is the four-level clinical union shared across the
system; a value outside the four is refused by StatusPill itself and no pill is drawn.
`isLast` stops the connector so the rail does not trail past the final entry. The event word
is fixed at `Recorded` and is not a prop, and no `locale` is forwarded, so a product that
needs either composes RelativeTime directly.
