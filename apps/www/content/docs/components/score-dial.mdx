A dial is the most persuasive graphic in the set and therefore the most dangerous. A number
inside a ring reads as authoritative, complete and personal regardless of how thin the
evidence behind it is, and a ring that fills up reads as a mark out of a maximum. Everything
in this component is arranged so the words carry the message and the arc only illustrates it:
cover the arc with your hand and nothing is lost.

## Preview

The default demo is a three-band dial with a status mapped to the middle band. Two things are
worth checking rather than believing. Turn the text size up: the arc, the score, the band name
and the derivation sentence all grow together, because the layout is stacked at every size and
there is no overlaid arrangement to reflow away from. Then look at it in greyscale: the band
name and the status word are still there, which is the whole reason they are visible text

The test for whether a dial is honest is simple: can you write the derivation sentence? If
*your sleep score combines time asleep, how often you woke, and how much of the night was
deep sleep* is a sentence you can stand behind, the dial is earned. If the derivation is
proprietary, unstable, or nobody in the room can describe it, the dial is a decorative
authority claim and [Value](./value.mdx) is the honest component.

The implementation makes that test hard to dodge. `derivation` is required, it is always
rendered, and an empty one produces a development warning and a visible sentence saying that
this dial cannot explain itself — which is true, and is not something anybody wants on screen.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** That the product computed a number from stated inputs, that the number
falls in a named band on a stated scale, and nothing else. The dial asserts a calculation,
not a fact about the reader's body.

**What it must never be read as.** A measurement, a diagnosis, or a comparison with other
people. A score of 62 is not sixty-two per cent of anything, it is not a mark out of a
hundred, and it is not a rank. This is why the scale renders as *on a scale of 0 to 100*
rather than *out of 100*: the second phrasing is the one that produces the reading the
paragraph above forbids, and the component would otherwise be arguing with itself.

**Which colour axis it may use.** The **status** axis, on the band the score fell in and on
the indicator, and only when the product has mapped that band to a clinical status. Only that
one band is tinted, never the whole set: a three-band dial coloured green, amber and red is a
traffic light, and a traffic light is a verdict. Every other band is drawn in the same neutral
tone and is told apart by its position, by a boundary mark, and by its name in the list
beneath the arc. The **category** axis tints the label and nothing else — never the track,
the bands or the indicator.

**Status is never carried by colour alone, here or anywhere.** When a band maps to a status,
the dial renders that status as a word from `CLINICAL_STATUS_META` and one of the four
distinct glyphs beside it, and stamps `data-status` on the elements that take the tint. The
band's own name is the product's word for that range of the scale; the status word is this
system's word for how much attention it wants. Both are shown, because they answer different
questions and a reader is entitled to both answers.

**Who owns the bands and the thresholds.** The consuming product, always. ScoreDial ships no
scales, no bands and no cut-offs, and there is no default anywhere in the file. A component
library that shipped a BMI banding would be shipping a contested clinical judgement into every
product that installed it. Hand it no bands and it draws the scale, places the number on it,
and says in words that it has no band for it — see
[the first example](#opsinjs-ships-no-bands) below.

**Derivation is mandatory content, not an option.**
[Risk and statistics](../health/risk-and-statistics.mdx) makes the wider argument: a bare
number with no denominator and no method invites the reader to fill in both, and they will
fill them in with something worse than the truth.

**Nothing derives a status.** The indicator's angle is arithmetic on `value`, `min` and `max`.
There is no rule in the source of the shape *if the score is past here then it needs
attention*, and a score outside its own scale is clamped onto the arc and reported in
development rather than quietly widening a scale the product declared.

<SafetyCallout severity="attention" evidence="opinion">
When only part of the input data was available, the dial must say so on its face — *based on
4 of 6* — rather than presenting a weaker score as if it were a full one. Products that
quietly compute a score from partial data teach readers to trust a number that sometimes means
much less than it appears to. `coverage` is how you say it; what was counted — nights,
readings, days — belongs in your derivation sentence, because this component does not know and
will not guess a noun.
</SafetyCallout>

The parts above are `data-slot` attributes, not exports. There is no `ScoreDial.Band` you can
import and no way to render one part without the others, which is what makes `BandName` and
`Derivation` genuinely mandatory rather than mandatory-by-convention: there is no composition
that omits them.

Two parts are here that the original specification's tree did not name, and both were forced
by its own accessibility section. `ScoreDial.Boundary` exists because bands have to be
distinguishable "by position, boundary marks and their names", and nothing was drawing the
marks. `ScoreDial.Status` exists because a status carried by a tint on an arc is a status
carried by colour alone, which
[the two colour axes](../health/two-colour-axes.mdx) forbids outright — the band's name is the
product's word for a range, and it is not a substitute for the system's word for a level.

There is no `size` prop. The dial is one arrangement at every text size, and it grows with the
reader rather than switching between layouts at a breakpoint.

### opsinjs ships no bands

does not reach for thirds of the scale, a population average, or a band set from somewhere
else.

## Content guidelines

Band names are the product's vocabulary. Keep them plain, non-judgemental, and free of the
words banned across the system: **normal**, **abnormal**, **good**, **bad**, **poor**,
**failed**. A band name describes a range of a scale; it does not describe the person whose
score landed in it.

The derivation sentence is one sentence in the second person, naming the inputs a reader would
recognise and the window they cover. It is also the only place band provenance can currently
be stated, so if the bands came from somewhere — a published instrument, your own clinical
review — say so there.

`coverage` renders as *Based on 4 of 6*, with no noun. Name the noun in the derivation
sentence: this component does not know whether the six were nights, readings or days, and it
will not guess.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"62. Fair. On a scale of 0 to 100. Based on how long you slept, how often you woke, and
    how much deep sleep you had, over the last 4 nights."** The band is named, the scale is
    stated, the method is disclosed, and the window is explicit.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **"Sleep score: 62"** in a coloured ring with no scale, no band and no method. Every
    missing piece is one the reader supplies from imagination, and *62 out of what* is the
    first thing they get wrong.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"Based on 4 of the last 6 nights."** States the gap so the reader can weigh the score
    themselves.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>

## Accessibility

Some of this is checked by a gate on every commit and the rest is argued. The split is
labelled, because `alpha` promises that accessibility is claimed only where it has been
checked, with the gaps listed rather than left out.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y` on every commit:**

- **Colour is never the only carrier of a status.** The static rig fails the build on any file
  that paints a status colour without also reading `CLINICAL_STATUS_META` and a lucide glyph,
  and on any status surface with no `data-status`. This component reads the word and the glyph
  from that table and stamps the attribute on the band, the indicator and the status line. The
  four glyphs are four distinct *shapes* — check, eye, triangle, octagon — which is what the
  measured CVD audit in `tokens/color.json` requires: `steady` and `attention` are identical
  under deuteranopia and in greyscale, and `steady` and `urgent` are identical under
  tritanopia. See [Colour independence](../accessibility/colour-independence.mdx).
- **The status words are never restated.** They are read from
  `CLINICAL_STATUS_META[level].word`; a copy written as a literal fails the same gate.
- **The two axes never resolve on one element.** The rig reads every JSX element and every
  `cn()` body and fails on any that takes colour from both. The category reaches the label and
  the status reaches the arc, and they are separate elements by construction.
- **No thresholds ship.** A11Y014 fails the build on any name matching
  `stale|threshold|cutoff|limit|minimum|maximum|band|plausib` given a numeric literal, in a
  default, a property or a JSX prop. There is no number in this component that decides
  anything clinical, and the gate is what keeps it that way.
- **No type size in `px`, no raw colour, no numbered ramp step, no class that resolves to
  nothing under the product theme.** All four are gate-enforced across `registry/**`.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **The graphic is a labelled image and nothing else.** `role="img"` with the whole component
  as its accessible sentence — *Example composite score: 62, on a scale of 0 to 100. Second
  example band. Watch. Based on 4 of 6. …* It is not a `meter`, not a `progressbar` and not a
  `slider`: all three announce a value within a range, which is the assertion this component
  is not entitled to make, and `progressbar` additionally implies a task advancing towards
  completion. It carries `focusable="false"` and is not in the tab order.
- **The text twin is visible, not hidden.** The band name, the status word, the scale, the band
  list and the derivation are ordinary text in the document. Nothing is `sr-only`, nothing is
  behind a hover, and no prop turns any of it off. The accessible sentence on the arc repeats
  it deliberately, so a reader who lands on the graphic hears what it depicts without going
  looking for the words.
- **Bands survive greyscale.** Only one band is ever tinted, the internal edges are drawn as
  radial marks, and every band is named with its range in the list beneath the arc.
- **At 200% text nothing truncates.** The layout is a stacked column at every size, so there is
  no overlaid arrangement to reflow away from and no case where the score sits inside a ring
  that stops fitting it. The arc's width is set in `em`, so it grows with the text rather than
  staying put beside it. Checked by eye in both themes; not yet checked by a machine.
- **Nothing animates.** There is no transition, no sweep and no count-up anywhere in the
  source, on first paint or on a change. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` there is nothing to
  degrade because there was never anything there —
  [Motion in health UI](../health/motion-in-health-ui.mdx) names the dial sweep specifically,
  and a sweep displays, for every frame of its travel, a score that is not true.

**Not yet checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- Contrast for the pairs this component actually composes. The roles it uses are measured
  individually — `status.<level>.ink-on-page`, `status.<level>.accent-on-page`,
  `category.<name>.ink-on-page`, `neutral.hairline-on-page` — but the arc against a card and
  the indicator against the band beneath it are not, and neither is the whole component under
  `prefers-contrast` or `forced-colors`, which are not implemented anywhere in opsinjs yet.
- Print. The arc should render as an outline with the indicator as a solid mark and the
  reading, band name and derivation as text. There is no print stylesheet in this repository
  to make that true, so it is a requirement rather than a result.
- What a screen reader does when the score changes while the dial is on screen. The component
  mounts no live region, deliberately — it is not entitled to speak on the caller's behalf —
  and what a caller should do instead is an open question on this page.

## API reference

<PropsTable name="ScoreDialProps" />

`bands` is required and opsinjs supplies no default for it — that is the single most important
line in the interface. An empty array is a legitimate value and renders the explicit no-band
state; it is not an error to be repaired with a guess.

`derivation` is required too, and it is the only prop whose absence changes what the component
says about itself rather than what it draws. `min` and `max` are both required because an
unbounded dial is unreadable, and `value` is `number | null` because a score of zero and a
score that was never calculated are different facts about somebody's record.

Three props are additions to the original specification, and they are named here rather than
slipped in. `precision` and `locale` exist because
[Numbers, units and precision](../health/numbers-units-precision.mdx) governs this page and
requires every number to render through [Value](./value.mdx), which takes both — without them
a fractional score would print whatever digits the double happened to hold. `className` is the
merge point every component in the system carries.

`ScoreBand` is exported alongside the props interface. The generator only emits a table for
interfaces named `<Pascal>Props`, so there is no generated one for it; this is the shipped
declaration, copied from `registry/bases/base/score-dial.tsx`:

```ts
export interface ScoreBand {
  /** Inclusive lower bound, on the score's own scale. */
  from: number
  /** Exclusive upper bound, on the score's own scale. */
  to: number
  /** The band in words, in the reader's language. Required. */
  name: string
  /** The clinical status the product mapped this band to, if it mapped one. */
  status?: ClinicalStatus
}
```

Bands are half-open, so contiguous bands can be written without a gap and without an overlap.
The one exception is the top of the scale: a score exactly equal to `max` falls in the band
whose `to` is `max`, because otherwise the highest score a scale allows would belong to no band
at all. Overlapping bands resolve to the first match in declaration order — the component does
not reorder, merge or repair them, and it has no rule of its own about which should win.

There is no `size`, no `color`, no `variant` and no prop that hides the band name or the
derivation. There is no `sweep`, `animate` or `duration` either: a dial that animates is the
failure this component's own doctrine page names first.
