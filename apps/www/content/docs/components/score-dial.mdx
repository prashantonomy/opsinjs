A dial is the most persuasive graphic in this set and therefore the most dangerous. A
number inside a ring reads as authoritative, complete and personal, regardless of how thin
the evidence behind it is. Everything in this specification is arranged around making the
derivation as visible as the score.

The test for whether a dial is honest is simple: can you write the derivation sentence? If
*your sleep score combines time asleep, how often you woke, and how much of the night was
deep sleep* is a sentence you can stand behind, the dial is earned. If the derivation is
proprietary, unstable, or nobody in the room can describe it, the dial is a decorative
authority claim and [Value](./value.mdx) is the honest component.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** That the product computed a number from stated inputs, that the number
falls in a named band on a stated scale, and nothing else. The dial asserts a calculation,
not a fact about the reader's body.

**What it must never be read as.** A measurement, a diagnosis, or a comparison with other
people. A score of 62 is not sixty-two per cent of anything, it is not a mark out of a
hundred, and it is not a rank. Where the underlying scale is a validated instrument, the
dial must not imply a clinical cut-off the instrument does not have.

**Which colour axis it may use.** The **status** axis, on the band the value falls in, and
only when the product has mapped that band to a clinical status. Bands are not statuses by
default: a three-band dial coloured green, amber and red is a traffic light, and a traffic
light is a verdict. When no status mapping exists the bands are drawn in neutral tones and
distinguished by position and label. The **category** axis may tint the dial's label and
icon, never the track or the bands.

**Who owns the bands and the thresholds.** The consuming product, always. ScoreDial ships
no scales, no bands and no cut-offs. A component library that shipped a BMI banding would be
shipping a contested clinical judgement into every product that installed it.

**Derivation is mandatory content, not an option.**
[Risk and statistics](../health/risk-and-statistics.mdx) makes the wider argument: a bare
number with no denominator and no method invites the reader to fill in both, and they will
fill them in with something worse than the truth.

<SafetyCallout severity="medium" evidence="opinion">
When only part of the input data was available, the dial must say so on its face — *based on
4 of 6 nights* — rather than presenting a weaker score as if it were a normal one. Products
that quietly compute a score from partial data teach readers to trust a number that
sometimes means much less than it appears to.
</SafetyCallout>

## Anatomy

<Anatomy name="score-dial" />

<CompositionTree name="score-dial" />

**Track** is the full sweep of the scale. **Bands** are the named regions the product
defined, drawn once and labelled in words. **Indicator** is the score's position.
**Reading** is the number and, more importantly, the band's name — the word is what the
reader remembers. **Derivation** is the sentence saying what went into it and over what
window. **Scale** states the bounds, because a dial with no visible bounds is a dial the
reader will assume runs to a hundred.

Proposed part hierarchy. This is the specification the implementation must match.

```text
ScoreDial                      root; owns value, scale, bands, no defaults
├── ScoreDial.Track            the full sweep, always fully drawn
│   ├── ScoreDial.Band         a named region; neutral unless the product maps a status
│   └── ScoreDial.Indicator    the score's position; the only status-coloured part
├── ScoreDial.Reading
│   ├── ScoreDial.Score        the number
│   └── ScoreDial.BandName     the band in words — never omitted, never colour alone
├── ScoreDial.Scale            the bounds, e.g. "out of 100"
└── ScoreDial.Derivation       what went into it, over what window, from how much data
```

`ScoreDial.BandName` and `ScoreDial.Derivation` are mandatory parts. A dial rendered without
them should fail loudly in development with a named error code rather than degrade quietly.

export interface ScoreBand {
  /** Inclusive lower bound on the score's own scale. */
  from: number

  to: number

  name: string

   * Optional mapping to the clinical status axis. Omit it and the band is drawn
   * in neutral tones: bands are not statuses unless the product says so.
   */
  status?: ClinicalStatus
}

export interface ScoreDialProps {
  label: string
  /** `null` renders the no-score state, which is not a score of zero. */
  value: number | null
  /** The scale's bounds. Both required: an unbounded dial is unreadable. */
  min: number
  max: number
  /** Contiguous, non-overlapping, covering the whole scale. */
  bands: ScoreBand[]
  /**
   * One sentence: what went into the score and over what window. Required.
   * A dial that cannot explain itself should not be drawn.
   */
  derivation: string
  /**
   * Data completeness behind the score, e.g. { available: 4, expected: 6 }.
   * When present and short, the dial shows its partial-data treatment.
   */
  coverage?: { available: number; expected: number }
  /** Label and icon treatment only. Never the track or the bands. */
  category?: HealthCategory
  measuredAt?: string
}

## Content guidelines

Lead with the band name, not the number. *Your sleep score is 62 — fair* is read as a
grade; *fair, 62 out of 100* is read as a description. The band names are the product's
vocabulary and they should be plain, non-judgemental, and free of the words banned across
the system: **normal**, **abnormal**, **good**, **bad**, **poor**, **failed**.

The derivation sentence is one sentence in the second person, naming the inputs a reader
would recognise and the window they cover.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"Sleep score 62 out of 100 — fair. Based on how long you slept, how often you woke,
    and how much deep sleep you had, over the last 4 nights."** The band is named, the scale
    is stated, the method is disclosed, and the window is explicit.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **"Sleep score: 62"** in a coloured ring with no scale, no band and no method. Every
    missing piece is one the reader supplies from imagination, and 62 out of what is the
    first thing they get wrong.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"Based on 4 of the last 6 nights."** States the gap so the reader can weigh the score
    themselves.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **Computing the score from whatever data exists and showing it identically.** The reader
    has no way to know that today's number rests on a third of the usual evidence.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

## Accessibility requirements

Requirements for the implementation, not results.

- **The dial is a static image with an accessible name**, not a `meter` and not a
  `progressbar`: neither role carries the band vocabulary, and `progressbar` implies a task
  advancing towards completion.
- **The accessible name is a sentence**, not a number: *sleep score 62 out of 100, fair,
  based on 4 of the last 6 nights*. It is the whole component in one utterance.
- **Bands are distinguishable without colour** by position, boundary marks and their names.
  Verified by the generated CVD audit — see
  [Colour independence](../accessibility/colour-independence.mdx).
- **The number and the band name meet the contrast floor against every band fill** they can
  appear over, in both themes.
- **At 200% text the dial reflows** to a stacked form with the score and band name above a
  reduced-height arc; the derivation sentence never truncates.
- **No entry animation carries meaning.** If the indicator sweeps into position, it is a
  single short transition, identical at every band, and removed entirely under
  `prefers-reduced-motion`. A dial that animates faster when the score is worse is exactly
  the failure [Motion in health UI](../health/motion-in-health-ui.mdx) prohibits.
- **Print** renders the arc as an outline with the indicator as a solid mark, plus the
  reading, band name and derivation as text.

**Easily confused with**

- [RangeBar](./range-bar.mdx) — measured value against a published range, rather than a
  calculated score against bands you defined. If you did the arithmetic, it is a dial.
- `progress` (considered, no page yet — see the [roster](./index.mdx)) — a task advancing
  towards a goal, not a score describing a state.
- [MetricTile](./metric-tile.mdx) — the compact readout. A tile can hold a miniature dial,
  but not the derivation sentence, which is why a dial rarely belongs in a grid.
- `goal-ring` (considered) — the ring the reader fills by doing something. A dial is
  something that happened to them; a goal ring is something they did.
