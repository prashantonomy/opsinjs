A dial is the most persuasive graphic in the set and therefore the most dangerous. A number
inside a ring reads as authoritative, complete and personal regardless of how thin the
evidence behind it is, and a ring that fills up reads as a mark out of a maximum. Everything
in this component is arranged so the words carry the message and the arc only illustrates it:
cover the arc with your hand and nothing is lost.

## Preview

The default demo has no bands, and that is deliberate rather than unfinished. `shadcn add`
copies this function verbatim into a consumer's project and `/r/score-dial.json` serves it as
text, so anything it shows is shipped: a band set here would be a score interpretation with no
clinical owner, installed into every product that ran the command. It shows the refusal
instead, drawing the scale, placing the number on it and saying in words that there is no
band. That is the most important thing this component does. The scale runs from ten to
twenty and the score is fourteen, because those are numbers nobody could mistake for their
own result.

Two things are worth checking rather than believing. Turn the text size up: the score, the band
name, the scale sentence and the derivation all grow, and the layout stays one stacked column
with nothing to reflow away from. The arc has a cap of its own, spelled in `rem` so it tracks
the reader's text size, but it never exceeds the column, so it grows only until the column binds
and then holds. On a phone the column is already the binding constraint, so the words grow and
the arc keeps its width, which is what nothing truncating and nothing overflowing asks of it.
Then look at the
[partial-data example](#a-score-built-from-less-than-usual) in greyscale: the band name and the
status word are still there, which is the whole reason they are visible text rather than an
`aria-label`.

The test for whether a dial is honest is simple: can you write the derivation sentence? Name
the inputs your product actually used and the window they cover, in the second person, in one
sentence a reader would recognise. Then be willing to put it on the same screen as the number.
If the derivation is proprietary, unstable, or nobody in the room can describe it, the dial is
a decorative authority claim and [Value](./value.mdx) is the honest component.

The implementation makes that test hard to dodge. `derivation` is required, it is always
rendered, and an empty one produces a development warning and a visible sentence saying that
this dial cannot explain itself. That is true, and it is not something anybody wants on screen.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** That the product computed a number from stated inputs, that the number
falls in a named band on a stated scale, and nothing else. The dial asserts a calculation,
not a fact about the reader's body.

**What it must never be read as.** A measurement, a diagnosis, or a comparison with other
people. A score of fourteen on a scale of ten to twenty is not a percentage of anything, it is
not a mark out of twenty, and it is not a rank. This is why the scale renders as *on a scale
of 10 to 20* rather than *out of 20*: the second phrasing is the one that produces the reading
the paragraph above forbids, and the component would otherwise be arguing with itself.

**Which colour axis it may use.** The **status** axis, on the indicator and on the band the
score fell in, and only when the product has assigned a level to the reading. Only that one
band is tinted, never the whole set: a three-band dial coloured green, amber and red is a
traffic light, and a traffic light is a verdict. Every other band is drawn in the same neutral
tone and is told apart by its position, by a boundary mark, and by its name in the list
beneath the arc. The **category** axis tints the label and nothing else. It never touches the
track, the bands or the indicator.

**Status is never carried by colour alone, here or anywhere.** When the product assigns a
level, the dial renders it as a word from `CLINICAL_STATUS_META` and one of the four distinct
glyphs beside it, and stamps `data-status` on the elements that take the tint. The band's own
name is the product's word for that range of the scale; the status word is this system's word
for how much attention it wants. Both are shown, because they answer different questions and a
reader is entitled to both answers.

**Who owns the bands and the thresholds.** The consuming product, always. ScoreDial ships no
scales, no bands and no cut-offs, and there is no default anywhere in the file. That includes
the zero-prop demo, which `shadcn add` copies into a consumer's project and which therefore
passes an empty `bands`. A component library that shipped a BMI banding would be shipping a
contested clinical judgement into every product that installed it. Hand it no bands and it
draws the scale, places the number on it, and says in words that it has no band for it. See
[the first example](#opsinjs-ships-no-bands) below.

**Every band names its own source.** `ScoreBand.source` is required and is rendered under the
band list, because two numbers that define an interval somebody is compared against are a
comparison a person chose, exactly like a reference range. A band set carries more weight than
a bare range rather than less, since it also puts a word on each interval. A band that arrives
with no source raises OPSIN-0004 and the dial says *nobody is named as setting these ranges*
rather than letting an unattributed comparison pass as a fact.

**Derivation is mandatory content, not an option.**
[Risk and statistics](../health/risk-and-statistics.mdx) makes the wider argument: a bare
number with no denominator and no method invites the reader to fill in both, and they will
fill them in with something worse than the truth.

**Nothing here derives a status.** `status` is a top-level prop and it is the product's
verdict on this reading, assigned from a reference range or a threshold the product owns.
That is exactly what `CLINICAL_STATUS_META.watch.assignedBy` says it must be. Band membership
is still arithmetic, and it selects a **name**: the product's own word for a stretch of the
product's own scale. It cannot reach a level. An earlier version of this component hung
`status` off each band and let the comparison pick one, which meant a rendering component was
deciding what somebody's number meant; the fix was to take the decision away from it rather
than to document it.

**Two of the four levels are refused.** A named action says who to contact and what to do, in
the first line. `attention` and `urgent` are both defined as carrying one, and a dial has
nowhere to put it. Passing either renders no status at all and reports why; `steady` and
`watch` are the two this surface can discharge honestly. A score that needs somebody to act
belongs in a [CareCard](./care-card.mdx), which carries the action.

**A score outside its scale gets no mark.** Not a mark at the end of the arc: a clamped
indicator draws a hundred and forty in the same place as exactly a hundred, so the picture
asserts something specific and false while the words beside it are true. The dial places
nothing, names the bounds and says *this score is outside the 10 to 20 scale, so we cannot
show where it sits*, and reports it in development. It does not widen a scale the product declared, because it cannot
tell whether the scale is wrong or the score is.

<SafetyCallout severity="attention" evidence="opinion">
When only part of the input data was available, the dial must say so on its face rather than
presenting a weaker score as if it were a full one. The words it uses are *based on 4 of 6*.
Products that quietly compute a score from partial data teach readers to trust a number that
sometimes means much less than it appears to. `coverage` is how you say it; what was counted
belongs in your derivation sentence, because this component does not know whether the count
was nights, readings or days, and it will not guess a noun.
</SafetyCallout>

The parts above are `data-slot` attributes, not exports. There is no `ScoreDial.Band` you can
import and no way to render one part without the others, which is what makes `BandName` and
`Derivation` genuinely mandatory rather than mandatory-by-convention: there is no composition
that omits them.

Three parts are here that the original specification's tree did not name, and each was forced
by something the page had already promised. `ScoreDial.Boundary` exists because bands have to
be distinguishable "by position, boundary marks and their names", and nothing was drawing the
marks. `ScoreDial.Status` exists because a status carried by a tint on an arc is a status
carried by colour alone, which
[the two colour axes](../health/two-colour-axes.mdx) forbids outright. The band's name is the
product's word for a range, and it is not a substitute for the system's word for a level.
`ScoreDial.BandSource` exists because the band list is a comparison shown without an owner
until somebody is named beneath it, which is what
[Reference ranges](../health/reference-ranges.mdx) requires of every comparison on a screen.

There is no `size` prop. The dial is one arrangement at every text size, and it grows with the
reader rather than switching between layouts at a breakpoint.

## Examples

### opsinjs ships no bands

The common case is a product with a number and no agreed bands for it. The arithmetic
usually lands long before anybody has decided what the answer means. Handed an empty `bands`,
the dial draws the scale, places the number on it, and says in words that it has no band. It
does not reach for thirds of the scale, a population average, or a band set from somewhere
else. This is also what the zero-prop demo shows, for the reason given under
[Preview](#preview): the demo is distributed code.

### A score built from less than usual

### Zero is a score; absence is not

Two dials that look almost the same and mean entirely different things. On the left a
calculation that ran and came to the bottom of its scale; on the right nothing calculated at
all, so there is no position to draw and no band to name.

There is a third state neither dial shows. A `value` that is not a finite number is a
calculation that ran and *failed*, and it is neither of these two. A division with no divisor
somewhere upstream is one way to arrive at one. The printed reading says *not available*, and
so does the arc's accessible sentence: the ear and the eye get the same answer, because the
alternative is telling a reader that a number which did exist was never taken.

## States

ScoreDial is not interactive. Nothing in it is focusable, it takes no hover, focus, pressed or
disabled treatment, and Tab passes straight over it, because it is a picture of a reading
somebody else assigned rather than a control a reader operates. The states worth documenting
here are the five data states, and the dial answers each of them in words a reader can weigh
rather than by vanishing or by drawing a mark it cannot stand behind.

| Data state | What ScoreDial renders |
| --- | --- |
| Error | A `value` that is not a finite number is a calculation that ran without arriving at a number. The reading prints *not available*, the band-name line reads "No range is named, because the score could not be worked out.", no indicator is drawn, and the arc's accessible sentence says *not available* too, so the ear and the eye are handed one answer. A scale of no width and a score outside its scale are refused the same way, with no mark and a sentence saying why, and each of the three is reported in development. |
| Loading | None of its own. ScoreDial formats a score the product has already calculated, so it never fetches and has nothing to wait for. A skeleton or a spinner belongs to the surface that composes the dial, which is where the wait actually happens. |
| Empty | A `value` of `null` is the no-score state, which is a calculation that never ran and is not a score of zero. The reading prints *no score yet*, the band-name line reads "No range is named, because there is no score yet.", no indicator is drawn, and the calculated line is withheld because there is no score for it to date. |
| Partial | `coverage` whose `available` count is below its `expected` count puts *Based on 4 of 6* on its own line under the reading and into the accessible sentence, so a score built from part of the usual evidence says so on its face. An empty `bands` array is the neighbouring case: the number is placed on the scale and the words state that no band is named for it, rather than a band being invented to fill the gap. |
| Stale | None of its own, and that is deliberate. The dial renders `calculatedAt` as a plain calculated date with no relative phrasing and no staleness tint, because a staleness window is a number opsinjs does not own for any metric. A dial that has a score but no instant says in words that nobody knows when it was calculated. A caller who needs an old score to read as old renders a RelativeTime beside the dial and hands the whole screen one `now`. |

## Content guidelines

Band names are the product's vocabulary. Keep them plain and non-judgemental: a band name
describes a range of a scale, and it does not describe the person whose score landed in it.
Every word banned across the system is banned here.
[Reference ranges](../health/reference-ranges.mdx) carries the list and the reason for each,
and the union is sixteen rather than the handful an author usually remembers: **normal**,
**abnormal**, **healthy**, **unhealthy**, **good**, **bad**, **poor**, **optimal**,
**perfect**, **elevated**, **positive**, **negative**, **failed**, **just**, **diagnosis**,
**don't worry**. *Optimal* and *Healthy* are the two most likely names for the top of a
wellness scale and both are on it. So is any ladder that runs from poor through fair to good,
which grades the reader rather than the range.

The derivation sentence is one sentence in the second person, naming the inputs a reader would
recognise and the window they cover. Band provenance is no longer its job: `ScoreBand.source`
carries that, it is required, and it is rendered under the band list.

`coverage` renders as *Based on 4 of 6*, with no noun. Name the noun in the derivation
sentence: this component does not know whether the six were nights, readings or days, and it
will not guess.

Everything the component says for itself is British English, and `locale` reaches only the
numbers and the date. If you are shipping in another language, the band names, the derivation
sentence and the label are yours to translate; the component's own sentences are not
translatable without forking the file, and that is a known gap rather than a decision.

    in the last seven days."** The band is named positionally, the scale is stated, the method

  </DoDont.Do>

## Motion

ScoreDial declares no transition, no sweep and no count-up. Nothing animates on first paint,
nothing animates when the score changes while the dial is on screen, and no prop turns motion
on. A health value is never animated onto this surface, because a dial that fills or a number
that counts up shows, for every frame of its travel, a score that is not true.
[Motion in health UI](../health/motion-in-health-ui.mdx) names the dial sweep as the first case
to avoid, and this component obeys it by having no motion of its own to reduce. Since the source
reads no motion token, `prefers-reduced-motion` has nothing to degrade: the arc is drawn once,
in its final position, and it holds there. A reader who needs an old score to read as old is
served by a RelativeTime beside the dial rather than by movement on the arc, because attention on
this surface is carried by a word and a glyph and never by motion.

## Accessibility

Some of this is checked by a gate and the rest is argued. The split is labelled, because
`beta` promises that accessibility is claimed only where it has been checked, with the gaps
listed rather than left out. The two measured contrast failures at the bottom of this section
are among them.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y` on every pull request and on every push to `main`**
(`.github/workflows/ci.yml`; a commit on a branch with no open pull request is not gated):

- **Colour is never the only carrier of a status.** The static rig fails the build on any file
  that paints a status colour without also reading `CLINICAL_STATUS_META` and a lucide glyph,
  and on any status surface with no `data-status`. This component reads the word and the glyph
  from that table and stamps the attribute on the band, the indicator and the pill. The
  four glyphs are four distinct *shapes*: circle, circle-dot, diamond and octagon, an ordinal
  set that borrows no meaning from medicine, from approval or from road signage. Their
  distinctness is what the measured CVD audit in `tokens/color.json` requires: `steady` and
  `attention` are identical under deuteranopia and in greyscale, and `steady` and `urgent` are
  identical under tritanopia. See [Colour independence](../accessibility/colour-independence.mdx).
- **The status words are never restated.** They are read from
  `CLINICAL_STATUS_META[level].word`; a copy written as a literal fails the same gate.
- **The two axes never resolve on one element.** The rig reads every JSX element and every
  `cn()` body and fails on any that takes colour from both. The category reaches the label and
  the status reaches the arc, and they are separate elements by construction.
- **No thresholds and no intervals ship.** Two rules, and it is worth knowing what each can
  see. A11Y014 fails the build on a *name* that reads like a threshold given a numeric
  literal. Those names include `stale`, `threshold`, a cut-off, a `limit`, a `minimum` or
  `maximum`, an expiry, a `band` and a plausibility bound; `scripts/check-a11y.mts` holds the
  exact pattern, and it is longer than any summary of it. A11Y015 fails the build on a
  *shape*: any object literal setting two numeric bounds with no `source` in the same literal.
  The pairs it counts as bounds are `low`/`high`, `from`/`to`, `lower`/`upper` and
  `start`/`end`. The second rule exists because the first could not see this component's own
  defect: an earlier version shipped a three-band set with a level mapped to the middle band,
  in the demo that `shadcn add` copies into consumer projects, and the bounds sat under
  property names the first rule does not look at, so the gate reported the file clean. Both
  rules now pass over this component, its three examples and the snippet above, and the
  shipped demo passes an empty `bands`.
- **No type size in `px`, no raw colour, no numbered ramp step, no class that resolves to
  nothing under the product theme.** All four are gate-enforced across `registry/**`.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **The graphic is a labelled image and nothing else.** `role="img"` with the whole component
  as its accessible sentence, which reads *Example composite score: 14, on a scale of 10 to
  20. Second example band. Watch. Based on 4 of 6. …* It is not a `meter`, not a `progressbar`
  and not a `slider`: all three announce a value within a range, which is the assertion this
  component is not entitled to make, and `progressbar` additionally implies a task advancing
  towards completion. It carries `focusable="false"` and is not in the tab order.
- **The text twin is visible, not hidden.** The band name, the status word, the scale, the band
  list, the band source and the derivation are ordinary text in the document. None of the
  mandatory visible words is hidden, nothing is behind a hover, and no prop of this component
  turns any of it off. The one `sr-only` span the dial contains is the subject phrase the
  StatusPill adds through `describes`, so a listener hears the level attached to what it is
  about rather than as a floating word.
  One caveat, because it is true: `className` is merged last and a class you pass wins, so
  `truncate`, `sr-only`, a fixed height or a zeroed type size will clip or hide the words while
  leaving the arc's accessible sentence intact. The prop's own doc comment says so. The
  accessible sentence repeats the twin deliberately, because the graphic is not a tab stop and
  cannot be landed on. The reader who hears it is therefore one reading linearly, and they hear
  a summary before the detail rather than a tree of unlabelled shapes.
- **Bands survive greyscale.** Only one band is ever tinted, every band is named with its range
  in the list beneath the arc, and the internal edges are drawn as radial marks derived from
  the band bounds by value. A band set written out of order, or one with a gap in it, therefore
  marks the edges it actually has. That last part was a real defect: the marks used to be taken
  by array position, which dropped a real edge for any band set not declared in ascending order.
- **At 200% text nothing truncates.** The layout is a stacked column at every size, so there is
  no overlaid arrangement to reflow away from and no case where the score sits inside a ring
  that stops fitting it. The arc's width is the smaller of a `rem` cap and the column, so it
  grows with the text until the column binds and then holds. That holding is the correct
  behaviour, not a failure of it: the requirement is that nothing truncates and nothing
  overflows, not that a picture redundant to the words keeps pace with them. Checked by eye in
  both themes; not yet checked by a machine. The
  hazard at 200% is not truncation but overflow: a fractional score with no precision stated
  prints every digit the double holds. Seventeen tabular figures with no break opportunity are
  wider than the column. `precision` is required in the type, so a TypeScript caller cannot
  reach this state; it is the JavaScript-consumer path the runtime report covers, because in a
  JavaScript project a required prop is advice rather than a guarantee. `precision` is
  load-bearing for reflow as well as for honesty, which is why the prop is required rather than
  left to the caller.
- **The indicator is drawn whole.** Its outer tip reaches a radius the `viewBox` is sized from
  rather than one typed in beside it. The two used to be independent, and for any score in the
  middle third of a scale the tip was clipped at the top of the box. About a fifth of the only
  mark that shows position was missing, at the place a reader looks first.
- **Nothing animates.** There is no transition, no sweep and no count-up anywhere in the
  source, on first paint or on a change. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` there is nothing to
  degrade because there was never anything there.
  [Motion in health UI](../health/motion-in-health-ui.mdx) names the dial sweep specifically,
  and a sweep displays, for every frame of its travel, a score that is not true.

**Measured, and failing. The roles and verdicts, with the numbers in the generated report below:**

- **The track is no longer the faint hairline the audit caught.** The whole track and every
  untinted band are stroked with the chrome border role, spelled `text-border` in the source and
  resolving through `--border` to `--opsin-chrome-border`. Wave 1 token work moved that role onto
  the neutral ladder at a mid-ramp step in both themes, so the arc is a rail a reader can see. The
  rig now gates that role: the pairs `neutral.border-on-page` and `neutral.border-on-card`,
  recorded in `lib/generated/contrast.json` once the contrast generator has run, measure the rail
  against the page and against the card a dial sits in. On a light card the rail clears the
  non-text floor. In dark it clears the WCAG ratio, but a one-pixel neutral line on a near-black
  page cannot reach the APCA non-text floor at any step the ladder holds, and that shortfall is
  the residual D5 states rather than tunes away.
- **The one tinted band is painted with a status accent, and in light that is the half to
  watch.** `status.<level>.accent-on-page` is the fill of the single meaning-bearing band. The
  four accents were re-seeded in wave 1, so the current verdict for each level is in the report
  below rather than fixed here. This is a light-first consumer-health system, so the light theme
  is the common half and the one to read first.
- **The indicator crossing a tinted band fails the non-text floor at every level in both
  themes.** `status.<level>.ink-on-accent` is the indicator's ink against the band it crosses, and
  it is the pairing that most needs to hold, because the indicator is the only mark that shows
  position. It fails worst in dark. This is a token-layer repair, not one the component can paint
  its way out of.
- **What follows from that.** The component's position is that the arc is redundant to the
  words: the band name, the status word, the scale, the band list and the derivation carry
  everything, the glyph carries the level a second time, and covering the arc with your hand
  loses nothing. That argument is what made a failing stroke tolerable at `beta`. The track's own
  repair has now happened, in the token layer, where wave 1 moved the border role onto the neutral
  ladder; the tinted-band accent and the indicator-on-band pair still need a token-layer fix,
  because this component cannot reach the floor with a colour the system does not own. The two
  roles it uses for text and for the untinted indicator on the page, `status.<level>.ink-on-page`
  and `category.<name>.ink-on-page`, pass in both themes.

**Not yet checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- The component does not answer `prefers-contrast`, so a reader who asks for more contrast gets
  the same strokes as everyone else. Contrast for the pairs the arc *composes* is no longer the
  unchecked case: the rail is gated against both the page and the card and both verdicts sit in
  the report below, and the indicator against the band beneath it has been measured and sits in
  the failing block above.
- Under forced colours the strokes follow the reader's theme rather than the author. A
  `forced-colors: active` rule now maps the track, the untinted bands, the marks and the
  indicator to `CanvasText`, and it maps the one band the reading fell in to `Highlight` so
  that band stays distinct from the track. All four status levels collapse to that single
  `Highlight` under the mode, so the level is told apart by the band word and the glyph rather
  than by colour, which is the same text twin the arc leans on everywhere else. A
  forced-colours palette is the reader's own, so nobody has opened the dial under a real one
  and the result is not claimed as measured.
- Print. The `@media print` block in `app/globals.css` is this documentation site's own
  chrome, and `shadcn add` does not ship it. That block is why a compliance reviewer's paper
  copy of *this page* collapses translucency, prints at 11pt on white and resolves link hrefs.
  `score-dial.tsx` carries no `print:` variant of its own either, so a consumer who copies the
  dial in gets no print treatment from opsinjs at all and the dial prints as whatever that
  app's own stylesheet leaves standing. Nothing here has been on paper in either setting. One
  thing is known rather than untested, and it holds wherever such a rule is written: a
  `[data-status] { border: 1pt solid #000 }` rule of the kind the docs chrome uses cannot
  outline an SVG `<path>` or `<line>`, because CSS `border` does not apply to SVG shapes, so
  that route will not give the tinted band an outline. This is an argument rather than a
  measurement. The words are what survive a printer dropping backgrounds: the reading, the band
  name, the scale, the band list and the derivation are text, and the word beside the number
  carries the level a second time.
- What a screen reader does when the score changes while the dial is on screen. The component
  mounts no live region, and that is deliberate, because it is not entitled to speak on the
  caller's behalf. What a caller should do instead is an open question on this page.

The `data-slot` values are how a rule of your own reaches one part of the dial, the arc, the band
name, the derivation or the whole thing, without an export existing for it. `data-status` is the
hook a product styles or scrapes a level by, and it sits only on the elements that carry the
status tint, never on a neutral band. The `<time>` element additionally carries a `dateTime`
attribute holding the ISO instant it was passed, which is the machine-readable copy of the
calculated date. There is no Base UI state attribute here, because the dial opens nothing and
toggles nothing: it is a static picture with a visible text twin.

## CSS variables

<CssVariablesTable name="score-dial" />

ScoreDial exposes no CSS custom property of its own for a consumer to set. Its colours are the
semantic token roles listed under [Tokens](#tokens) below, read straight from the token layer
rather than through a component-scoped variable, so there is nothing of its own to override here.
It does read two sizing tokens by name, `--opsin-measure-comfortable` to cap the column's width
and `--opsin-graphic-dial` to cap the arc's, each written with an inline fallback so a consumer
who installed the dial without the token layer still gets a bounded width; but it reads those, it
does not declare them, and neither is a hook the component owns. To recolour or resize any part,
write a rule against its `data-slot`. The table above has no rows because there is no
component-scoped variable to list.

## Tokens

<TokenTable component="score-dial" />

These are the token roles ScoreDial actually reads, resolved from its source by `tokensUsedBy`
and generated by `scripts/build-tokens.mts` rather than listed by hand, so they cannot drift from
what the component draws. They are semantic roles and never raw values: the category ink role
tints the label, the status ink and accent roles paint the indicator and the one band the reading
fell in, the chrome border role draws the track and every untinted band, and the neutral
foreground and muted roles carry the words and the marks. A health component never reads a
category token and a status token onto one element, which is the two colour axes kept apart in
the token layer itself: the category reaches the label, the status reaches the arc, and no single
element takes a colour from both.

## API reference

<PropsTable name="ScoreDialProps" />

`bands` is required and opsinjs supplies no default for it. That is the single most important
line in the interface. An empty array is a legitimate value and renders the explicit no-band
state; it is not an error to be repaired with a guess.

`derivation` is required too, and it is the only prop whose absence changes what the component
says about itself rather than what it draws. `min` and `max` are both required because an
unbounded dial is unreadable, and `value` is `number | null` because a score of zero and a
score that was never calculated are different facts about somebody's record. A value that is
not a finite number is a third state again, printed and spoken as *not available*.

`status` is the product's verdict on this reading and it is an input, never a derivation.
Its type is `"steady" | "watch"` rather than `ClinicalStatus`, which is a deliberate
narrowing: `attention` and `urgent` are both defined as carrying a named action, this surface
has nowhere to put one, and a bare *Urgent* under a composite score is a level with no route
to a person attached. Passing either renders no status and reports why. A JavaScript caller
passing something outside the four levels gets OPSIN-0021, and `unknown` gets OPSIN-0011,
because it is the absence of an assertion rather than a fifth level.

Three props are additions to the original specification, and they are named here rather than
slipped in. `precision` and `locale` exist because
[Numbers, units and precision](../health/numbers-units-precision.mdx) governs this page and
requires every number to render through [Value](./value.mdx), which takes both. `precision` is
required on this component, and the reason is the kind of number a dial holds. A measurement
arrives with a resolution its instrument fixed, so Value can round to it; a composite score
arrives from arithmetic with no resolution of its own, so there is no set of digits a dial can
honestly show without being told how many. With none stated Value rounds nothing, and one
double prints up to seventeen digits in a single unbreakable tabular token that is wider than a
phone column at 200% text. `locale` reaches
every number on the surface, not only the score: the bounds, the band edges and the coverage
counts go through the same formatter, because a reader does not change number systems between
two lines of one card. `className` is the merge point every component in the system carries,
and it is the one way a caller can still hide the mandatory words. The accessibility section
says how.

`calculatedAt` is named for what happened. Nothing on this dial was measured, so a `<time>`
element stamped `measured` beside it would tell a reader, and any consumer reading the DOM
contract, that an instrument produced the number at that instant. The prop stays optional, and
its absence is stated in words rather than passed over in silence: a dial with a score but no
instant still renders the calculated line, saying nobody knows when the score was computed, so
a reader is never left to read a stale score as a fresh one. That is the reason RangeBar shows
an absent `measuredAt` rather than hiding the whole line when nobody set it.

`ScoreBand` is exported alongside the props interface. The generator only emits a table for
interfaces named `<Pascal>Props`, so there is no generated one for it; this is the shipped
declaration, copied from `registry/bases/base/score-dial.tsx`:

```ts
export interface ScoreBand {
  /** Inclusive lower bound, on the score's own scale. */
  from: number
  /** Exclusive upper bound, on the score's own scale. */
  to: number
  /** The band in words, in the reader's language. Required. */
  name: string
  /** Whose band this is: a published instrument, a guideline body, your own clinical review. Required. */
  source: string
}
```

There is no `status` on a band, and its removal is the largest change this interface has had.
A band-to-level mapping is an interpretation rule with a numeric boundary in it, and letting a
comparison inside a rendering component select from it meant the component was deciding what a
reading meant.

Bands are half-open, so contiguous bands can be written without a gap and without an overlap.
The one exception is the top of the scale: a score exactly equal to `max` falls in the band
whose `to` is `max`, because otherwise the highest score a scale allows would belong to no band
at all. Overlapping bands resolve to the first match in declaration order. The component does
not reorder, merge or repair them, and it has no rule of its own about which should win. The
boundary marks on the arc are the one thing that does not depend on that order: they are
derived from the bounds by value, so an unordered or gapped set still marks the edges it has.

There is no `size`, no `color`, no `variant` and no prop that hides the band name or the
derivation. There is no `sweep`, `animate` or `duration` either: a dial that animates is the
failure this component's own doctrine page names first.
