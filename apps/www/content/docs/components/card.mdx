Card is the most-used component in any design system and the one that most often becomes a
dumping ground. Its specification is short on purpose: a Card is a boundary and some padding,
and every feature added to it is a feature that makes it harder to say what it means.

The one thing that changed on the way from specification to implementation is the default
rung, and it changed because the specification was written in a vocabulary the tokens no
longer use. It is set out under [Anatomy](#anatomy) rather than buried in a changelog.

## Preview

Two cards: the full anatomy with the page supplying its own heading level, and the same card
at the compact density beside it, so the density prop reads as a difference rather than as a
percentage. There is no number in it, no status pill and no category tint. A Card holding a
value, a coloured pill and a paragraph is a [ResultCard](./result-card.mdx) drawn without any
of a ResultCard's guarantees, and a preview that showed one would teach the mistake this
component's boundary exists to prevent.

The first three exclusions are the ones that decide whether a health product's clinical
contracts hold. A `Card` containing a number, a coloured pill and a paragraph looks exactly
like a `ResultCard` and carries none of its guarantees. That card has no staleness treatment,
no attribution and no axis discipline, and nothing in review will catch it. This is why Card
has no `status` prop, no `variant` and no `color`: the prop surface offers no way to express a
clinical meaning. It is not a wall. `className` is merged onto the root, and the static
accessibility rig reads `registry/**` and never a consumer's project, so a card tinted from
the status axis by a class name is a thing somebody can write and nothing in this system will
see. That gap is also the honest answer to why ResultCard exists rather than being a Card
with conventions.

### Choosing the rung

The default is `card`, and most cards should stay there. The decision table on
[Choosing a layer](../foundations/materials/choosing-a-layer.mdx) moves it in exactly two
directions:

| The card is | Rung | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| resting on the page, not tappable | `card` | nothing is lifted, so nothing should look lifted |
| a link, or being dragged | `raised` | it is an object, and an object may look lifted |
| part of the page rather than a block on it | `canvas` | there is no boundary worth drawing |

Never above `raised`. `sheet`, `overlay` and `scrim` composite what is behind them, and
`tokens/material.json` is explicit that a health value may not sit on one: a number a reader
is trying to read must not sit over a moving backdrop. A card on a translucent rung is also
the second blur in a stack of two, which the same file caps at three for the whole screen. The
component warns in development and renders anyway. A composition mistake is not a reason to
take somebody's content off the screen.

**Header** holds a title and optional supporting elements. **Body** is the content.
**Footer** holds actions or metadata. All three are optional; a Card with only a body is the
common case and should not require ceremony. `<Card>` around a paragraph is a complete,
correct card.

Card is one of exactly three components in the system that export real compound parts, and
the test it passes is narrow: the consumer supplies the content of three ordered slots, and
no prop could carry them. `Card.Header`, `Card.Body` and `Card.Footer` are exported functions
assigned onto `Card` after their declarations, so `Card.Header` and `CardHeader` are the same
function. Everything else in the tree is a `data-slot`, which is enough to style and not
enough to take apart. `Card.Title` and `Card.Description` arrive as props on the header for
the same reason in reverse: a prop can carry them, so a compound export would be ceremony.

`Card.Title` renders whichever heading level the surrounding page needs, because the page
passes the element: `title={<h3>Recent readings</h3>}`. A card that hard-codes its own level
produces a document outline that jumps from h1 to h3, or a screen of eleven h2s, depending on
which mistake the library made. A card that takes the level as a number is the same mistake
with an extra prop. The slot resets the passed element's font and margins to its own, so the
same title looks identical under an h2 and under an h4: the level is in the outline, never in
the type size. Pass a plain string instead and the title is styled text with no place in the
outline, which is a legitimate choice for a card nobody needs to navigate to.

### The default rung is `card`, and the specification said `raised`

The proposed API on this page defaulted `rung` to `"raised"`, and that default has been
corrected rather than implemented. It was written in the rung vocabulary this system retired
in [ADR 0014](../project/decisions/0014-material-rung-names.mdx), where `raised` was the name
for the rung a resting block of content sits on. On the ladder the tokens publish, `raised` is
one rung higher: `tokens/material.json` gives it a drop shadow and describes it as "menus,
popovers, tooltips, a dragged card", while the rung named `card` is "the default home for a
health value", opaque and *deliberately shadowless*, because "a grid of shadowed cards on a
phone reads as clutter, and the shadow is doing work a 1px boundary does better and cheaper".

Taking the old word literally would have put a shadow under every resting card in the system.
Translate the rung names by job, never by position, and read
[the material ladder](../foundations/materials/the-ladder.mdx) before mapping an older design
file: three of the retired names are also current names and two of them moved.

"Products may move a card down the ladder, never off it" survives the correction and still
means what it says.

## Examples

### The whole card as one link

The only interactive shape a Card has. Tab through it: each card is one stop, not one per
line. Hover it: the title underlines, because a whole-card link with no affordance on its
text leaves a reader guessing which part of it is the link. Note what is not in it. A card
that is a link holds no buttons, because a reader cannot tell what tapping the gap between
two of them will do, and a keyboard user reaches a control nested inside a control.

### Cards on a sheet, and the rung that stays put

A translucent rung may never contain another translucent rung. The sheet is the layer; the
cards on it stay opaque on the `card` rung, bounded by a line. Two stacked blurs cost twice
as much, composite against each other rather than against the page, and produce a surface
whose contrast floor nobody has measured. The floor is published per rung, not per stack.

## Content guidelines

A card holds one idea. If the title needs *and* in it, it is two cards. Titles are noun
phrases in sentence case, not sentences and not questions.

Do not use a card as a decorative frame for a single line of text: a boundary with nothing
to bound reads as a control the reader is meant to do something with.

Card ships no copy of its own. It has no default title, no empty-state text and no label
anywhere. Every word inside one is the product's, which means every word inside one is the
product's to check against [Plain-English A to Z](../content/plain-english-a-z.mdx). The
component cannot help here, and a component that supplied a friendly default would be putting
words a clinician never saw onto a health surface.

## Accessibility

Some of this is checked by a gate on every commit and the rest is an argument. They are
separated below because `alpha` promises that accessibility is claimed only where it has been
checked, with the gaps listed rather than left out.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y` on every commit:**

- **Every colour is a token, and the chrome-only names are rejected by name.** The static rig
  fails the build on a hex literal, an `oklch()`, a Tailwind palette colour or a numbered ramp
  step anywhere in `registry/**`, and separately on a fixed list of the roles, radii and
  variables that exist in `app/globals.css` and not in `app/product.css`. That list is a
  denylist of the names already known to be chrome-only, which is not the same thing as
  proving that every token a component reaches for resolves under the product theme: a name
  outside the list that resolved in one stylesheet and not the other would pass silently. The
  muted foreground on the description, the border it grows for print, and the focus ring on
  a link card are the three colours Card paints of its own. Everything else is Surface's
  material.
- **No `px` font size, and no banned word** in copy or in an identifier. Card ships no default
  product copy, and that means no title, no empty-state text and no label. It is not wordless
  even so, and the rule reads what is there: the development warning about a translucent rung,
  and the demo strings in the file `shadcn add` hands to a consumer. So the clean result is a
  result about text that exists rather than a pass over an empty file.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **A card is not a landmark or a region.** It has no role, no name and no place in the
  accessibility tree unless it is a link, and giving every card a region role would fill the
  rotor with entries that mean nothing. A card becomes a region when a product decides it has
  a heading worth navigating to, which is a decision about that screen and not about this
  component.
- **The title's heading level is set by the page**, so the document outline stays continuous.
  The slot resets the element's own font and margins, so choosing `h4` over `h3` changes the
  outline and nothing visual. That is what makes it safe to choose correctly.
- **A card that is a link is one control.** One tab stop, a floor of
  `--opsin-target-minimum` on both axes carried by the component rather than by the product
  stylesheet's attribute backstop, and a focus ring declared in the component for the same
  reason: a card whose ring depends on a file it was not installed with is a card that loses
  it silently. Both axes because the backstop sets both. A card is usually far wider than
  its floor, and a link card dropped into a narrow column is not. The floor is a rem, so it
  grows when a reader raises their text size instead of pinning at 44 device pixels.
- **The boundary is not conveyed by colour alone.** The rung draws a hairline edge as well as
  a fill, and the edge is a lightness difference that survives greyscale. Under
  `prefers-contrast: more` Surface thickens it from the hairline width to the emphasis width.
- **Padding does not collapse at 200% text.** Every step is a rem, so the padding scales with
  the root font size rather than holding its pixel value while the type grows. The most
  common Dynamic Type failure in any system is a card that keeps its height and clips its
  content.
- **Print renders the boundary as a rule.** The rung paints its fill with a background and its
  edge with an inset box-shadow, and a browser drops both when it prints; a printout is how a
  reading most often reaches a clinician, so the card grows a real border at print time and
  only at print time.
- **Nested cards are announced no differently from unnested ones.** That is a reason to avoid
  nesting rather than a reason to add roles, and this component adds none.

**Not yet checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- The 44px floor on a link card, and the separation between two targets in a footer. Both are
  declared in the component and neither has a published measurement on this page. The layout
  half of the rig is not blind to this component. It enumerates every built registry entry,
  so `card`, `card-as-a-link` and `card-on-a-sheet` are all in its nightly target list, and it
  measures the hit area against the floor and checks for overflow at 1×, 1.3× and 200%. What
  is missing is a recorded result, so treat the floor as declared rather than demonstrated.
- Contrast for the description's muted foreground against the card's fill, and for the focus
  ring against the `card`, `canvas` and `raised` fills a link card may sit on. Neither of
  those has been measured in either theme or under increased contrast. `<ContrastReport>`
  below takes its rows as a prop and nothing wires the measured file into it yet; run
  `pnpm run contrast` and read the results rather than trusting a number retyped into a page.
- Whether a link card is recognisable as a link before anybody points at it. The title
  underlines on hover and on focus and both are transient; at rest a link card is drawn
  exactly like a card that is not one. The `raised` rung is not the answer, because any card
  may take it and a printout drops the shadow. A product whose list mixes link cards and
  static ones therefore supplies its own resting cue until this component has one.
- The exclusion between `href` and interactive children. It is a rule, not a gate. See the
  [Status](#status) section for why a runtime check would be worse than none.
- Behaviour when a reader's own stylesheet strips backgrounds and shadows. The print border
  answers the printed case; the reader-stylesheet case has not been looked at.
- `forced-colors: active`. Nothing in opsinjs answers it yet.

## API reference

### Card

<PropsTable name="CardProps" />

`rung` defaults to `"card"`, for the reasons under [Anatomy](#anatomy). Its six members are documented at [MaterialRung](../reference/api/MaterialRung.mdx), and they are the token names: a rung is only a rung because a `--opsin-material-<rung>-*` property resolves for it. `density` chooses between two
multipliers on Tailwind's density-scaled spacing scale, and not between two steps of the fixed
`--opsin-space-*` scale. The two multipliers are five times `--spacing` and four. The 5 and
the 4 are borrowed from what `tokens/space.json` publishes for a card's inner padding and for
the step below it, but the values that render are not those steps and are not on the 4px grid:
`--spacing` is the product theme's own, and the document's density attribute moves it again.
That second movement is the point of using the scaled scale here. A reader who asks their
whole interface for a denser layout gets one inside a card too, and the two controls multiply.

There is no `status`, no `variant` and no `color`. A Card that could be tinted by clinical
status would be a `ResultCard` with none of its obligations, which is the exact failure the
component boundary exists to prevent. There is no `category` either, and that refusal is worth
stating because [category identity](../health/category-identity.mdx) names `card` among the
components it governs: a category tint identifies a family of measurements, and a plain Card
holds no measurement to identify. A surface that needs the category axis is the one under a
ResultCard or a MetricTile, and those two components own that decision rather than inheriting
it from here. There is no `as` and no polymorphism either: the root is
a `div`, or an `a` when `href` is set, and a card that needs to be something else contains
that thing rather than becoming it.

`href` is a rule the type cannot state. A card that is a link may hold no other interactive
element, and `children` is a `ReactNode`. Interactivity is not in its type, and a runtime
walk would catch a raw `<button>` and miss every wrapped one. It is documented, it is in the
examples by omission, and it is an open question on this page.

### Card.Footer

<PropsTable name="CardFooterProps" />

The footer's gap is the one measurement in the card that does not move with density.
`--opsin-target-separation` is the published minimum between two adjacent controls, and a
footer with two buttons in it is the case it was published for: padding may tighten when
somebody asks for a denser interface, and the distance between two things a thumb has to hit
may not. The footer reads that token directly rather than a spacing step that happens to hold
the same value today, so a retune of the published minimum moves every card footer with it and
no gate has to notice.
