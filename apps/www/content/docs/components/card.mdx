Card is the most-used component in any design system and the one that most often becomes a
dumping ground. Its specification is short on purpose: a Card is a boundary and some padding,
and every feature added to it is a feature that makes it harder to say what it means.

The first three exclusions are the ones that decide whether a health product's clinical
contracts hold. A `Card` containing a number, a coloured pill and a paragraph looks exactly
like a `ResultCard` and carries none of its guarantees — no staleness treatment, no
attribution, no axis discipline — and nothing in review will catch it.

## Anatomy

<Anatomy name="card" />

<CompositionTree name="card" />

**Header** holds a title and optional supporting elements. **Body** is the content.
**Footer** holds actions or metadata. All three are optional; a Card with only a body is the
common case and should not require ceremony.

Proposed part hierarchy. This is the specification the implementation must match.

```text
Card                           root; a Surface at the raised rung by default
├── Card.Header
│   ├── Card.Title             heading level chosen by the page, not by the card
│   └── Card.Description       optional supporting line
├── Card.Body                  the content
└── Card.Footer                actions or metadata
```

`Card.Title` renders whichever heading level the surrounding page needs. A card that hard-codes
its own level produces a document outline that jumps from h1 to h3, or a screen of eleven h2s,
depending on which mistake the library made.

## Proposed API

<NotBuiltYet name="card" />

```ts
// PROPOSED. Not implemented. A specification for review, not an API to generate
// code against. It will change without a deprecation cycle.

import type { MaterialRung } from "opsinjs"

export interface CardProps {
  /**
   * Which rung of the material ladder. Defaults to "raised". Products may move
   * a card down the ladder, never off it.
   */
  rung?: MaterialRung
  /** Padding scale. Affects space only, never type size. */
  density?: "comfortable" | "compact"
  /**
   * Makes the whole card a single link. Mutually exclusive with interactive
   * children: a card that is a link may not contain buttons, and the type
   * should say so rather than the documentation.
   */
  href?: string
  children: React.ReactNode
}
```

There is no `status`, no `variant` and no `color`. A Card that could be tinted by clinical
status would be a `ResultCard` with none of its obligations, which is the exact failure the
component boundary exists to prevent.

## Content guidelines

A card holds one idea. If the title needs *and* in it, it is two cards. Titles are noun
phrases in sentence case, not sentences and not questions.

Do not use a card as a decorative frame for a single line of text: a boundary with nothing
to bound reads as a control the reader is meant to do something with.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"Recent readings"** as a card title, with the readings inside it.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **"Recent readings and upcoming appointments"** in one card. Two subjects sharing a
    boundary means the boundary tells the reader nothing.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **A card whose whole surface is a link, containing text and no other controls.**
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **A clickable card with two buttons inside it.** The reader cannot tell what tapping the
    gap between them will do, and neither can a keyboard user.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

## Accessibility requirements

Requirements for the implementation, not results.

- **A card is not a landmark or a region by default.** Giving every card a region role fills
  the rotor with meaningless entries; a card becomes a region only when it has a heading worth
  navigating to.
- **The title's heading level is set by the page**, so the document outline is continuous.
- **A card that is a link is one control**, meeting the 44pt floor, with an accessible name
  from its title, and containing no other interactive elements.
- **The boundary is not conveyed by colour alone**: a border or an elevation change survives
  grayscale and increased contrast, and the card is still legible when a reader's own
  stylesheet removes backgrounds.
- **Padding does not collapse at 200% text.** A card that keeps its height and clips its
  content at large text sizes is the most common Dynamic Type failure in any system.
- **Nested cards are announced no differently from unnested ones**, which is a reason to avoid
  nesting rather than a reason to add roles.
- **Print** renders the card boundary as a rule or an outline, not as a background fill that a
  printer will drop.
