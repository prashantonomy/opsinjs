Empty states are where a product either explains itself or looks broken. In a health
product there is a third possibility, and it is the one to design against: implying that
empty means fine. Everything in this component follows from that. Most of all, it writes no
sentence about the reader's data, and says so out loud when nobody supplied one.

## Preview

Three of the five reasons: the two empties that are hardest to keep apart, and the error
state that must never be mistaken for either. They stack on a narrow surface and sit side by
side once there is room. In the first the reader can fix the emptiness themselves. In the
second the product has declined to draw something and owes an explanation of what it is
waiting for. The third is a load that did not complete, which says the readings are safe
rather than gone and offers a way to try again. Nothing on any card is cheerful about the
absence, and none invents a number.

The first exclusion is the one that produces the worst experience when it goes wrong. An
empty state shown during loading tells the reader there is nothing there, and a proportion of
them will leave before the data arrives and conclude the app lost their readings. The
[Skeleton](./skeleton.mdx) page states the same rule from the other side: never put a
skeleton where a value may not turn up, because the honest resolution of that load is this
component.

The five reasons are not decoration on a single empty. `nothing-yet` and `no-matches` look
identical on screen and are opposites underneath. In one the data does not exist. In the
other it does, and a control is hiding it. `not-enough` is the one that carries a safety
obligation, because it is what a view renders when it has decided that drawing something
would be dishonest. That obligation is scoped. `not-enough` carries it for a surface with no
chart at all, and a series too short to plot inside a chart card is
[TrendSparkline](./trend-sparkline.mdx)'s own refusal, stated in the card rather than by a
whole-surface empty state. `could-not-load` is the one that is not about the reader's data at all: a
fetch tried and failed, so the readings are safe rather than gone, and it is the only reason
that draws a marker of its own and requires a way to try again.

The parts are `data-slot` names, not exports. There is no `EmptyState.Title` to import and no
way to render one without the others. That is what keeps the title, the reason and the body
from drifting apart into three components that each tell a different half of the story.

`EmptyState.Action` is a wrapper carrying the slot, with the control inside it. That is not
tidiness: [Button](./button.mdx) stamps its own `data-slot="button"`, and one element cannot
carry two slot names. Style the wrapper, and the control keeps the treatment its own
component owns.

`reason` renders nothing for the four empties and a neutral word with an icon for
`could-not-load`. It is required because the four empties need four different sentences, one
of them a safety statement, and because the fifth reason is an error that must not be taken
for an absence. For the four empties it stays invisible, since a component that turned a
reason into a sentence would be writing that sentence about somebody else's data; for
`could-not-load` it draws its own marker in neither axis of colour, so a load that did not
complete never reads as an empty list.

## Examples

### A filter matched nothing

The reason a reader can be actively misled by. A list that goes blank after a filter looks
exactly like a list that has nothing in it, so the body names the filter as the cause and the
action removes it. This is also the example that crosses the client boundary: `onSelect` is a
function, and functions do not travel from a server component.

It is longer than an empty state needs to be because it carries the two things this component
will not do on the caller's behalf, and this is the one case that needs both. The first is the
announcement [Empty and first use](../patterns/empty-and-first-use.mdx) requires on a filter
change: a polite live region, owned by the list rather than by the empty state, mounted from
the start so nothing is missed and written once per activation rather than once per render.
The second is focus. Clearing the filter unmounts the empty state, and the empty state
contains the control the reader pressed. Without a deliberate move, focus therefore falls to
`<body>` and a screen-reader user's cursor is thrown to the top of the document. The restored
list takes it instead.

### What happens when nobody wrote the body

The first card is the component refusing to help. There is no default sentence for an empty
health surface, so the omission is stated rather than filled. The second is the same component
with two sentences. That is the entire fix, and it is why the first also logs a development
warning naming the reason it was given. They stack on a narrow surface and sit side by side
once there is room for both.

<ComponentPreview name="empty-state-no-copy-supplied" kind="example" align="start" />

### An illustration, and what it is not allowed to do

Cover the picture and the empty state still says what is absent, why, and what to do about
it. That is the test. The component keeps it out four ways. The picture is out of the
accessibility tree, out of the tab order with `inert`, off paper, and out of the layout once
the content area it is given falls below 17rem.

**The illustration is `aria-hidden` and `inert` in the rendered DOM**, which you can check
in the frame below rather than take on trust. The drop threshold is a different kind of claim.
The picture carries `@max-[17rem]:hidden`, a container query that reads the padded content box
rather than the surface, so the width it measures is what is left after the root's `px-4` is
taken off. A 390px phone leaves 308px inside that box and a 360px phone 278px, both above the
17rem threshold, so the picture stays; a 300px embedded card leaves 266px and loses it. A
browser resize pass at those three widths confirms the picture is present on the two phones and
gone in the narrow card. The earlier 20rem threshold was measured against the same content box,
where a 390px phone already falls to 308px, so its true crossover was a 402px viewport and it
dropped the picture on every phone the system is built for. Whether it leaves in print is a
further gap, because that is a media query and no capture has produced a printed page. The
accessibility section says which of these a standing gate covers.

## States

EmptyState has no interaction states of its own. The root is a plain container:
no hover, focus, pressed or disabled treatment on the title, the body or the
illustration, and none of the three is ever a control. `EmptyState.Action` and
`EmptyState.Secondary` are the exception, because each wraps a
[Link](./link.mdx) or a [Button](./button.mdx): the hover, focus, pressed and
disabled states a reader meets there belong to those components and are
documented on their own pages rather than repeated here.

The five data states the system defines (see [Data
states](../foundations/data-states.mdx)) do not divide evenly across `reason`.
Three of the five reason values carry Empty, one carries Partial, and one
carries Error; the remaining two data states are not this component's to render
at all.

| Data state | What EmptyState renders |
| --- | --- |
| Error | `reason="could-not-load"`: the neutral "Could not load" marker before the title, a title and body that say the request did not complete rather than that a reading is missing, a mandatory action, and, when no usable action was given, a visible line admitting no way back was offered rather than a silent control. |
| Loading | Nothing. EmptyState is never the right surface while a request is still in flight; the [Skeleton](./skeleton.mdx) preserves the layout instead, and mounting an empty state during a load is the confusion the exclusion list in "When to use it" names first. |
| Empty | `reason="nothing-yet"`, `"no-matches"` or `"nothing-left"`: a title, a body naming what is absent and why, and at most one action. None of the three draws a marker of its own; the difference between "never existed", "hidden by a filter" and "used up and gone" lives entirely in the words the caller writes. |
| Partial | `reason="not-enough"`: the same title-and-body shape, except the body is a safety statement rather than an explanation, stating the rule a view is waiting for and how far off it is, because there is data, only not enough of it for this view to be honest. |
| Stale | Nothing. A stale value is real and present, so the surface it belongs to has content to show and is never empty; staleness is `staleAfterHours` on [MetricTile](./metric-tile.mdx), not a reason this component accepts. |

## Content guidelines

Say what is not here, then why, then what to do. Never use an empty state to be cheerful about
an absence: *No results. Great news!* is a clinical claim the product cannot make, because a
reader with no results has an empty list, not a clean bill of health. The same goes for *all
clear*, *nothing to report* and *you are all caught up*: each one turns a gap in the data into
a statement about the person.

For `not-enough`, state the rule and the gap. The reader is not being told off; they are being
told what the view is waiting for. This is the one reason whose body is not an explanation but
a safety statement, and it is the one most often left empty. The sentence carries three facts
and no more: how many readings there are, how many a trend needs, and the window they are
counted over. [TrendSparkline](./trend-sparkline.mdx) states the too-short case in that same
shape, so a reader who meets the refusal in a chart card and again on a bare surface meets one
sentence built the same way.

For `could-not-load`, say the system failed to load, never that the reading is missing. A
request that did not complete is a fact about the network or the service, not about the
person, so the words point at the load and offer a way to try again. Do not let the copy imply
the reading itself is gone or that an absence is a result: *No readings* on a failed fetch
tells the reader a falsehood about their own record.

This component ships no copy about the reader, and that is a deliberate constraint rather than
an unfinished job. There is no default body, no default disclaimer, no default emergency
number, no threshold and no default reassurance, because opsinjs does not know which product
it is in, what that product is regulated as, or where its reader is. The four reader-facing
strings in the file are admissions or statements about the system, never a sentence about the
reader: an EmptyState given no body renders a plain line saying there is nothing here and the
app does not say why, one given a blank title says the screen has no heading, the error state
carries the words "Could not load", and an error left with no way back says so in a line of
its own. All of them are written for the reader rather than the developer. An author who sees
one fixes it, and a reader who sees one has at least not been told something untrue.

The guard behind the body line is wider than a missing prop, because that is not how a body
actually goes missing. `{rows.length > 0 && "…"}` passes `false` when the list is empty, and
`{items.length && "…"}` passes the number `0`; without the guard the first renders an empty
paragraph and the second renders a bare digit on a screen that is meant to explain an absence.
Both are treated as no body, both warn in development, and the digit is refused rather than
printed. Zero, none and unknown are three different things, and an absence is never a number.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"No readings yet. Once you add your first blood pressure reading, you will see it here,
    with the range your product compares it against. [Add a reading]"** Names the absence, says
    what will change it, offers one action. It also attributes the range rather than calling
    it the reader's.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **"Nothing to see here! 🎉"** Cheerful about missing health data, no explanation, and
    nothing to do. It reads as reassurance and it is not reassurance about anything.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

A range is a comparison somebody chose, not a fact about the reader, so *your range* is the
one phrasing to avoid even in a sentence about a reading that does not exist yet. [Reference
ranges](../health/reference-ranges.mdx) requires every band to name its owner, whether a
laboratory, a device maker or a clinician. An empty state is where the habit is easiest to
lose, because there is no number on screen to make the omission obvious.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"Not enough readings for a trend yet. We draw one once there are enough for the line to
    mean something, and there are not yet. [Add a reading]"** The rule, the gap, and no
    implication that the reader has done anything wrong.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **An empty chart area with the axes drawn.** It looks like something broke, and readers
    who interpret it as *your values are zero* are not being unreasonable.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

Fill in the actual window from the metric's own `minimumWindow`, which the consuming product
declares and [TrendSparkline](./trend-sparkline.mdx) reads. There is no number in the exemplar
above and none in this component, because opsinjs ships no thresholds for any metric in any
population. See [ADR 0012](../project/decisions/0012-synthetic-example-data.mdx). A day count
or a reading count written here would be a clinical rule with no owner and no date, and a
threshold in a Do block is a threshold somebody copies.

## Motion

EmptyState uses no motion. It does not animate when it mounts, and none of its
parts, not the illustration, the title, the body or either action, transitions
when `reason` or the content changes; a caller who swaps `reason` on a mounted
EmptyState replaces the rendered content in place with no transition of any
kind. That is deliberate: an empty state's job is to state an absence plainly,
and animating an admission that there is nothing here would spend motion on the
one moment it is least suited to draw attention to itself. No part of the file
declares a transition token.

Because it declares no transition, there is nothing for `prefers-reduced-motion`
to reduce. The illustration's appearance and disappearance across the
`@max-[17rem]` container-query boundary is a layout change rather than a motion
effect: the picture is present, or it is gone, with no cross-fade, resize or
other animation carrying it between the two.

## Accessibility

Some of these are checked by a gate on every commit. The rest are arguments, and they are
labelled as such: `beta` promises that accessibility is claimed only where it has been
checked, with the gaps listed rather than left out.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y` on every commit:**

- **No banned word reaches copy or an identifier.** The static rig reads every string literal
  and every JSX text node in this file against the generated list from `tokens/glossary.json`
  plus the six from [Reference ranges](../health/reference-ranges.mdx), and then reads the
  identifiers as well. The four reader-facing strings this component ships are the missing
  body, the missing title, the "Could not load" marker and the line an error shows when it
  carries no way back. They pass the same gate a product's own copy would.
- **No type size in `px`, no raw colour, no numbered ramp step.** Every size is one of the
  eleven semantic steps and every colour is a bridged role, so a reader's own text size
  reaches the whole component and both themes are somebody's measured decision.
- **Nothing that resolves to nothing under the product theme.** `bg-secondary`,
  `bg-destructive` and the rest of the docs-chrome palette are refused by the same gate, which
  is what stops a component looking correct in review and arriving colourless in the product.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **Neither colour axis is used at all.** An empty state is not a clinical level and not a
  category, so the never-mix rule is satisfied by having nothing to mix. This bullet sits here
  rather than above because the gate cannot substantiate it. `checkAxisConflict` fails an
  element carrying *both* axes; it is equally quiet about a component that uses one and about
  a component that uses none. The rendered tree carries only `data-slot`, which is true by
  inspection and not by measurement.
- **The title is a real heading.** `titleLevel` renders `h2` through `h6` and defaults to `2`.
  There is no way for this component to know the outline of the screen it lands on, so the
  default is a starting point and not an answer. Check it, the way you would check any
  heading. A blank `title` renders a visible line saying none was supplied, and warns, rather
  than emitting an empty heading nothing but a screen reader would notice.
- **The `href` action is a real control with its own target floor.** It is an anchor, it is
  announced as a link, and it carries `min-block-size` *and* `min-inline-size` of
  `var(--opsin-target-minimum, 2.75rem)`. That value is a rem, so it grows with the reader's
  text size rather than pinning at 44 device pixels, and it has a fallback so the floor does
  not silently vanish in a project that installed this file without `tokens.generated.css`. An
  action with neither a destination nor a handler renders nothing at all and warns.
- **Nothing depends on colour**, controls included. The state is carried by the words: no
  tint, no icon standing in for a sentence, no legend. The recommended action and the quieter
  alternative differ by the size of the box, a boundary and the step of the type rather than
  by hue, so both survive greyscale, and no card action is brand-filled on either transport.
  The quieter alternative is underlined in both of its forms, so it is identifiable as a
  control without relying on a hover fill that a touch user never sees and a low-vision reader
  may not resolve.
- **The layout stacks by construction**, because it is a single column at every width. The
  body is capped at the tight measure so a centred paragraph does not lose the reader's return
  sweep.
- **Print renders the title and the body.** An empty section in a printed summary still says
  why it is empty, which is what stops a reader concluding the printer dropped a page.
- **It mounts no live region, and the caller has to.** A view that becomes empty should be
  announced once, politely, and that announcement belongs to the region that changed rather
  than to the component inside it. A `role="status"` here would speak on every keystroke of a
  search field and speak twice wherever the caller had already done the right thing. What that
  leaves the caller holding is real work, and opsinjs ships no helper for it: [Screen
  readers](../accessibility/screen-readers.mdx) lists the absent announcement helper as a
  known gap. The filter example above is the caller's half written out by hand. That half is a
  permanently mounted polite region owned by the list, written once per activation, plus a
  deliberate focus move to the restored list, because clearing the filter unmounts the button
  the reader pressed.

**Not yet checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- Contrast for the title, the body and both action treatments against the card surface they
  land on, in both themes. `<ContrastReport component="empty-state">` below lists the measured
  neutral token pairs this component draws its colour from, the body text, the secondary text
  and the hairline boundary against the page in both themes. Those are the token pairs the
  component uses, not a measurement of the component itself, so the specific pairings against
  the card surface are argued from the tokens and are not among the rows below.
- **Both transports of `action` render the same pair.** The `href` form renders
  [Link](./link.mdx) `emphasis="action"` and the `onSelect` form renders
  [Button](./button.mdx) `variant="secondary"`, and both take the same neutral pair, `bg-card`
  with the body foreground, because both resolve to the one card action recipe in
  `button.tsx`. So the two transports cannot drift, the recommended step reads at one weight
  whether the caller passed `href` or `onSelect`, and neither form is the one to prefer for a
  contrast reason. That pair is argued from the tokens rather than measured here: the neutral
  rows below are text against the page, and the action's pairing against the card surface is
  not one of them.
- The 44×44 hit area, survival at 1.3× and usability at 200% are the layout half of the
  accessibility rig. It runs only in the nightly workflow, where a browser is installed at job
  time, and it has not been run against this component.
- `prefers-contrast` and `forced-colors` are not implemented anywhere in opsinjs yet, so no
  increased-contrast claim can currently be made by anyone, here or elsewhere.
- **The print drop needs a printed page, and no capture has produced one.** `aria-hidden`
  and `inert` are in the rendered DOM and can be read off the preview above, and the container
  drop has been checked by a one-off browser resize pass but by no standing gate, as the bullet
  below records. The print rule cannot be read off anything: the
  illustration carries `print:hidden` so a printed summary shows the title and the body and
  nothing else, but that is a media query, and the nightly layout half does not print a page,
  so it is not on a path to being measured by the rig as it stands.
- **Whether the illustration leaves inside a narrow container.** The picture carries
  `@max-[17rem]:hidden`, a *container* query rather than a media query, and a container query of
  type `inline-size` reads the padded content box, so the width it measures is what is left
  after the root's `px-4`. A one-off browser resize pass reads 308px inside that box at a 390px
  phone and 278px at a 360px phone, both above 17rem so the picture stays, and 266px inside a
  300px embedded card, below it so the picture goes. That is a hand check, not a standing gate:
  the rig runs no container-resize check, so a regression here would pass every commit, which is
  why this still sits under what a gate does not cover. The earlier 20rem threshold read against
  the same content box, where a 390px phone already falls to 308px, so its true crossover was a
  402px viewport and it dropped the picture on every phone.
- Whether 17rem is the right threshold for dropping the illustration. It is a judgement about a
  picture nobody has studied, not a measurement of what serves the reader; it is set to clear
  the padded box on a phone and drop inside a narrow card, and no theme step falls in the band
  between the two, so it is a bespoke value. The threshold is written in `rem` so that it can
  follow a reader who has raised their root font size, which is behaviour this component expects
  of a container query and has not measured in any engine.

EmptyState carries none of the other three attributes in the vocabulary. There is
no `data-status`, because an absence is not a clinical level; no `data-category`,
because it is not a kind of measurement; and no `data-opsinjs-value`, because it
never renders a reader's own number and refuses one outright on the rare body
that resolves to a bare digit. The eight `data-slot` values above, most of them
conditional on which parts actually render, are the only hook this component
offers a rule of your own.

## CSS variables

<CssVariablesTable name="empty-state" />

EmptyState exposes no custom property of its own for a consumer to set. Its
colours are the semantic neutral roles listed under Tokens below, read straight
from the token layer rather than through a component-scoped variable, so there
is nothing of its own to override here. The one custom property the file reads

the type scale rather than to this component. To recolour any part, write a rule

## API reference

<PropsTable name="EmptyStateProps" />

`reason` and `title` are the two required props. `titleLevel` is an addition to the
specification rather than a substitute for anything on it: the accessibility requirement asks
for "a real heading at the level the surrounding page needs", and `title: string` on its own
has no way to say which level that is.

The five reason values are written into `EmptyStateProps` as a union rather than exported as
a named `EmptyReason` type. A registry file has three public exports. They are the props
interface, the component and the zero-prop demo, so a caller who needs the union names it as
`EmptyStateProps["reason"]`. The specification writes it as an export; that difference is
recorded here rather than resolved in favour of a wider surface.

Four things are checked at runtime as well as typed, because this file ships as source into
JavaScript projects where a type is advice: a blank `title`, a body that renders no text, a
body that is only a number, and an action with no label or with nothing to do. Each renders
the honest fallback or nothing at all, and each warns in development once per distinct
offender rather than on every render. A warning channel that repeats is one somebody switches
off. None of them is an `OPSIN-` code: those describe mistakes a consumer makes with the
clinical API, they are a versioned contract generated from `tokens/errors.json`, and a
component may not mint one.
