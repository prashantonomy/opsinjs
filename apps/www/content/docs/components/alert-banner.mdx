AlertBanner is the loudest thing opsinjs will ship, and the specification is mostly a set of
limits. A banner that appears when it should not is not a small mistake: it spends the
reader's attention, and attention spent on a false alarm is not available for the real one.

The first exclusion carries most of the weight. *Would anything be lost if the reader did
not see this?* If the answer is no, it is a [Callout](./callout.mdx). Banners are for the
messages where the answer is yes, and a product that cannot tell the difference will end up
with readers who scroll past both.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** That the product has determined this screen needs the reader's
attention at one of the four levels, and — for `attention` and `urgent` — that there is
something specific to do about it.

**What it must never be read as.** A diagnosis, a clinical instruction from a person, or a
substitute for emergency services. A banner may tell somebody to call for help; it may never
imply that the app has assessed them. The distinction lives in the sentence: *your reading
is above the level your clinic asked us to tell you about* is a threshold being reported,
and *you are having a hypertensive crisis* is a diagnosis the app cannot make.

**Which colour axis it may use.** The **status** axis, and only that. A banner carries no
category colour, ever, even when it is about a single category's data: a sleep-tinted urgent
banner makes the urgency look like a brand colour. The level's word appears in the heading,
not only in the colour and the icon.

**The escalation budget.** At most **one** `urgent` banner per screen and at most **two**
banners in total. If the product's rules generate more, the product must merge them into one
banner that names the most serious first. This is the central rule of
[Alarm fatigue](../health/alarm-fatigue.mdx) and it is the reason this component exists as a
governed surface rather than as a `div` with a red border.

**Urgency is never carried by motion.** No pulsing, no flashing, no shaking, no colour cycling
— at any level, under any circumstances. Movement is not more perceptible to everyone, it is
actively harmful to some readers, and it does not survive `prefers-reduced-motion`. See
[Motion in health UI](../health/motion-in-health-ui.mdx).

<SafetyCallout severity="high" evidence="opinion">
An `urgent` banner must never be the only place a serious message appears, and must never be
dismissible without an explicit acknowledgement the product records. A reader who swipes a
banner away on a bus has not been informed, and the product cannot tell the difference
between dismissal and understanding.
</SafetyCallout>

## Anatomy

<Anatomy name="alert-banner" />

<CompositionTree name="alert-banner" />

**Icon** and **Heading** carry the level; the heading contains the level's word so it
survives grayscale and a screen reader. **Body** is one or two sentences of plain English —
what happened, and what it means for the reader. **Actions** are at most two, the first being
the one the product recommends. **Dismiss** is present only when the level permits it.
**Timestamp** says when the condition was detected, which is often the difference between a
banner that makes sense and one that does not.

Proposed part hierarchy. This is the specification the implementation must match.

```text
AlertBanner                    root; role depends on level, not on styling
├── AlertBanner.Icon           one distinct glyph per level; decorative
├── AlertBanner.Heading        contains the level's word, e.g. "Needs attention"
├── AlertBanner.Body           what happened and what it means; 2 sentences maximum
├── AlertBanner.Timestamp      when the condition was detected
├── AlertBanner.Actions        at most 2; the first is the recommended one
└── AlertBanner.Dismiss        absent at `urgent` unless acknowledgement is recorded
```

A banner with no `AlertBanner.Actions` at `attention` or `urgent` is a composition error:
telling somebody that something needs action without offering one is the most common way a
health product creates anxiety it cannot resolve.

## Content guidelines

The heading names the level and the subject in under eight words. The body says what
happened and what it means for this reader, in that order, in the second person. Actions are
imperative and concrete.

No exclamation marks, at any level. No capitals for emphasis. No *immediately*, *critical*,
*danger*, *emergency* unless the product genuinely means the reader should stop and call for
help, in which case say exactly that and give them the number.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"Needs attention — your blood pressure reading is high. Your last reading was 168/104,
    which is above the level your clinic asked us to tell you about. Contact your clinic
    today."** Level, fact, threshold with an owner, and one clear action.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **"⚠️ WARNING: Dangerously high blood pressure!"** Shouting, a diagnosis the app cannot
    make, no number, no source and nothing to do. It produces fear and no decision.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"Watch — your device has not synced since Tuesday. Readings after that may be
    missing."** States the consequence, so the reader knows why it matters.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **"Sync error (code 4)."** An internal fact with no consequence attached. The reader
    cannot tell whether to ignore it or to worry about it, so they will do one of the two at
    random.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

## Accessibility requirements

Requirements for the implementation, not results.

- **The role follows the level.** `steady` and `watch` are ordinary regions announced when
  reached. `attention` uses a polite live region. `urgent` uses `role="alert"` and is
  announced immediately — and it is the only level that may.
- **A banner present on load is not announced as a change.** Live-region announcements are
  for banners that appear after the page settles; announcing everything on load produces a
  wall of speech and teaches readers to ignore it.
- **The level's word is in the heading**, so grayscale, colour-vision deficiency and
  text-only rendering all preserve the meaning.
- **The heading is a real heading** at the right level for its position, so a screen-reader
  user can find it by heading navigation without scanning the page.
- **Focus is not stolen.** A banner never moves focus on appearance. If the product needs a
  response before anything else can happen, that is a [Dialog](./dialog.mdx) and it should
  say so.
- **Dismiss and actions meet the 44pt floor** with 8pt separation, and dismiss has a name
  that says what it dismisses, not just *close*.
- **Entrance is a state change, not a movement**, at every level, and is removed entirely
  under `prefers-reduced-motion` — with nothing lost, because nothing was ever carried by
  the movement.
- **Contrast** is measured for every level's heading, body, icon and action against its fill,
  in both themes and under increased contrast.

**Easily confused with**

  nothing. The most commonly mistaken pair in the system.
