AlertBanner is the loudest thing opsinjs ships, and the implementation is mostly a set of
limits. A banner that appears when it should not is not a small mistake: it spends the
reader's attention, and attention spent on a false alarm is not available for the real one.

## Preview

The preview is one banner, at `attention`, rather than a specimen sheet of all four levels.
The demo is distributed code and a row of four banners is the escalation budget being broken
by the first thing anybody sees. The four levels are told apart on the
[StatusPill](./status-pill.mdx) page, where the same four words and glyphs are the whole
component and nothing is interrupted to show them.

Two things here are worth checking rather than believing. Turn the text size up: the banner
is a stacked column at every width, so it grows and the actions wrap onto their own lines
instead of shrinking under the target floor. Then look at it in greyscale. The fill goes,
and the word *Needs attention* is still the first thing in the heading, which is the whole
reason the pill sits inside the heading rather than beside it.

    { label: "See the reading and the range", href: "/results/example" },

  asked us to tell you about; you can see the reading and that range together.

The first exclusion carries most of the weight. *Would anything be lost if the reader did
not see this?* If the answer is no, it is a [Callout](./callout.mdx). Banners are for the
messages where the answer is yes, and a product that cannot tell the difference will end up
with readers who scroll past both.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** That the product has determined this screen needs the reader's
attention at one of the four levels. At `attention` and `urgent` it also asserts that there
is something specific to do about it. Nothing else. The component compares nothing, holds no
threshold and has no rule that raises or lowers a level: `status` is assigned by the
consuming product from a reference range or a clinically reviewed threshold that the product
owns.

**What it must never be read as.** A clinical judgement about the reader, an instruction
from a person, or a substitute for emergency services. A banner may tell somebody to call
for help; it may never imply that the app has assessed them. The distinction lives in the
sentence: *your reading is above the level your clinic asked us to tell you about* is a
threshold being reported, and *you are having a hypertensive crisis* is a clinical
conclusion the app cannot reach.

**Which colour axis it may use.** The **status** axis, and only that. A banner carries no
category colour, ever, even when it is about a single category's data: a sleep-tinted urgent
banner makes the urgency look like a brand colour. There is no `category` prop, so the
collision is refused by the API rather than by a paragraph.

**The level's word is not the caller's to write.** The banner renders a
[StatusPill](./status-pill.mdx) as the first thing inside its heading, so the word comes
from `CLINICAL_STATUS_META` and cannot disagree with `status`. The original specification
asked the caller to put it in the `heading` string; a rule a caller can break silently is
not a rule, and a heading reading *Urgent* over `status="watch"` is two verdicts on one
surface with nothing to catch it. Your heading names the subject; the component names the
level.

**The role follows the level.** This is the one component in opsinjs entitled to
`role="alert"`, and only at `urgent`. Everything else in the system announces politely or
not at all, and nothing may mount a live region on a caller's behalf.

| Level | What the root carries | Actions | Dismiss |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **steady** | No role and no live region. Ordinary content in the reading order. | Optional | With `onAcknowledge` |
| **watch** | The same. | Optional | With `onAcknowledge` |
| **attention** | `aria-live="polite"` and `aria-atomic`. It waits for a gap. | Required, and reported if missing | With `onAcknowledge` |
| **urgent** | `role="alert"`, which carries assertive and atomic with it. It interrupts. | Required, and reported if missing | With `onAcknowledge`, which is also the record |

**Where an `urgent` banner stops, and the emergency path begins.** `urgent` means *this may
need help today*, which is the same trigger
[Emergency and escalation](../health/emergency-and-escalation.mdx) uses to enter a path with
eight requirements. A page that lists that doctrine therefore has to say which of the two a
given banner is. A banner may carry an `urgent` finding whose route is *contact a person
today*. At that level it takes **one** action and not two: a second button is a decision,
and this is the moment a person is least able to make one. The dismiss control stays,
because on this path the acknowledgement is the record.

A finding whose route is *stop and call for help now* leaves the banner. That path suppresses
every other thing on the screen, persists across a background and a relaunch, is redundant
across channels, and records the acknowledgement with a timestamp. This component does
none of those four. It cannot see the screen; it never removes itself but records nothing;
and `onAcknowledge` takes no argument, so what it reports is that a press happened and not
when. A banner used there is an emergency surface's job done badly, and opsinjs ships no
emergency surface: the doctrine page is the specification, and building it is the product's.

**One departure from the doctrine, named rather than left to be found.**
[Clinical status semantics](../health/clinical-status-semantics.mdx) rule 6 asks for a polite
announcement at `steady` and `watch`; the table above gives those two levels no live region
at all, and the implementation follows the table. The rule is describing a status that
changes on a surface the reader is already looking at; a banner is a surface that arrives,
and a `steady` banner announcing itself is an interruption whose entire content is that
nothing needs attention. That is the shape [Alarm fatigue](../health/alarm-fatigue.mdx)
spends its length arguing against. The cost is real and lands on the use this page argues
hardest for: a screen-reader user already on the page when a de-escalation banner appears
is never told that the earlier alert has been withdrawn. If your product needs that
sentence heard rather than seen, put it where the reader is going next.

**`steady` is legal, and it is the level to be careful with.** Its one honest use is
de-escalation: saying that a condition the product raised earlier has stopped being true.
A reader told on Tuesday that their device had stopped sending is owed the sentence that
says it started again, and no other component delivers it. A Callout carries no level, so
it cannot close one. What it is not is a place for a message that needs nothing. It still
spends one of the two banners the screen is allowed, and a banner that can say *nothing
needs attention* is a banner a product will reach for whenever it wants to be noticed.

**The escalation budget.** At most **one** `urgent` banner per screen and at most **two**
banners in total. If the product's rules generate more, the product must merge them into one
banner that names the most serious first. This is the central rule of
[Alarm fatigue](../health/alarm-fatigue.mdx), which specifies that a screen mounting a
second `urgent` banner "should fail loudly in development instead of rendering two".
**The component does not do that**, which is a decision rather than an omission and a place
where this page and its doctrine page disagree. A banner can only ever see itself. A counter
in module scope counts renders rather than banners, because it accumulates across requests
in a warm server process and across route changes on the client. It would therefore report a
screen that is correct, and a check that fires on correct code is a check somebody switches
off within a day. The budget is a property of a screen, and the places it can honestly be
enforced are a screen-level lint and a review. What the component does instead is report the
mistakes one render can see: a missing action at `attention` or `urgent`, more than two
actions, a `recommended` flag on an action that is not first, and a dismiss control with
nothing to report to.

**Two notes on counting**, because this is the page a reader will come to for it. First, an
`urgent` banner emits *two* elements carrying `data-status="urgent"`. They are the root
and the StatusPill inside its heading. OPSIN-0005's message is phrased as a count of
surfaces, so a check written to that description has to count
`[data-slot="alert-banner"][data-status="urgent"]` and the equivalent per-component slots
rather than the bare attribute, or it reports two for one correct banner. Second, this page
itself puts an `urgent` safety note above an `urgent` banner in the example further down.
Every preview mounts in an iframe, so those are two documents rather than one screen and the
budget does not formally apply across them. They are nevertheless visible together on one
scroll, and saying so is better than quietly lowering one of them on the page that teaches
the rule.

**Urgency is never carried by motion, at any level, under any circumstances.** No pulsing,
no flashing, no shaking, no colour cycling. The component ships no entrance transition at
all, so there is nothing to remove under `prefers-reduced-motion` and nothing that has to be
switched off to be safe. See [Motion in health UI](../health/motion-in-health-ui.mdx).

<SafetyCallout severity="urgent" evidence="opinion">
An `urgent` banner must never be the only place a serious message appears, and must never be
dismissible without an explicit acknowledgement the product records. A reader who swipes a
banner away on a bus has not been informed, and the product cannot tell the difference
between dismissal and understanding. The component holds up its half of this: `dismissible`
renders nothing at any level without an `onAcknowledge` to report to. It cannot hold up the
other half, which is whether the message still exists somewhere after the banner has gone.
</SafetyCallout>

The parts are `data-slot` attributes, not exports. There is no `AlertBanner.Actions` you can
import and no way to nest one part inside another: which parts exist is decided by the data
the component was given, and a banner cannot render a dismiss control without a callback for
it, because there is no way to hand it one.

Two departures from the proposed tree are worth naming. **`AlertBanner.Icon` does not
exist**, and what stands in its place is a composed `StatusPill` carrying the word, the
glyph, the colour and `data-status` together. The four carriers arrive as one unit, from the
component that owns them. **`AlertBanner.Dismiss` is last rather than first**: a dismiss
floated into the top corner puts the way out ahead of the message in the reading order, for
the reader least able to skip back. Nothing here is re-arranged by CSS against the DOM, so
the order announced is the order seen.

**A part whose control is a composed `Button` carries its slot on the element around it**,
and that is worth knowing before you write a selector. `Button` writes its own
`data-slot="button"` after the props it was passed, so one handed down from here is
replaced. That is correct of Button and inconvenient here. The wrapper is where
`alert-banner-action` and `alert-banner-dismiss` live, so the DOM contract says the same
thing whether the action was given an `href` or an `onSelect`. This is checked against
rendered markup rather than reasoned from the source; the first version of this page
documented the slot on the control, and the control did not have it.

The slots are for restyling, and one of them is not for hiding. `display: none` on
`[data-slot="alert-banner-heading"]` takes the level's word out of the accessibility tree
and leaves a coloured box. A coloured box on its own is the status carried by colour alone,
and the failure every rule on this page is arranged to prevent. No prop can do it; only a
stylesheet can, and this is the page asking you not to.

## Examples

### Two is the ceiling

The budget drawn at the line rather than described: two banners on one surface, the more
serious first, because a reader meets them in DOM order whether they are looking or
listening. A third here would not be a busier screen. It would be a screen that has taught
its reader to scroll past the second one.

### An urgent banner is acknowledged, not dismissed

`dismissible` on its own renders nothing at any level. The control needs an `onAcknowledge`
to report to, and at `urgent` that callback is also the record. The record is what lets a
product tell dismissal apart from understanding. What replaces the banner is the second half
of the rule: the message is still on the screen after the interruption has gone.

It carries **one** action, because its body says the clinic wants to hear from the reader
today and that is the same-day case above. It also shows what the component cannot do
anything about: acknowledging destroys the control the reader pressed, so focus falls to
`<body>`. A keyboard user is returned to the top of the document and a listening reader's
cursor is reset with nothing spoken, which is the worst possible moment to be sent away from
the sentence that outlived the banner. The example leaves that unhandled on purpose so it is
visible. A product that removes an acknowledged banner owns the recovery.

## Content guidelines

The heading names the subject in under eight words, and it does not name the level: the
component renders that word itself, at the front of the heading, where it cannot be lost or
contradicted. The body is one or two sentences in the second person, and actions are
imperative and concrete.

**The body's ordering is per level, and it is not one rule for all four.** At `steady` and
`watch` it says what happened and then what it means for this reader: there is nothing
specific to do at those levels, and an instruction first would invent one. At `attention`
and `urgent` it opens with the instruction and then gives the finding.
[Clinical status semantics](../health/clinical-status-semantics.mdx) asks for "direct and
calm, action first" at `attention` and "action in the first line" at `urgent`, and
[Emergency and escalation](../health/emergency-and-escalation.mdx) makes it the first of its
eight requirements. It matters more on this surface than it would elsewhere, because the DOM
order here is fixed at heading, body, timestamp, actions: the first sentence of the body is
the only place an action can be moved forward to. An earlier version of this page asked for
fact-then-meaning at all four levels and every shipped surface obeyed it; the demo, the
snippet above, the Do below and two of the examples have been rewritten.

**A banner that reports a comparison against a range carries a route to the range.**
[Reference ranges](../health/reference-ranges.mdx) is unambiguous that a value compared to a
range is a value whose range the reader can see, and a banner has nowhere to show one:
*outside the range your clinic set*, on a surface with no way through to the reading, asks
the reader to trust a rule they cannot look at. So one of the two actions reaches the surface
where the reading and the range are both visible. The exception is the same-day `urgent`
case, which is allowed one action and spends it on the route to a person; there the reading
and its range are reachable from the screen the banner sits on rather than from the banner.
Whose range it is is named in every case. An unowned band is the other half of the same
rule.

No exclamation marks, at any level. No capitals for emphasis. No *immediately*, *critical*,
*danger*, *emergency* unless the product genuinely means the reader should stop and call for
help, in which case say exactly that and give them the number for their country. opsinjs
ships no emergency number, because it does not know where the reader is.

## Accessibility

Six of these are checked by a gate on every commit. The rest are arguments, and they are
labelled as such: `alpha` promises that accessibility is claimed only where it has been
checked, with the gaps listed rather than left out.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y` on every commit:**

- **No status word is restated.** The four words are read from `CLINICAL_STATUS_META` by the
  composed StatusPill, and a copy of one written as a literal anywhere in this file fails the
  gate.
- **No colour literal, no ramp step and no primitive token.** The four fills resolve through
  `--opsin-status-<level>-surface`, `-ink` and the bare `-<level>` line role and through
  nothing else.
- **No `px` type size**, so every word on the banner scales with the reader's setting.
- **No class that resolves to nothing under `/view`.** This is the failure that looks correct
  in review, because the documentation renders components inline in its own document where
  the docs palette exists.
- **No shipped threshold and no unowned interval.** There is no staleness default, no
  boundary and no number in this component at all.
- **One element does not carry both axes.** There is no `category` prop, so the collision has
  no route in.

The colour-carrier rule reports this file as a **warning rather than a pass**, and the
distinction is worth stating rather than glossing. The gate reads one file at a time. It can
see a status fill and `data-status` here and no `CLINICAL_STATUS_META` and no glyph, and it
cannot follow the import to find that both went to StatusPill. Its own source names this
component as the shape its escape hatch exists for. Confirm by eye, as it asks: the word and
the glyph are the first things inside the heading.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **The role follows the level**, exactly as the table under
  [Clinical meaning](#clinical-meaning) sets out. The `steady`/`watch` departure from
  doctrine is named there rather than here. Nobody has listened to it in a screen reader,
  and the case that most needs testing is whether a real heading survives heading navigation
  inside `role="alert"`. Several screen readers flatten the semantics of an alert's subtree.
- **A banner that escalates in place is the untested half of that contract.** Everything
  above is about a banner arriving. A caller who re-renders the same `<AlertBanner>` with a
  higher `status` gets `role` and `aria-live` patched onto a DOM node the screen reader has
  already registered, and a live region is registered when its node is inserted: adding the
  role afterwards commonly announces nothing. The
  [staged alert recipe](../recipes/staged-alert.mdx) escalates exactly this way, and
  [Screen readers](../accessibility/screen-readers.mdx) requires the escalation to
  interrupt. Until somebody has listened to it, escalate by remounting the element with a
  `key` that contains the level, and the `status` prop doc says so where a caller will read
  it.
- **`role="alert"` at `urgent` wraps three interactive controls, and nobody has decided
  whether that is right.** A live region is announced as flattened text, so *Call your
  clinic* is spoken as prose with no role information and no way to act on it from the
  announcement; ARIA points at `alertdialog` where a message needs a response through
  controls, and this component is deliberately not one. It traps no focus and moves none.
  What a reader is expected to do after the announcement is find the `h2`, and the root has
  no accessible name to help them get there. That is the honest answer today and it is
  written down rather than left to be discovered.
- **At `attention` and `urgent` the whole banner re-announces whenever anything inside it
  changes**, because the region is atomic. The likeliest thing to change is the timestamp,
  whose phrase is a function of `now`, and RelativeTime positively instructs a product to
  re-render with a fresh one. A screen that ticks `now` on a minute boundary therefore
  interrupts a listening reader in full, once a minute, assertively at `urgent`. That is a
  re-raise on a timer, and [Alarm fatigue](../health/alarm-fatigue.mdx) forbids exactly
  that. The constraint is stated on the `now` prop rather than enforced: hold `now` still
  while an `attention` or `urgent` banner is mounted. Excluding the timestamp from the
  region is the other repair and it has not been done, because it would be a claim about
  assistive-technology behaviour that nobody here has verified.
- **The dismiss control's accessible name repeats the heading, so inside an atomic region
  the heading is spoken twice**. The heading opens the announcement and closes it, after
  the actions. It is the right call for a control met out of context in a list of buttons
  and the wrong one inside a single announcement, and the component cannot have it both
  ways: hiding the suffix from the region while keeping it on the control needs a generated
  id, which needs `useId`, which would make every banner in every product a client
  component. The repeat is the lesser cost and this is it being named.
- **A banner present on load is not announced as a change**, and this component implements
  none of that. Screen readers do not announce live regions that were in the document at
  load; they announce what arrives afterwards. The behaviour is inherited rather than
  written, and the alternative is to mount the role after a frame, which moves an
  announcement decision into a timer.
- **The level's word is in the heading**, structurally, because the pill is the heading's
  first child rather than a string the caller was asked to include.
- **The heading is a real heading**, at whichever of `h2` to `h6` the caller says. There is
  no `h1`: a banner is never a page.
- **Focus is never moved**, which is not the same as focus being safe. No `.focus()` call
  exists in the file, at any level; if the product needs a response before anything else can
  happen, that is a [Dialog](./dialog.mdx). What the component cannot prevent is focus being
  *destroyed*. A product that stops rendering an acknowledged banner removes the control the
  reader pressed, focus falls to `<body>`, a keyboard user is returned to the top
  of the document and a screen-reader user's cursor is reset with nothing spoken, because
  whatever replaced the banner is ordinary content. Not moving focus and destroying focus
  are different things, and only the first is this component's to promise. The
  acknowledgement example leaves the second unhandled so it can be seen; a product owns the
  recovery.
- **Nothing moves.** There is no entrance transition to remove under
  `prefers-reduced-motion`, and no state in the component that could animate one.
- **Every control clears the 44pt floor in both axes**, in `rem` so it grows with the text
  rather than pinning at 44 CSS pixels while the label doubles, and the actions sit
  `--opsin-target-separation` apart. Reasoned from the CSS; measuring it in a browser is the
  nightly layout job's, and that job has not run against this component.
- **Reading order is the DOM order**, and nothing is re-arranged by CSS against it. That
  order is heading, body, timestamp, actions, dismiss.
- **At 200% text the banner grows.** It is a stacked column at every width with no fixed
  height and no overflow container, the actions wrap onto their own lines rather than
  shrinking, and a long unbreakable token in the body breaks instead of widening the page.
  **Reasoned from the CSS**, like the bullet above it: every class was read against
  under the pinned Tailwind, and no one has yet put the rendered preview in front of an eye
  at 200% in either theme. The nightly layout job measures exactly this and has not run
  against this component. An earlier version of this page said it had been checked by eye,
  which was a completed check asserted rather than performed.

**Measured, and it is worth being exact about which pairs:**

- The pairs on the banner's own fill **are** measured, in both themes, for all four levels:
  `<level> ink on <level> surface` at the body floor, which is the heading and the body, and
  `<level> line on <level> surface` at the non-text floor, which is the boundary. All of
  them pass. The pill's own fill against the banner's is the same pair. Both take the same
  level's `-surface` role, so what separates them is the pill's `-line`, and that pair is
  measured too. The figures are published and dated at
  [Contrast](../reference/generated/contrast.mdx); this page does not restate them, because
  a contrast number that a person typed is a number that drifts from the one the gate
  computes. An earlier version of this page said none of them had been measured. That was a
  fabricated gap, which corrupts an audit exactly as much as a fabricated pass and buries
  the pairs below.

**Not yet checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- **Every neutral role sitting on a status fill**, which is a scope combination
  `scripts/check-contrast.mts` does not currently emit at all: the first action's `bg-card`
  island and its `text-foreground` on each of the four tints, the second action's
  `border-border` boundary against them, the `bg-muted` that appears under status ink on
  hover, and the focus ring on a status fill. Four tints, two themes, several pairs each,
  and none of them in `lib/generated/contrast.json`. This is the real gap the bullet above
  was hiding, and it is the price of the decision that keeps a control from taking the
  level's colour.
- Whether a `-line` boundary that clears the non-text floor is enough to read the pill as a
  distinct object when its fill and the banner's are the same token. That is a legibility
  question rather than a contrast one, and it needs eyes rather than a script.
- `forced-colors` is implemented nowhere in opsinjs. `prefers-contrast: more` is implemented
  in [Surface](./surface.mdx#accessibility) alone rather than in the token layer, and this
  banner renders no Surface, so nothing drawn here moves under the mode. The
  increased-contrast half of the requirement therefore cannot currently be met by anyone.
- The root has no accessible name and is therefore not a landmark. A screen-reader user gets
  the heading and no boundary. There is no start, no end, and no announcement of where the
  banner stops.

## API reference

<PropsTable name="AlertBannerProps" />

`status` is required and is an input, never a derivation. There is no `unknown`: it is the
absence of an assertion rather than a fifth level, and an interruption with no level is an
interruption with no meaning. A JavaScript caller passing something outside the four gets
OPSIN-0021, `unknown` gets OPSIN-0011, and in both cases **nothing is rendered**. That
refusal costs more here than it does on a pill, because the product's message does not reach
the reader. It is still the right way round. Rendering the message on a neutral surface
would move a clinical statement onto a component with no level, no budget and no reviewer;
rendering a plausible level would be this component inventing a verdict about somebody's
health.

Five props are additions to the original specification, and they are named here rather than
slipped in. **`headingLevel`** exists because "a real heading at the right level for its
position" is not something a component can know; it defaults to `h2` and the caller is the
only one who can be right. A value outside `2` to `6` falls back to `h2` and is reported. Such
a value is the `null` a CMS-driven caller passes to mean "no override", or the `1` somebody
reaches for after reading that there is no `h1`. The type does not save you here: this file
ships as source into JavaScript projects, the tag map returns `undefined` for anything else,
and `undefined` in element position is a render error that takes the whole screen down rather
than a heading at the wrong depth. A wrong outline is recoverable; a throw is not. **`now`** exists because
[RelativeTime](./relative-time.mdx) requires it: a component that read the clock itself
would be impure and would give two timestamps on one screen different answers across a
minute boundary. **`dismissLabel`** is the override for the one word this component owns.
**`locale`** reaches the timestamp, which is the only formatted thing on the surface.
**`className`** is the merge point every component in the system carries, and it is the one
way a caller can still hide what this one insists on. See the accessibility section.

`heading` changed meaning. The specification required it to contain the level's word; the
implementation renders that word itself, so the heading names the subject and nothing else.
An empty heading is reported and the banner still renders its level, because a level with no
subject is more useful than nothing at all.

`actions` is capped at two by the specification and by nothing in the type. Passing three
renders three and reports it: which one to drop is the product's decision, and silently
dropping the third would hide an action somebody wrote. A value that is not an array at all
is treated as none and reported, for the same reason `headingLevel` is guarded. Calling
`.map` on it throws, and a throw on this surface removes the screen the message was on.
`recommended` is honoured only on the first action. Emphasis follows position, because a
screen-reader user meets the actions in DOM order and the recommended one has to be the one
they meet first.

`dismissible` needs `onAcknowledge` at **every** level, not only at `urgent`. The
specification made acknowledgement a condition of urgency, and the reason it gave was that
the product cannot tell dismissal apart from understanding. That reason is not a property of
urgency. It is a property of this component never removing itself: the caller decides what is
on screen, so a dismiss control with no callback would acknowledge a press and change
nothing. What is different at `urgent` is what the callback is for: a record, rather than a
remembered preference.

`AlertAction` is exported alongside the props interface. The generator only emits a table for
interfaces named `<Pascal>Props`, so there is no generated one for it; this is the shipped
declaration, copied from `registry/bases/base/alert-banner.tsx`:

```ts
export interface AlertAction {
  /** Imperative and concrete, naming what happens. */
  label: string
  /** Where it goes. Renders an anchor. */
  href?: string
  /** What it does. Renders a Button. Supply one of the two. */
  onSelect?: () => void
  /** Honoured on the first action only; emphasis follows position. */
  recommended?: boolean
}
```

There is no `category`, no `variant`, no `tone` and no `color`. There is no `animate`, no
`duration` and no `pulse`: urgency carried by motion is the failure this component's own
doctrine page names first. There is no `onDismiss` separate from `onAcknowledge`, because
the distinction between the two is a record the product keeps rather than two controls.
