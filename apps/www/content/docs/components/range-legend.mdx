RangeLegend is the caption for a RangeBar, written out as a list. It names the tones a bar draws: the neutral band that is the reference range, and each clinical status colour a product may put on a reading. It draws no scale, states no reading and reaches no verdict, so on its own it is at worst useless rather than misleading.

That restraint is the whole design. The roster declined a floating legend because a legend that can be separated from its chart is a legend that will be, and the answer here is that the legend owns no data. It takes the same band definitions you gave the bar, and it belongs in the same container as the bar. Move it somewhere else and it stops being a legend, because there is nothing beside it to be the legend of.

## Preview

Check that the neutral band swatch is the same colour as a RangeBar's band, that each status swatch is the same colour as that level's tick, and that every status row carries a word and a distinct glyph as well as a colour. Turn the text size up: the swatches hold their size while the words wrap, and nothing is truncated.

  ]}

The distinction that matters most is the first one. A RangeBar carries its own words: the summary sentence names the reading, the range and the position, and a StatusPill names the level with a word and a glyph. So a legend beside a single bar mostly repeats what is already there. It earns its place only when several bars share one vocabulary, and then it says that vocabulary once instead of many times.

Wherever you use it, keep it in the same container as the bars it names. The legend takes the band definitions you gave those bars, and it is a caption. A caption on the wrong page is not a caption.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** RangeLegend asserts exactly one thing: these are the tones the bar beside it draws, and here is what each one means, in these words. It asserts nothing about any particular reading, nothing about a cause, and nothing about what happens next. It is a key, and a key describes the map rather than the territory.

**What it must never be read as.** It is not a scale, not a diagnosis and not a verdict on the reader. A row that reads "Needs attention" names a tone a product may assign; it does not say that the reader's own result is in that tone. The reading and its level live on the bar, never in the key. The words "normal" and "abnormal" are banned from every row, as they are everywhere in opsinjs, and [Reference ranges and normal](../health/reference-ranges.mdx) carries the whole list and the argument behind it.

**Which colour axis it may use.** The status axis, and the neutral reference range band, and nothing else. A row is either the neutral band that the bar draws for the usual range, or one of the four clinical status tones. It may never use the category axis: a category colour identifies what a reading is about, and a legend of range tones is not about identity, so no swatch is category tinted and no element carries `data-category`. Putting an identity colour and an urgency colour in the same key is the collision [The two colour axes](../health/two-colour-axes.mdx) exists to prevent. Status here never travels as colour alone: every status row draws the fill, the level's distinct glyph and the word together, so the level survives greyscale and colour vision deficiency.

**Who owns the thresholds and the words.** The consuming product, always. RangeLegend ships no band names, no ranges and no levels of its own. The words in every row arrive as props, because the product owns the ranges those words describe, and shipping band names inside a component library would be shipping clinical decisions to every product that installed it. The swatch colours are the two shared axes read straight from the token layer, and they are the only thing this component supplies.

## Content guidelines

Write each row's label in the reader's words, and make a status row's word the product's own plain phrasing for that level rather than a clinical register. The description is a short second line, not a paragraph: it says who set the range or what the tone means, and it stops. A legend is a key, and a key that runs long stops being scanned.

Never write a verdict on the reader into a row. "Worth watching" names a tone; "your result is poor" names a person, and the banned word list at [Reference ranges and normal](../health/reference-ranges.mdx) is the same here as everywhere. Keep the neutral band's word about the range rather than about the reader: "the usual range", not "fine" or "good".

## Accessibility

Some of this is enforced by a gate on every pull request and every push to `main`, and the rest is argued and labelled as such. At `alpha`, accessibility is claimed only where it has been checked, with the gaps listed rather than left out. Nothing here has been through a review with a disabled user.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y`:**

- **Colour is never the only carrier of a status.** A status row reads the glyph shape from `CLINICAL_STATUS_META` and prints the word as the label, so the four levels are four distinct shapes and four distinct words rather than one shape in four colours. The gate fails the build on a status colour drawn without a glyph and a word.
- **No element takes colour from both axes.** The gate reads every JSX element and fails on one carrying both `data-category` and `data-status`. This file writes no category colour and no `data-category` at all, and stamps `data-status` only on a status row, so a mixture cannot arise.
- **No shipped threshold and no raw colour literal.** Every word in the key arrives as a prop, no range or level is written into the component or its examples, and every colour is a semantic token role from the two shared axes.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **The words are in the accessibility tree; the picture is not.** Each swatch and each glyph carries `aria-hidden`, so a screen reader meets the list of labels and descriptions and skips the decoration. A reader who cannot see the swatches loses the colours and keeps every word.
- **The legend is not interactive.** Nothing takes focus, answers a key or a pointer, or animates. It is a list of names, so there is no state for `prefers-reduced-motion` to reduce.
- **At 200% text the rows already stack and wrap.** The swatches hold their size in units independent of the type step while the words wrap beneath, and nothing is truncated and no ellipsis is set anywhere in the file.

**Not checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- Every contrast pair: each status swatch fill against the host ground, the neutral swatch's hairline against it, and the glyph ink against it. `scripts/check-contrast.mts` has not been run for this component, which is why the report below is empty rather than reassuring. The swatches are `-line` roles and the glyph is drawn in a neutral ink, and this component sets no surface of its own, so every one of these pairs is painted on whatever ground the host provides rather than the matching `-surface` the token contract tunes against.
- How the decorative swatch list plays for a screen reader user, and whether the list should carry an accessible name that points at the bar it explains. It has been reasoned about and not listened to.
- What the swatches draw under `forced-colors: active`. A status fill is stripped and the swatch keeps a `CanvasText` outline so the chip stays visible, the glyph shape and the word carry the level, and the neutral swatch keeps its border. None of this has been put in front of a machine or a high contrast user.

## API reference

<PropsTable name="RangeLegendProps" />

`bands` is the whole of the component: an array of rows, each a word, an optional description and an optional tone. Omit a row's `tone` for the neutral reference range band, which is what the bar draws for the usual range and for a reading with no verdict. Set it to a clinical status level to name a tone the product has assigned.

`tone` is typed to the four `ClinicalStatus` levels rather than to `string`, for the same reason RangeBar types its `status`: a legend that accepted a fifth tone would be naming a colour the bar cannot draw. [Clinical status semantics](../health/clinical-status-semantics.mdx) is where those four levels are defined. A value outside the four is refused with a development warning and the row falls back to the neutral band rather than being approximated into a level. An empty `bands` array renders nothing and warns in development, because a legend with no rows is a key to a picture it forgot to name.
