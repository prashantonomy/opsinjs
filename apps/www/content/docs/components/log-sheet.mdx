Daily logging fails on friction. Every second between the intention to record something and
the record existing loses entries, and lost entries make every trend downstream less true.
LogSheet exists to make that path short, and most of its specification is about resisting the
pressure to put one more field on it.

It is a client component, and that is a requirement rather than an optimisation. It holds the
time the entry is about, whether the reader moved it, the note, and whether the discard
question is on screen. It also extends [Sheet](./sheet.mdx), which mounts a portal, traps
focus and listens for a drag. None of that has a CSS answer.

## Preview

One field, a time defaulted to the moment the sheet opened, an optional note, and one save.
Type something and then try to leave: the escape key, the background, a downward drag and the
close control all ask the same question in the same place.

[Sheet](./sheet.mdx) is what LogSheet extends: the surface, the detents, the focus trap, the
keyboard-safe inset and the dismissal routes are all its. [Field](./field.mdx) wires the time
control and the note. The wiring is the label relationship, the description list and the
invalid state, none of which this component hand-rolls. [Button](./button.mdx) is the save
action and the two answers to the discard question. LogSheet imports no npm package of its
own; `@base-ui/react` and `lucide-react` arrive with those three.

The first exclusion is the one that decides whether a product's logging works. A sheet with
seven fields is a form, and a form belongs on a page where it can breathe, be saved as a
draft, and be abandoned without losing the reader's place. Sheets are for the entries people
make every day; forms are for the ones they make once.

**The ceiling is not enforced, and the component says so rather than pretending.** `children`
is an opaque element tree, and `React.Children` sees only direct children. A product that
wraps its own two fields in a component of its own would therefore be counted as having one. A
count that is wrong in the common case teaches the wrong lesson twice: it clears a sheet that
is too long and complains about one that is not. Better an unenforced rule than a check nobody
can trust.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** That the reader recorded this value, at this time, themselves. That is a
weaker and more specific claim than a device measurement, and the record preserves the
difference: `LogEntry.provenance` is the literal `"self-reported"` and there is no prop that
changes it. A self-reported reading and one read from a cuff over Bluetooth are not
interchangeable downstream. See
[Data provenance and device accuracy](../health/data-provenance-and-device-accuracy.mdx). A
device-assisted flow is a different surface, not this one with a flag on it.

**What it must never be read as.** A clinical observation, or an assessment of anything.
Logging is recording. The sheet does not evaluate what is entered, does not colour it by
status, and does not tell the reader whether their number is worth acting on. That is a job
for the surface that displays the value afterwards, if the product has grounds for it.

**Which colour axis it may use.** The **category** axis, on one band beneath the sheet's
heading, so a reader knows whether they are logging sleep or a heart measurement. That band
carries `data-category`, no words and no glyph, which is what the category axis is entitled to
say: it is identity, not urgency, and the title above it already names what is being logged in
full. The **status** axis does not appear on this surface at any price. Colouring a field
amber while somebody is typing their own measurement into it is a verdict delivered
mid-keystroke, and it is the fastest way to teach a person to stop logging honestly.

**Plausibility is not judgement, and this component performs none.** LogSheet holds no
thresholds, no ranges and no bounds, and it never blocks a save for any reason. Where a
warning about an unlikely value belongs is on the control that knows what was measured, which
is the product's. See [ReadingInput](./reading-input.mdx) for that contract. What LogSheet
guarantees is only that nothing it does can stop the save going through: unlikely values
happen, and a log that refuses them is a log with a hole in it exactly where the interesting
data was.

**An empty entry is the product's to keep or drop, not the sheet's to block.** An entry whose
`values` are all null and whose note is absent is a record of nothing, and it still reaches
`onSave`, because this component blocks no save for any reason and an emptiness test is a
validation like any other. Whether to store it, drop it, or ask the reader again is the
product's decision, and the place to make it is the first line of `onSave`, where the product
can see the record it is about to keep. The example under Two fields, and the window the
product owns shows that decision as two lines at the top of its handler. Disabling the save
action is refused on purpose: it would make the sheet a form that argues with the reader, and
a reader who cannot make the sheet go away by pressing the obvious button will press it anyway
or lose their place.

**Timing is part of the record, and the two times are stored separately.** `occurredAt` is the
time the entry is *about*, defaulted to the moment the sheet opened and editable throughout.
`recordedAt` is the time the save happened, read at the save and never editable. An emptied
time control saves with the time of the save and is not marked as re-dated, so the two fields
never disagree.

**`backdated` is the reader's action, not arithmetic.** It is true when the reader moved the
time control away from the time the sheet opened at. Deriving it from `occurredAt !==
recordedAt` is the obvious implementation and is wrong: those two always differ by however
long the entry took to type, so every record would come back marked and the flag would mean
nothing. Clearing the control is an absence rather than a re-dating, so an emptied time is
false here and its `occurredAt` equals its `recordedAt`.

**`maxBackdateDays` is the product's number and nothing is substituted for it.** Omit it and
no earliest date is offered and none is stated. Supply it and the platform's date picker is
told where the window starts and the field's guidance names the date in words. The window
opens at the start of the named day, so the whole of the earliest day the guidance names is
offered. This sheet refuses no entry, so a time the reader types outside the window still
saves. The platform's picker is a separate matter: on a touch device the picker is the only
way to change the time and it will not offer a value below the window, so there the window is
enforced by the platform even though the sheet enforces nothing.

Every part above is internal structure identified by `data-slot`, and none of them is an
export. Content arrives as a prop or as `children`; there is no `LogSheet.Time` to render on
its own and no way to have the save action without the sheet around it.

Three of the parts sit in a wrapper element rather than on the control itself, and it is worth
knowing why before you write a selector: `Button` stamps `data-slot="button"` after spreading
whatever it was given, so a slot passed to it never reaches the DOM. `LogSheet.Save`,
`LogSheet.Keep` and `LogSheet.Discard` are therefore the wrappers, each holding one `Button`.
The alternative was hand-rolling three controls, which would teach in shipped source that the
primary action at the foot of a sheet is a styled `<button>`.

The **header, title and close control belong to Sheet**, which is why they are not listed as
LogSheet's parts. The specification's tree named a `LogSheet.Cancel` "always present, never
discards without asking". That is Sheet's close control, and this component keeps the second
half of the promise by intercepting every dismissal route rather than by rendering a second
exit. Two exits in one header is a choice a reader should not have to make.

Nesting a second `LogSheet` inside a `LogSheet`, or putting a navigation control inside one,
is a composition error: a capture sheet has one exit and one destination. Sheet raises a
development warning for the first of those on its own behalf.

## States

LogSheet is interactive, and its interaction states belong to the parts it composes rather than
to anything it draws itself. Hover, focus and pressed on the save action and on the two answers
are [Button](./button.mdx)'s; focus and the invalid treatment on the time control and the note
are [Field](./field.mdx)'s; the drag, the focus trap and the return of focus to the trigger are
[Sheet](./sheet.mdx)'s. The one interaction this component owns is the footer swap: while there
is unsaved input, a dismissal replaces the save action with the discard question in place, and
answering *Keep editing* puts it back. Nothing here is ever disabled. The save action stays
active at every moment, on purpose, because a capture surface that greys out its own save is a
form arguing with the reader, and a reader who cannot make the sheet go away by the obvious
button loses their place instead.

The five canonical data states are display concerns, and LogSheet captures rather than displays,
so most of them belong to the surface that shows a reading afterwards rather than to the sheet
that recorded it. The row for each says what this component actually does.

| Data state | What LogSheet renders |
| --- | --- |
| Error | Nothing of its own. It runs no validation, marks no field invalid, and never blocks a save. An implausible value is the product's to warn about on its own control, and a save that does not reach storage is a downstream state on a surface this component cannot see. |
| Loading | Nothing. It holds no async work of its own: `onSave` returns void and the sheet reads the clock only at open and at save. A saving or queued state after `onSave` returns is the product's surface, which is one of the open questions above. |
| Empty | The sheet still opens, and an entry whose `values` are all null with no note still reaches `onSave`, because the save is never blocked and the action is never disabled. Whether to store, drop, or re-ask is the first line of the product's handler. |
| Partial | The ordinary case, passed through untouched. A `null` for a control the reader left alone travels in `values` verbatim, kept distinct from `0` and from a key that is absent, and nothing is coerced or dropped for being empty. |
| Stale | Not applicable. Staleness is a reading ageing on a display surface. A fresh entry carries `recordedAt`, the save time, beside `occurredAt`, the time it is about, and how old a stored reading looks later is [RelativeTime](./relative-time.mdx)'s job, not this sheet's. |

## Content guidelines

The title is what is being logged, as a noun phrase: *Blood pressure*, *Morning weight*, *How
you slept*. The save action says what it saves. It is *Save reading*, not *Done*, and never
*OK*. `saveLabel` is required for that reason: there is no honest default, and a component
that shipped one would let every product skip the rule without noticing it had one.

Do not ask for anything you can infer, and do not ask for anything you will not use. Every
field is a small tax paid every single day, and the fourth field is the one that stops people
logging at all.

The note is opt-in through its own label, so a product that has no use for free text does not
pay for it. When you do want one, name what it is for: *Anything worth remembering* asks a
different question from *Notes*, and gets a different answer.

## Motion

LogSheet declares no transition of its own. The category band does not animate in, the footer
swap between the save action and the discard question is an instant re-render, and no element
this file renders carries a duration or an easing. That swap is deliberately without motion: the
discard question is the most consequential thing on the surface, and a reader who triggered it
by trying to leave needs to see it at once rather than watch it arrive.

The only movement a reader sees is the sheet arriving and leaving, and that motion is
[Sheet](./sheet.mdx)'s, read by Sheet rather than by this file. It runs on
`--opsin-ease-spring-sheet` paired with `--opsin-duration-spring-sheet`, a spring tuned
overdamped so the surface reaches its resting height without passing it and springing back,
because a capture sheet that bounces at the top of its travel reads as a dropped object. Under
`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` that token's own fallback replaces the slide with a cross-fade
in place, because a sheet that appears with no travel still needs to be seen to appear.

<MotionCurve token="--opsin-ease-spring-sheet" />

A health value is never animated on first paint anywhere in this component. There is no count-up
on the number a reader types, no sweep, and no motion tied to the value at all. The product's own
fields render their contents with nothing this component adds, so a measurement never appears to
move toward a figure it has not reached yet.

## Accessibility

Three of these are checked by a gate on every commit. The rest are arguments, and they are
labelled as such: `beta` promises that accessibility is claimed only where it has been
checked, with the gaps listed rather than left out. Nothing on this page has been measured in
a browser.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y` on every commit:**

- **No status colour anywhere on this surface.** The static rig fails the build on any file
  that paints a status colour without the word and the glyph beside it, and it fails on any
  element that takes colour from both axes at once. This component paints one category band
  and nothing else, so the check has nothing to complain about. That is the point: a capture
  surface that could be tinted by status would be one somebody eventually tints.
- **The category band carries `data-category`.** The attribute and the class are two spellings
  of one decision, and the attribute is the DOM contract a product's stylesheet, its print
  rules and its own tests key on.
- **No threshold, bound or interval ships in this file or its examples.** A11Y014 and A11Y015
  fail the build on a name like `plausible` or `cutoff` given a number, and on any object
  literal holding two numeric bounds with nobody's name on it. `maxBackdateDays` has no
  default here and none in the demo.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **The sheet is a modal dialogue named by its title**, focus trapped while open and returned
  to the trigger on close. All of that is Sheet's, unchanged, and Sheet's page says the same
  thing about it.
- **The sheet carries no description of its own, by design.** Sheet exposes `Sheet.Description`,
  which wires the popup's `aria-describedby`, and this component renders none. A form sheet's
  content is its fields, so a sentence describing the whole sheet would be copy this component
  invented rather than anything the reader needs. The title names what is being logged, each
  field carries its own visible label, and the time control carries a hint when there is a
  window or an emptied control to explain. The one place a description earns its keep is the
  discard question, and its two answers already carry one. A tool inspecting the open sheet
  therefore finds no `aria-describedby` on the container, and that absence is a decision rather
  than an oversight.
- **Focus is not moved to the first field on open.** The specification asked for that and this
  component declines, for three reasons: a component moving focus on appearance is refused by
  the repository's own focus doctrine; landing in a control raises the software keyboard over
  a sheet the reader has not read yet; and a screen-reader user who arrives inside an input
  never hears the name of the surface that just took the screen. Sheet moves focus into the
  popup, where the title is announced, and the first field is one `Tab` away.
- **Every field has a visible, persistent label.** The time and the note get theirs from
  [Field](./field.mdx), which has no way to hide one. The product's own fields are the
  product's responsibility, and `Field` is the component that makes them cheap to get right.
- **The discard question is announced by focus rather than by a live region.** This component
  mounts no live region, because it is not entitled to speak on the caller's behalf. The way a
  keyboard or screen-reader user learns that a question has appeared is therefore that focus
  is on it. Focus lands on *Keep editing*, which is the non-destructive answer; the
  destructive one is never the default. It moves in reply to the reader asking to leave, not
  on appearance. Both answers carry `aria-describedby` pointing at the question's own
  paragraph, so the sentence that says what is at stake is voiced with the button's name
  rather than being left unsaid.
- **Answering Keep editing puts focus back where it was.** The control the reader was in when
  the question appeared is remembered before the footer is replaced, and focus is moved back to
  it before the answers unmount, so a keyboard or screen-reader user returns to the field they
  were typing in rather than to the top of the dialog. When the question went up over a scrim
  tap, where focus was outside the sheet, it falls back to the sheet's close control rather
  than dropping focus on the page behind.
- **While the question is up the header close control does not withdraw it.** Escape, a scrim
  tap and a drag are ambient gestures and answer with Keep editing, but the close control is a
  deliberate act: tapping it again leaves the question standing and returns focus to the
  answers, so the same gesture never flips the sheet between two states without ever closing
  it.
- **The question is asked in place, not in a second modal surface.** A confirmation dialogue
  opening from a sheet is two stacked modal surfaces, a focus order nobody can predict, and an
  escape key with two plausible meanings.
- **Nothing blocks the save.** No validation runs here, and no field is marked invalid by this
  component. `min` on the time control constrains the platform picker, not the save: on a
  platform with a keyboard a time below the window can still be typed and saved, while on a
  touch device the picker is the only way to set the time and will not offer a value below the
  window, so the platform enforces there what this component never does.
- **Numeric input mode and autocomplete are the product's to declare** on its own controls.
  `Field.Control` sets neither, deliberately: the right `autocomplete` token depends on what
  is being asked for, and a guess would fill somebody's postcode into a field asking for
  something else.
- **At 200% text the sheet scrolls internally and the save action stays pinned.** Both are
  Sheet's, and both are argued from its source rather than observed.

- Contrast for the category band against the sheet's material, in both themes and under

## API reference

<PropsTable name="LogSheetProps" />

`LogSheetProps` extends `Omit<SheetProps, "children" | "footer" | "modal" | "dismissible">`,
so **`open`, `onOpenChange`, `title`, `detents` and `className` come from
[Sheet](./sheet.mdx) unchanged** and are documented in its table rather than repeated in this
one. That is [ADR 0021](../project/decisions/0021-modal-surfaces-own-their-open-state.mdx):
this component's specification said it "extends Sheet" and declared neither `open` nor
`onOpenChange`, which as written left it unopenable. It inherits both rather than minting a
third copy of the same pair.

Three of Sheet's props are omitted rather than inherited, and each omission is a promise.
`footer` is not the caller's to supply, because the save action is this component's, which is
what makes "no autosave and no implicit commit" checkable. `modal` is omitted because the
sheet *is* a modal dialogue, and `modal={false}` would leave the page behind focusable
underneath a half-typed record. `dismissible` is the third. The discard question depends on
the ambient close routes reaching this component, and `dismissible={false}` makes Sheet cancel
all three before they arrive, so the sheet would refuse to close and never say why.

`LogEntry` is exported from the same file. It does not appear in the table above, which is
generated only from interfaces whose name ends in `Props`.

```tsx
export interface LogEntry {
  values: Record<string, number | string | null>
  occurredAt: string
  recordedAt: string
  backdated: boolean
  provenance: "self-reported"
  note?: string
}
```

`values` is the whole answer to the question this component's specification could not answer.
`children` is an opaque element tree and `onSave` has to return structured data, and there is
no way to get from one to the other without a mechanism. Three were available. A **registration
context** was rejected because it needs every control to opt in, and that includes the ones
the product wrote itself, which is most of them. A collector that silently returns an
incomplete record is worse than none. A **`<form>` and `FormData`** was rejected because
`FormData` returns `""` for a control the reader emptied and `""` for one they never touched,
and returns strings for everything: `null` is a first-class absence here, distinct from `0`
and distinct from *not typed yet*, and a channel that cannot carry the distinction turns "no
answer" into "zero" somewhere downstream in somebody's record. A **controlled `values` prop**
was chosen, because the product already holds this state to render its own controlled inputs.
Nothing is duplicated, and the key is simply written down beside the control. `children` stays
genuinely opaque: this component never walks it, never counts it and never reads a value out
of it.

So the division is: the product owns every field it rendered, and this component owns exactly
the two it renders itself, plus the four facts about the record that only it is in a position
to state. Nothing is added to `values`, nothing is dropped from it for being empty, and
nothing in it is coerced.

`onDiscard` takes an argument the original signature did not. The specification said "the
product decides whether to keep a draft" and then handed the product nothing to keep; widening
the parameter list is the smallest repair, and it is the same widening Sheet made to
`onOpenChange` for the same reason. A zero-argument handler is still assignable, so
`onDiscard={clearForm}` typechecks unchanged.

`onSave` does not close the sheet, and neither does anything else in this component. `open`
belongs to the product, which is the only party that knows whether the save reached anywhere.
A queued entry, a rejected one and a stored one all arrive here identically, and a sheet that
closed itself would have decided the reader was finished on the strength of a function call
returning. Both examples close it themselves, in one line. A save does reset what "unsaved" is
measured against, so a product that keeps the sheet open to retry or to log a second entry is
not afterwards told that it holds input nobody has saved.

There is no `fields` prop, no `values` schema, no `saveOnEnter`, and no way to make the save
action anything other than the last thing in the tab order.

## Cost

No bundle size has been measured for this component. A real figure comes from a production build,
none has been taken, and inventing one is forbidden, which is exactly what this section exists to
prevent. Bundle cost is a stable-phase gate rather than a beta one, so the figure below stays
absent until a build produces it. What can be said without a measurement is the shape of the
cost: LogSheet carries `"use client"`, so importing it forces a client boundary on the page that
renders it, and it composes [Sheet](./sheet.mdx), [Field](./field.mdx) and [Button](./button.mdx)
while importing no npm package of its own, so a project that already installs those three adds the
capture contract and little else, though how much that is stays unmeasured until a build reports
it.
