Daily logging fails on friction. Every second between the intention to record something and
the record existing loses entries, and lost entries make every trend downstream less true.
LogSheet exists to make that path short, and its specification is mostly about resisting the
pressure to put one more field on it.

The first exclusion is the one that decides whether a product's logging works. A sheet with
seven fields is a form, and a form belongs on a page where it can breathe, be saved as a
draft, and be abandoned without losing the reader's place. Sheets are for the entries people
make every day; forms are for the ones they make once.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** That the reader recorded this value, at this time, themselves. That is
a weaker and more specific claim than a device measurement, and the record must preserve the
difference: a self-reported blood pressure and one read from a cuff over Bluetooth are not
interchangeable downstream. See
[Data provenance and device accuracy](../health/data-provenance-and-device-accuracy.mdx).

**What it must never be read as.** A clinical observation, or an assessment of anything.
Logging is recording. The sheet does not evaluate what is entered, does not colour it by
status, and does not tell the reader whether their number is good — that is a job for the
surface that displays the value afterwards, if the product has grounds for it.

**Which colour axis it may use.** The **category** axis, on the sheet's heading and icon, so
a reader knows whether they are logging sleep or blood pressure. The **status** axis may not
appear on a capture surface at all. Colouring a field amber while somebody is typing their
own blood pressure into it is an implied verdict delivered mid-keystroke, and it is the
fastest way to teach a person to stop logging honestly.

**Plausibility is not judgement.** The sheet may warn that a value looks unlikely — a
digit-transposition, a unit confusion, a decimal in the wrong place — because those are data
quality problems. It may never block the entry: unlikely values happen, and a log that
refuses them is a log with a hole in it exactly where the interesting data was. See
[ReadingInput](./reading-input.mdx) for the plausibility contract in detail.

**Timing is part of the record.** Every entry carries the time it refers to, not the time it
was typed, and the two are stored separately. A reading logged at 9pm for 8am that morning is
a backdated entry and downstream surfaces need to know that.

## Anatomy

<Anatomy name="log-sheet" />

<CompositionTree name="log-sheet" />

**Header** names what is being logged and offers the escape. **Fields** are the entry
controls, ordered so the most important one is focused first. **Time** is the when, defaulted
to now and always editable. **Note** is an optional free-text line, which people use more
than product teams expect. **Save** is one primary action; **Cancel** is available at every
moment and never discards silently.

Proposed part hierarchy. This is the specification the implementation must match.

```text
LogSheet                       root; extends Sheet; keyboard-safe insets
├── LogSheet.Header
│   ├── LogSheet.Title         what is being logged; category-tinted icon
│   └── LogSheet.Cancel        always present; never discards without asking
├── LogSheet.Fields            at most ~5; the first is focused on open
│   ├── ReadingInput           a numeric measurement with its unit
│   └── Field                  any other single-value control
├── LogSheet.Time              defaults to now; backdating is explicit and marked
├── LogSheet.Note              optional free text
└── LogSheet.Save              one primary action; states what it saves
```

Nesting a second `LogSheet` inside a `LogSheet`, or putting a navigation control inside one,
is a composition error: a capture sheet has one exit and one destination.

```ts
// PROPOSED. Not implemented. A specification for review, not an API to generate
// code against. It will change without a deprecation cycle.

import type { HealthCategory } from "opsinjs"

export interface LogEntry {
  /** What the entry refers to, keyed by the product. */
  values: Record<string, number | string | null>
  /** The time the entry is ABOUT. Defaults to now, always editable. */
  occurredAt: string
  /** The time the entry was RECORDED. Set by the component, never editable. */
  recordedAt: string
  /** True when occurredAt was moved away from recordedAt by the reader. */
  backdated: boolean
  /** Always "self-reported" from this component. Never inferred as a device reading. */
  provenance: "self-reported"
  note?: string
}

export interface LogSheetProps {
  title: string
  category?: HealthCategory
  /** Rendered in order. The first is focused on open. Keep it under five. */
  children: React.ReactNode
  /** How far back an entry may be dated, in days. The product owns this. */
  maxBackdateDays?: number
  /** Called only on explicit save. There is no autosave and no implicit commit. */
  onSave: (entry: LogEntry) => void
  /**
   * Called when the reader dismisses with unsaved input. The component asks
   * before discarding; the product decides whether to keep a draft.
   */
  onDiscard?: () => void
}
```

## Content guidelines

The title is what is being logged, as a noun phrase: *Blood pressure*, *Morning weight*, *How
you slept*. The save action says what it saves — *Save reading*, not *Done*, and never *OK*.

Do not ask for anything you can infer, and do not ask for anything you will not use. Every
field is a small tax paid every single day, and the fourth field is the one that stops people
logging at all.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"Blood pressure — Systolic, Diastolic, Time (now), Save reading."** Three inputs, a
    sensible default, and a save button that says what it does.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **"Log entry — Systolic, Diastolic, Pulse, Arm used, Position, Cuff size, Mood, Notes,
    Submit."** Every field is defensible and the set is unusable. Ask for the extras once, in
    settings, not every morning.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"You have not saved this reading. Discard it?"** on dismissal with unsaved input.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **Closing the sheet on a background tap and losing what was typed.** The reader tried to
    do the right thing and the product punished them for a stray gesture.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

## Accessibility requirements

Requirements for the implementation, not results.

- **The sheet is a modal dialogue** named by its title, with focus moved to the first field
  on open, trapped while open, and returned to the trigger on close.
- **The keyboard never covers the save action.** The sheet resizes to the visual viewport and
  respects safe-area insets; the primary action stays reachable at every detent — see
  [Layout](../foundations/layout.mdx).
- **Every field has a visible, persistent label**, never a placeholder standing in for one.
  Placeholder-as-label disappears exactly when the reader needs it and is invisible to some
  assistive technology — see [Field](./field.mdx).
- **Numeric fields declare their input mode and autocomplete** so the right keyboard appears
  and the reader does not have to hunt for a decimal point.
- **Plausibility warnings are advisory, associated with their field, announced politely, and
  never block save.** They are announced once, not on every keystroke.
- **Dismissal with unsaved input asks first**, by keyboard as well as by gesture. Escape is
  the keyboard equivalent of the background tap and behaves identically.
- **Every gesture has a control.** Drag-to-dismiss and drag-between-detents each have a
  button equivalent — see [Target size and motor](../accessibility/target-size-and-motor.mdx).
- **At 200% text the sheet scrolls internally**, the save action stays pinned, and no field
  label truncates.
- **Reduced motion** removes the slide; the sheet appears at its detent.
