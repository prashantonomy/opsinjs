Skeletons are a layout tool that products routinely turn into a truth claim. A grey rounded
rectangle where a blood pressure reading will be tells the reader that a reading exists, and
sometimes one does not. Everything unusual about this component follows from refusing that:
the shape vocabulary has four members and none of them is a measurement, the root says
nothing to a screen reader, and the appearance delay is CSS rather than a timer so that the
placeholder is there before the JavaScript is.

## Preview

The four shapes, which are the whole vocabulary. What is worth looking at is the absence:
there is no shape at the proportions of a reading and no prop that will produce one. Turn
the text size up in the preview toolbar and the placeholders grow with it. A skeleton sized
in pixels reserves the space the content took at 100% and hands the reader a jump at 200%.

## When to use it

<WhenToUse
  use={[
    "Content whose size and position are known in advance, arriving within roughly a second, where the alternative is the layout jumping when it lands.",
    "Lists and grids where a known number of items is loading and the reader benefits from seeing the shape of what is coming.",
    "Any surface where a spinner would replace a whole screen with nothing.",
  ]}
  avoid={[
    { case: "There is nothing coming. The surface is genuinely empty.", instead: "empty-state" },
    { case: "Loading did not complete.", instead: "alert-banner" },
    { case: "The wait is long enough that the reader needs to know it is a long wait.", instead: "callout" },
    { case: "The shape and size of what is coming are unknown, so the skeleton would guess.", instead: "spinner" },
    { case: "A single control is busy after the reader pressed it.", instead: "button" },
  ]}
/>

The last exclusion matters more than it looks. A button that has been pressed should show
its own busy state in place, keeping its label and its size; replacing it with a skeleton
loses the reader's place and the button's meaning at once.

The first exclusion is the one that costs most, and it is a decision made before the request
returns rather than after. If a reading may not exist, the value's seat must be left empty
while the request is in flight, because a placeholder there is a promise the data may not be
able to keep. The second example below shows that case, and [EmptyState](./empty-state.mdx)
is the surface that catches it.

**Block** is one placeholder shape. **Group** arranges several to match a known layout.
**Shimmer** is the optional movement, and it is a preference rather than a part: it has no
element of its own, because a shimmer that could be addressed separately is a shimmer that
could be given a second job. The component is deliberately tiny. Everything that makes a
skeleton good is a decision about what shapes to use and for how long, and neither is
something a component can decide.

The parts are identified by `data-slot` and are styleable from outside; none of them is an
export. There is no `Skeleton.Block` to render on its own, which is what stops a caller
assembling a value-shaped placeholder out of parts the shape vocabulary refuses to give
them directly. Sizing goes on the root through `className`, and that is expected rather
than exceptional: the component owns the vocabulary of shapes and the caller owns the
knowledge of what is coming.

## Examples

### A list that is still arriving

Three rows, nine skeleton roots, twelve placeholder blocks, and one announcement. Every
skeleton root is `aria-hidden`, so the count of blocks is invisible to a screen reader; the
single polite message sits on the region and is mounted by the caller, because a component
that announces on the caller's behalf announces once per instance.

Look at where that message sits. It is a sibling of the busy list rather than a child of it,
because `aria-busy="true"` tells assistive technology to hold off on everything beneath it.
That includes a live region. A `role="status"` inside the element that is busy is the one
message the reader does not get. What this static example cannot show is the announcement
itself: a live region reports mutations, and here the region and its text arrive together in
the first paint, so a screen reader reads the message in reading order rather than speaking
it on arrival. See the unmet list under Accessibility.

Every skeleton in it also carries `appearAfterMs`, which is the half of the pattern that
gets left out. A list that comes back from a warm cache in eighty milliseconds should never
have shown a placeholder at all, and the threshold sits at the call site because it is a
fact about a particular request rather than about placeholders in general.

### A row that may turn out to be empty

The label gets a placeholder because every row has a label. The value's seat gets height and
no shape, because whether there is a reading is the question the request was sent to answer.
The panel beside it is one of the answers, and it is the one that catches products out.

This one is a side-by-side of two moments rather than a loading surface, so it deliberately
mounts no `role="status"` and no `aria-busy`: a screen reader meeting it hears the two
captions and nothing else. That is a limit of the demonstration and not a second pattern. A
real implementation of the left-hand panel still owes its region the one polite message the
example above shows.

## States

Skeleton has no interaction states of its own. The root carries no click handler, no
`tabIndex` and no focus ring, and Accessibility below explains why: a placeholder a reader
could tab to or press would put a stop in their path that vanishes the moment the real
content lands, which is worse than no stop at all. There is no hover, focus, pressed or
disabled treatment to describe here because none exists, by design rather than by omission.

The five canonical data states describe a value or a list a component is standing in for,
and Skeleton is unusual among the catalogue in that it renders for exactly one of them and
declines the rest on purpose.

| Data state | What Skeleton renders |
| --- | --- |
| Error | Nothing. Skeleton has no error rendering of its own. Once a request fails, the surface that composed it removes the skeleton and shows the component that owns the failure, such as [Callout](./callout.mdx). |
| Loading | The whole of what this component exists for. One of the four shapes, text, line, block or circle, stands in for the footprint of content that has not arrived, hidden from assistive technology, and, when `appearAfterMs` is set, absent until the delay has passed so a fast response never shows one at all. |
| Empty | Nothing. When to use it, above, names this as the exclusion that costs most: if a reading may not exist, the honest loading treatment leaves the value's seat empty rather than shaped, and the surface resolves into [EmptyState](./empty-state.mdx) once the request answers. |
| Partial | Nothing that Skeleton itself decides. The component has no notion of a placeholder half filled in: each block is either drawn or it is not. A caller assembling a result that has arrived in parts shows the parts that have arrived and leaves Skeleton over the parts that have not. |
| Stale | Nothing on its own, though [Offline and stale data](../patterns/offline-and-stale-data.mdx) draws skeletons while a cached view is refreshed rather than freezing the last reading in place. That is a choice the composing surface makes about how to treat a refresh, not a state Skeleton represents or renders any differently for. |

## Content guidelines

A skeleton contains no content, which is the point. The guideline is about the surrounding
copy: once a wait passes the point where a skeleton is reassuring, replace it with words such
as *this is taking longer than usual*, because a skeleton that has sat there long enough reads
as a frozen app rather than as a wait. [Data states](../foundations/data-states.mdx) puts
that point at a few seconds and then says the number itself is a product decision; opsinjs
holds no figure of its own, and nobody here has measured one. The component does not do the
replacement for you and has no prop that would, and the replacement is a
[Callout](./callout.mdx).

Never place a skeleton where a value will not be. If a reading may not exist, the honest
loading state resolves into an [EmptyState](./empty-state.mdx), and the loading treatment
should not have promised otherwise.

  </DoDont.Dont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **No skeleton at all for a wait too short to be worth drawing**, using
    `appearAfterMs`. The number is yours: [Data states](../foundations/data-states.mdx) says
    a skeleton below roughly a tenth of a second is worse than nothing, and leaves the choice

  </DoDont.Do>

    eye, and it makes a fast product feel unstable.

## Motion

Skeleton carries two animations, and reduced motion treats them in opposite ways because they
mean different things. Neither shows a health value, so the rule that a measurement never
animates on first paint, with no count-up and no sweep, has nothing to act on here: there is
no figure to protect from movement, only a shape's arrival and a sheen crossing it.

The first is the appearance delay. When `appearAfterMs` is set, the root fades in from
`opacity: 0` over `--opsin-duration-fast` (140ms where the token is absent), eased with
`--opsin-ease-standard` (`cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)` where that token is absent). This is
timing rather than movement: a reader who asked for less motion did not ask for more
flashing, so the delay itself survives `prefers-reduced-motion` untouched and only the
fade's own duration collapses, to 1ms, in the component's own shipped stylesheet. That
collapse is not the easing token's declared fallback taking effect; `--opsin-ease-standard`'s
own reduced-motion value is `linear`, and the component does not switch to it, because a
1ms animation shows no discernible curve regardless of which one is named. What actually
does the work is `app/product.css` collapsing `--opsin-duration-fast` to the same 1ms below
its own `@import`, and the component repeats that collapse locally so the guarantee holds
for a project that installs this file without the rest of opsinjs and falls through to the
token's literal fallback instead.

The second is the shimmer, on by default, and the one animation a reader watches for the
whole wait. It is a gradient sheen sweeping across the fill on `--opsin-duration-shimmer`
(1600ms where the token is absent), eased with the same `--opsin-ease-standard`, and the
whole rule is declared inside a `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference` query rather than
behind a collapsed duration. Under reduce the rule never applies at all and the static tint
is there from the first frame, which lands on the same outcome as
`--opsin-duration-shimmer`'s own reduced-motion fallback of 0ms by a different route: the
token would collapse a duration to zero, and the component instead removes the animation
declaration entirely, and a sheen that never starts and a sheen with no length look
identical.

<MotionDemo easing="--opsin-ease-standard" duration="--opsin-duration-shimmer" />

The sweep is bounded on its own terms too, with no help from the preference:
`animation-iteration-count: 2` stops it at about 3.2 seconds of total movement, under the
five seconds past which WCAG 2.2 SC 2.2.2 would ask for a pause, stop or hide control that no
opsinjs component owns, and `animation-fill-mode: both` leaves the block on the static tint
once the second cycle ends, the same end state `shimmer={false}` gives from the first frame.

## Accessibility

Some of this is enforced on every commit and the rest is argued. The two lists are kept
apart on purpose: `beta` promises that accessibility is claimed only where it has been
checked, with the gaps named rather than left out.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y` on every commit:**

- **No status colour can reach a skeleton.** The static rig fails the build on any file that
  paints from the status axis without also reading `CLINICAL_STATUS_META` and a glyph. A
  skeleton cannot render either, because it is `aria-hidden` and carries no words. So the
  rule in [Uncertainty and staleness](../health/uncertainty-and-staleness.mdx) that a
  loading placeholder never shows a status colour is enforced here rather than trusted.
- **Every colour is a role that exists in the product theme.** `bg-placeholder-fill` and
  `border-placeholder-line` are among the surface roles `app/product.css` bridges. The gate fails on
  `bg-secondary`, `bg-accent` and the rest, which resolve to a colour in these docs and to
  nothing at all at `/view`.
  [Lyra and the docs chrome](../theming/lyra-and-the-docs-chrome.mdx) covers that difference.
- **No type size in pixels, anywhere in the file.** That is the whole of what the gate reads:
  A11Y003 matches an arbitrary `text-[…px]` and a `font-size` declared in px, and nothing
  else. It does not check that every length in the file is relative, and not every length is.
  The hairline is Tailwind's `border`, which is one device pixel, and the appearance keyframe
  carries a duration in milliseconds. What the gate does establish is that no reader's text
  multiplier is opted out of; whether the layout survives at 200% is a separate question and
  it is in the unchecked list below.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not measured:**

- **Skeletons are hidden from assistive technology.** The root carries `aria-hidden`, so a
  dashboard with eleven of them is silent. They convey nothing, and announcing eleven of
  them is worse than silence.
- **The component mounts no live region, ever.** One `role="status"` on the region, one
  message such as *loading your results*, and never one per skeleton. That the component
  refuses to mount it is a fact about the code rather than a claim about what a reader
  hears: a live region per instance is how a list of six placeholders becomes six
  interruptions. What is actually announced, and when, is in the unmet list below.
- **The shimmer is off under `prefers-reduced-motion`**, leaving the static tint behind. The
  shimmer rule lives inside a `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference` query in the component's
  own stylesheet, so the suppression is in the same cascade as the preference and holds on the
  server-rendered HTML rather than after hydration. Movement across a large area is a genuine
  trigger for some readers and it carries no information here. See
  [Reduced motion](../accessibility/reduced-motion.mdx).
- **The default shimmer is bounded, so SC 2.2.2 (Pause, Stop, Hide) does not engage.** The
  sweep runs two cycles at the shimmer duration token and then stops on the static tint, which
  is `animation-iteration-count: 2` at `var(--opsin-duration-shimmer, 1600ms)`: about 3.2
  seconds of movement, short of the five seconds past which the criterion turns on. There is no
  pause control and this bullet claims none; the point is that the motion ends on its own
  before the threshold, and `animation-fill-mode: both` leaves the block at the same static
  fill a caller gets by passing `shimmer={false}`. The movement is a gradient sweep across the
  fill rather than an opacity pulse, so the block never loses contrast mid-cycle.
- **The appearance delay is not motion and is not suppressed.** Under
  `prefers-reduced-motion` the fade collapses to about a millisecond and `appearAfterMs` is
  untouched, because a reader who asked for less movement did not ask for more flashing.
- **Nothing here is focusable or interactive**, so there is no target to measure and no
  keyboard behaviour to describe. That is the contract rather than an omission.
- **Print renders nothing.** The root is `print:hidden`. A printed page has no loading state,
  and a grey rectangle on paper is indistinguishable from a printer fault.
- **The loading placeholder paints a measured neutral pair, and its boundary clears the
  non-text floor against every ground but its own fill in the dark theme.** These figures
  come from the contrast pipeline that `pnpm run contrast` drives, not from `check:a11y`, so
  they are measured and published but they are not gated by `pnpm check`. The block fills
  from `--opsin-placeholder-fill` and draws its edge from `--opsin-placeholder-line`, an
  opaque pair added to the product theme for this shape, in place of the `--muted` over
  `--border` it used to paint, which sat far below the floor on the light-first page. The
  pipeline measures each of those two roles against the page, a card and the muted ground,
  and it measures the boundary against its own fill as `neutral.placeholder-line-on-fill`.
  The boundary role clears the 3:1 and Lc 45 non-text floor against the page, a card and the
  muted ground in both themes, because a bounded edge is what lets a loading block read as a
  shape rather than a solid rectangle. Against its own fill the boundary clears the 3:1
  non-text ratio in both themes, but in the dark theme it sits below the Lc 45 APCA floor.
  That one shortfall is stated rather than tuned away, and it is kept in the unmet
  list below: darkening the boundary to reach Lc 45 on its own fill would push it toward the
  solid rectangle the pale fill is chosen to avoid. The fill itself is published as advisory
  and stays deliberately below the floor: a fill dark enough to clear it would be a solid
  block sitting where a reading is about to land, which is the one thing this component
  exists to refuse. The measured figures for every pair render in the ContrastReport below
  rather than being repeated here.

**Not met, or not yet checked, and listed rather than omitted:**

- **The 200% and 1.3× layout claims have not been run.** Every length that decides the size
  of a placeholder here is an `em` or a `rem`, and that covers the bar heights, the block,
  the circle and the corner. The widths are percentages, and the two together are the
  argument for why the shapes should grow with the reader's text rather than hold the space
  it took at 100%. It is an argument. The rig that would settle it is the layout half of
  `check-a11y`, which needs Playwright and therefore runs nightly rather than on a commit,
  and nobody has run it against this component. See
  [Text resizing and zoom](../accessibility/text-resizing-and-zoom.mdx).
- **"The resolved content occupies the same space" is half the caller's, and the text shape's
  half now holds with no precondition on any current browser.** The half that stays the
  caller's is the one the bullet opens with: every shape other than text takes the height it
  is given, and the component has no way to know how tall the block or the circle should be.
  The text shape's half used to carry a precondition and no longer does. Each bar is still
  `1em` of the inherited font size, but the margin around it is derived from the inherited
  line box as `calc((1lh - 1em) / 2)`, so a bar plus its two margins is exactly one line of
  the surrounding text at whatever type step the container sets, with nothing for the caller
  to declare. A browser without the `lh` unit falls back to the body step's multiplier,
  `calc((var(--opsin-text-body-leading, 1.294) - 1) * 0.5em)`, which is exact at the body step
  and close at the others, the old approximation, and that fallback is the only reason this
  bullet is still in the unmet list rather than out of it. The escape hatch is honest again
  too: spacing set through `[data-slot="skeleton-block"]` now works, because the value moved
  from an inline style to a class, and an author rule beats a class on source order without
  `!important`.
- **The placeholder boundary sits below the APCA non-text floor against its own fill in the
  dark theme.** It clears the 3:1 non-text ratio there but falls below the Lc 45 APCA floor
  on its own fill. This is the one placeholder pair that does not clear both
  floors, and the met bullet above says why it is published as it stands rather than darkened:
  a boundary dark enough to reach Lc 45 on its own fill would read as the solid rectangle the
  pale fill exists to avoid. Whether a darker dark-theme line role is the better answer is a
  theme decision, not one this component makes.
- **`prefers-contrast` and `forced-colors` are not implemented anywhere in opsinjs**, so no
  increased-contrast claim can be made by this component or any other.
- **Nothing has been measured about what a screen reader announces, and neither example is the
  case that announces.** The doctrine is settled and the component holds to it: one
  `role="status"` on the region, one message, mounted by the caller. Two things about the
  demonstration are not settled. A static example renders its region with the message already
  inside it. A live region reports mutations, so a region built that way is read in reading
  order rather than spoken on arrival; the case that announces is a region that is already on
  the surface when the message is written into it. And a live region nested inside an element
  carrying `aria-busy="true"` is withheld for as long as that element stays busy, which is why
  the first example puts the message beside the busy list rather than in it. No screen-reader
  pass has been run against either example, by anyone, and no result is recorded anywhere.

Every part Skeleton draws carries a `data-slot`, which is what makes the parts styleable
from outside with no export of their own. Neither colour axis reaches this component: there
is no `data-status` and no `data-category` anywhere in the file, because a skeleton is
`aria-hidden` and carries no words, so a status colour or a category colour would have
nothing to attach its meaning to. There is no `data-opsinjs-value` either, for the same
reason the shape vocabulary has four members and none of them is a measurement: a skeleton
never renders a reading, rounded or otherwise, so it has no value to expose.

## CSS variables

<CssVariablesTable name="skeleton" />

Skeleton declares no custom property of its own. The one place a value is set directly on
the root outside its class list is the appearance delay, an inline `animationDelay` in
milliseconds applied only when `appearAfterMs` resolves to a number greater than zero, which
is a plain CSS property rather than a `--` custom property and carries no colour. Every colour
the component paints, the placeholder fill, the placeholder
line and the shimmer sheen, is a `var()` read straight from a theme-owned role with no
literal colour fallback, so an install without the opsinjs token sheet drops the gradient
and keeps the static tint rather than painting an unmeasured neutral. Those roles are listed
under Tokens rather than here, because they are token consumption and not a property this
component defines.

## Tokens

<TokenTable component="skeleton" />

These are the token roles Skeleton reads, resolved by `tokensUsedBy` and generated by
`scripts/build-tokens.mts` rather than listed here by hand, so the table cannot drift from
what the component actually consumes. They are colour, motion and type roles only: the

holding here in the strongest possible form: there is no clinical colour of either kind for
this component to mix.

## API reference

<PropsTable name="SkeletonProps" />

`Skeleton` is a **server component**. It has no state, no timer and no event handler:
`appearAfterMs` is an `animation-delay` with `animation-fill-mode: backwards` rather than a
`setTimeout`, which is what lets the placeholder hold the layout open before hydration and
with JavaScript switched off. The connections where a jump is worst are the connections
where the script has not arrived. The keyframe that makes that possible ships in the one
stylesheet this component carries. That sheet ships on every instance rather than only when
`appearAfterMs` is set, and React hoists it into the document head and de-duplicates it by
`href`, so a screen full of skeletons still carries it once. See
[Server and client components](../handbook/server-and-client-components.mdx).

## Cost

<BundleSize name="skeleton" />

No size has been measured, so none is shown, and inventing one is out of the question.
Bundle cost is read from a real build, it is a stable-phase gate rather than a beta one, and
none has been taken for this component. Skeleton being a server component with no client
runtime of its own does not excuse the figure: the shipped stylesheet, the shape map and the
line-count repair still ship as source and still have a real, unmeasured size, and a caller
composing many skeletons on one screen pays for the stylesheet once rather than once per
instance, which a bundle figure would need to say rather than assume.

- [Surface](./surface.mdx) is what skeletons frequently sit on, and that pairing is where
  reduced transparency and reduced motion interact.
- [Button](./button.mdx) owns its own busy state; never replace a pressed button with a
  skeleton.

**Used by**

- [Choose a component](../recipes/choose-a-component.mdx) draws the roster it explains, and
  reserves each specimen's space with a skeleton while the previews load.
- [Daily log screen](../screens/daily-log-screen.mdx) holds the day's entries behind
  skeletons until the log resolves, so the sheet does not jump as rows arrive.
- [Empty and first use](../patterns/empty-and-first-use.mdx) shows a skeleton first, then
  decides between the content and the empty state once the fetch returns.
- [Offline and stale data](../patterns/offline-and-stale-data.mdx) draws skeletons while a
  cached view is refreshed, rather than freezing the last reading in place.
- [Results screen](../screens/results-screen.mdx) reserves the result cards and range bars
  with skeletons while the results load.
- [Trends screen](../screens/trends-screen.mdx) holds the chart and tiles behind skeletons,
  none of them shaped like a number, until the series arrives.

**Governed by**

- [Data states](../foundations/data-states.mdx)
- [Reduced motion](../foundations/motion/reduced-motion.mdx)
- [Interaction states](../foundations/interaction-states.mdx)
- [Uncertainty and staleness](../health/uncertainty-and-staleness.mdx) has the rule the
  Accessibility section leans on: a loading placeholder shows no plausible number, no
  chart-shaped block and no status colour.
- [Motion in health UI](../health/motion-in-health-ui.mdx) governs the movement. The shimmer
  is the one thing here a reader watches for the whole wait, so its tempo runs on
  `--opsin-duration-shimmer` and stops after two cycles: it is tunable, it collapses under
  reduced motion with every other duration, and it is bounded rather than endless.
- [Empty and first use](../patterns/empty-and-first-use.mdx) and
  [Offline and stale data](../patterns/offline-and-stale-data.mdx) cover the two ways a
  loading state resolves into something other than the content it drew.

Those last four name `skeleton` in their own `implements`, and this page does not carry a
`governedBy` in its frontmatter, because a non-health component page does not. `check:ia`
reports that asymmetry as a `CAT003` warning here and on every other component in the same
position; it is accepted rather than answered, and the list above is the answer in prose.
