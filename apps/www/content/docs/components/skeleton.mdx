Skeletons are a layout tool that products routinely turn into a truth claim. A grey rounded
rectangle where a blood pressure reading will be tells the reader that a reading exists, and
sometimes one does not. Everything unusual about this component follows from refusing that:
the shape vocabulary has four members and none of them is a measurement, the root says
nothing to a screen reader, and the appearance delay is CSS rather than a timer so that the
placeholder is there before the JavaScript is.

## Preview

The four shapes, which are the whole vocabulary. What is worth looking at is the absence:
there is no shape at the proportions of a reading and no prop that will produce one. Turn
the text size up in the preview toolbar and the placeholders grow with it — a skeleton
sized in pixels reserves the space the content took at 100% and hands the reader a jump at
200%.

## When to use it

<WhenToUse
  use={[
    "Content whose size and position are known in advance, arriving within roughly a second, where the alternative is the layout jumping when it lands.",
    "Lists and grids where a known number of items is loading and the reader benefits from seeing the shape of what is coming.",
    "Any surface where a spinner would replace a whole screen with nothing.",
  ]}
  avoid={[
    { case: "There is nothing coming — the surface is genuinely empty.", instead: "empty-state" },
    { case: "Loading did not complete.", instead: "alert-banner" },
    { case: "The wait is long enough that the reader needs to know it is a long wait.", instead: "callout" },
    { case: "The shape and size of what is coming are unknown, so the skeleton would guess.", instead: "spinner" },
    { case: "A single control is busy after the reader pressed it.", instead: "button" },
  ]}
/>

The last exclusion matters more than it looks. A button that has been pressed should show
its own busy state in place, keeping its label and its size; replacing it with a skeleton
loses the reader's place and the button's meaning at once.

The first exclusion is the one that costs most, and it is a decision made before the request
returns rather than after. If a reading may not exist, the value's seat must be left empty
while the request is in flight, because a placeholder there is a promise the data may not be
able to keep — see the second example below, and
[EmptyState](./empty-state.mdx) for the surface that catches it.

**Block** is one placeholder shape. **Group** arranges several to match a known layout.
**Shimmer** is the optional movement, and it is a preference rather than a part: it has no
element of its own, because a shimmer that could be addressed separately is a shimmer that
could be given a second job. The component is deliberately tiny — everything that makes a
skeleton good is a decision about what shapes to use and for how long, and neither is
something a component can decide.

The parts are identified by `data-slot` and are styleable from outside; none of them is an
export. There is no `Skeleton.Block` to render on its own, which is what stops a caller
assembling a value-shaped placeholder out of parts the shape vocabulary refuses to give
them directly. Sizing goes on the root through `className`, and that is expected rather
than exceptional: the component owns the vocabulary of shapes and the caller owns the
knowledge of what is coming.

## Examples

### A list that is still arriving

Three rows, nine skeleton roots, twelve placeholder blocks, and one announcement. Every
skeleton root is `aria-hidden`, so the count of blocks is invisible to a screen reader; the
single polite message sits on the region and is mounted by the caller, because a component
that announces on the caller's behalf announces once per instance.

Look at where that message sits. It is a sibling of the busy list rather than a child of it,
because `aria-busy="true"` tells assistive technology to hold off on everything beneath it —
including a live region. A `role="status"` inside the element that is busy is the one message
the reader does not get. What this static example cannot show is the announcement itself: a
live region reports mutations, and here the region and its text arrive together in the first
paint, so a screen reader reads the message in reading order rather than speaking it on
arrival. See the unmet list under Accessibility.

Every skeleton in it also carries `appearAfterMs`, which is the half of the pattern that
gets left out. A list that comes back from a warm cache in eighty milliseconds should never
have shown a placeholder at all, and the threshold sits at the call site because it is a
fact about a particular request rather than about placeholders in general.

### A row that may turn out to be empty

The label gets a placeholder because every row has a label. The value's seat gets height and
no shape, because whether there is a reading is the question the request was sent to answer.
The panel beside it is one of the answers, and it is the one that catches products out.

This one is a side-by-side of two moments rather than a loading surface, so it deliberately
mounts no `role="status"` and no `aria-busy`: a screen reader meeting it hears the two
captions and nothing else. That is a limit of the demonstration and not a second pattern — a
real implementation of the left-hand panel still owes its region the one polite message the
example above shows.

## Content guidelines

A skeleton contains no content, which is the point. The guideline is about the surrounding
copy: once a wait passes the point where a skeleton is reassuring, replace it with words —
*this is taking longer than usual* — because a skeleton that has sat there long enough reads
as a frozen app rather than as a wait. [Data states](../foundations/data-states.mdx) puts
that point at a few seconds and then says the number itself is a product decision; opsinjs
holds no figure of its own, and nobody here has measured one. The component does not do the
replacement for you and has no prop that would, and the replacement is a
[Callout](./callout.mdx).

Never place a skeleton where a value will not be. If a reading may not exist, the honest
loading state resolves into an [EmptyState](./empty-state.mdx), and the loading treatment
should not have promised otherwise.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **Three text lines and a block where a result card is loading**, resolving into the card
    at the same size.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **A large rounded rectangle where the blood pressure number goes.** It says a number is
    coming. When the answer is *we have no reading for you*, the product has already implied
    otherwise.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **No skeleton at all for a wait too short to be worth drawing**, using
    `appearAfterMs`. The number is yours: [Data states](../foundations/data-states.mdx) says
    a skeleton below roughly a tenth of a second is worse than nothing, and leaves the choice
    above that to the request. The examples on this page pass 200 ms, which is one choice for
    one list and not a figure this system holds.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **A skeleton that flashes for one frame on every navigation.** It is noise, it draws the
    eye, and it makes a fast product feel unstable.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

## Accessibility

Some of this is enforced on every commit and the rest is argued. The two lists are kept
apart on purpose: `alpha` promises that accessibility is claimed only where it has been
checked, with the gaps named rather than left out.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y` on every commit:**

- **No status colour can reach a skeleton.** The static rig fails the build on any file that
  paints from the status axis without also reading `CLINICAL_STATUS_META` and a glyph — and
  a skeleton cannot render either, because it is `aria-hidden` and carries no words. So the
  rule in [Uncertainty and staleness](../health/uncertainty-and-staleness.mdx) that a
  loading placeholder never shows a status colour is enforced here rather than trusted.
- **Every colour is a role that exists in the product theme.** `bg-muted` and `border-border`
  are two of the eleven surface roles `app/product.css` bridges. The gate fails on
  `bg-secondary`, `bg-accent` and the rest, which resolve to a colour in these docs and to
  nothing at all at `/view` — see
  [Lyra and the docs chrome](../theming/lyra-and-the-docs-chrome.mdx).
- **No type size in pixels, anywhere in the file.** That is the whole of what the gate reads:
  A11Y003 matches an arbitrary `text-[…px]` and a `font-size` declared in px, and nothing
  else. It does not check that every length in the file is relative, and not every length is —
  the hairline is Tailwind's `border`, which is one device pixel, and the appearance keyframe
  carries a duration in milliseconds. What the gate does establish is that no reader's text
  multiplier is opted out of; whether the layout survives at 200% is a separate question and
  it is in the unchecked list below.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not measured:**

- **Skeletons are hidden from assistive technology.** The root carries `aria-hidden`, so a
  dashboard with eleven of them is silent. They convey nothing, and announcing eleven of
  them is worse than silence.
- **The component mounts no live region, ever.** One `role="status"` on the region, one
  message — *loading your results* — and never one per skeleton. That the component refuses to
  mount it is a fact about the code rather than a claim about what a reader hears: a live
  region per instance is how a list of six placeholders becomes six interruptions. What is
  actually announced, and when, is in the unmet list below.
- **The shimmer is off under `prefers-reduced-motion`**, leaving the static tint behind. It
  is suppressed by the `motion-safe:` variant, so the suppression is in the same cascade as
  the preference and holds on the server-rendered HTML rather than after hydration. Movement
  across a large area is a genuine trigger for some readers and it carries no information
  here — see [Reduced motion](../accessibility/reduced-motion.mdx).
- **The appearance delay is not motion and is not suppressed.** Under
  `prefers-reduced-motion` the fade collapses to about a millisecond and `appearAfterMs` is
  untouched, because a reader who asked for less movement did not ask for more flashing.
- **Nothing here is focusable or interactive**, so there is no target to measure and no
  keyboard behaviour to describe. That is the contract rather than an omission.
- **Print renders nothing.** The root is `print:hidden`. A printed page has no loading state,
  and a grey rectangle on paper is indistinguishable from a printer fault.

**Not met, or not yet checked, and listed rather than omitted:**

- **The non-text contrast expectation is not met, and cannot be with the palette as it
  stands.** The product theme bridges one neutral fill role and one neutral boundary role,
  and neither was chosen to sit at the non-text floor against the page or against a card in
  either theme. Using both together is the strongest pairing available and it is still short.
  The pair the block actually paints — `--muted` over a `--border` hairline — is in none of
  the measurements this repo runs, which is why `<ContrastReport>` below has no data for this
  component. The nearest pair that *is* measured is `neutral.hairline-on-page`, and it fails:
  APCA Lc 22.42 / WCAG 1.47 in light and Lc −8.21 / WCAG 1.90 in dark, against a non-text
  floor of Lc 45 and 3:1. That measurement is of `--opsin-neutral-300`, a darker line than the
  `--border` here, so it is a ceiling on what this component reaches rather than a stand-in
  for measuring it. The default setting works against it rather than for it: `shimmer` resolves
  to Tailwind's `pulse`, whose only keyframe is `50% { opacity: 0.5 }`, so for part of every
  cycle the block is composited further toward the surface behind it and the gap widens. Two
  things are owed here — the two roles need measuring in the token pipeline, and the pair needs
  to clear the floor. Neither is fixable in this file, and this is the claim to fix first.
- **The 200% and 1.3× layout claims have not been run.** Every length that decides the size
  of a placeholder here is an `em` or a `rem` — the bar heights, the block, the circle, the
  corner — and the widths are percentages, which is the argument for why the shapes should
  grow with the reader's text rather than hold the space it took at 100%. It is an argument.
  The rig that would settle it is the layout half of `check-a11y`, which needs Playwright and
  therefore runs nightly rather than on a commit, and nobody has run it against this
  component — see [Text resizing and zoom](../accessibility/text-resizing-and-zoom.mdx).
- **"The resolved content occupies the same space" is half the caller's, and the text shape's
  half has a precondition.** Each bar is `1em` of the font size the skeleton inherits, and the
  gap between bars comes from the body step's leading. Those are the same number only where
  the skeleton sits at the body step; anywhere else — including a container that sets no step
  at all, which is the default — the reservation is close rather than exact, and it is short by
  the difference between the two steps. Put the surrounding step on the skeleton, or set the
  spacing through `[data-slot="skeleton-block"]`, where it has to be exact. Every other shape
  takes the height it is given, and the component has no way to know what is coming. Where the
  two disagree the layout still shifts, and the fix is on the call site.
- **`prefers-contrast` and `forced-colors` are not implemented anywhere in opsinjs**, so no
  increased-contrast claim can be made by this component or any other.
- **Nothing has been measured about what a screen reader announces, and neither example is the
  case that announces.** The doctrine is settled and the component holds to it: one
  `role="status"` on the region, one message, mounted by the caller. Two things about the
  demonstration are not settled. A live region reports mutations, so a region that is rendered
  with its message already inside it — which is what a static example is — is read in reading
  order rather than spoken on arrival; the case that announces is a region that is already on
  the surface when the message is written into it. And a live region nested inside an element
  carrying `aria-busy="true"` is withheld for as long as that element stays busy, which is why
  the first example puts the message beside the busy list rather than in it. No screen-reader
  pass has been run against either example, by anyone, and no result is recorded anywhere.
- **WCAG 2.2 SC 2.2.2 (Pause, Stop, Hide) is not met by the default.** `shimmer` is on unless
  the caller turns it off, and it resolves to `pulse 2s … infinite`: it runs for as long as the
  skeleton is mounted. Past five seconds that is automatic movement, presented in parallel with
  other content, with no pause, stop or hide mechanism — and `prefers-reduced-motion` is an
  operating-system preference rather than the page-level mechanism the criterion asks for, so
  the `motion-safe:` suppression above answers a different requirement. The exception for
  essential motion does not apply, precisely because this page says the movement carries no
  information. Two mitigations exist and both are the caller's: pass `shimmer={false}`, or
  replace the skeleton with the words Content guidelines describes before the wait gets that
  long. Neither is enforced here — see [Reduced motion](../accessibility/reduced-motion.mdx),
  which is where the five-second rule is stated.

## API reference

<PropsTable name="SkeletonProps" />

`Skeleton` is a **server component**. It has no state, no timer and no event handler:
`appearAfterMs` is an `animation-delay` with `animation-fill-mode: backwards` rather than a
`setTimeout`, which is what lets the placeholder hold the layout open before hydration and
with JavaScript switched off — the connections where a jump is worst are the connections
where the script has not arrived. The keyframe that makes that possible is the one piece of
CSS this component ships, it is emitted only when `appearAfterMs` is set, and React hoists
and de-duplicates it. See
[Server and client components](../handbook/server-and-client-components.mdx).
