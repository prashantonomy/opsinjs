DoseTracker draws a record of medicine doses a product has already logged: a list of entries, each a medicine name, a time and one of four states, taken, missed, skipped or scheduled. It renders that log and nothing else. It computes no figure across the entries, it holds no schedule and no drug data of its own, and it reaches no verdict about any dose.

The restraint is the whole component, and it is the reason the roster declined a dose tracker for so long. Dose data drives dosing decisions, so a component that shows a missed dose sits one product decision away from implying what to do about it, and telling a person what to do about a missed medicine is prescribing. The answer here is that the component asserts only the log: it says a dose was missed, and it stops. What a reader should do next, and whether a missed dose matters at all, belong to the product and to a component built to carry advice, never to this one.

Check that each entry tells its state twice, in a word and in a distinct shape, so the four states stay four states in greyscale. The exclusion that matters most is the second, and it is the reason this component exists in the shape it does. The moment a dose log gains a line telling the reader what to do about a missed dose, it has crossed from recording a fact to giving advice, and advice about medicine is the product's to give in a [CareCard](./care-card.mdx) where the guidance and its author are explicit, never a side effect of a list. DoseTracker draws the miss and says nothing about it on purpose.

The first exclusion keeps the component from drifting into a general log. A dose event has a medicine name and a taken or missed state, which is a narrower shape than an arbitrary daily entry; a symptom note, a meal or a mood belongs in a [LogSheet](./log-sheet.mdx), which records entries the product defines rather than doses with a fixed set of states.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** DoseTracker asserts exactly the log the product handed it: these medicines, at these times, in these states. It asserts nothing about a schedule it does not hold, nothing about whether any dose matters, and nothing about a figure summed across the doses. A "Taken" marker says the product recorded that dose as taken; a "Missed" marker says the product recorded it as missed. Neither says anything further.

**What it must never be read as.** It is not advice, not a diagnosis, not an adherence score and not an instruction. A "Missed" row names a fact, never a fault, and never a next step: it does not say the reader did anything wrong, it does not say what to do now, and it does not imply that a clinician needs to be called. The component computes no adherence percentage, because a percentage is a summary the product may want to act on, and a presentation layer that produced one would be manufacturing a clinical figure nobody authored. The words banned everywhere in opsinjs are banned here too, and [Clinical interaction guidelines](../health/clinical-interaction-guidelines.mdx) is where the line between recording a fact and prescribing a response is drawn.

**Which colour axis it may use.** Neither, and that is deliberate. A dose state is a fact about a log entry, not a level of urgency, so a missed dose is not therefore an urgent one and no marker carries `data-status` or a status tint. The component names no kind of measurement either, so no marker carries `data-category` or a category tint. Every marker draws only neutral chrome and tells its state with a word and a distinct shape, so the state survives greyscale and colour-vision deficiency without ever borrowing a colour that means urgency. Painting a missed dose the red that means "act now" is precisely the axis mixing [The two colour axes](../health/two-colour-axes.mdx) exists to prevent, because a reader cannot tell that red from the red of a reading that needs attention.

**Who owns the thresholds and the words.** The consuming product, always. DoseTracker ships no schedule, no drug data, no dose, no threshold and no default text of its own. Every medicine name, every time and every state arrives as a prop, because the product owns the medication record those facts come from, and shipping any of it inside a component library would be shipping a clinical decision to every product that installed it. What the component supplies is the neutral layout, the four state shapes and the two shared colour axes read straight from the token layer, of which it uses neither.

## Content guidelines

Write each medicine name in the product's own words, the words a reader already knows their medicine by, and keep the note line short and factual: "with food", or who marked the dose, not a paragraph and never an instruction. A note that tells the reader what to do about a dose is advice wearing a note's clothes, and advice belongs in a [CareCard](./care-card.mdx) where its author is explicit.

Never let the log compute a verdict. Do not sum the taken rows into an adherence percentage and print it above the list, and do not tint a missed row to make it look urgent. A missed dose is a fact, and how much it matters is a clinical judgement the product owns and states in its own words, in a component built to carry it. The banned word list at [Reference ranges and normal](../health/reference-ranges.mdx) applies to every name and note here as it does everywhere.

## Accessibility

Some of this is enforced by a gate on every pull request and every push to `main`, and the rest is argued and labelled as such. At `alpha`, accessibility is claimed only where it has been checked, with the gaps listed rather than left out. Nothing here has been through a review with a disabled user.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y`:**

- **No status colour, no category colour, no colour literal.** Every marker draws only the neutral chrome roles, so no element carries `data-status` or `data-category`, and the two axes cannot be mixed on a row. The gate reads every element and would fail the build on a colour drawn from either axis or on a raw colour value, and there is neither here.
- **The state is never colour alone.** Each marker reads a word and a distinct lucide shape from a fixed table, so the four states are four words and four shapes rather than one shape in four colours. There is no colour on the markers at all, which is the strongest form of that rule.
- **No px type size and no shipped threshold.** The names, markers and times use the semantic type steps, so a reader who has turned their text size up gets a larger log, and no schedule, dose or threshold is written into the component or its examples.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **The words are in the accessibility tree; the shapes are not.** Each marker shape carries `aria-hidden`, so a screen reader meets the name, the state word and the time, and skips the decoration. A reader who cannot see the shapes loses them and keeps every word.
- **The list is not interactive.** Nothing takes focus, answers a key or a pointer, or animates. It is a static list, so there is no keyboard contract to honour and no motion for `prefers-reduced-motion` to reduce.
- **At 200% text the rows wrap rather than clip.** The markers grow with the type step, the times wrap to a second line at a narrow width, and no name or time is truncated and no ellipsis is set anywhere in the file.

**Not checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- Every contrast pair: each marker's hairline and its muted ink against the host ground. `scripts/check-contrast.mts` has not been run for this component, which is why the report below is empty rather than reassuring. The marker is a `-line` role with the muted ink, and this component sets no surface of its own, so every pair is painted on whatever ground the host provides rather than the matching `-surface` the token contract tunes against.
- How the list of names, states and times plays for a screen reader user, and whether the optional note is heard as belonging to its entry. It has been reasoned about and not listened to.
- What the markers draw under `forced-colors: active`, where the hairline is replaced by the platform's own pair. None of this has been put in front of a machine or a high contrast user.

## API reference

<PropsTable name="DoseTrackerProps" />

`entries` is the whole of the log: an array of doses, each a medicine name, an ISO time, one of the four states and an optional note. An empty array renders nothing and warns in development, because a tracker with no entries is a log of a day nobody recorded. A state outside the four renders its row with no marker and warns rather than being approximated into a shape, because a dose log records only the facts it knows and never invents a fifth.

`now` is required and forwarded straight to [RelativeTime](./relative-time.mdx), for the reason RelativeTime requires it of its own callers: a component that read the clock itself would be impure and would make one list of doses disagree with itself across a minute boundary. Read the clock once where the screen is rendered, with `new Date().toISOString()`, and pass the same instant here and to every other timestamp on the screen. Each entry's `time` and this `now` go to RelativeTime unmodified, so it owns what a future, malformed or stale instant renders as. `label` is the optional accessible name for the list, and naming the log, such as "Today's doses", is worth doing wherever the surrounding heading does not already say what the list is.
