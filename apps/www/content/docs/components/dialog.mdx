Dialog is the only component in the system that takes something away from the reader:
their place, their focus and their ability to do anything else. That makes *is this
really a dialog?* the first question on this page, and most of the time the honest
answer is no.

## Preview

An ordinary dialog: a question, the consequence of each answer, and two actions with
initial focus on the one that changes nothing. Open it and press Escape, then open it
and press Return without moving focus. Both should leave the reading where it was.

Narrow the preview below the `sm` breakpoint and the dialog floats inset from all four
edges with rounded corners, sitting low enough to stay within a thumb's reach rather
than centred. It is still a dialog while it does that: same role, same trap, same rules
about who may dismiss it.

The preview and the two examples below render the same controls: the trigger and both
actions are the product's own [Button](./button.mdx) rather than styled `<button>`
elements, so the type step, the target floor and the hover and pressed states are the
same across all three. The deletion action is where they part. The preview gives it the
`secondary` variant while the deletion example gives it `destructive`, and the boundary
ink is what tells those two apart.

The preview and the gallery tile both open on the trigger alone, not on the dialog. The
surface portals to the end of the document, so a version that opened by default would
blanket the whole page instead of sitting inside this frame. Press the trigger and the
dialog itself arrives.

Onboarding tours, feature announcements, rating prompts and newsletter offers are not
dialogues. They are interruptions with a modal costume, and in a health product they
are interruptions to somebody trying to find out about their own body.

**This component is rung 4 of the escalation ladder, and rung 4 is rationed.** The
ladder runs ambient pill, in-place card, screen-level banner, then this.
[Alert escalation](../patterns/alert-escalation.mdx) sets out the rungs and
[Alarm fatigue](../health/alarm-fatigue.mdx) holds the budget that meters them. The
budget is one line long and this component cannot enforce it: **at most one
interruptive modal per session that the reader did not start themselves.** A dialog
the reader opened by pressing *Delete this reading* does not spend it; a dialog the
product opened at them does, and it is the most expensive thing in the system to
spend. Nothing in this file counts, because the budget is held at the screen and at
the session and a component sees neither. That leaves the count to the product, and
the second unrequested modal in a session is a defect that will not show up in any
gate here.

**Dialog is not the emergency surface**, and a health doctrine page currently disagrees
with that sentence. [Emergency and escalation](../health/emergency-and-escalation.mdx)
lists `dialog` among the components it is implemented by, and then requires of an
emergency surface: one primary route and no competing choices, no auto-dismiss, the
contrast floor met with no material translucency, and a state that survives a relaunch
until it is explicitly acknowledged. This component is shaped for two answers, sits on
a rung that is still translucent, its tint now near opaque but with no way for a caller
to make it fully opaque, and remembers nothing between mounts. Use it for a decision, not for an emergency; the disagreement is on the
other page's `implements` list and is recorded here rather than resolved here.

The exclusion that costs most is [Sheet](./sheet.mdx), and the test that separates them
is not the screen size. A sheet is a place the reader chose to go and may leave; a
dialog is a question they did not ask for and must answer. This component will not
turn into the other one at any width, so choosing wrongly is a decision that stays
wrong on every device rather than one that quietly repairs itself on a phone.

At 390 the two now read apart on sight as well as in obligation. The dialog floats inset
from all four edges with rounded corners and shows a visible Close beside its glyph,
while the sheet docks to an edge, spans the full width there and carries a drag handle
the reader may pull to dismiss. The silhouette follows the obligation: the surface a
reader must answer no longer wears the shape of the one they may leave, and a reader who
learns the inset panel means a question keeps that reading on the phone where they most
need it. The test between them is still obligation rather than size.

The parts are `data-slot` attributes and not exports. There is no `Dialog.Actions` to
import and no way to render a container without a title, which is what makes "the
dialog is always named" a fact about the DOM rather than a request in prose. Base UI
adds `data-open`, `data-starting-style` and `data-ending-style` to the scrim, the
viewport and the container; those pass through untouched. Nothing here carries
`data-status`, `data-category` or `data-opsinjs-value`, because a dialog renders no
measurement and asserts no clinical level.

**The tree is the parts, not every element.** Two groups sit in the rendered DOM and
in no row above, and a selector written from the tree alone will miss both. Base UI's
portal renders its own pointer-blocking `div` as the first child of the portal, a
sibling of the scrim and the viewport. It carries `role="presentation"`, has no
`data-slot`, and is fixed to the viewport. It is also what actually stops a press
reaching the page; the scrim only shows that. And `Dialog.Container` holds a Surface,
so the header, the body and the actions are its **grandchildren**: the chain is
`dialog-container` → `surface` → `surface-content` → the three regions, with
`surface-backdrop`, `surface-scrim` and `surface-edge` beside them and `aria-hidden`
on all three. So `[data-slot=dialog-container] > [data-slot=dialog-header]` matches
nothing; the descendant form matches. Neither group is announced, so nothing here
changes what a reader hears.

Two parts are not in the specification's tree and are here on purpose. **Viewport** is
the fixed box the container is positioned inside; having a real element to place
against is what lets the dialog float inset and low on a phone and sit centred on a
laptop with no transform, no measurement and no JavaScript. **Header** and **Body**
split what the flat tree could not: the accessibility requirement below asks for the
dialog to scroll internally *with its actions pinned*, and a single flat region cannot
have one part that scrolls and two that do not.

The container is a Surface at the **`sheet`** rung, not the `overlay` rung the
specification names. The six rung names became the token names in
[ADR 0014](../project/decisions/0014-material-rung-names.mdx), and the vocabulary the
specification was written in used `overlay` for the rung that covers the page while
leaving it recognisable. That rung is now `sheet`. This ladder's `overlay` is chrome
that content scrolls beneath, such as a pinned toolbar or a tab bar. Translating the
old name by spelling rather than by job would therefore put a modal dialog on the rung
built for a tab bar, and it would compile. See
[The material ladder](../foundations/materials/the-ladder.mdx).

A dialog opening from within a sheet is a composition error. If the sheet needs a
confirmation, the confirmation belongs inside the sheet. Nesting rule N3 in
[Choosing a layer](../foundations/materials/choosing-a-layer.mdx) says the same thing:
at most one covering surface at a time.

## Examples

### Confirming something that cannot be undone

The title asks the question, the description says what each answer does, and the
action that changes something comes **first** so that the safest one is last. The
last position is where `initialFocus="safest"` lands. A stray Return keeps the reading.

### An answer is needed

`severity="alert"` closes four exits at once: the role becomes `alertdialog`, the
close control is absent, the scrim does not dismiss, and Escape does not close.
Pressing Escape moves focus to the dialog itself. Because the container is named by the
title and described by the description, a screen reader reads the question and its
consequence again, so the reader hears the question rather than nothing, and it does
this whether or not focus had already moved off the safest action. What it still does
not do is speak the reason the key was refused, because the title is the question and
not the reason. That is the residual gap listed under Accessibility, not a subtlety of
this example.

## States

Dialog's own interaction states are open and closed, which Base UI tracks and this
component consumes through `data-starting-style` and `data-ending-style` on the scrim
and the container. The transition those two states drive is documented in full under
Motion, immediately below. Hover, focus and pressed happen on the parts a caller
supplies, not on anything Dialog draws itself: the close control built here shows a
muted background on hover and the platform's focus ring on focus-visible, and every
control inside `actions` carries whichever states [Button](./button.mdx) gives it.
There is no disabled state on the dialog itself. `severity="alert"` removes the close
control outright rather than disabling it, and a control inside `actions` or `children`
that a caller marks disabled is that control's own state rather than the dialog's.

The five canonical data states mostly describe a surface that holds a reading, and
Dialog holds none. Each row says what this component actually does, rather than what a
component in this family typically does.

| Data state | What Dialog renders |
| --- | --- |
| Error | Nothing of its own. Dialog runs no validation and fetches nothing; its own defects, an alert dialog with no `actions`, or `actions` with nothing focusable inside it, surface as development-only console warnings rather than as anything a reader sees. What the reader sees on a genuine error is whatever `children` renders. |
| Loading | Nothing. `open` arrives already resolved from the caller, so there is no request in flight for this component to reflect, and it renders no skeleton or spinner in its place. A dialog that opens to show the outcome of a pending action carries that state through `children`. |
| Empty | Not a state Dialog tracks as data. `title` is required and always renders; a confirmation with no `description` and no `children` is a legitimate minimal shape on an ordinary dialog. On an alert dialog the same absence is instead a gap in the required copy, and the component answers it with a development warning under Accessibility rather than with a rendered empty state. |
| Partial | Not applicable. Whatever partial value a caller passes travels inside `children`, unread and untouched by this component. |
| Stale | Not applicable. Dialog renders no measurement and keeps no notion of when its contents were last true; a reading's age is [RelativeTime](./relative-time.mdx)'s job, rendered inside `children` if a caller puts one there. |

## Content guidelines

The title asks the question. The description says what happens if the reader says yes,
and what happens if they say no. Action labels are verbs that name the outcome, never
*OK* and *Cancel*, which force the reader back up to the title to work out what they
are agreeing to.

Never phrase the dismissing action as a judgement about the reader. *No thanks, I do
not care about my health* is a real pattern in real products and it is contemptuous.

On an alert dialog the description has one extra job, and this component will not do
it for you: it is where the reader is told that an answer is needed and what each
answer costs. Escape will not let them out, there is no close control, and opsinjs
does not know what your two answers mean, so it ships no sentence about them. An alert
dialog with no `description` raises a development warning for exactly this reason, and
a blank one counts as none. A reader cannot tell an empty string from an absent prop,
so neither can the warning.

The warning about `actions` works the same way and looks at the rendered DOM rather than
at the prop, because `null`, `false`, `""` and an empty fragment are all values that are
not `undefined` and all render an actions row with nothing in it. On an alert dialog
that is a surface with no close control, a refused Escape key and nothing to press.

## Motion

Dialog is the second surface in this family, after [Sheet](./sheet.mdx), whose arrival
and departure are more than an opacity swap. The container takes
`--opsin-ease-spring-sheet` on `--opsin-duration-spring-sheet`, an overdamped spring at
483ms that `tokens/motion.json` reserves for large surfaces travelling a long distance:
sheets, dialogs, full-screen pushes, chosen so the panel settles at its resting position
rather than passing it and springing back. Opacity, translate and scale all ride that
same clock together, because splitting a spring's own curve across two durations
produces a different spring rather than a faster one. The exit swaps to
`--opsin-ease-exit` on the shorter `--opsin-duration-base`, 220ms, because something
leaving accelerates and does not ask the reader to watch it finish. The scrim behind the
panel fades on its own pair, `--opsin-ease-standard` and the same
`--opsin-duration-base`, and under reduced motion its transition is removed outright
rather than substituted, so it snaps to full opacity instead of crossfading.

Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` the container keeps only its opacity transition;
the translate that carries the phone's rise from the bottom and the scale that carries
the wider screen's grow-in are pinned to their resting values, so nothing moves but the
fade. That fallback is the token's own, not this file's invention: `tokens/motion.json`
states `spring-sheet`'s `reducedMotion` entry as an easing of `linear` and a duration of
120ms, with the note that this is a cross-fade in place because a sheet that appears
with no travel still needs to be seen to appear, and `app/tokens.generated.css` is what
substitutes those values for `--opsin-ease-spring-sheet` and
`--opsin-duration-spring-sheet` under the query. A panel that popped between frames
under reduced motion would trade a vestibular problem for a comprehension one, which is
the trade [Motion in health UI](../health/motion-in-health-ui.mdx) argues against for
this exact family of surfaces.

<MotionCurve token="--opsin-ease-spring-sheet" />

Depth is carried by the scrim and by the surface's edge rather than by the entrance, and
no prop removes either. `children` is where a caller's own content rides this same
entrance, and that content may hold a health value: the container animates opacity and
offset, never a number inside it, so nothing here can put a reading through a count-up
or a sweep, which [Motion in health UI](../health/motion-in-health-ui.mdx) rules out for
this surface family. Whether that reasoning holds once somebody has actually watched a
value arrive this way is still open, and it stays listed as unmeasured under
Accessibility rather than as settled here.

## Accessibility

Some of this is checked by a gate in continuous integration. Most of it is argued, and
the things nobody has measured are listed rather than left out. That separation is
what `beta` promises.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y` on every pull request and every push to `main`:**
(There is no commit hook in this repository, so a local commit is checked by nobody.)

- **Every colour here is a role token.** The static rig fails the build on a hex
  literal, an `oklch()` call, a Tailwind palette colour or a numbered ramp step
  anywhere under `registry/`, so the scrim, the surface and the close control all
  follow dark mode and increased contrast rather than pinning a value.
- **No class that resolves only in the docs chrome.** `bg-popover`, `bg-accent` and
  `rounded-2xl` are the roles a shadcn dialog reaches for. They are declared in
  `app/globals.css` and produce no CSS at all under the product theme. The gate fails
  on them by name.
- **No type size in `px`.** The title and the description take steps from the
  eleven-step ramp, so they scale with the reader's own text size. See
  [Dynamic type](../foundations/typography/dynamic-type.mdx).
- **No banned word, in copy or in an identifier.**

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not measured:**

- **The role matches whether dismissal is valid.** `severity="alert"` swaps Base UI's
  alert-dialog root in rather than toggling props, so `role="alertdialog"`, the forced
  modality and the refusal to dismiss on an outside press arrive together and cannot
  be got three-quarters right by a later edit.
- **Named by its title and described by its description**, both associated
  programmatically by the primitive rather than by adjacency. An empty title raises a
  development warning; there is no prop that hides it. A blank `description` is treated
  as no description: the element is not rendered, so nothing is wired to
  `aria-describedby` and announced as empty, and on an alert dialog the missing-
  description warning fires for a blank string exactly as it does for an absent one.
- **Focus moves in on open, is trapped while open, and returns on close** to whatever
  had it when the dialog opened. The return also happens when the dialog closes
  because the product set `open` to false rather than because the reader acted. The
  component renders no trigger part, so the return target is the previously focused
  element rather than a control this component had to be told about.
- **Initial focus is never on the scrim or the container while a control exists.**
  `initialFocus="safest"` resolves to the *last* control in `actions`, which is where
  the ordering rule puts the answer that changes nothing; `content` resolves to the
  first control inside `children`, and falls through the safest action before it
  reaches the primitive's own behaviour. That middle link matters: an alert dialog has
  no close control, so the primitive's first-tabbable default would land on the *first*
  action, which the ordering rule reserves for the answer that changes something.
  Neither value ever resolves to a control outside the dialog.
- **The cost of `safest` is that it is the last tab stop.** The DOM runs close control,
  body, actions, so from where focus starts the first Tab wraps round to the
  close control and the other answer is behind a Shift + Tab. A
  reader moving forwards meets the exit before they meet the alternative. That is the
  trade the default makes, and it is a trade rather than a free win.
- **The actions row changes direction rather than wrapping.** Below the `sm` breakpoint
  the two answers stack and each fills the panel's content box, so neither is a narrow
  pill stranded in the middle of the screen and both are a thumb-width target; the
  ordering rule puts the safest answer last in the DOM, so stacking lands it at the
  bottom, nearest the thumb. From `sm` up the row returns to a right-aligned line. The
  trade is the one Button raises about two full-width controls: two boxes of equal width
  read as two primary actions unless something else separates them, and here the variant
  weights do the separating, because `primary` carries an opaque fill while `secondary`
  and `destructive` carry a boundary and no fill, and the ordering rule fixes which
  answer is which. A caller who passes two controls of the same variant gets a row that
  reads as two of the same answer, and this component does not check that.
- **Escape closes an ordinary dialog and does not close an alert one.** Base UI's alert
  dialog closes on Escape out of the box; this component cancels that close through the
  change event, and the refusal is complete in every configuration. The *response* to
  the key is to move focus to the popup element itself. Because that container carries
  the role, is named by the title and described by the description, landing focus on it
  makes a screen reader read the question and its consequence again, so the reader hears
  the question rather than silence. This happens in the default configuration too:
  `initialFocus="safest"` has put focus on the safest action, the popup is never the
  element that already holds focus, so the announcement happens rather than being the
  no-op that focusing the already focused control would be. What it does not do is speak
  the reason the key was refused. The title is the question and not the reason, so a
  product that must say why an answer is required writes that sentence into `description`,
  because the component mounts no live region on the caller's behalf and never will. See
  the gap list below.
- **The page behind is hidden and unreachable, and it is not `inert`.** While the dialog
  is open the primitive marks everything outside the popup `aria-hidden`, the scrim covers
  the viewport so a pointer never reaches the page, and Tab is held inside by the focus
  trap; the scrim only shows what those three do. None of them sets the `inert` attribute,
  and the difference is observable: focus moved programmatically from behind the dialog
  takes and keeps focus on a control the accessibility tree has just been told is not
  there. An autofocus in a background component and a late `ref.focus()` are two ways
  that happens. A press on the non-dismissing scrim was a third: because the background is
  `aria-hidden` and not `inert`, the browser would blur the focused control onto `<body>`,
  leaving the reader focused inside that hidden tree on the one surface they may not leave
  until they answer. The component now prevents the default on `pointerdown` so the press
  never takes focus, and on an alert dialog it puts focus back on the popup. The
  programmatic routes remain, so the honesty about `inert` still holds: closing that hole
  is a change to the component rather than to this page, and until it is made, build on
  this sentence rather than on the word *inert*.

  The scrim's own behaviour is separate. Under `prefers-reduced-transparency` it **loses
  its blur and becomes more opaque**: the token layer swaps the tint for the rung's opaque
  fallback and forces its alpha to 1, and that fallback is itself still slightly translucent
  (`--opsin-material-scrim-opaque` in `app/tokens.generated.css`), so the page behind
  stays recognisable rather than being replaced by a flat panel. The dimming is never
  reduced by the preference. Being able to see through it less is the point of it.
- **At 200% text the dialog scrolls internally.** The container stops at the height of
  the viewport, the body scrolls, and the header and the actions are pinned outside it,
  so the buttons never end up below the fold. Scroll chaining is contained. The title
  and the description wrap rather than clip, so a token with no break opportunity in it
  loses no characters. This is the CSS reasoning and not a report; the final bullet below
  says why no capture has ever confirmed it.
- **A reader who asked for less still sees the surface arrive, as a cross-fade.** The
  entrance takes `spring-sheet`, the overdamped curve on its own duration that
  [Choreography](../foundations/motion/choreography.mdx) reserves for sheets, dialogs and
  full-screen pushes, and the exit takes `ease-exit`, shorter because a thing leaving
  accelerates. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` the token layer collapses that spring
  duration to 120ms and its easing to linear, and the component pins the travel and the
  scale to their resting values while it keeps transitioning opacity, so the panel fades
  in rather than popping between frames. Two reasons hold that shape. `tokens/motion.json`
  makes it policy that an opacity change under 120ms survives the query, and removing the
  cue outright would trade a vestibular problem for a comprehension one, which
  [Motion in health UI](../health/motion-in-health-ui.mdx) is the page that argues against.
  Both durations, the spring entrance and the 120ms reduced-motion collapse, belong to
  the token layer and are lost with it. A consumer who installs the file without
  `app/tokens.generated.css` has no published duration for this entrance, because the
  duration is spelled as a token with no literal fallback, unlike the travel and the
  scale, so what a token-less install cross-fades at is unspecified rather than a figure
  this page can promise. Depth is carried by the scrim and the surface's edge, and no
  prop removes either.

**Not checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- **Contrast.** Title and description against the sheet rung's material, the close
  control's hover fill, and the focus ring against both the dialog and the scrim behind
  it, in both themes. The floor for this material is now measured against its tint
  composited over the worst-case backdrop, the darkest and the lightest a product can
  put behind it, rather than against the opaque fallback that flattered it, so the
  published figure below describes the panel a reader actually sees. What stays
  unmeasured for this component is the close control's hover fill and the focus ring
  where it crosses the scrim behind the panel.
- **The 44 by 44 target and the 8pt separation.** The close control asserts the 44px
  floor in rem, and after this run the actions row asserts a larger one on its answers
  too. The actions row sets its gap to twice
  the published **separation** floor, reading it as `--opsin-space-4` with a literal
  `1rem` fallback so it survives a `shadcn add` install without the token sheet, and it
  does so because the component's own ordering rule sets an irreversible answer beside a
  safe one rather than because the separation floor asks for it. That floor is
  `--opsin-target-separation`, and `tokens/space.json` scopes it to adjacent targets
  whose visible boxes are smaller than 44px, which these are not. The row now asserts a
  `comfortable` 48px floor on every direct child, read as `--opsin-target-comfortable`
  with a literal `3rem` fallback, because this is the one surface in the system where a
  mis-tap is irreversible and `tokens/space.json` names comfortable the default for a
  primary action. The floor is a minimum and not a height, so a taller control a caller
  passes still wins, and the component emits no development warning when an answer comes
  in undersized, so nothing tells a caller who nested a small control inside a larger
  child that the visible target is below the floor. A caller who passes a plain 24px
  button is caught twice under the stylesheet these previews render in: `app/product.css`
  floors every `button`, `[role="button"]`, `select`, `textarea` and non-hidden `input`
  at 44px through `min-block-size`, and the row then lifts that answer to 48px. A consumer
  who installs this file without `app/product.css` gets the row's 48px floor alone.
  Neither figure is verified by the nightly layout job, and the reason is structural
  rather than a wait for the job to run: `scripts/check-a11y.mts` measures only the
  elements inside `#opsin-view-root`, this component portals its panel to the end of the
  document and out of that root, and `scripts/capture-registry.mts` never opens the
  dialog, so that job can only ever measure the closed demo's trigger. See
  [Target size and motor](../accessibility/target-size-and-motor.mdx).

  What is still owed here is a measurement: the rendered target size of each
  answer, and the separation between the two, with the dialog open, at every
  density. That measurement waits on capture tooling that can open the dialog
  and on a hit-area pass that reaches the portalled panel outside
  `#opsin-view-root`, neither of which exists yet.

- **Escape on an alert dialog does not speak the reason it was refused, and the
  re-announcement is unverified.** The key is refused in every configuration, which is
  the part that works, and the response is now to move focus to the popup element itself.
  Because the popup carries the role, the name and the description, that focus move is
  expected to make a screen reader read the question and its consequence again, so a
  reader who presses Escape before moving anywhere hears the question rather than
  silence. Two things keep this on the list.
  [Alert escalation](../patterns/alert-escalation.mdx) asks that *"if the answer is
  genuinely required, Escape is disabled and the reason is announced rather than the key
  silently doing nothing."* The title is the question and not the reason, so a product
  that must state why an answer is required writes that sentence into `description`,
  because the component mounts no live region on the caller's behalf and never will. And
  the re-announcement itself is the behaviour every screen reader is expected to produce
  from a focus move onto a labelled, described container, which is not the same as a
  behaviour somebody in this repository has put a screen reader on and watched. It stays
  written down until somebody does.
- **The close control's accessible name defaults to English.** `closeLabel` exists so a
  product can translate it, in the same way `StatusPill.label` exists, and the default
  it ships is the word *Close*. A product whose readers do not read English and which
  does not pass the prop has an exit named in a language they cannot read. The word is
  visible beside the glyph, so this reaches a reader who sees it as much as one who hears
  it, and either way it is the difference between a named exit and an unlabelled button.
  There is no locale detection here and there will not be one; the gap is the default,
  and it is the caller who closes it.
- **A pinned header with a very long title.** The header does not scroll, so at 200%
  text a title that is a paragraph rather than a question would leave the body a sliver.
  The content guidelines say the title is a question; that has not been tested against a
  title that is not one. The title wraps rather than clips, so this is about how much
  room is left, not about characters going missing.
- **Whether a value inside `children` may ride the entrance.** The container animates
  opacity and offset, and `children` is where the props documentation says a card
  holding a health value belongs. Those two facts together mean a number can arrive on
  an animating surface. [Motion in health UI](../health/motion-in-health-ui.mdx) bans
  count-ups, dial sweeps and the progressive reveal of a result: a number that is at
  its final value on every frame, inside a container that fades in, is not any of
  those, and under reduced motion it does not move at all. That is the reading this
  page takes, and it is a reading rather than a ruling from the page that owns the rule.
- **`prefers-contrast: more` and `forced-colors`.** `forced-colors` is no longer
  unimplemented across opsinjs, and it is answered here through Surface, because both the
  scrim and the popup material are Surfaces. Surface draws its edge as an outline rather
  than a box-shadow or an inset ring, which forced colours keeps and recolours to the
  system ink, and it hides its backdrop with `forced-colors:hidden`. So under a Windows
  contrast theme both the scrim and the sheet-rung panel keep a visible boundary.
  `prefers-contrast: more` is implemented in [Surface](./surface.mdx#accessibility) alone
  rather than in the token layer, and under it the material drops to its opaque fallback
  and its edge widens to the emphasis width. Two gaps stay open, and the dialog papers
  over neither. The scrim's dimming is still lost, because a forced-colours palette
  replaces the tint with Canvas, so the scrim reads as a boundary rather than a veil. And
  nobody has opened this component under a real Windows contrast theme, so all of the
  above is reasoned from what Surface draws rather than read off a tested screen, and no
  increased-contrast claim can yet be made from measurement.
- **No capture has ever seen this component open.** The demo renders closed and the
  surface portals to the end of the document, outside the `#opsin-view-root` that the
  screenshot and layout tooling frames, so every image-derived claim on this page is a
  claim about a trigger button. Dark mode, 200 per cent text, forced colours and the
  eight anatomy slots are all in that set: the tooling has photographed the trigger and
  nothing behind it. This is why the reasoning above is reasoning rather than a reading
  off a screenshot, and it holds until the capture step learns to open the dialog first.

## API reference

<PropsTable name="DialogProps" />

**`sheetBelow` is not implemented and is not a prop.** The specification asked for the
dialog to render as a Sheet below a given width, and in the same API asked
`severity="alert"` to remove every way of leaving without answering. A Sheet is
draggable and dismissible by design, so the two together would have made an alert
dialog dismissible with a thumb on a phone and not on a laptop. It would have done that
silently, at whichever width the caller happened to pass. What the sentence under that
prop was complaining about is a complaint about shape, and the shape is here: below the
`sm` breakpoint the dialog floats inset from all four edges with rounded corners and
sits low within a thumb's reach, in CSS, with no prop and no change of component. The
role, the trap, the hidden background and the dismissal rules are identical at every
width.

**`actions` is a prop this component added.** The specification's part tree requires a
`Dialog.Actions` region, caps it at two and fixes their order, and its proposed API had
no way to fill one. A slot prop is how content arrives in this system where there is no
dot-notation export, and having the component own the region is also what makes the
actions pinnable, focusable by `initialFocus` and reachable by the Escape handler. The
cap of two is stated and not enforced: `ReactNode` does not say how many controls are
inside it, and `React.Children.count` cannot see through a fragment.

**`closeLabel` is a prop this component added**, and it exists for one word. The close
control's visible word is the only reader-facing string this file owns, and shipping
it as an untranslatable English literal would have meant that in a product whose readers
do not read English the exit from a modal surface has no name. `StatusPill.label` exists
for the same reason and this follows it. It does not solve the problem, it moves it to
somebody who can solve it: the default is still *Close*, and a caller who never passes
the prop is where they started. It is ignored on an alert dialog, which renders no close
control.

The `actions` cap of two is not the only rule stated rather than enforced. The row sets
a gap at twice the published target **separation** floor and floors every answer at the
`comfortable` 48px target **size**, but it cannot see whether the controls a caller puts
in it are ordered safest last, which is the rule this surface most depends on. `actions` is also a `ReactNode`, so `null`, `false`,
`""` and an empty fragment are all legal values that render a row with nothing in it.
On an alert dialog that is a surface with no exit whatever, which is why the development
warning for it looks at the rendered DOM through the same ref `initialFocus` uses rather
than at the prop.

**`children` is optional**, though the proposed API marked it required. The part tree
calls `Dialog.Content` optional in the same document, and most confirmations are a
question, a consequence and two verbs with nothing in between.

`onOpenChange` takes one argument. Base UI reports a reason alongside it; every reason
that matters here is handled inside the component, and the Escape refusal is the whole
of it. A second parameter that only sometimes matters is a second parameter people copy
without reading.

## Cost

No bundle size has been measured for this component. A real figure comes out of a
production build, none has been taken, and inventing one is forbidden, which is exactly
what this section exists to prevent; bundle cost is a stable-phase gate rather than a
beta one, so the figure below stays absent until a build produces it. What can be said
without a measurement is the shape of the cost: Dialog carries `"use client"`, so
importing it puts a client boundary on the page that renders it, and it composes
[Surface](./surface.mdx) and [Button](./button.mdx) while importing `@base-ui/react`'s
`Dialog` and `AlertDialog` primitives together with `lucide-react`'s close glyph. A
project that already installs Surface and Button for another component adds the dialog
contract and those two dependencies, though how much that is stays unmeasured until a
build reports it.
