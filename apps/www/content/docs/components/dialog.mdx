Dialog is the only component in the system that takes something away from the reader: their
place, their focus and their ability to do anything else. That makes *is this really a
dialog?* the first question on this page, and most of the time the honest answer is no.

Onboarding tours, feature announcements, rating prompts and newsletter offers are not
dialogues. They are interruptions with a modal costume, and in a health product they are
interruptions to somebody trying to find out about their own body.

## Anatomy

<Anatomy name="dialog" />

<CompositionTree name="dialog" />

**Scrim** blocks and dims the page. **Title** is the question or the subject and is the
dialog's accessible name. **Description** gives the consequence. **Actions** are at most two,
ordered so the least destructive is the one a reader reaches by accident. **Close** is present
on ordinary dialogues and absent on alert dialogues, where dismissing without answering is not
a valid outcome.

Proposed part hierarchy. This is the specification the implementation must match.

```text
Dialog                         root; role depends on whether dismissal is valid
├── Dialog.Scrim               blocks and dims; page behind is genuinely inert
└── Dialog.Container           a Surface at the overlay rung
    ├── Dialog.Title           the accessible name; a question where possible
    ├── Dialog.Description     the consequence, in one or two sentences
    ├── Dialog.Content         optional; anything more than that
    ├── Dialog.Close           present on dialog, absent on alertdialog
    └── Dialog.Actions         at most 2; destructive action is never the default focus
```

A dialog opening from within a sheet is a composition error. If the sheet needs a
confirmation, the confirmation belongs inside the sheet.

## Content guidelines

The title asks the question. The description says what happens if the reader says yes, and
what happens if they say no. Action labels are verbs that name the outcome, never *OK* and
*Cancel*, which force the reader back up to the title to work out what they are agreeing to.

Never phrase the dismissing action as a judgement about the reader — *No thanks, I do not care
about my health* is a real pattern in real products and it is contemptuous.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"Delete this reading? It will be removed from your history and from any trends it
    appears in. [Delete reading] [Keep it]"** Question, consequence, two verbs.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **"Are you sure? [OK] [Cancel]"** Sure about what, and which button does which thing? A
    reader answering quickly has a fifty per cent chance of being wrong.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **Initial focus on "Keep it"**, the action that changes nothing.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **Initial focus on "Delete reading"**, so a stray Return key destroys data. Focus is a
    default, and defaults should be safe.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

## Accessibility requirements

Requirements for the implementation, not results.

- **Correct role.** `dialog` when dismissal is a valid outcome, `alertdialog` when it is not.
  They differ in how assistive technology announces them and in what a reader expects Escape
  to do.
- **Named by its title**, described by its description, both associated programmatically
  rather than merely adjacent.
- **Focus moves in on open, is trapped while open, and returns to the trigger on close** —
  including when the dialog closes because the underlying data changed rather than because the
  reader acted.
- **Initial focus is never on a destructive action**, and never on the scrim or the dialog
  container when a control is available.
- **Escape closes an ordinary dialog.** On an alert dialog it does not, and the dialog does not
  simply swallow the key: it makes clear that an answer is needed.
- **The page behind is inert**, not merely visually dimmed, so screen readers and keyboard
  navigation cannot wander out of the dialog and get lost behind the scrim.
- **Actions meet the 44pt floor** with 8pt separation, and their order is consistent across the
  product.
- **`prefers-reduced-motion` removes the scale and fade**; the dialog appears in place. Depth
  is then carried by the scrim and the edge, which is why neither is optional.
- **At 200% text the dialog scrolls internally** with its actions pinned and reachable, and
  never grows beyond the viewport with its buttons off-screen.
