Dialog is the only component in the system that takes something away from the reader:
their place, their focus and their ability to do anything else. That makes *is this
really a dialog?* the first question on this page, and most of the time the honest
answer is no.

## Preview

An ordinary dialog: a question, the consequence of each answer, and two actions with
initial focus on the one that changes nothing. Open it and press Escape, then open it
and press Return without moving focus — both should leave the reading where it was.

Narrow the preview below the `sm` breakpoint and the dialog meets the bottom edge of
the screen, takes the full width and rounds only its top corners. It is still a dialog
while it does that: same role, same trap, same rules about who may dismiss it.

Onboarding tours, feature announcements, rating prompts and newsletter offers are not
dialogues. They are interruptions with a modal costume, and in a health product they
are interruptions to somebody trying to find out about their own body.

**This component is rung 4 of the escalation ladder, and rung 4 is rationed.** The
ladder runs ambient pill, in-place card, screen-level banner, then this — see
[Alert escalation](../patterns/alert-escalation.mdx) for the rungs and
[Alarm fatigue](../health/alarm-fatigue.mdx) for the budget that meters them. The
budget is one line long and this component cannot enforce it: **at most one
interruptive modal per session that the reader did not start themselves.** A dialog
the reader opened by pressing *Delete this reading* does not spend it; a dialog the
product opened at them does, and it is the most expensive thing in the system to
spend. Nothing in this file counts, because the budget is held at the screen and at
the session and a component sees neither — so the count is the product's to keep, and
the second unrequested modal in a session is a defect that will not show up in any
gate here.

**Dialog is not the emergency surface**, and a health doctrine page currently disagrees
with that sentence. [Emergency and escalation](../health/emergency-and-escalation.mdx)
lists `dialog` among the components it is implemented by, and then requires of an
emergency surface: one primary route and no competing choices, no auto-dismiss, the
contrast floor met with no material translucency, and a state that survives a relaunch
until it is explicitly acknowledged. This component is shaped for two answers, sits on
a translucent rung with no way for a caller to make it opaque, and remembers nothing
between mounts. Use it for a decision, not for an emergency; the disagreement is on the
other page's `implements` list and is recorded here rather than resolved here.

The exclusion that costs most is [Sheet](./sheet.mdx), and the test that separates them
is not the screen size. A sheet is a place the reader chose to go and may leave; a
dialog is a question they did not ask for and must answer. This component will not
turn into the other one at any width, so choosing wrongly is a decision that stays
wrong on every device rather than one that quietly repairs itself on a phone.

The parts are `data-slot` attributes and not exports. There is no `Dialog.Actions` to
import and no way to render a container without a title, which is what makes "the
dialog is always named" a fact about the DOM rather than a request in prose. Base UI
adds `data-open`, `data-starting-style` and `data-ending-style` to the scrim, the
viewport and the container; those pass through untouched. Nothing here carries
`data-status`, `data-category` or `data-opsinjs-value`, because a dialog renders no
measurement and asserts no clinical level.

**The tree is the parts, not every element.** Two groups sit in the rendered DOM and
in no row above, and a selector written from the tree alone will miss both. Base UI's
portal renders its own pointer-blocking `div` — `role="presentation"`, no `data-slot`,
fixed to the viewport — as the first child of the portal, a sibling of the scrim and
the viewport, and it is what actually stops a press reaching the page; the scrim only
shows that. And `Dialog.Container` holds a Surface, so the header, the body and the
actions are its **grandchildren**: the chain is `dialog-container` → `surface` →
`surface-content` → the three regions, with `surface-backdrop`, `surface-scrim` and
`surface-edge` beside them and `aria-hidden` on all three. So
`[data-slot=dialog-container] > [data-slot=dialog-header]` matches nothing; the
descendant form matches. Neither group is announced, so nothing here changes what a
reader hears.

Two parts are not in the specification's tree and are here on purpose. **Viewport** is
the fixed box the container is positioned inside; having a real element to place
against is what lets the dialog sit on the bottom edge of a phone and in the middle of
a laptop with no transform, no measurement and no JavaScript. **Header** and **Body**
split what the flat tree could not: the accessibility requirement below asks for the
dialog to scroll internally *with its actions pinned*, and a single flat region cannot
have one part that scrolls and two that do not.

The container is a Surface at the **`sheet`** rung, not the `overlay` rung the
specification names. The six rung names became the token names in
[ADR 0014](../project/decisions/0014-material-rung-names.mdx), and the vocabulary the
specification was written in used `overlay` for the rung that covers the page while
leaving it recognisable. That rung is now `sheet`. This ladder's `overlay` is chrome
that content scrolls beneath — a pinned toolbar, a tab bar — so translating the old
name by spelling rather than by job would put a modal dialog on the rung built for a
tab bar, and it would compile. See [The material ladder](../foundations/materials/the-ladder.mdx).

A dialog opening from within a sheet is a composition error. If the sheet needs a
confirmation, the confirmation belongs inside the sheet — which is also nesting rule
N3 in [Choosing a layer](../foundations/materials/choosing-a-layer.mdx): at most one
covering surface at a time.

## Examples

### Confirming something that cannot be undone

The title asks the question, the description says what each answer does, and the
action that changes something comes **first** so that the safest one is last — which
is where `initialFocus="safest"` lands. A stray Return keeps the reading.

<ComponentPreview name="dialog-confirming-a-deletion" kind="example" align="start" />

### An answer is needed

`severity="alert"` closes four exits at once: the role becomes `alertdialog`, the
close control is absent, the scrim does not dismiss, and Escape does not close.
Pressing Escape returns focus to the last action — which is a response if focus had
moved off it, and is nothing at all if it had not, because this example takes the
default `initialFocus="safest"` and focus starts there. Try it from the button and
then try it after a Tab: the second one moves a ring, the first is silent. That
difference is the open gap listed under Accessibility, not a subtlety of this example.

## Content guidelines

The title asks the question. The description says what happens if the reader says yes,
and what happens if they say no. Action labels are verbs that name the outcome, never
*OK* and *Cancel*, which force the reader back up to the title to work out what they
are agreeing to.

Never phrase the dismissing action as a judgement about the reader — *No thanks, I do
not care about my health* is a real pattern in real products and it is contemptuous.

On an alert dialog the description has one extra job, and this component will not do
it for you: it is where the reader is told that an answer is needed and what each
answer costs. Escape will not let them out, there is no close control, and opsinjs
does not know what your two answers mean, so it ships no sentence about them. An alert
dialog with no `description` raises a development warning for exactly this reason, and
a blank one counts as none — a reader cannot tell an empty string from an absent prop,
so neither can the warning.

The warning about `actions` works the same way and looks at the rendered DOM rather than
at the prop, because `null`, `false`, `""` and an empty fragment are all values that are
not `undefined` and all render an actions row with nothing in it. On an alert dialog
that is a surface with no close control, a refused Escape key and nothing to press.

## Accessibility

Some of this is checked by a gate in continuous integration. Most of it is argued, and
the things nobody has measured are listed rather than left out — that separation is
what `alpha` promises.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y` on every pull request and every push to `main`:**
(There is no commit hook in this repository, so a local commit is checked by nobody.)

- **Every colour here is a role token.** The static rig fails the build on a hex
  literal, an `oklch()` call, a Tailwind palette colour or a numbered ramp step
  anywhere under `registry/`, so the scrim, the surface and the close control all
  follow dark mode and increased contrast rather than pinning a value.
- **No class that resolves only in the docs chrome.** The roles a shadcn dialog reaches
  for — `bg-popover`, `bg-accent`, `rounded-2xl` — are declared in `app/globals.css`
  and produce no CSS at all under the product theme. The gate fails on them by name.
- **No type size in `px`.** The title and the description take steps from the
  eleven-step ramp, so they scale with the reader's own text size. See
  [Dynamic type](../foundations/typography/dynamic-type.mdx).
- **No banned word, in copy or in an identifier.**

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not measured:**

- **The role matches whether dismissal is valid.** `severity="alert"` swaps Base UI's
  alert-dialog root in rather than toggling props, so `role="alertdialog"`, the forced
  modality and the refusal to dismiss on an outside press arrive together and cannot
  be got three-quarters right by a later edit.
- **Named by its title and described by its description**, both associated
  programmatically by the primitive rather than by adjacency. An empty title raises a
  development warning; there is no prop that hides it. A blank `description` is treated
  as no description: the element is not rendered, so nothing is wired to
  `aria-describedby` and announced as empty, and on an alert dialog the missing-
  description warning fires for a blank string exactly as it does for an absent one.
- **Focus moves in on open, is trapped while open, and returns on close** to whatever
  had it when the dialog opened — including when the dialog closes because the product
  set `open` to false rather than because the reader acted. The component renders no
  trigger part, so the return target is the previously focused element rather than a
  control this component had to be told about.
- **Initial focus is never on the scrim or the container while a control exists.**
  `initialFocus="safest"` resolves to the *last* control in `actions`, which is where
  the ordering rule puts the answer that changes nothing; `content` resolves to the
  first control inside `children`, and falls through the safest action before it
  reaches the primitive's own behaviour. That middle link matters: an alert dialog has
  no close control, so the primitive's first-tabbable default would land on the *first*
  action, which the ordering rule reserves for the answer that changes something.
  Neither value ever leaves focus outside an inert page.
- **The cost of `safest` is that it is the last tab stop.** The DOM runs close control,
  body, actions, so from where focus starts the first Tab wraps round to the
  close control and the other answer is behind a Shift + Tab. A
  reader moving forwards meets the exit before they meet the alternative. That is the
  trade the default makes, and it is a trade rather than a free win.
- **Escape closes an ordinary dialog and does not close an alert one.** Base UI's alert
  dialog closes on Escape out of the box; this component cancels that close through the
  change event, and the refusal is complete in every configuration. The *response* to
  the key is not: focus returns to the safest action, which moves a ring and announces
  a control when focus had wandered, and does nothing whatever in the default
  configuration, where `initialFocus="safest"` has already put focus on that control
  and focusing it again is a specification no-op. See the gap list below.
- **The page behind is inert**, not merely dimmed — the primitive marks it, the scrim
  only shows it. Under `prefers-reduced-transparency` the scrim **loses its blur and
  becomes more opaque**: the token layer swaps the tint for the rung's opaque fallback
  and forces its alpha to 1, and that fallback is itself still slightly translucent
  (`--opsin-material-scrim-opaque` in `app/tokens.generated.css`), so the page behind
  stays recognisable rather than being replaced by a flat panel. The dimming is never
  reduced by the preference — being able to see through it less is the point of it.
- **At 200% text the dialog scrolls internally.** The container stops at the height of
  the viewport, the body scrolls, and the header and the actions are pinned outside it,
  so the buttons never end up below the fold. Scroll chaining is contained. The title
  and the description wrap rather than clip, so a token with no break opportunity in it
  loses no characters. This is the CSS reasoning and not a report: nobody has looked at
  this component at 200%, in either theme.
- **Nothing animates for a reader who asked for less.** The entrance and exit use
  `--opsin-duration-base`, which the product theme collapses to 1ms under
  `prefers-reduced-motion`, and the component also declares `transition-none` under the
  same query so a consumer without that stylesheet gets the same answer. The dialog then
  appears in place, and depth is carried by the scrim and the surface's edge — which is
  why no prop removes either.

**Not checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- **Contrast.** Title and description against the sheet rung's material, the close
  control's hover fill, and the focus ring against both the dialog and the scrim behind
  it, in both themes. The floor for a material is measured against its opaque fallback,
  and nothing below has been measured for this component yet.
- **The 44 by 44 target and the 8pt separation.** The close control asserts the floor
  in rem, and is the only thing here that does. The actions row asserts the
  **separation** — its gap is `--opsin-space-2`, which is the published
  `--opsin-target-separation` — and asserts no floor at all: it has no minimum height
  and no child selector, so the target size of an answer is whatever the control the
  caller passed in `actions` carries. A caller who passes a plain 24px button gets a row
  that meets no floor and no warning from this component. Neither the floor nor the
  separation is verified until the nightly layout job runs, which needs a browser this
  repository deliberately does not install. See
  [Target size and motor](../accessibility/target-size-and-motor.mdx).
- **Escape on an alert dialog is unanswered in the default configuration**, and this is
  a known limitation rather than an open question. The key is refused, which is the part
  that works. The response — focus to the safest action — produces nothing when focus is
  already there, which is where `initialFocus="safest"` puts it, so a reader who presses
  Escape before moving anywhere gets no ring, no announcement and no sentence.
  [Alert escalation](../patterns/alert-escalation.mdx) states the requirement this
  misses: *"if the answer is genuinely required, Escape is disabled and the reason is
  announced rather than the key silently doing nothing."* opsinjs does not meet that
  today. Every repair on the table was worse than the gap — the first action is the one
  that changes something, a component may never mount a live region on the caller's
  behalf, and moving focus to the container relies on an announcement nobody in this
  repository has put a screen reader on. It stays written down until somebody does.
- **The close control's accessible name defaults to English.** `closeLabel` exists so a
  product can translate it, in the same way `StatusPill.label` exists, and the default
  it ships is the word *Close*. A product whose readers do not read English and which
  does not pass the prop has an exit named in a language they cannot read, which for a
  screen-reader user is the difference between an exit and an unlabelled button. There
  is no locale detection here and there will not be one; the gap is the default, and it
  is the caller who closes it.
- **A pinned header with a very long title.** The header does not scroll, so at 200%
  text a title that is a paragraph rather than a question would leave the body a sliver.
  The content guidelines say the title is a question; that has not been tested against a
  title that is not one. The title wraps rather than clips, so this is about how much
  room is left, not about characters going missing.
- **Whether a value inside `children` may ride the entrance.** The container animates
  opacity and offset, and `children` is where the props documentation says a card
  holding a health value belongs — so a number can arrive on an animating surface.
  [Motion in health UI](../health/motion-in-health-ui.mdx) bans count-ups, dial sweeps
  and the progressive reveal of a result: a number that is at its final value on every
  frame, inside a container that fades in, is not any of those, and under reduced motion
  it does not move at all. That is the reading this page takes, and it is a reading
  rather than a ruling from the page that owns the rule.
- **`prefers-contrast: more` and `forced-colors`.** Neither is implemented anywhere in
  opsinjs yet, so no increased-contrast claim can currently be made by anybody. The
  surface strengthens its own edge under `contrast-more`; the dialog adds nothing to
  that.

## API reference

<PropsTable name="DialogProps" />

**`sheetBelow` is not implemented and is not a prop.** The specification asked for the
dialog to render as a Sheet below a given width, and in the same API asked
`severity="alert"` to remove every way of leaving without answering. A Sheet is
draggable and dismissible by design, so the two together would have made an alert
dialog dismissible with a thumb on a phone and not on a laptop — silently, at whichever
width the caller happened to pass. What the sentence under that prop was complaining
about is a complaint about shape, and the shape is here: below the `sm` breakpoint the
dialog meets the bottom edge, takes the full width and rounds only its top corners,
in CSS, with no prop and no change of component. The role, the trap, the inertness and
the dismissal rules are identical at every width.

**`actions` is a prop this component added.** The specification's part tree requires a
`Dialog.Actions` region, caps it at two and fixes their order, and its proposed API had
no way to fill one. A slot prop is how content arrives in this system where there is no
dot-notation export, and having the component own the region is also what makes the
actions pinnable, focusable by `initialFocus` and reachable by the Escape handler. The
cap of two is stated and not enforced: `ReactNode` does not say how many controls are
inside it, and `React.Children.count` cannot see through a fragment.

**`closeLabel` is a prop this component added**, and it exists for one word. The close
control's accessible name is the only reader-facing string this file owns, and shipping
it as an untranslatable English literal would have meant that in a product whose readers
do not read English the exit from a modal surface has no name. `StatusPill.label` exists
for the same reason and this follows it. It does not solve the problem, it moves it to
somebody who can solve it: the default is still *Close*, and a caller who never passes
the prop is where they started. It is ignored on an alert dialog, which renders no close
control.

The `actions` cap of two is not the only rule stated rather than enforced. The row
asserts the target **separation** and nothing about target **size** — that belongs to
the controls a caller puts in it — and `actions` is a `ReactNode`, so `null`, `false`,
`""` and an empty fragment are all legal values that render a row with nothing in it.
On an alert dialog that is a surface with no exit whatever, which is why the development
warning for it looks at the rendered DOM through the same ref `initialFocus` uses rather
than at the prop.

**`children` is optional**, though the proposed API marked it required. The part tree
calls `Dialog.Content` optional in the same document, and most confirmations are a
question, a consequence and two verbs with nothing in between.

`onOpenChange` takes one argument. Base UI reports a reason alongside it; every reason
that matters here is handled inside the component — the Escape refusal is the whole of
it — and a second parameter that only sometimes matters is a second parameter people
copy without reading.
