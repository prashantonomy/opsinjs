An avatar answers one question, who, and it answers it in the fastest way a screen can. A
face beside a message, a circle of initials beside a name in a list, a neutral person where
neither is known. It carries no other meaning, and the discipline in this component is
mostly about the meanings it refuses to carry.

The name is the whole of it. It is the picture's alt text and the source of the initials at
once, so a screen reader and a sighted reader are told the same thing from the same field,
and there is no way for the two to drift apart. What opsinjs does not supply is the picture.
A photo of a person is the product's to store, to serve and to moderate, and this component
draws the circle around whatever the product hands it without vouching for what is inside.

The avatar is not a control, and the moment a face becomes pressable it becomes something a
keyboard user has to be able to reach and a screen reader has to announce as a button. That
is a different component. Wrap the avatar in a button or a link when a press has to do
something, and let the wrapper own the focus and the name while the avatar rides along
inside it. And resist the urge to let an avatar carry authority. A clinician's face styled
to look more official than a patient's would say on whose authority a message stands, which
is a claim a product makes in words, through a citation, and never a claim a circle makes by
looking the part.

Names are not all one shape, and the initials have to hold up across the shapes a real

## Content guidelines

Pass the person's real name, spelled the way they spell it, and pass nothing else through
`name`. It is the alt text a screen reader reads and the source of the initials at once, so
a placeholder like "User" or "Unknown" becomes both a wrong label and wrong initials. When
there is genuinely no person, there is no avatar to draw.

A photo is the product's to supply and to moderate. opsinjs ships no default face and no
placeholder person, and it does not check, crop or filter what a `src` points at. If a
product cannot stand behind an uploaded image, it should not pass it, and the initials will
carry the person until it can.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"Ada Vance", with her photo, or "AV" when there is none.** One field feeds
    both the picture's alt text and the initials, so the label and the mark can
    never say different things.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **A generic silhouette used for everyone, with no name behind it.** A
    nameless circle identifies nobody, and a screen reader announces an
    unlabelled image or says nothing at all.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

## Accessibility

The avatar is presentation, not interaction. It takes no focus and is not a tab stop,
because it does nothing when it is pressed. When it sits inside a button or a link, that
control owns the focus and the accessible name, and the avatar is decorative inside it.

When the avatar shows a fallback, the root becomes `role="img"` with the person's name as
its `aria-label`, and the initials or the glyph inside are hidden, so a listener hears the
name once rather than hearing a stray letter or nothing. When the avatar shows a picture,
the picture's own `alt` carries the name and the root stays a plain container, so the name
is still announced once. A name that is empty is the one gap: the component cannot invent a
label, so it warns in development, draws the neutral person glyph, and leaves the circle
unlabelled rather than announcing a nameless image.

The circle is sized in rem, so it grows when the reader enlarges text rather than staying
fixed while everything around it grows. The contrast of the initials and the glyph on the
muted fill is drawn from the theme's neutral pair and has not been measured on this
component's own surfaces, which is one of the open questions above.

## API reference

<PropsTable name="AvatarProps" />

`name` is the only required prop, and it is required for a reason the type cannot enforce on
its own: a JavaScript caller can still pass an empty string. When it resolves to nothing,
the component warns in development and falls back to the neutral person glyph rather than
rendering a circle that names nobody. `src` is optional, and a missing or broken one is a
fallback rather than an error. `size` changes only the diameter, never the meaning.
