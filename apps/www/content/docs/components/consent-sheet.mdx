One sheet asks for one permission. If you have three to ask for, that is three sheets, each
shown when it becomes relevant — never one sheet with three paragraphs and a single button.
Bundled consent is not consent to any of its parts, and the reader who would have said yes to
two of the three has been given no way to say so; see
[Consent and disclosure](../health/consent-and-disclosure.mdx).

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** That the product is asking for one specific permission, that the reader
has been told what will be collected, what it will be used for, who will see it and how long
it will be kept, and that a decision either way has been recorded.

**What it must never be read as.** A condition of using the product, unless it genuinely is
one and the sheet says so plainly. It must never be read as permanent: every consent this
component takes must be withdrawable from somewhere the reader can find without asking, and
the sheet says where.

**Which colour axis it may use.** Neither. A consent sheet is not a clinical status and not a
category. Status colour on a consent surface reads as a warning about the reader's health,
which is a false alarm about a completely different subject.

**Refusal is a first-class outcome.** The decline control has the same size, the same
prominence and the same visual weight as the accept control, and it is never a plain-text
link beneath a filled button. If the product cannot function after a refusal, the sheet says
so before the reader chooses, not after.

**What the product records.** A decision, a timestamp, the identifier and version of the
text that was shown, and the scope consented to. The component's job is to hand all of that
back; storing it is the product's.

<SafetyCallout severity="urgent" evidence="opinion">
No pre-ticked boxes, no default-on switches, no *agree* that is also *continue*, and no
sheet that cannot be closed without deciding. Every one of these is a way of collecting a
decision the reader did not make, and in health data the consequences of a decision nobody
made are borne entirely by the person who did not make it.
</SafetyCallout>

## Anatomy

<Anatomy name="consent-sheet" />

<CompositionTree name="consent-sheet" />

**Heading** states what is being asked, as a question. **Purpose** says what the data is
for, in the reader's terms. **Scope** lists what is collected, who sees it and how long it is
kept — three facts, not a paragraph. **Withdrawal** says where to change this later.
**Decisions** are two equally weighted controls. **Details** is the optional full text for
the reader who wants it, never hidden behind a link labelled only *terms*.

Proposed part hierarchy. This is the specification the implementation must match.

```text
ConsentSheet                   root; extends Sheet; not dismissible by accident
├── ConsentSheet.Heading       what is being asked, phrased as a question
├── ConsentSheet.Purpose       what it is for, in one or two sentences
├── ConsentSheet.Scope         what is collected · who sees it · how long it is kept
├── ConsentSheet.Details       optional full text, disclosed in place
├── ConsentSheet.Withdrawal    where to change this decision later — mandatory
└── ConsentSheet.Decisions
    ├── ConsentSheet.Accept    equal weight to Decline
    └── ConsentSheet.Decline   equal weight to Accept; never a text link
```

A sheet whose `Accept` and `Decline` differ in size or prominence is a composition error and
should fail in development. `ConsentSheet.Withdrawal` is mandatory: a consent with no exit
is not revocable, whatever the copy says.

## Proposed API

<NotBuiltYet name="consent-sheet" />

```ts
// PROPOSED. Not implemented. A specification for review, not an API to generate
// code against. It will change without a deprecation cycle.

export interface ConsentScope {
  /** What is collected, in the reader's words. */
  collected: string
  /** Who can see it. Name them; "our partners" is not an answer. */
  sharedWith: string
  /** How long it is kept, and what happens at the end. */
  retention: string
}

/** What the product must store. The component produces it; it never stores it. */
export interface ConsentDecision {
  granted: boolean
  /** ISO timestamp of the decision. */
  at: string
  /** Stable id of the consent being asked for. */
  consentId: string
  /**
   * Version of the text that was actually shown. Without it the product cannot
   * later say what the person agreed to.
   */
  textVersion: string
}

export interface ConsentSheetProps {
  consentId: string
  textVersion: string
  /** Phrased as a question the reader can answer yes or no to. */
  heading: string
  /** One decision per sheet. Bundling is a design error, not a prop. */
  purpose: string
  scope: ConsentScope
  /** Where to change this later. Required. */
  withdrawalPath: string
  /** Full text, disclosed in place rather than behind a link. */
  details?: React.ReactNode
  /** What the reader loses by declining. Stated before they choose, not after. */
  consequenceOfDeclining?: string
  onDecision: (decision: ConsentDecision) => void
}
```

There is no `defaultGranted`, no `required` and no `hideDecline`. Their absence is the
specification.

## Content guidelines

Ask a question. Use *we* for the product and *you* for the reader. Say what the data is for
in terms of what the reader gets, not in terms of the product's internal purposes:
*so we can show your readings next to the range your clinic uses* is a purpose;
*for analytics and service improvement* is a category.

Never write *by continuing you agree*. Never write *we value your privacy*. Never label the
decline control *not now* when it means *no*, or *maybe later* when there is no later.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"Can we store your blood pressure readings? We keep them so you can see how they change
    over time. Only you and the clinic you choose can see them. They are deleted 12 months
    after you stop using the app. You can change this in Settings at any time."** Question,
    purpose, scope, retention, withdrawal.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **"We value your privacy. To give you the best experience, we process your health data in
    accordance with our Privacy Policy. [Accept] [Manage preferences]"** No question, no
    scope, no retention, and a second control that is a maze rather than a refusal.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"No, do not store them"** as a full-size control beside **"Yes, store them"**.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **A large filled "Allow" with a small grey "Not now" underneath.** The visual hierarchy
    is the real question being asked, and it is not a neutral one.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

## Accessibility requirements

Requirements for the implementation, not results.

- **The sheet is a modal dialogue** with a name from its heading, focus moved to the heading
  on open, focus trapped while open, and focus returned to the trigger on close.
- **Escape closes the sheet without deciding**, and closing without deciding is recorded as
  no decision — never as consent.
- **Accept and decline are adjacent in the tab order** and identical in target size, meeting
  the 44pt floor with 8pt separation. Neither is auto-focused; auto-focusing accept is a
  keyboard dark pattern.
- **The scope facts are a list**, not a paragraph, so they can be navigated item by item
  rather than heard as prose.
- **The full details disclose in place** and are announced as expanded, rather than opening a
  new context the reader has to navigate back from.
- **Nothing is conveyed by colour**: the difference between accept and decline is their
  words, not their fills, and the sheet is usable in grayscale.
- **At 200% text the sheet scrolls** with the decision controls remaining reachable and
  never overlapped by the keyboard or the safe-area inset — see
  [Layout](../foundations/layout.mdx).
- **Reduced motion** removes the sheet's slide entirely; it appears in place.
- **Print and export** produce the full consent text, the scope, the decision and its
  timestamp. People are entitled to a copy of what they agreed to.
