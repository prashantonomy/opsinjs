A value without its provenance is read with the standing of the most trustworthy value on the
screen. A wrist estimate rendered like a clinic measurement is read like a clinic measurement,
and the reader has no way to tell a reading from a guess an algorithm made about a process it
cannot observe. This component puts the origin of a number back beside the number, in the
reader's own plain words. What it does not do is supply those words, and that refusal is the
larger half of the component.

## Preview

The citation carrying everything it can hold: the plain words of where a value came from, a
link to the fuller source, and the date it was last checked. The provenance names a device
that could belong to no product and the link points at a placeholder anchor, because the
product that installs the component writes the source and opsinjs ships none of it. The state

SourceCitation composes [Link](./link.mdx) for the destination of the fuller citation, so
installing it installs Link with it. The link and any other navigation link on the surface are
then the same control, with one focus ring and one underline offset between them.

Provenance held behind a tap is absent from the glance and from the value's accessible name,
which are the two places a reviewer checks for it. The point of this component is that the
origin is on the same surface as the number, so put it there. A citation on a detail screen the
reader has to reach for is a citation most readers never see, which is close enough to no
citation at all.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** Where a piece of health information came from, in the product's own plain
words, and, when the product supplies one, the date that provenance was last checked and a link
to the fuller source. It asserts nothing about what the value means. There is no reference
range, no threshold and no verdict in this component, and it holds no reading of its own.

**Which of the provenance rules it carries.**
[Data provenance and device accuracy](../health/data-provenance-and-device-accuracy.mdx) sets
ten rules. This component carries two of them and no more. It renders **rule 2**, that
provenance is visible to the reader in plain words at the value, and it gives **rule 6** a
home, that a manufacturer's accuracy claim is attributed and linked rather than restated as the
product's own or paraphrased into a stronger one. The other eight rules stay the caller's. The
component holds no provenance class, so it cannot keep rule 3, which bounds a value's status by
its class; it checks no wording against the four classes, so it cannot keep rule 5, that the
string names the device rather than the marketing; and it would render a wrong provenance
string as readily as a right one. It reports where it can and asserts nothing it cannot.

**What it must never be read as.** A guarantee that the source is right, that it is current, or
that anybody checked it. `checkedOn` is the date the product recorded, and the component makes
no claim beyond that. It is not a freshness verdict: this component holds no staleness boundary
and says nothing about whether the source can still be relied on. Deciding a source is out of
date belongs to the product, and a value old enough to doubt is wrapped in
[RelativeTime](./relative-time.mdx) beside it, not signalled by this component.

**Which colour axis it may use.** Neither, exactly as with DisclaimerNote. There is no `status`
prop and no `category` prop, the root carries neither `data-status` nor `data-category`, and
there is no fill of any kind. Category colour would make a statement about where a number came
from read as a statement about what the number is; status colour would make it read as a level.
Provenance is neither. See [The two colour axes](../health/two-colour-axes.mdx).

**Who owns the words.** The consuming product. opsinjs ships no provenance: no default source,
no placeholder citation, no specimen DOI, no URL, no study and no date. The implementation is
written so that this cannot be got round by accident, and a citation rendered with no source
prints an admission that none was supplied rather than inventing one.

<SafetyCallout severity="attention" evidence="opinion">
Never restate a manufacturer's accuracy figure as your own, and never invent one to fill the
space. Attribute the claim and link it through `more`, in the manufacturer's own terms. A
figure copied from a design system or paraphrased into something stronger is a provenance
failure of exactly the kind this component exists to make visible.
</SafetyCallout>

That is the whole component. The parts are identified by `data-slot` and are styleable from
outside; none of them is an export, and there is no way to render one without the root. There is
no heading part and no dismiss control: a citation with a heading is a section, and a citation
the reader can turn off is a source the product would rather they did not read.

## Examples

### A device estimate naming its own method

The provenance sits at the value, in the reader's own plain words: the device that produced the
estimate, the inputs it is worked out from, and the class of the figure, which is an estimate
rather than a measurement. It names the device rather than the marketing, and the `more` link
points at where the fuller accuracy document would live, attributed rather than restated. The
value is a phrase and not a number, so nothing here can be mistaken for somebody's own reading.

<ComponentPreview name="source-citation-a-device-estimate" kind="example" align="start" />

### When nobody said where it came from

Provenance arrives from a data layer, a content table or a flag, and every one of those can
return nothing without anybody noticing. The citation prints an admission rather than rendering
empty, because an empty one passes review, passes every gate in this repository, and reaches a
reader as a value whose source has silently gone missing. [DisclaimerNote](./disclaimer-note.mdx)
refuses in the same shape and for the same reason.

The boundary is worth stating, because it is narrower than that promise sounds. The component
detects a missing *source*: `undefined`, `false` from an `&&` branch, `0` from the same branch on
an empty collection, an empty array, a whitespace-only string and a source that is nothing but a
number are all read as none supplied. A caller who wraps the provenance in an element is taken at
their word, so an empty string inside `<strong>{source}</strong>` still renders an empty citation
with no admission. Printing the admission over somebody's own markup would be a false accusation
on their screen, so the component does not; the cost is that this one route stays open, and it is
stated here rather than left for a reader to find.

## Content guidelines

Write the source in the reader's own words, and name the concrete thing rather than the brand's
marketing. "From your Model X cuff" and "you entered this on the day" are provenance a reader can
weigh; "clinically validated technology" is not, because it names nothing checkable. Say the
class of the figure where it matters: an estimate is called an estimate, and a self-reported
figure names the reader as its source. Where a device does not measure what the number looks
like it measures, say so, though that sentence is the caller's to write and this component does
not check that it was written.

Name the link for where it goes. "Read how this estimate is worked out" and "see the accuracy
document" are destinations; "learn more", "source" and a bare "here" are not, and a screen reader
can list every link on a page with no sentence around any of them. `more` takes the label and the
destination together so a link cannot be added without one, and a label from a short list of
vague ones raises a development warning. That list is English, so the identical failure in another
language is not caught.

Never restate a manufacturer's accuracy figure as your own, and never invent one. Attribute it
and link it through `more`. A cited figure the reader can follow to its source is worth more than
a confident number with no owner, and a paraphrase that makes the claim stronger is the failure
rule 6 names.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"Estimated by your watch from movement and heart rate, so read it as an estimate."** Names
    the device, the inputs and the class of the figure, in words the reader can weigh.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **"Powered by clinically validated technology."** Names no device and no method, restates a
    marketing claim as the product's own, and leaves the reader nothing to check.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

The admission line the component prints when no source is supplied is English, and there is no
prop through which a product can translate it, for the reason DisclaimerNote gives: a prop that
accepts the admission is one keystroke from a prop that accepts the citation, which is the thing
this component exists to refuse. A product shipping in another language guards the missing case
upstream, supplying its own copy for it, and the absence of a translation seam here is recorded in
[ADR 0005](../project/decisions/0005-no-lang-segment-yet.mdx).

## Accessibility

Some of what follows is checked by a gate on every commit and the rest is argued. They are
labelled separately, because `alpha` promises that accessibility is claimed only where it has
been checked, with the gaps listed rather than left out. Nobody has yet listened to this
component in a screen reader.

**Checked, by the gates on every commit:**

- **No `px` type size anywhere.** The source and the date are set at `text-opsin-footnote`, a rem
  from the generated ramp, so the citation scales with the reader's own text size rather than
  staying put at a fixed size.
- **No raw colour literal and no ramp step.** The source ink is the neutral `--foreground` role
  and the date ink is `--muted-foreground`, so both follow the theme; a hex, an `oklch()` or a
  numbered ramp step fails the build.
- **No banned word in the one reader-facing string this component owns, none in its development
  warnings, and none as a code identifier.** That single string is the line printed when no source
  is supplied.
- **No shipped threshold and no unowned numeric interval.** No clinical number is written into
  this component and none passes through it.

**Enforced by the API rather than by a gate:**

- **The source is readable, not paled.** It takes the foreground ink at the footnote step. It is
  smaller than the value it sits under, because it is secondary to it, and it is not a pale grey,
  because provenance set one step down in muted ink complies with the letter of rule 2 and none of
  its purpose.
- **The link names its destination.** `more` carries the label and the href together, so a link
  cannot be added without a label, and a label from a short list of vague ones raises a
  development warning.
- **It cannot be tinted from either axis without being told.** There is no colour prop.
  `className` reaches the root, which is the one hole, and a class from either axis raises a
  development warning naming what to use instead.
- **It invents no date.** A `checkedOn` value that does not resolve to a real calendar date is
  refused rather than rendered as a plausible wrong one, and the component never reads the clock.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **The link keeps the target floor an inline link sheds.** SC 2.5.8 exempts an inline target, so
  Link's inline form carries no floor. This link is not inside a sentence: it is a control on its
  own line, so it re-adds `--opsin-target-minimum` on both axes. Both axes, because SC 2.5.8 is a
  44x44 region and a short label in a language with shorter words would otherwise sit under the
  floor. The floor is declared in rem so it grows with the reader's text size, and no browser has
  measured it. See [Target size and motor](../accessibility/target-size-and-motor.mdx).
- **It reflows at 200% text** without truncating and without horizontal scroll: no fixed height,
  `min-w-0` with `wrap-break-word` on the source and the date, and `wrap-anywhere` on the link, so
  a long device name or a bare word breaks rather than widening the layout past the viewport.
- **The date reads as one phrase.** The prefix "Last checked on" and the formatted date sit in one
  `<time>` element, so a listener meets "Last checked on 14 March 2026" rather than a bare date
  with nothing to place it by. The prefix is English, in the same position RelativeTime's event
  words hold and translate for no caller.
- **There is no animated transform to reduce.** No transition and no animation anywhere in the
  component or either example. The composed inline link's `active:translate-y-px` is an instant
  one-pixel shift on `:active`, not an animation, so `prefers-reduced-motion` has nothing here to
  act on.

**Not yet checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- Whether the provenance reaches a screen-reader user who arrives at the value on its own. This
  component renders the provenance beside the value and does not put it in the value's accessible
  name, so the association is the caller's to make. It is the open question above.
- The measured contrast of the source ink and the date ink on the surfaces a citation sits on. The
  neutral token pairs are published below; the rendered pixels are not.

## API reference

<PropsTable name="SourceCitationProps" />

`source` is typed optional and is required by the contract, which is the same shape
[DisclaimerNote](./disclaimer-note.mdx) uses for its own words. Marking it required buys nothing,
because `{reading.source}` with an undefined `reading.source` type-checks either way. It costs the
ability to render the missing state at all, which is the state a product is most likely to ship by
accident and the one that most needs seeing.

`checkedOn` is formatted from what the caller supplied and from nothing else. A string is a strict
`YYYY-MM-DD` calendar date and a number is milliseconds since the epoch; both are pinned to UTC so
the day does not drift, and a value that is not a real calendar date is refused rather than
rendered as a plausible wrong one. There is no clock read and no live timer.
