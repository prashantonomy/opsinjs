BodyMap is the most careful build in the system, because a body diagram makes a claim about
who the reader is before it records anything. Its answer is to draw nobody in particular. The
figure is an abstract outline, front and back, and the component records only which regions
the reader pointed at. It names no symptom, grades no severity, and decides nothing about
what a marked region means.

It is a controlled multi-select. You hand it the regions and the current selection, and it
hands back the next selection. Everything a marked region could be taken to mean stays with
the product that reads the value.

## Preview

Mark a region and read the result in greyscale. The claim this component has to earn is that
a selected region is distinguishable without colour, so check that the muted fill, the
hairline and the tick tell a marked region from an unmarked one with the hue removed. Left
and right are the person's own sides, so the "Left arm" marker sits on the viewer's right.

The exclusion that matters is realism. If a product needs an anatomically detailed figure,
this component is the wrong tool and always will be, because a detailed figure is an
illustration commission that has to represent a range of bodies rather than one abstract
outline. Use a labelled [Field](./field.mdx) and words instead. What BodyMap offers is a
coarse, neutral pointer, and its honesty depends on staying coarse.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** Only that the reader indicated these regions. Nothing more. It does not
assert what is there, whether it hurts, how strongly, since when, or what it means. A marked
"chest" is the reader pointing at their chest, and it is not a symptom, not a finding, and
not a diagnosis.

**What it must never be read as.** A symptom checker, a triage, or a diagnosis. The
component runs no rules over a selection and reaches no conclusion from it. A product that
treats a marked region as evidence of a condition has added a judgement the component does
not make and does not endorse, and it owns that judgement completely.

**Which colour axis it may use.** Neither. A marked region carries no clinical level and
names no category, so a selection is shown with a muted fill, a hairline and a tick, and
never with a hue. Painting a region in a status colour would say the location is urgent, and
painting it in a category colour would say which body system it belongs to, and both are
claims this input has no basis to make. See [The two colour axes](../health/two-colour-axes.mdx).

**Who owns the region vocabulary and any interpretation.** The consuming product. The
component ships a generic set of region words so it has something to render, and a product
replaces them with its own copy and its own language. What a marked region is taken to mean,
and whether a selection triggers anything at all, is the product's decision and never the
component's.

The silhouette is not a part you can style through a slot, on purpose. It is decorative and
aria-hidden, and it is drawn from primitive shapes rather than a lifelike outline so that it
is abstract by construction. A product that needs the figure to look like a specific body
should not be reaching for this component at all.

### Pairing the map with a checkbox list

A diagram is a spatial control, so it is offered alongside a plain list rather than instead
of one. Here the map and a native checkbox list are bound to the same selection, so a reader
can answer with whichever suits them and a keyboard or assistive-technology user has a
robust path to the identical result. Ship both together.

## Content guidelines

Label the group with the question the reader is answering, and keep the region words plain.
"Head", "Left arm" and "Lower back" say where without saying anything the map cannot
support. Do not smuggle a diagnosis or a symptom into a region label, and do not read one
out of a selection.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **A group labelled "Where are you noticing something?" with regions named "Chest" and "Left arm".** The words say where, and nothing more, which is all the input can honestly carry.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **A region relabelled "Chest pain" or "Fracture".** That is a symptom or a diagnosis the map cannot make, presented as though the reader confirmed it by pointing.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **The map shipped next to a checkbox list bound to the same answer.** Some readers point, some read a list, and both reach the same value.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **The map as the only way to answer.** A spatial control with no list behind it shuts out readers for whom pointing at a small target is the hard part.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

## Accessibility

Every region is a real, focusable, labelled button, so the map is reachable and operable
from the keyboard, and its selected state is carried by `aria-pressed` rather than by any
pixel. That is argued from the implementation rather than verified by a screen-reader pass,
and the gaps are listed rather than left out, which is what `alpha` promises.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet tested:**

- **Each region is a button with a name.** The button's accessible name is the region
  label, and its pressed state is `aria-pressed`, so a screen-reader user hears the region
  and whether it is marked. The whole map is a group named by `label`, and each figure is a
  group named for its view.
- **The selected state is not colour.** A marked region gains a muted fill, a hairline and a
  tick, and an unmarked one shows a plus, so the difference survives greyscale, a reader
  stylesheet that strips fills, and colour vision deficiency. The pixel is a backstop;
  `aria-pressed` is the carrier.
- **The targets meet the floor.** Each marker is at least the 44 by 44 minimum on both axes,
  carried in `rem` so it grows with the reader's text size, with a literal fallback for an
  app that has not wired the token sheet.
- **Left and right are the subject's sides.** The `left-arm` marker sits on the viewer's
  right, matching clinical convention, so a product's own copy and any read-back stay
  consistent with how the reader would name their own side.

**Known gaps, listed rather than omitted:**

- **A map is a spatial control.** Pointing at a target is exactly the task some readers
  cannot do, which is why the page pairs the map with a checkbox list bound to the same
  value and asks a product to ship both. The list is the robust path, not a courtesy.
- **The default schematic is nine tab stops.** Each region is its own stop, so a full front
  and back map is a long tab sequence. Whether it should become a single roving toolbar is
  an open question above.
- **Increased contrast and forced colours are unverified.** The markers draw only neutral
  chrome roles and carry no `forced-colors:` rule of their own, so under a high-contrast
  theme they inherit whatever the surface does and nothing more. Nobody has opened the map
  under a real high-contrast theme yet.
