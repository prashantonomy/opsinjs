The tile is the component most likely to be over-used, because a dashboard of numbers looks
productive and costs nothing to build. Its specification is mostly about what a tile is not
allowed to do without a larger surface behind it.

A tile that cannot be opened is a dead end. If a reader glances at a number, wonders what it
means, and has nowhere to go, the tile has raised a question the product refuses to answer.
Every clinical tile should lead to a [ResultCard](./result-card.mdx) or an equivalent
surface.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** That this measurement had this value, in this unit, at this time. Three
claims, all of which must be on the tile: a number without a unit is ambiguous, and a number
without a time is undated, which for health data is the same as being wrong.

**What it must never be read as.** A current reading, unless it is one. The single most
common failure of health dashboards is a tile showing a number from six weeks ago in exactly
the typography of one taken this morning. Past the product's staleness threshold the tile
must change visibly — a muted treatment plus an explicit relative time, not a small grey
timestamp — as
[Uncertainty, staleness and missing data](../health/uncertainty-and-staleness.mdx) requires.

**Which colour axis it may use.** The **category** axis, on the icon and the label, so a
reader can find the sleep tiles among the heart tiles in a grid of eight. The **status**
axis may appear only as an embedded [StatusPill](./status-pill.mdx) or a single status mark,
never as the tile's background. A grid of status-coloured tiles is a heat map of somebody's
body, and it is unreadable at exactly the moment it matters most — see
[Alarm fatigue](../health/alarm-fatigue.mdx).

**Who owns the staleness threshold.** The product. What counts as an old reading is clinical
and differs by measurement: a blood-pressure reading from last week may be current, and a
blood-glucose reading from four hours ago may not be.

## Anatomy

<Anatomy name="metric-tile" />

<CompositionTree name="metric-tile" />

**Label** is what was measured. **Reading** is the number and unit, and it is the only large
element. **Time** is the relative time with an absolute value available. **Trend** is an
optional embedded sparkline, drawn small and captioned in the tile's accessible name rather
than beside it. **Status** is an optional pill. Everything is optional except label, reading
and time.

Proposed part hierarchy. This is the specification the implementation must match.

```text
MetricTile                     root; a link or button when it leads somewhere
├── MetricTile.Header
│   ├── MetricTile.Icon        category-tinted; never carries status alone
│   └── MetricTile.Label       what was measured
├── MetricTile.Reading
│   └── Value                  number + unit; the only display-size element
├── TrendSparkline             optional; no caption of its own at tile size
├── StatusPill                 optional; the only status-coloured element
└── MetricTile.Time            <RelativeTime>; changes treatment when stale
```

A tile with more than one status-bearing element, or with a `Value` that is not the largest
thing on it, is a composition error.

## Content guidelines

Labels are two or three words in the reader's vocabulary — *resting heart rate*, not *RHR*,
and not *HeartRateResting*. Units are displayed as symbols and spoken in full. Never
abbreviate the measurement into an acronym the reader has to learn to use your dashboard.

There is no room for explanation on a tile, so the tile must not raise a question it cannot
answer. If the number needs a caveat, the caveat belongs on the surface the tile opens.

## Accessibility requirements

Requirements for the implementation, not results.

- **Each tile has one accessible name that is a whole sentence**: *resting heart rate, 58
  beats per minute, measured this morning*. Scanning a grid by tab or by rotor must not
  produce a sequence of bare numbers.
- **When the tile is interactive it is a single link or button**, with the whole tile as the
  target, meeting the 44pt floor with 8pt separation between tiles. Nested interactive
  elements inside a tile are prohibited — see
  [Target size and motor](../accessibility/target-size-and-motor.mdx).
- **Staleness is not conveyed by opacity alone.** The muted treatment is accompanied by
  explicit text, so it survives increased-contrast and grayscale.
- **The embedded sparkline is decorative at tile size** and is hidden from assistive
  technology; its information is folded into the tile's accessible name instead.
- **At 200% text the grid becomes a single column** and the tile grows; the reading never
  truncates and the unit never wraps away from its number.
- **Contrast** is measured for the label, the reading, the unit and the muted stale
  treatment, in both themes.
- **Print** renders tiles as a plain list of label, reading, unit and date.
