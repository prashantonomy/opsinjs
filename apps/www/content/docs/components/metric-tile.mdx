The tile is the component most likely to be over-used, because a dashboard of numbers looks
productive and costs nothing to build. Most of its specification is about what a tile is not
allowed to do without a larger surface behind it, and most of the implementation is about
refusing to fill a silence the product left on purpose.

A tile that cannot be opened is a dead end. If a reader glances at a number, wonders what it
means, and has nowhere to go, the tile has raised a question the product refuses to answer.
Every clinical tile should lead to a [ResultCard](./result-card.mdx) or an equivalent
surface, which is what `href` is for.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** When there is a reading, that this measurement had this value, in this
unit, at this time. Three claims, all of which must be on the tile: a number without a unit
is ambiguous, and a number without a time is undated, which for health data is the same as
being wrong. `label`, `value` and `now` are always required, and `measuredAt` is required
beside a reading, where an instant this component cannot locate in time renders nothing at
all rather than a number a reader would take for today's. When `value` is `null` there is no
measurement to date, so the third claim is withheld and `measuredAt` is `null`: a tile that
named a measurement time above "no reading yet" would claim a moment nobody took a reading. A
tile with no reading therefore asserts one thing and not three, that nothing has been
measured, and it says nothing about when anyone last looked, because the component holds no
such datum and will not invent one. This is the same line [Value](./value.mdx) draws, that
zero is a reading and absence is not: a reading of nought is a measurement with a time, an
absence is neither, and the tile keeps them apart by dating the first and dropping the time
line under the second rather than pairing "no reading yet" with a moment nobody measured.

**Where the number came from, and why the tile cannot say.** The tile names the moment a
reading belongs to and defaults to *Measured*. `event` now lets the product choose another
moment, but every moment word names when, not where the number came from, and there is still
no provenance prop, so a figure that a device estimated, or that somebody typed in from
memory, is announced in exactly the words a laboratory result gets. Until that is closed, do
not route a figure nobody measured through `measuredAt`: the tile will assert a provenance
the product never claimed. This is one of
the two obligations on this page's list of governors that the component does not meet. The
other is the missing masking affordance, and both are listed above rather than left to be
discovered.

**What it must never be read as.** A current reading, unless it is one. The single most
common failure of health dashboards is a tile showing a number from six weeks ago in exactly
the typography of one taken this morning. Past the product's staleness boundary the whole
tile mutes and the timestamp says *may be out of date* in words. The treatment is a tone
change and a sentence rather than a small grey date, which is what
[Uncertainty, staleness and missing data](../health/uncertainty-and-staleness.mdx) requires.

**Which colour axis it may use.** The **category** axis, on the icon and the label, so a
reader can find the sleep tiles among the heart tiles in a grid of eight. The **status**
axis appears only as an embedded [StatusPill](./status-pill.mdx), never as the tile's
background. No element on a tile carries both, so there is no OPSIN-0001 for this component
to raise. The never-mix rule is a check on an element, not on a props object.

**Who owns the staleness boundary.** The product, and only the product. What counts as an
old reading is clinical and differs completely from one measurement to the next, and opsinjs
holds no such number for any measurement in any population. It holds none as a default, none
in an example, and none as an illustration in this paragraph, because a number written down
anywhere is a number somebody copies. `staleAfterHours` has no default and will not get one.
Omitted, there is no stale treatment at all, which is the honest output when nobody has said
what stale means here.

**Who assigns the level.** The product, and nothing here derives one. There is no comparison
of a value against anything in this component's source, because it has no range, no
threshold and no reader.

**A level and a destination belong together, and nothing here enforces it.**
[Clinical status semantics](../health/clinical-status-semantics.mdx) says of `attention`
that there is always a named action; a tile has no room for a sentence, so on a tile the
action is the tile. `status` can nevertheless be set on a tile with no `href`, and nothing
warns. Pairing them is the product's to remember. A check for it belongs in the shared
warning channel, which this component cannot add a code to on its own.

<SafetyCallout severity="attention" evidence="opinion">
A grid of status-coloured tiles is a heat map of somebody's body, and it is least readable at
the moment it matters most. Colour a tile for what the reading is *about*; put how much
attention it needs in a pill, with the word in it, on the one or two tiles the product has
actually said something about. Filling the rest with a reassuring level is inventing
verdicts. See [Alarm fatigue](../health/alarm-fatigue.mdx).
</SafetyCallout>

The parts above are identified by `data-slot` and are styleable from outside. None of them is
an export: there is no `MetricTile.Reading` component, no way to render a label without a
reading, and no prop that removes the timestamp.

**There is no sparkline, and that is the one real departure from the part tree above.** The
specification asked for a `trend` array drawn small, decorative, hidden from assistive
technology. [TrendSparkline](./trend-sparkline.mdx) shipped with a mandatory, visible
caption and a `role="img"` plot named by it, because a line through somebody's readings is a
claim that a pattern exists and the sentence beside it is the only part that says which
pattern. Those two cannot both hold on one element. That left two honest options. One is a
tile big enough to carry a real caption, which is a card, and the other is no trend at all.
This is the second: the tile is a number, a unit and a date, and the reader taps through to
the surface that can draw a line and explain it. A product that wants a line in a grid cell
composes TrendSparkline in its own layout at a size where the caption fits, which is what
the sparkline's own row-of-tiles example shows.

**`Surface` is in the tree because it is in the DOM.** The tile does not paint its own
material; it sits on the `card` rung like every other card-shaped thing in the system, so a
product that re-tunes the ladder re-tunes tiles with it.

### No boundary was supplied

The same measurement three hours old and five weeks old, with nothing said about when a
reading of this kind stops being current. Most dashboards are actually in that state. Both
tiles are set identically, and nothing about the older one says it is older except the date
`RelativeTime` prints. That is the argument for supplying the boundary, and for taking it
from somebody who knows what was measured.

The muted treatment itself is described on this page and demonstrated by none of its
previews. Showing it takes a number of hours; opsinjs holds none for any measurement in any
population; and the version of this example that carried one taught the idea better and
shipped a boundary into every repository that installed it. In a worked example a number is
read as a recommendation whatever the caption beside it says.

## States

An interactive tile is a link, so its interaction states are a link's. Hovering it or reaching
it with the keyboard underlines the label, keyboard focus draws the two-pixel offset ring the
component declares in its own class, and a press shifts the whole tile down one pixel, which is
the one acknowledgement a one-handed tap on a touch screen can show. There is no disabled state,
because a tile with nowhere to go is a static readout rather than a disabled link, and a static
tile (one set with no `href`) carries none of these states at all.

Three of the five data states are the composing grid's to supply and two are the tile's own.
Loading belongs to a [Skeleton](./skeleton.mdx) the layout swaps in so the grid keeps its
shape, and a system error belongs to the [EmptyState](./empty-state.mdx) the surface shows in
place of a tile. What the tile owns is the empty reading and the stale one, because those are
the two a dashboard most often draws as though they were fresh.

| Data state | What MetricTile renders |
| --- | --- |
| Error | Two behaviours, handled apart. A `measuredAt` this component cannot locate in time beside a reading, whether it is malformed, carries no offset, or is `null` next to a real value, is refused: the tile renders nothing at all and reports it once in development, because a number with a date it cannot stand behind is worse than no number. A `value` that is present but is not a finite number falls to the absence branch, where [Value](./value.mdx) shows its own not-available wording and reports it rather than this tile raising a second complaint. A system-level fetch error is the grid's to draw as an [EmptyState](./empty-state.mdx). |
| Loading | None of its own. A reading still arriving is covered by a [Skeleton](./skeleton.mdx) the composing grid swaps in so the layout holds its shape, which is where "when to use it" sends you. The tile takes no loading prop and shows no spinner. |
| Empty | `value={null}` is the no-reading state, and it is not a reading of zero. The label stays, [Value](./value.mdx) drops the absence to the headline step in the muted foreground so it is never the loudest thing in a grid, and the time line is dropped because there is no measurement to date, so `measuredAt` is `null` here. |
| Partial | No state of its own. A reading is present in full or absent entirely, because `value` is a number or `null` and a compound reading is two measurements that take two tiles. Optional parts a caller leaves out, a status, a category tint or an icon, are simply not rendered, which is composition rather than a partial reading. |
| Stale | Past the product's `staleAfterHours` the label, any icon and the reading mute to the muted foreground and the time line adds *may be out of date* in words, so the state survives greyscale and print. The muting sits on the parts and not the root, so the embedded [StatusPill](./status-pill.mdx) keeps the verdict the product assigned. When the age cannot be checked at all the tile treats the reading as possibly out of date rather than asserting it is current. With no `staleAfterHours` there is no stale treatment, which is the honest output when nobody has said what stale means for this measurement. |

## Content guidelines

Labels are two or three words in the reader's vocabulary. Write *resting heart rate*, not
*RHR*, and not *HeartRateResting*. Units are displayed as symbols and spoken in full, which
`Value` does from the unit table; a symbol the table does not hold is shown as written rather
than pronounced by guesswork. Never abbreviate the measurement into an acronym the reader has
to learn before they can use your dashboard.

There is no room for explanation on a tile, so the tile must not raise a question it cannot
answer. If the number needs a caveat, the caveat belongs on the surface the tile opens. If
there is no such surface, the caveat means the reading does not belong on a tile.

## Motion

MetricTile declares no transition and no animation, so there is nothing for
`prefers-reduced-motion` to reduce. The one movement it makes is the press cue on a linked
tile, a single one-pixel downward shift under `:active` that carries no transition of its own
and so lands in one frame rather than easing. The hover and focus underline on the label is a
change of state and not a travel, and it declares no transition either. The tile consumes no
token from `tokens/motion.json`, which means re-tuning a spring or a duration there changes
nothing it renders.

A health value is never animated on first paint here, and that is a rule rather than an
oversight. There is no count-up on the number and no sweep across the tile, because a figure
that counts up has shown, for every frame of the count, a number that was not true, which is
what [Motion in health UI](../health/motion-in-health-ui.mdx) forbids. When a reading changes
while the tile is on screen the tile re-renders to the new figure with no motion at all, so
nothing on it moves to announce a change that its words and its date already carry.

## Accessibility

Some of this is checked by a gate on every commit. The rest is argument, and it is labelled
as such: `beta` promises that accessibility is claimed only where it has been checked, with
the gaps listed rather than left out.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y` on every commit:**

- **Neither axis lands on one element.** The static rig fails the build on any element
  carrying both `data-status` and `data-category`, and on any element resolving a category
  colour and a status colour through the same CSS property. The icon and the label carry
  `data-category` and no status; the pill carries `data-status` and no category; the root
  carries neither.
- **No status is drawn here at all.** This file paints no status colour, reads no status
  word and stamps no `data-status`. Every one of those belongs to the embedded
  [StatusPill](./status-pill.mdx), which owes the word, the glyph and the colour together.
- **No type size in pixels, and no colour literal.** Every size comes from the eleven-step
  ramp and every colour from a role token, so the tile scales with the reader's own text
  size and re-derives itself for dark mode.
- **No staleness number ships, anywhere.** A threshold-shaped name given a numeric literal
  under `registry/bases`, `registry/examples` and `registry/screens` fails the build. The
  build checks those three directories and no others, so the same name in `lib/` or `app/`
  is unchecked. The exemption that lets an `EXAMPLE_`-prefixed constant carry a number
  deliberately does not open for a staleness prop: renaming a boundary does not stop it
  being one. Neither this component, nor its demo, nor either of its examples carries one,
  which is why the muted stale treatment appears in no preview on this page.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **An interactive tile is one link and one tab stop**, with the whole tile as the target and
  the 44pt floor carried on both axes in `rem`, so it grows when the reader raises their text
  size. Nothing this component renders inside it is focusable: the pill is a `span`, the icon
  wrapper is `aria-hidden`, the trailing chevron is an `aria-hidden` span, and there is no
  second control. The `icon` slot takes a node from
  the caller, though, and a control passed there would be focusable inside an `aria-hidden`
  subtree. A control there is a second tab stop in a one-stop component, and it is missing
  from the accessibility tree. The prop says it must not be interactive and nothing checks
  that, so the claim above is scoped to what the component controls. See
  [Target size and motor](../accessibility/target-size-and-motor.mdx).
- **A linked tile is visibly a control before anyone touches it.** At rest it carries a
  trailing chevron a static tile does not, so a reader scanning a list on a phone can tell a
  door from a plain readout with no pointer and no keyboard, and the cue survives touch, dark
  theme, greyscale and print. Hovering the tile or reaching it with the keyboard underlines
  the label, the same answer [Card](./card.mdx) gives on the surface the two share; keyboard
  focus also draws the two-pixel offset ring the component declares in its own class rather
  than borrowing one from a product stylesheet it was not installed with; and a press shifts
  the whole tile down one pixel, which is the one acknowledgement a one-handed tap on a touch
  screen can show. The chevron is decorative and hidden from assistive technology, so the
  link's accessible name is unchanged: it stays the label, the reading, the time and any
  level, in that order.
- **Its accessible name is a whole sentence.** A link takes its name from its content, and
  the parts are separated by visually-hidden commas, so the first tile of the second example
  above is announced *Example measurement, 14 steps, Measured 3 hours ago, on 6 April 2026
  at 06:00 UTC, Watch* rather than as a run of fragments. `RelativeTime` always appends the
  exact date and time, and names the offset only when no `timeZone` was given, because a
  named IANA zone is already the reader's own clock; a sighted reader now sees this on screen
  as well rather than only in the name, and the reader's locale decides the word order. A tile
  with a status therefore announces five clauses and not three. A tile with no reading
  announces its label and the absence sentence and stops, because there is no instant and no
  measurement word to read after them.
- **The unit as it is written on screen is not in that name**, and this is a constraint
  rather than a claim. `Value` hides the symbol from assistive technology and substitutes the
  spoken form, so a tile whose visible text is *kg* is named *kilograms*, and a link whose
  visible label has no substring in common with its accessible name is a WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.3
  failure. It does not bite in anything shipped here only because *steps* is both the symbol
  and the spoken form. This component offers no repair: there is no `aria-label` escape, and
  an `aria-label` would replace the whole sentence rather than mend one clause of it.
  `Value`'s own documentation assigns the duty to whoever makes a reading tappable, which is
  this component, so it is recorded here rather than left for a product using *kg*, *mmHg* or
  *%* to discover.
- **A static tile has no accessible name of its own**, and this is a real gap rather than an
  omission. Composing one would mean this component formatting the number itself, or minting
  ids for `aria-labelledby`, which needs a hook and a client boundary. Its content is in the
  same order and says the same words; it is not a single object in the rotor. Pass `href`
  where you can. The embedded `StatusPill` is given `describes` in that variant and not in
  the linked one, so the level keeps a subject either way: inside a link the label is already
  in the same name, and outside one the pill carries the label itself rather than floating in
  the reading order as a level with nothing attached to it.
- **Staleness is never carried by tone alone.** The tone and the words are two verdicts
  reached in two files, and they agree because they are the same expression on the same
  parse: this component reads an instant with the pattern, the field checks and the
  `Date.UTC` round trip `RelativeTime` uses, and compares elapsed milliseconds against the
  threshold multiplied out exactly as `RelativeTime` does. An earlier version of this file
  diverged on both, and a timestamp one accepted and the other refused produced a fully
  greyed tile with no words in it. That is tone alone, which is the state the doctrine
  forbids. Because the words are the carrier, the state survives greyscale, increased
  contrast and a black-and-white printout. The duplication itself is an open question above,
  not a solved problem.
- **The muted treatment is carried by the parts, not by the tile's root.** That is what stops
  a `className` from outside deleting it, because `tailwind-merge` files an opsin type step
  and an opsin colour in one conflict group and keeps whichever arrives last. It is also what
  keeps the muting off the `StatusPill`: a verdict the product assigned is not withdrawn
  because the reading has aged.
- **Reading order matches visual order**: label, reading, time, status, because per
  D8 the level closes the reading order, after the instant it qualifies. Nothing is
  re-arranged by CSS against the DOM.
- **Nothing animates.** No count-up, no entrance transition, no pulse. A number that counts
  up has displayed, for every frame of the count, a figure that is not true. See
  [Motion in health UI](../health/motion-in-health-ui.mdx).
- **No live region is mounted.** When a reading changes on screen the tile says nothing on
  the caller's behalf; only [AlertBanner](./alert-banner.mdx) is entitled to interrupt.

**Not yet checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- Contrast for the label, the reading, the unit, the category tint and the muted stale
  treatment, as this tile composes them, in both themes and under increased contrast. The
  token pairs the tile draws its colour from are now measured under the `category`,
  `materials` and `neutral` scopes it declares in `registry/catalogue.ts`, and the report
  below lists them. That is a measurement of those token pairs and not of this tile: the
  specific combinations it assembles, a category ink and a status tint each sitting on the
  `card` material with the muted treatment over them, are not measured as a set, so they stay
  on this list. Both `prefers-contrast: more` and `forced-colors: active` are now answered,
  and neither has
  been measured here. Surface draws the tile's edge as an outline rather than a fill or a
  box-shadow, so under forced colours the browser keeps that outline and repaints it in
  `CanvasText` while it drops everything a background carries, and the tile's boundary does
  not depend on a colour the mode removes. Under `prefers-contrast: more` Surface widens the
  same edge. This component declares no forced-colours border of its own: the boundary is
  Surface's to draw and the tile only leans on it. Nobody has opened the result under a real
  Windows contrast theme, which is why both stay on this list rather than moving to the
  checked one.
- Behaviour at 200% text. The tile sets no fixed height, no `truncate` and no
  `whitespace-nowrap`, and `Value` keeps the number and its unit together with a no-break
  space. The behaviour has nevertheless not been observed at that size, and `className` can
  override every one of those from outside. The grid in the first example does not reflow
  under this site's own 200% control either: Tailwind's breakpoints are `rem` inside a media
  query, and `rem` there resolves against the document's initial font size rather than the
  root element's computed one, so the columns stay put while the type doubles.
- Print. The exact date shows on screen by default now, so it is in the printed output too
  rather than reaching paper by a print-only rule, and the tile declares `print:border` so
  its boundary does not depend on a background the browser drops. Its forced-colours sibling
  is a separate mechanism argued from the same premise: forced colours keeps Surface's edge
  outline and repaints it in `CanvasText`, whereas print needs the tile's own `print:border`
  because a printer keeps that outline but draws it in the rung's screen edge colour, which is
  invisible on white paper. Whether that reads as a
  boundary on paper has not been looked at, and the docs site's own print rules do not travel
  with the component. It prints as a tile and not as the plain list the specification asks
  for: turning a grid into a list is a decision about the grid, which a tile cannot see.
- Masking a reading on a shared screen. There is no mask, no reveal and no sensitivity prop,
  so a reading cannot be obscured without the layout moving.
  [On-screen privacy](../health/on-screen-privacy.mdx) names this component. `Value` owns the
  digits and has no mask either, so the open question is where masking belongs rather than
  what this component should add on its own.

The tile carries its own `data-slot` on the root and on each part, so a rule of your own reaches
the header, the label, the reading or the time line without an export. It stamps `data-category`
on the icon and the label alone, which is the one axis the tile's own surface may carry, and it
stamps no `data-status` anywhere: the level lives on the embedded [StatusPill](./status-pill.mdx),
which owes the word, the glyph and the colour together, so `data-status` rides on that pill and
never on the tile. `data-opsinjs-value` belongs to [Value](./value.mdx), holding the unrounded
number a product scrapes or styles by, and an empty string beside an absence rather than a zero.
The two axes stay on two elements here by construction, because a category attribute and a status
attribute never land on one node.

## CSS variables

<CssVariablesTable name="metric-tile" />

MetricTile exposes no custom property of its own for a consumer to set. Its colours are the
semantic token roles listed under Tokens below, the category ink on the icon and label and the
muted foreground on a stale reading, read straight from the token layer rather than through a
component-scoped variable, so there is nothing of its own to override here. It does read two
token roles directly by name, the corner shape it shares with every card and the target-minimum
floor that keeps a linked tile tappable, and both are roles it consumes rather than variables it
declares. To restyle any part, write a rule against its `data-slot`.

## Tokens

<TokenTable component="metric-tile" />

These are the tokens the tile actually reads, generated from its source by
`scripts/build-tokens.mts` rather than listed by hand. They are the category ink roles for the
icon and the label, the muted foreground for a stale reading, the `card` material and its rung,
the spacing steps that set the rhythm between the parts, and the type steps for the label and the
time line. A health component never reads a category token and a status token onto one element,
which is why no status role appears among the tile's own tokens: the level's colour belongs to
the embedded StatusPill, and a row here that paired a category tint with a status tint on one
element would be the two-axis defect this table exists to expose.

## API reference

<PropsTable name="MetricTileProps" />

Five props must be passed and none has a default: `label`, `value`, `measuredAt`, `now` and
`precision`. `label` and `value` are two of the tile's three claims, and `measuredAt` is the
third when there is a reading, dating it. It is typed `string | null` rather than a plain
string, because a tile whose `value` is `null` has no measurement to date and passes `null`
there. The compiler still asks for the prop, so a caller cannot forget the instant beside a
reading and ship an undated number, and `null` is the honest answer beside an absence. `now`
is the instant the age is measured against, read once where the screen is rendered and passed
to every tile on it. A component that read the clock itself would let a grid of eight
disagree with itself across a minute boundary. `precision` is required so that a TypeScript
caller cannot forget it and ship the raw digits an arithmetic result carries. The specification asks for no default
float rendering anywhere, and a required prop puts that in the compiler rather than in a
development warning alone.

Two departures from the specification's proposed API are in that table rather than hidden
behind it. **`value` is `number | null`**, not `number | string | null`: every number a
reader sees goes through [Value](./value.mdx), which formats it, resolves the spoken form of
the unit and keeps absence apart from zero, and a pre-formatted string is a number somebody
has already rounded with no spoken unit behind it. A compound reading is two measurements
and takes two tiles. **There is no `trend`**, for the reason set out under Anatomy. `now`,
`timeZone`, `showOffset`, `event`, `precision`, `locale`, `icon`, `render` and `className` are
additions: `now`
and `timeZone` are forced by `RelativeTime`, which owns the timestamp, so `timeZone` puts the
reading on the reader's own wall clock without a caller reaching past the tile, and the
offset label then drops because a named zone is already the reader's own clock; `showOffset`
also reaches `RelativeTime`, and `showOffset={false}` drops that same offset label for a
reading the reader's own device took, where the zone cannot differ from the reader's and a
label like "UTC+1" would only puzzle them, and the tile forwards it rather than deciding it
because it cannot tell where the number came from; `event` names which moment the timestamp
belongs to and defaults to *measured*, reaching `RelativeTime` as well, and because it names
the moment and never the origin, the provenance gap stays open; `precision`
and `locale` come from
[Numbers, units and precision](../health/numbers-units-precision.mdx); and `icon` from
opsinjs shipping no category icon set. The demo and the examples on this page pass
`locale="en-GB"` so their captured dates read day first, the order
[Numbers, dates and time](../content/numbers-dates-and-time.mdx) mandates. The prop stays
optional: a product passes its own BCP 47 tag, and with none set the date follows the runtime
locale rather than a house default. A demo that set no locale would capture whatever order the
build machine's locale spells.

Three props a reader may go looking for are absent, and each absence is a gap rather than a
decision: there is no provenance prop, so the tile asserts measurement it cannot verify;
there is no masking prop; and there is no way to require a destination beside a level that
asks for action. All three are recorded under Status with what closing them would take.

`ClinicalStatus` and `HealthCategory` are declared once, in the substrate, and imported from
`@/lib/opsinjs` rather than from this component.

## Cost

<BundleSize name="metric-tile" />

Bundle cost is measured from a real build, and no such measurement has been taken for any
