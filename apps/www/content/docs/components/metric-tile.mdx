The tile is the component most likely to be over-used, because a dashboard of numbers looks
productive and costs nothing to build. Most of its specification is about what a tile is not
allowed to do without a larger surface behind it, and most of the implementation is about
refusing to fill a silence the product left on purpose.

A tile that cannot be opened is a dead end. If a reader glances at a number, wonders what it
means, and has nowhere to go, the tile has raised a question the product refuses to answer.
Every clinical tile should lead to a [ResultCard](./result-card.mdx) or an equivalent
surface, which is what `href` is for.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** That this measurement had this value, in this unit, at this time. Three
claims, all of which must be on the tile: a number without a unit is ambiguous, and a number
without a time is undated, which for health data is the same as being wrong. `label`,
`value`, `measuredAt` and `now` are required for that reason, and a `measuredAt` this
component cannot locate in time renders nothing at all rather than a number a reader would
take for today's.

**Where the number came from, and why the tile cannot say.** It always says *Measured*.
`RelativeTime`'s event is fixed here and there is no provenance prop, so a figure that a
device estimated, or that somebody typed in from memory, is announced in exactly the words a
laboratory result gets. Until that is fixed, do not route a figure nobody measured through
`measuredAt`: the tile will assert a provenance the product never claimed. This is one of
the two obligations on this page's list of governors that the component does not meet. The
other is the missing masking affordance, and both are listed above rather than left to be
discovered.

**What it must never be read as.** A current reading, unless it is one. The single most
common failure of health dashboards is a tile showing a number from six weeks ago in exactly
the typography of one taken this morning. Past the product's staleness boundary the whole
tile mutes and the timestamp says *may be out of date* in words. The treatment is a tone
change and a sentence rather than a small grey date, which is what
[Uncertainty, staleness and missing data](../health/uncertainty-and-staleness.mdx) requires.

**Which colour axis it may use.** The **category** axis, on the icon and the label, so a
reader can find the sleep tiles among the heart tiles in a grid of eight. The **status**
axis appears only as an embedded [StatusPill](./status-pill.mdx), never as the tile's
background. No element on a tile carries both, so there is no OPSIN-0001 for this component
to raise. The never-mix rule is a check on an element, not on a props object.

**Who owns the staleness boundary.** The product, and only the product. What counts as an
old reading is clinical and differs completely from one measurement to the next, and opsinjs
holds no such number for any measurement in any population. It holds none as a default, none
in an example, and none as an illustration in this paragraph, because a number written down
anywhere is a number somebody copies. `staleAfterHours` has no default and will not get one.
Omitted, there is no stale treatment at all, which is the honest output when nobody has said
what stale means here.

**Who assigns the level.** The product, and nothing here derives one. There is no comparison
of a value against anything in this component's source, because it has no range, no
threshold and no reader.

**A level and a destination belong together, and nothing here enforces it.**
[Clinical status semantics](../health/clinical-status-semantics.mdx) says of `attention`
that there is always a named action; a tile has no room for a sentence, so on a tile the
action is the tile. `status` can nevertheless be set on a tile with no `href`, and nothing
warns. Pairing them is the product's to remember. A check for it belongs in the shared
warning channel, which this component cannot add a code to on its own.

<SafetyCallout severity="attention" evidence="opinion">
A grid of status-coloured tiles is a heat map of somebody's body, and it is least readable at
the moment it matters most. Colour a tile for what the reading is *about*; put how much
attention it needs in a pill, with the word in it, on the one or two tiles the product has
actually said something about. Filling the rest with a reassuring level is inventing
verdicts. See [Alarm fatigue](../health/alarm-fatigue.mdx).
</SafetyCallout>

The parts above are identified by `data-slot` and are styleable from outside. None of them is
an export: there is no `MetricTile.Reading` component, no way to render a label without a
reading, and no prop that removes the timestamp.

**There is no sparkline, and that is the one real departure from the part tree above.** The
specification asked for a `trend` array drawn small, decorative, hidden from assistive
technology. [TrendSparkline](./trend-sparkline.mdx) shipped with a mandatory, visible
caption and a `role="img"` plot named by it, because a line through somebody's readings is a
claim that a pattern exists and the sentence beside it is the only part that says which
pattern. Those two cannot both hold on one element. That left two honest options. One is a
tile big enough to carry a real caption, which is a card, and the other is no trend at all.
This is the second: the tile is a number, a unit and a date, and the reader taps through to
the surface that can draw a line and explain it. A product that wants a line in a grid cell
composes TrendSparkline in its own layout at a size where the caption fits, which is what
the sparkline's own row-of-tiles example shows.

**`Surface` is in the tree because it is in the DOM.** The tile does not paint its own
material; it sits on the `card` rung like every other card-shaped thing in the system, so a
product that re-tunes the ladder re-tunes tiles with it.

### A summary grid

The arrangement the component exists for. Every tint is category, so a reader can find the

### No boundary was supplied

The same measurement three hours old and five weeks old, with nothing said about when a
reading of this kind stops being current. Most dashboards are actually in that state. Both
tiles are set identically, and nothing about the older one says it is older except the date
`RelativeTime` prints. That is the argument for supplying the boundary, and for taking it
from somebody who knows what was measured.

The muted treatment itself is described on this page and demonstrated by none of its
previews. Showing it takes a number of hours; opsinjs holds none for any measurement in any
population; and the version of this example that carried one taught the idea better and
shipped a boundary into every repository that installed it. In a worked example a number is
read as a recommendation whatever the caption beside it says.

## Content guidelines

Labels are two or three words in the reader's vocabulary. Write *resting heart rate*, not
*RHR*, and not *HeartRateResting*. Units are displayed as symbols and spoken in full, which
`Value` does from the unit table; a symbol the table does not hold is shown as written rather
than pronounced by guesswork. Never abbreviate the measurement into an acronym the reader has
to learn before they can use your dashboard.

There is no room for explanation on a tile, so the tile must not raise a question it cannot
answer. If the number needs a caveat, the caveat belongs on the surface the tile opens. If
there is no such surface, the caveat means the reading does not belong on a tile.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>

</DoDont>

## Accessibility

Some of this is checked by a gate on every commit. The rest is argument, and it is labelled
as such: `alpha` promises that accessibility is claimed only where it has been checked, with
the gaps listed rather than left out.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y` on every commit:**

- **Neither axis lands on one element.** The static rig fails the build on any element
  carrying both `data-status` and `data-category`, and on any element resolving a category
  colour and a status colour through the same CSS property. The icon and the label carry
  `data-category` and no status; the pill carries `data-status` and no category; the root
  carries neither.
- **No status is drawn here at all.** This file paints no status colour, reads no status
  word and stamps no `data-status`. Every one of those belongs to the embedded
  [StatusPill](./status-pill.mdx), which owes the word, the glyph and the colour together.
- **No type size in pixels, and no colour literal.** Every size comes from the eleven-step
  ramp and every colour from a role token, so the tile scales with the reader's own text
  size and re-derives itself for dark mode.
- **No staleness number ships, anywhere.** A threshold-shaped name given a numeric literal
  under `registry/bases`, `registry/examples` and `registry/screens` fails the build. The
  build checks those three directories and no others, so the same name in `lib/` or `app/`
  is unchecked. The exemption that lets an `EXAMPLE_`-prefixed constant carry a number
  deliberately does not open for a staleness prop: renaming a boundary does not stop it
  being one. Neither this component, nor its demo, nor either of its examples carries one,
  which is why the muted stale treatment appears in no preview on this page.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **An interactive tile is one link and one tab stop**, with the whole tile as the target and
  the 44pt floor carried on both axes in `rem`, so it grows when the reader raises their text
  size. Nothing this component renders inside it is focusable: the pill is a `span`, the icon
  wrapper is `aria-hidden`, and there is no second control. The `icon` slot takes a node from
  the caller, though, and a control passed there would be focusable inside an `aria-hidden`
  subtree. A control there is a second tab stop in a one-stop component, and it is missing
  from the accessibility tree. The prop says it must not be interactive and nothing checks
  that, so the claim above is scoped to what the component controls. See
  [Target size and motor](../accessibility/target-size-and-motor.mdx).
- **Its accessible name is a whole sentence.** A link takes its name from its content, and
  the parts are separated by visually-hidden commas, so the first tile of the second example
  above is announced *Example measurement, 14 steps, Watch, Measured 3 hours ago, on 6 April 2026
  at 06:00 UTC* rather than as a run of fragments. `RelativeTime` always appends the exact
  date, the time and the zone, and the reader's locale decides the word order. A tile with a
  status therefore announces five clauses and not three.
- **The unit as it is written on screen is not in that name**, and this is a constraint
  rather than a claim. `Value` hides the symbol from assistive technology and substitutes the
  spoken form, so a tile whose visible text is *kg* is named *kilograms*, and a link whose
  visible label has no substring in common with its accessible name is a WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.3
  failure. It does not bite in anything shipped here only because *steps* is both the symbol
  and the spoken form. This component offers no repair: there is no `aria-label` escape, and
  an `aria-label` would replace the whole sentence rather than mend one clause of it.
  `Value`'s own documentation assigns the duty to whoever makes a reading tappable, which is
  this component, so it is recorded here rather than left for a product using *kg*, *mmHg* or
  *%* to discover.
- **A static tile has no accessible name of its own**, and this is a real gap rather than an
  omission. Composing one would mean this component formatting the number itself, or minting
  ids for `aria-labelledby`, which needs a hook and a client boundary. Its content is in the
  same order and says the same words; it is not a single object in the rotor. Pass `href`
  where you can. The embedded `StatusPill` is given `describes` in that variant and not in
  the linked one, so the level keeps a subject either way: inside a link the label is already
  in the same name, and outside one the pill carries the label itself rather than floating in
  the reading order as a level with nothing attached to it.
- **Staleness is never carried by tone alone.** The tone and the words are two verdicts
  reached in two files, and they agree because they are the same expression on the same
  parse: this component reads an instant with the pattern, the field checks and the
  `Date.UTC` round trip `RelativeTime` uses, and compares elapsed milliseconds against the
  threshold multiplied out exactly as `RelativeTime` does. An earlier version of this file
  diverged on both, and a timestamp one accepted and the other refused produced a fully
  greyed tile with no words in it. That is tone alone, which is the state the doctrine
  forbids. Because the words are the carrier, the state survives greyscale, increased
  contrast and a black-and-white printout. The duplication itself is an open question above,
  not a solved problem.
- **The muted treatment is carried by the parts, not by the tile's root.** That is what stops
  a `className` from outside deleting it, because `tailwind-merge` files an opsin type step
  and an opsin colour in one conflict group and keeps whichever arrives last. It is also what
  keeps the muting off the `StatusPill`: a verdict the product assigned is not withdrawn
  because the reading has aged.
- **Reading order matches visual order**: label, reading, status, time. Nothing is
  re-arranged by CSS against the DOM.
- **Nothing animates.** No count-up, no entrance transition, no pulse. A number that counts
  up has displayed, for every frame of the count, a figure that is not true. See
  [Motion in health UI](../health/motion-in-health-ui.mdx).
- **No live region is mounted.** When a reading changes on screen the tile says nothing on
  the caller's behalf; only [AlertBanner](./alert-banner.mdx) is entitled to interrupt.

**Not yet checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- Contrast for the label, the reading, the unit, the category tint and the muted stale
  treatment, in both themes and under increased contrast. `scripts/check-contrast.mts` holds
  no pairs for this component, which is why the report below is empty rather than green.
  `prefers-contrast` and `forced-colors` are not implemented anywhere in opsinjs yet, so no
  increased-contrast claim can currently be made by anyone. One pair on that list is worse
  than unmeasured: `tailwind-merge` drops `StatusPill`'s own ink colour before it reaches the
  DOM, because its type step and its colour land in one conflict group. The pill's word and
  glyph therefore render in whatever colour they inherit, on a status-tinted fill. That is a
  defect in [StatusPill](./status-pill.mdx) rather than in this component and is reported
  there, but it lands on the one element on a tile that carries a clinical verdict.
- Behaviour at 200% text. The tile sets no fixed height, no `truncate` and no
  `whitespace-nowrap`, and `Value` keeps the number and its unit together with a no-break
  space. The behaviour has nevertheless not been observed at that size, and `className` can
  override every one of those from outside. The grid in the first example does not reflow
  under this site's own 200% control either: Tailwind's breakpoints are `rem` inside a media
  query, and `rem` there resolves against the document's initial font size rather than the
  root element's computed one, so the columns stay put while the type doubles.
- Print. The date is always in the printed output because `RelativeTime` puts it there, and
  the tile declares `print:border` so its boundary does not depend on a background the
  browser drops. Whether that reads as a boundary on paper has not been looked at, and the
  docs site's own print rules do not travel with the component. It prints as a tile and not
  as the plain list the specification asks for: turning a grid into a list is a decision
  about the grid, which a tile cannot see.
- Masking a reading on a shared screen. There is no mask, no reveal and no sensitivity prop,
  so a reading cannot be obscured without the layout moving.
  [On-screen privacy](../health/on-screen-privacy.mdx) names this component. `Value` owns the
  digits and has no mask either, so the open question is where masking belongs rather than
  what this component should add on its own.

## API reference

<PropsTable name="MetricTileProps" />

Four props are required and none has a default: `label`, `value`, `measuredAt` and `now`.
The first three are the tile's three claims; the fourth is the instant the age is measured
against, read once where the screen is rendered and passed to every tile on it. A component
that read the clock itself would let a grid of eight disagree with itself across a minute
boundary.

Two departures from the specification's proposed API are in that table rather than hidden
behind it. **`value` is `number | null`**, not `number | string | null`: every number a
reader sees goes through [Value](./value.mdx), which formats it, resolves the spoken form of
the unit and keeps absence apart from zero, and a pre-formatted string is a number somebody
has already rounded with no spoken unit behind it. A compound reading is two measurements
and takes two tiles. **There is no `trend`**, for the reason set out under Anatomy. `now`,
`precision`, `locale`, `icon` and `className` are additions: the first is forced by
`RelativeTime`, the next two by
[Numbers, units and precision](../health/numbers-units-precision.mdx), and the fourth by
opsinjs shipping no category icon set.

Three props a reader may go looking for are absent, and each absence is a gap rather than a
decision: there is no provenance prop, so the tile asserts measurement it cannot verify;
there is no masking prop; and there is no way to require a destination beside a level that
asks for action. All three are recorded under Status with what closing them would take.

`ClinicalStatus` and `HealthCategory` are declared once, in the substrate, and imported from
