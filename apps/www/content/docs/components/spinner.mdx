Spinner is an indeterminate loading indicator: a small ring that turns while a brief, in-place wait resolves. It says nothing about what is arriving or how long it will take, only that something is on its way, and that narrowness is the whole reason it sits so low on the list of things to reach for.

The design decision that shapes the component is that a skeleton is almost always the better answer. A skeleton is the outline of the thing itself, so it says what is arriving; a spinner says only that something is. A spinner earns its place in the few spots where the shape of what is coming is genuinely unknown, or where there is no room to draw an outline at all, and everywhere else the honest indicator is a [Skeleton](./skeleton.mdx).

## When to use it

<WhenToUse
  use={[
    "A brief in-place wait whose result has no known shape, such as a short action whose response could take one of a few forms, where a skeleton would have no single outline to promise.",
    "A spot too small to draw an outline in, such as a single toolbar slot or a compact inline control, where a skeleton has nothing to say and a quiet ring reads as working.",
    "A wait short enough that a skeleton would flash in and straight back out, where a calm ring is steadier than an outline that barely appears before the content replaces it.",
  ]}
  avoid={[

    { case: "The wait belongs to a control performing its own action, such as a submit button saving a reading, where the control already shows that it is working.", instead: "button" },
    { case: "You can measure how far through a known sequence of steps the reader is, so the wait is determinate rather than indeterminate.", instead: "stepper" },
  ]}

The exclusion that matters is the first, and it is the reason this component sits so low on the roster. A skeleton is the outline of the thing that has not arrived, so it tells the reader what is coming and roughly how large it will be, and a spinner dropped where that outline could have gone has discarded all of it. So the default indicator across the system is a [Skeleton](./skeleton.mdx), and a spinner is what remains only when there is no shape to draw or no room to draw it in.

The determinate case is worth naming precisely, because it is not what this component is for. A wait you can measure, a download at forty percent or a job three of five steps through, is a determinate wait, and the honest indicator states how far along it is rather than turning forever. For a named sequence of steps that is a [Stepper](./stepper.mdx). For a raw percentage bar it is a progress bar, which opsinjs delegates to Base UI rather than shipping; the reasoning is on the [Progress](./progress.mdx) page. A spinner that stands in for either shows motion where it could have shown position.

The parts above are identified by `data-slot` and are styleable from outside; none of them is an export, because there is nothing to slot into a spinner and no way to render a ring without its live region. There is deliberately no colour prop and no `Spinner.Track`: the ring is one arc in the ink it inherits, and a second faint track ring would either need a second colour, which puts the component near the axes it stays off, or an opacity wash, which drifts the arc towards its background. The one route by which colour reaches the ring is `className`, and the two-colour-axes rule applies to it in full.

## Content guidelines

Keep a spinner to a brief wait, and name what is being waited for. Where the screen has the room, "Loading your readings" tells a reader which part of the page is not ready in a way that a bare "Loading" does not. If the wait is long enough that a reader will wonder whether anything is happening, a ring is the wrong indicator: a long wait wants either a skeleton that shows what is coming or a determinate indicator that shows how far along it is, because a ring that turns for thirty seconds says nothing new after the first one.

Do not spin where a shape could be shown. A list, a card, a chart and a paragraph all have outlines, so their loading state is a [Skeleton](./skeleton.mdx) that promises the shape rather than a ring that hides it. Reserve the ring for the wait with no shape and no room, and let the visible caption beside it carry the words while the ring carries only the motion.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **A ring beside "Loading" for a two-second action with no known shape.** The
    ring shows that work is happening, the caption names the wait, and the whole
    thing is gone before a reader has time to wonder what is coming, which is the
    wait a spinner is for.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **A ring where a list is loading.** The list has an outline, so its loading

    coming and roughly how long. A ring there throws that shape away and leaves the
    reader waiting on a turning circle with nothing to expect.
  </DoDont.Dont>

## Accessibility

Some of this is enforced by a gate on every pull request and every push to `main`, and the rest is argued and labelled as such. At `alpha`, accessibility is claimed only where it has been checked, with the gaps listed rather than left out. Nothing here has been through a review with a disabled user.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y`:**

- **No status colour, no colour literal, no ramp step.** The ring draws only in `currentColor`, so it carries no `data-status`, reads no clinical vocabulary, and cannot mix the two axes. The gate reads every element and would fail the build on a colour drawn from either axis or on a raw colour value, and there is neither here.
- **No px size.** The ring is sized in `em` and the visible caption in a semantic type step, so a reader who has turned their text size up gets a larger ring rather than a pinned one.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **The wait is announced, and the ring is not.** The root is a `role="status"` live region with the `label` as its `aria-label` and a visually hidden copy of the text inside it, so a screen reader announces the wait by name when the spinner mounts. The SVG ring is `aria-hidden`, so the decorative motion is kept out of the accessibility tree.
- **The label is required.** A spinner with no name announces a wait for something the reader cannot identify, so a missing or empty `label` raises a development warning rather than rendering an unnamed live region.
- **It stops under reduced motion.** `motion-reduce:animate-none` holds the ring still for a reader who has asked the platform for less movement, and because the wait is carried by the live region and the hidden text rather than by the motion, nothing a reader needs is lost with the ring stopped.

**Not checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- The contrast of the ring against the surfaces it sits on, in either theme. `scripts/check-contrast.mts` has not been run for this component, which is why the report below is empty rather than reassuring.
- What the ring draws under `forced-colors: active`, where an SVG stroke is left where the author set it rather than remapped to a system colour, and there is no `forced-colors` rule here to remap it. None of this has been put in front of a machine or a high contrast user.

## API reference

<PropsTable name="SpinnerProps" />

`label` is required, and it is the load-bearing prop: it is the accessible name for the wait, applied as the region's `aria-label` and rendered as a visually hidden copy inside it, so the wait is announced by name rather than as a bare "busy". `size` chooses between two neutral weights and nothing else, both sized in `em` so the ring scales with the surrounding text. There is no colour prop, because a spinner is on neither the category axis nor the status axis; colour reaches the ring only through `className`, and the two-colour-axes rule applies to it in full.

## Related

<RelatedComponents name="spinner" />

**Easily confused with**

- [Skeleton](./skeleton.mdx) is the outline of content that has not arrived, so it says what is coming rather than only that something is. It is the default loading indicator across the system, and a spinner is what remains where no shape can be drawn.
- [Button](./button.mdx) shows its own busy state while it performs an action, and it draws that busy glyph inline rather than composing a Spinner. A wait that belongs to a control belongs on the control.
- [Progress](./progress.mdx) and a stepped [Stepper](./stepper.mdx) show a determinate wait, how far along a known task the reader is. A spinner is for the indeterminate wait, where there is no position to show.

**Used by**

- No pattern, recipe or screen composes it yet. It is a neutral primitive a product drops into its own brief, shapeless waits, and [Button](./button.mdx) draws its own busy glyph rather than reaching for this one.
