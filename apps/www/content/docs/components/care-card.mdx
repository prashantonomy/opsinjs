CareCard is where a health product stops describing and starts asking. That makes
attribution the whole specification: the reader must always be able to tell whether a
clinician said this, a rule fired, or the product is guessing.

A CareCard with no verb is not a CareCard. If you cannot write the instruction as *do this
thing, by this time*, what you have is information, and information belongs in a
[Callout](./callout.mdx) where it is not dressed up as a demand.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** That someone — named — is asking this reader to do this specific thing,
optionally by a stated time, in response to a stated reason. Four claims, and the author is
one of them.

**What it must never be read as.** Advice from a clinician who has assessed this person,
unless a clinician has assessed this person and the card says so. This is the most important
sentence on the page. A card that says *repeat this test in three months* with no author is
read as clinical advice, and the product has no way to withdraw a claim it never explicitly
made. Attribution is a required part, not a footnote.

**Which colour axis it may use.** The **status** axis, on the card's heading rule and icon,
never as a full-bleed background fill. The **category** axis may not appear at all: a card
telling somebody to contact their clinic is not a sleep card or a heart card, it is an
instruction, and tinting it by category makes the instruction look like a topic.

**Urgency lives in the heading.** *Do this today*, *do this within a week*, *when you next
get a chance* — a phrase in the heading, not a colour and not an icon. A reader who scans
only the headings must come away with the right timing, and a reader with no colour
perception must lose nothing.

**Who owns the rules.** The consuming product. CareCard has no clinical logic, no default
timings, and no library of instructions.

<SafetyCallout severity="high" evidence="opinion">
At most one urgent CareCard per screen, and it names an action the reader can actually take
right now. A card that says *seek medical attention immediately* with no number, no link and
no next step has transferred the product's anxiety to the reader without transferring
anything useful. If the instruction is to call emergency services, say the words and give
the number for the reader's country.
</SafetyCallout>

## Anatomy

<Anatomy name="care-card" />

<CompositionTree name="care-card" />

**Heading** carries the instruction and its timing. **Reason** is one sentence saying what
prompted it, which is what turns an order into something a reader can evaluate.
**Attribution** names the author — a clinic, a service, or the product's own rule.
**Actions** are at most two. **Deadline** is the date or window, rendered as a
[RelativeTime](./relative-time.mdx) with the absolute date always available.

Proposed part hierarchy. This is the specification the implementation must match.

```text
CareCard                       root; surface stays neutral, heading rule carries status
├── CareCard.Heading           the instruction and its timing, in words
├── CareCard.Reason            one sentence: what prompted this
├── CareCard.Attribution       who is asking — required, never inferred
├── CareCard.Deadline          optional; <RelativeTime> with the absolute date available
└── CareCard.Actions           at most 2; the first is the recommended one
```

A CareCard without `CareCard.Attribution` is a composition error and should fail loudly in
development. Everything else on this list is optional; the author is not.

## Proposed API

```ts
// PROPOSED. Not implemented. A specification for review, not an API to generate
// code against. It will change without a deprecation cycle.

import type { ClinicalStatus } from "opsinjs"

/** Urgency as words, because a colour cannot say "within a week". */
export type CareUrgency = "when-convenient" | "this-week" | "today"

export interface CareAction {
  label: string
  href?: string
  onSelect?: () => void
  recommended?: boolean
}

export interface CareCardProps {
  /** The instruction. Starts with a verb. */
  heading: string
  /** Rendered in the heading as words, never as colour alone. */
  urgency: CareUrgency
  /**
   * Who is asking. Required, and free text rather than an enum, because
   * "your GP surgery" and "an automatic reminder from this app" are both true
   * answers and the difference matters more than any category we could invent.
   */
  attribution: string
  /** One sentence: what prompted this instruction. */
  reason?: string
  /** ISO date. Rendered relatively, with the absolute date always available. */
  dueBy?: string
  /** At most two. */
  actions?: CareAction[]
  /**
   * Optional link to the clinical status of the thing that prompted this, for
   * the heading rule and icon only.
   */
  status?: ClinicalStatus
}
```

## Content guidelines

Start with a verb. Put the timing in the heading. Attribute in every card, in the reader's
words: *your GP surgery asks*, *your clinic's automatic reminder*, *this app, based on the
range you set*. Say the reason in one sentence — a reader who understands why will comply
more reliably and worry less than one who has only been told.

Never write *you must*, *you need to*, *failure to*, or *it is important that you*. They add
pressure without adding information, and pressure is the thing this component is most likely
to over-supply.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"Book a repeat blood test in the next week. Your clinic asks for a repeat because your
    last result was slightly outside the usual range. Due by 12 October."** Verb, timing,
    author, reason, deadline.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **"Action required: abnormal result. Please contact your healthcare provider as soon as
    possible."** No author, a banned word, no reason, and a timing so vague the reader
    cannot tell whether to phone now or next month.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"This is an automatic reminder from this app, not advice from your clinic."** One
    sentence that tells the reader exactly how much weight to give it.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **Leaving attribution out because the design looks cleaner without it.** The reader fills
    the gap with the most authoritative answer available, which is the wrong one.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

## Accessibility requirements

Requirements for the implementation, not results.

- **The card is a region with an accessible name** taken from its heading, so it can be
  found by region or heading navigation without reading the page.
- **Urgency is in the text.** Nothing about the timing is carried by colour, icon or position
  alone, so grayscale, colour-vision deficiency and text-only rendering lose nothing.
- **Reading order is heading, reason, attribution, deadline, actions**, matching the visual
  order.
- **Actions meet the 44pt floor** with 8pt separation, and their names say what they do
  rather than *here* or *more*. A telephone action is a real `tel:` link with the number
  visible as text as well.
- **A passed deadline is stated in words**, not signalled by a colour change, and the card
  says what to do about it now.
- **The card never appears with an entrance animation that implies urgency**; see
  [Motion in health UI](../health/motion-in-health-ui.mdx).
- **At 200% text nothing truncates**, and the actions stack rather than shrinking.
- **Print** renders the whole card including attribution and deadline. These cards are
  printed and taken to appointments, and the author is the part that must survive.
