ScaleInput is a row of numbered points for a self-report the product reads: the reader taps one point to say where they sit between two ends. Its home is a single question a product asks over and over so a reader can watch it move, a comfort rating or a mood rating being the shape it was built for, where the same scale on Monday and on Friday is the whole value.

The design decision that shaped the whole component is that opsinjs ships no scale of its own. A rating is comparable over time only when its number of points and its end words stay fixed, and those belong to whichever validated instrument the product has chosen. So the reader is rating something the product names, across as many points as the product passes, between end words the product supplies. opsinjs contributes the chrome and the keyboard behaviour and nothing a clinician would recognise.

The distinction that matters most is the last one. A ScaleInput captures one rating; a validated instrument is usually several rated items under one heading with a defined way of reading them together, and opsinjs ships no dedicated questionnaire component today. Build such an instrument from a [Field](./field.mdx) per item, each holding whatever control the item needs, and keep the reading of the items together in the product where the instrument's own rules live. A ScaleInput is one item of that, never the whole of it.

The other two exclusions are about the shape of the answer. If the reader answers in their own words, the control is a [Textarea](./textarea.mdx), because a scale cannot hold a sentence. If the choices are a fixed set that has no low end and no high end, the control is a [RadioGroup](./radio-group.mdx), because numbering unordered options as a scale invents an ordering the product did not mean.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** ScaleInput asserts exactly one thing: the reader chose this point on a scale the product defined. It records a self-report and hands it straight back through `onValueChange`. It adds no total, no band, no verdict and no interpretation on top of the number, because what a rating means is the product's to say, in the product's own words, on the product's own surfaces.

**What it must never be read as.** It is not a score, not a measurement and not a reading the component produced. The number is what the reader said about themselves, so it must never be dressed up as a result opsinjs derived. A high point at the far end is a high self-report and nothing more: whether it matters is a clinical judgement this component does not make and must not appear to make. It never diagnoses, triages or advises, and [Who this is for](../health/who-this-is-for.mdx) is why a layperson rating themselves is owed a plain scale rather than a clinical register.

**Which colour axis it may use.** Neither. A rating is a self-report, not a level of urgency and not a kind of measurement, so the scale carries neither `data-status` nor `data-category` and draws only neutral chrome. The chosen point is lifted by the neutral primary role, the same emphasis a ticked checkbox uses, deliberately not by the clinical status axis: painting a far point as urgent would put the component's own verdict on the reader's answer, which is the collision [The two colour axes](../health/two-colour-axes.mdx) exists to prevent. If a product decides a rating matters, it says so on a separate status surface with the word and the glyph that surface carries, never by tinting the point the reader pressed.

**Who owns the thresholds and the words.** The consuming product, always. ScaleInput ships no anchor words, no default number of points, no scale, no scoring and no default text of its own. The point count arrives through `points` and the end words through `minLabel` and `maxLabel`, because the instrument those belong to is a clinical decision the product makes, and shipping a scale inside a component library would be shipping that decision to every product that installed it. [Clinical interaction guidelines](../health/clinical-interaction-guidelines.mdx) and [Numbers, units and precision](../health/numbers-units-precision.mdx) set out why the number and its meaning stay with the product.

### No answer yet

The honest empty state, before the reader has answered. `value` is `null`, so the scale draws with no point chosen rather than defaulting to a middle point the reader never picked. This is why the value is `number | null` and not a plain number: a scale that guessed a starting answer would hand the product a reading nobody gave, so an unanswered scale stays empty until it is answered.

## Content guidelines

Write the `label` as the thing being rated in the reader's words, "Comfort right now" rather than "1 to 10", because it is the accessible name a screen-reader user hears before the points and the only place the question is stated. Keep the end words short and opposite, a low end and a high end the reader places themselves between, and take both from the instrument the product uses rather than inventing them at the call site, so the scale means the same thing on every screen it appears on.

Never write a verdict into an end word. A word names one end of the scale; it does not tell the reader what their answer says about them. Keep the number of points fixed for a given question across the whole product, because a reader who rated themselves on a ten point scale last week cannot compare that to a five point scale this week, and a scale whose length drifts is a scale that measures nothing over time.

## Accessibility

Some of this is enforced by a gate on every pull request and every push to `main`, and the rest is argued and labelled as such. Accessibility is claimed only where it has been checked, with the gaps listed rather than left out. Nothing here has been through a review with a disabled user.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y`:**

- **The scale is one tab stop with a roving focus.** Base UI's RadioGroup renders `role="radiogroup"` on its Composite primitive, each point is a Radio rendering `role="radio"` with `aria-checked`, and selection follows focus, which is the WAI-ARIA radio group pattern rather than a bespoke one. A row of separate buttons would be one tab stop per point, which is the contract this control exists to refuse.
- **No element takes colour from both axes.** The gate reads every JSX element and fails on one carrying both `data-category` and `data-status`. This file writes neither, so a mixture cannot arise, and the chosen point is the neutral primary role rather than a status colour.
- **No shipped scale and no raw colour literal.** The point count and the end words arrive as props, no scale, threshold or default word is written into the component or its examples, and every colour is a semantic token role.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **Every point clears the target floor.** Each point floors its hit area at `--opsin-target-minimum` in rem on both axes, so a 44pt target survives and grows with the reader's text size rather than pinning at a device pixel, and the row wraps rather than shrinking a point below the floor to stay on one line.
- **The focus ring is carried in the component's own classes.** A project installed without the product stylesheet keeps the ring, because it is written into the point's class list rather than left to a sheet that might be absent.
- **The end words are in the accessible name of their points.** The first point's accessible name folds in `minLabel` and the last point's folds in `maxLabel`, so a screen-reader user hears the number and the end word together rather than a bare number at each end.

**Not checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- Every contrast pair: the chosen point's primary fill on its foreground ink, and the resting point's card surface and border hairline, against the two grounds this scale is dropped onto. `scripts/check-contrast.mts` has not been run for this component, which is why the report below is empty rather than reassuring, and this component sets no surface of its own, so each pair is painted on whatever ground the host provides.
- How the row plays for a screen-reader user, and whether folding the end words into the first and last point's name reads as a scale rather than a list of numbers. It has been reasoned about and not listened to.
- Whether the scale owes Home and End as well as the Arrow keys. The underlying RadioGroup does not bind them, so they keep the browser default, and no reader has been asked whether a ten point scale needs a jump to each end.

## API reference

<PropsTable name="ScaleInputProps" />

`points` and the two end words are the whole of what a product supplies, and none of them has a default: opsinjs ships no scale length and no anchor words, because those belong to the instrument the product uses. A `points` below two renders nothing and warns in development, because a rating with one point is not a scale.

`value` and `onValueChange` make this a controlled component with no internal selection state. `value` is `number | null`: a whole number from 1 to `points` for a chosen point, or `null` for no answer yet, which draws the scale empty rather than defaulting to a middle point. A number outside that range renders nothing chosen and warns in development. `label` is required, because a radiogroup with no accessible name is a defect the type system cannot otherwise prevent.
