A disclaimer that appears everywhere is read nowhere, and a disclaimer that appears nowhere
is a liability. This component exists so the placement decision is made once, in the system,
rather than twenty times by twenty different people under deadline.

Repetition is the failure mode this component exists to prevent. One note per surface, placed
by the rule, and never repeated beside every individual result: a screen that seems to need
four notes is a screen showing four things that each need explaining, and four identical
notes will explain none of them.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** That this product provides information rather than clinical assessment,
that it does not replace advice from a clinician, and — where relevant — that it is not a
medical device.

**What it must never be read as.** A waiver, an excuse, or a substitute for designing safely.
A disclaimer does not make an unsafe screen safe, and no regulator, and no reader, has ever
been persuaded otherwise. It also must never be read as *this number is unreliable*: the
note is about the product's role, not about the quality of the measurement.

**Which colour axis it may use.** Neither. The note is quiet, neutral and typographic. Status
colour on a standing disclaimer is a permanent false alarm, and category colour makes a legal
statement look like a topic.

**Where it goes.** Below the content it qualifies, above the surface's footer, once per
surface. Never above the result — a reader who came to see a number should see the number
first. Never in a collapsed section, and never in a modal the reader must dismiss before
they can read the thing they came for.

**Who owns the words.** The consuming product, in consultation with whoever is accountable
for it. opsinjs does not ship legal text, and nothing on this page is legal advice.
[Regulatory context](../health/regulatory-context.mdx) sets out what DCB0129, DCB0160, the
EU MDR and FDA SaMD expectations ask of the *consuming* product — the design system confers
no status of any kind.

<SafetyCallout severity="attention" evidence="opinion">
If a product finds itself relying on this component to make a screen acceptable, the screen
is the problem. Fix the screen. The note exists to set expectations honestly, not to
transfer risk onto the reader.
</SafetyCallout>

## Anatomy

<CompositionTree name="disclaimer-note" />

**Icon** is optional and never a warning glyph — this is not an alert. **Text** is one or two
sentences. **Link** points to a fuller statement for readers who want it. That is the whole
component, deliberately: every part that could be added is a part that could be used to make
the note louder.

Proposed part hierarchy. This is the specification the implementation must match.

```text
DisclaimerNote                 root; neutral, quiet, never status-coloured
├── DisclaimerNote.Icon        optional; informational glyph, never a warning triangle
├── DisclaimerNote.Text        1–2 sentences
└── DisclaimerNote.Link        optional; to the product's fuller statement
```

## Content guidelines

Two sentences at most. Say what the product does, then what it does not do. Use plain words:
*this app*, not *the Service*; *a doctor or nurse*, not *a qualified healthcare
professional*, unless a specific obligation requires the formal phrase.

Never write *always consult your doctor before making any decisions* on every screen. It is
unactionable, it is not what the reader is being asked to do, and repeated often enough it
teaches people that the app has nothing useful to say.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"This app shows you your own readings and information about them. It does not diagnose
    conditions and it is not a substitute for advice from your doctor or nurse."** States the
    role, then the limit, in words a reader will actually parse.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **"The Service is provided for informational purposes only and does not constitute medical
    advice, diagnosis or treatment. Reliance on any information provided is solely at your own
    risk."** Written for a court, not for a reader, and the final clause is an attempt to move
    risk rather than to inform.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **One quiet note beneath the results, in the same place on every results screen.**
    Predictable, findable, ignorable by people who have read it once.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **A yellow warning-styled panel at the top of every screen.** It occupies the position the
    reader's actual information should have, it looks like an alert, and within a week nobody
    sees either.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

## Accessibility requirements

Requirements for the implementation, not results.

- **The note is ordinary text in the reading order**, not a live region, not an alert, and
  never announced over what the reader is doing.
- **It meets the same contrast floor as body text.** Legal text set in pale grey is a
  well-established way of complying with the letter of a requirement and none of its
  purpose — see [Contrast conformance](../accessibility/contrast-conformance.mdx).
- **The text is at least the body text size** and scales with the rest of the page at 200%.
  It never becomes a smaller class of type that Dynamic Type leaves behind.
- **If an icon is present it is decorative** and never a warning triangle: the shape sets the
  register before a word is read.
- **The link, when present, names its destination** — *read our full statement*, not
  *learn more*.
- **Print** includes the note. A printed result that omits the product's own statement about
  what it is misrepresents it.
