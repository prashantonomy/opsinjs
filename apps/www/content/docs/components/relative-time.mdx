Relative time is a small kindness that turns into a hazard at the edges. *Two days ago* is
easier to act on than a date; *fourteen months ago* is not, and *this morning* attached to a
reading from a different time zone is simply false.

The third exclusion is a threshold the product must choose. Somewhere past a few weeks,
relative time stops helping and starts obscuring; the component switches to the absolute date
at a boundary the product sets, and it says which boundary it used.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** That the event it labels happened at that instant, and — through its
staleness treatment — whether the product still considers the associated value current.

**What it must never be read as.** Freshness of the data as a whole. *Updated 5 minutes ago*
on a dashboard means the app synced five minutes ago; it does not mean the readings are five
minutes old. Conflating the two is one of the most common and most consequential errors in
health dashboards, and the component's labels must always name which event they refer to:
*measured*, *recorded*, *received*, *synced*.

**Which colour axis it may use.** Neither, in its ordinary form. The stale treatment is a
muted, non-status typographic change accompanied by explicit words — never an amber tint,
which would put a clinical status onto a fact about the clock.

**Staleness is clinical and belongs to the product.** What counts as old differs completely
by measurement: a weight from last week is current, a continuous glucose reading from twenty
minutes ago may not be. RelativeTime therefore ships no default threshold. When a product
supplies one and it is exceeded, the component says so in words as well as in style — *4
months ago · may be out of date*. See
[Uncertainty, staleness and missing data](../health/uncertainty-and-staleness.mdx).

**Precision follows recency.** Minutes for the last hour, hours for the last day, days for
the last fortnight, then dates. Announcing *2 hours and 14 minutes ago* implies a precision
the reader neither needs nor believes.

## Anatomy

<Anatomy name="relative-time" />

<CompositionTree name="relative-time" />

**Prefix** names the event — *measured*, *synced*, *recorded* — and is not optional in health
contexts, where a bare relative time is ambiguous about what it refers to. **Relative** is the
human phrase. **Absolute** is the exact date and time, always reachable and always present in
the accessibility tree. **Staleness** is the explicit note that appears past the threshold.

Proposed part hierarchy. This is the specification the implementation must match.

```text
RelativeTime                   root; renders a <time> element with a machine datetime
├── RelativeTime.Prefix        which event this is: measured · recorded · synced
├── RelativeTime.Relative      the human phrase, precision following recency
├── RelativeTime.Absolute      exact date and time; always in the accessibility tree
└── RelativeTime.Staleness     explicit words past the product's threshold
```

The root always renders a `time` element carrying a machine-readable datetime, so the exact
instant survives copy, export and scraping even when the visible text is a phrase.

## Content guidelines

Always name the event. *Measured 3 days ago* is a fact; *3 days ago* is a fragment the reader
completes with whichever assumption is most convenient.

Use the words people use. *This morning*, *yesterday*, *last Tuesday* beat *8 hours ago* for
anything within a day or two, because that is how people locate their own actions in time.
Past a fortnight, use the date.

Never write *just now* for anything the product cannot vouch for to the minute, and never
write *up to date* — it is a judgement about the data, not a statement about the clock.

## Accessibility requirements

Requirements for the implementation, not results.

- **The absolute date and time are always in the accessibility tree**, not only on hover or
  focus. A relative phrase alone is the least useful form for anyone who cannot see the
  surrounding context.
- **The announcement is a whole phrase**: *measured 3 days ago, on 14 September at 7:14 am*.
- **Staleness is stated in words**, never carried by opacity or colour alone, so it survives
  grayscale, increased contrast and print.
- **The element does not announce itself when it ticks over.** A relative time that refreshes
  on an interval must not be a live region; a reader would hear it interrupt for no reason.
- **Hydration is safe.** Rendering a relative phrase on the server produces a value that is
  already wrong on arrival; the component resolves this without a visible flash and without a
  mismatch warning — see
  [Server and client components](../handbook/server-and-client-components.mdx).
- **Time zones are explicit.** The absolute form states the zone when it differs from the
  reader's current one, because a reading *at 7:14 am* in another zone is not the reading they
  remember taking.
- **Print** renders the absolute date and time, plus the staleness note. A relative phrase on
  paper is undated.
