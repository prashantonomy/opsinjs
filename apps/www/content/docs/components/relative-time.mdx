Relative time is a small kindness that turns into a hazard at the edges. *Two days ago* is
easier to act on than a date; *fourteen months ago* is not, and a phrase attached to the
wrong event is false in the most reassuring possible direction. The wrong event is the app's
last sync rather than the reading's own timestamp.

## Preview

Five rows across the ladder: minutes, hours, a few days and about a dozen days, and then a
reading months old. The two recent rows show the phrase on its own, because within a day
recency is the point; the three older rows show the exact date beside the phrase, because past
a day a phrase alone starts to cost the reader arithmetic. The date is in the accessibility
tree and in print for every row, including the two recent ones, and it only comes onto the
screen once the reading is older than a day. Four of the five keep their zone label; the
measured row shows the date with no zone because that reading came from the reader's own
device, where the zone cannot differ from theirs and the label would only be noise. That is
the one exception to the house rule that an absolute time always carries its zone, and it is
why the first Do further down, an issued clinic result, still reads "UTC" while this device
row does not. No row carries a
staleness threshold, so no row says anything about staleness: opsinjs has no number to put
there, and in a preview the number is the part that gets copied. The muted stale treatment is
therefore described on this page and demonstrated by none of its previews.

The third exclusion is a threshold the product chooses. `absoluteAfterDays` is where it goes,
and it defaults to one day, which is where the content guidance keeps a relative phrase for
recency and switches to the absolute date beyond about a day. Below the boundary a sighted
reader sees the phrase alone, and at or above it the exact date is shown beside the phrase so
nobody has to count backwards from a phrase on its own. The date is in the accessibility tree
and in print at every age regardless, so this prop moves only what is on screen. It is a
legibility boundary and not a clinical one: moving it changes only when the date joins the
phrase, and it can neither add nor remove the staleness note.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** That the named event happened at that instant. Where the product has
supplied a threshold and that threshold has been passed, it also asserts that the product no
longer considers the associated value current. Nothing else. It does not know what was
measured, and it has no view about whether the reading is still true.

**What it must never be read as.** Freshness of the data as a whole. *Synced 5 minutes ago*
on a dashboard means the app spoke to a server five minutes ago; it does not mean the
readings are five minutes old. Conflating the two is one of the most common and most
consequential errors in health dashboards, which is why `event` is required, has no default,
and is never inferred: the component would have to pick one, and the reassuring pick is the
wrong one.

**What it asserts about *now*.** Less than it looks, and it does not find out for itself.
`now` is a required prop: the component never calls the clock, so the phrase is a statement
about two instants the caller named, computed once, and it owns no timer to revise it. A
surface left open for an hour is still showing the phrase it was rendered with. Four things
keep that a limitation rather than a lie. The `time` element's machine-readable timestamp is
exact and never moves. The absolute date and time are always in the accessibility tree and
always in print, and they come onto the screen beside the phrase once the reading is older
than a day, so what can go out of date is never all that a reader has. And the phrase rounds
*down* at every
rung except the minute floor, which rounds up to *1 minute ago*, while the staleness verdict
is computed from the exact elapsed time rather than from the phrase. Rounding can therefore
never make a stale reading look fresh. A product whose surface stays open across its own
boundary re-renders with a fresh `now`.

Reading the clock once per screen rather than once per timestamp is worth having for its own
sake: twenty rows that each ask the runtime what time it is can straddle a minute boundary
halfway down the list and disagree with one another about a reading they were all rendered
beside.

**Which colour axis it may use.** Neither, in any state. The stale treatment is a muted,
non-status typographic change carried by explicit words. It is never an amber tint, which
would put a clinical status onto a fact about the clock and leave the reader unable to tell
that amber from the one that means *needs attention*.

**Staleness is clinical and belongs to the product.** What counts as old differs completely
between one measurement and the next, and this component cannot see which one it has.
RelativeTime ships no default threshold, no per-event default and nothing derived from
`event`; no such number appears in its source, in its demo or in either of its examples. Omit
`staleAfterHours` and there is no stale treatment at all, which is the honest output when
nobody has said what old means here. Supply it and the timestamp always says something: past
the boundary it carries the words, and where the age could not be checked at all it carries
the same hedged words rather than falling silent, because a stale reading rendered in exactly
the tone of a fresh one is the first failure
[Uncertainty, staleness and missing data](../health/uncertainty-and-staleness.mdx) names.
The age goes unchecked where a threshold did not arrive as a number, and where a `now` could
not be parsed.

**It de-emphasises the timestamp, and only the timestamp.** The muted treatment reaches the
`time` element and nothing around it. The doctrine asks for more than that: in the stale
state the *value* is de-emphasised too and there is a route to refresh it, and neither is
something a formatter that cannot see the value is able to deliver. A surface that embeds
this component still owes both. The staleness words are one carrier of that state and not the
whole of it.

**Precision follows recency.** Minutes for the last hour, hours for the last day, and days
beyond that, with the exact date shown beside the phrase past a day. Announcing *2 hours and
14 minutes ago* implies a precision the
reader neither needs nor believes. The smallest thing the component will say is *1 minute
ago*: *just now* is forbidden for anything the product cannot vouch for to the minute, and a
phrase fixed at render time can vouch for nothing finer.

<SafetyCallout severity="attention" evidence="opinion">

The parts are `data-slot` names, not exports. There is no `RelativeTime.Absolute` to render
on its own, and that is the point: every one of them is a way of saying *when*, and a caller
who could take the absolute date out of the tree while keeping the phrase would have removed
the only part that is still true tomorrow.

There is no `status` prop, no `color` prop and no variant. The one visual state the component
has is the muted stale treatment, and it is reached by passing a threshold and exceeding it.
It is never reached by asking for it directly. Nothing opsinjs ships passes one, so no preview
on this page renders that state. It is `text-muted-foreground` on the phrase and the absolute
date rather than on the root, and the event word and the staleness words "may be out of date"
are deliberately left at full strength, so the phrase that names the state stands out exactly
when the state changes. Addressing those two slots rather than the root is also a merge-order
defence and not a style: `cn()` files an opsin type-size class in the same conflict group as a
colour, so a caller's `className` on the root would otherwise delete the muting and leave a
stale reading looking fresh.

## States

RelativeTime is a leaf formatter and a server component. It is not focusable, it
holds no control, and it has no hover, focus, pressed or disabled state, so the
interaction column of a states matrix is empty by design rather than unfilled. The
one visual state it owns is the muted stale treatment, and that is reached only by
supplying a threshold and exceeding it, never by asking for it directly. The rest
of the canonical data states belong to the surface that composes the timestamp,
because a formatter runs after the data has arrived and cannot see the fetch that
produced it.

| Data state | What RelativeTime renders |
| --- | --- |
| Error | No error surface of its own. A malformed input is refused instead: an `at` that is not an ISO 8601 timestamp with an offset, or an `event` outside the five, renders nothing at all and warns on the development console rather than showing a plausible-looking guess. An error reaching the data layer belongs to the surface that composed the timestamp. |
| Loading | Nothing to load. The component is handed a resolved string and renders synchronously in one pass, so a skeleton or a spinner is the concern of the surface fetching the reading. |
| Empty | No empty state. `at` is required, and a timestamp that cannot be read renders nothing rather than a placeholder. A reading that is absent altogether is shown by the surface around it, which is why [Value](./value.mdx) renders an explicit absence and this component does not. |
| Partial | The one place it renders part of itself. Where `now` cannot be parsed, or the reading is timestamped in the future, the relative phrase is dropped and the exact date is rendered on its own, because a dated timestamp is never wrong where a computed age would be a guess. |
| Stale | The component's only visual state. Past `staleAfterHours`, or wherever a supplied threshold could not be applied, the phrase and the exact date recede to `text-muted-foreground` while the event word and the words "may be out of date" stay at the inherited foreground, and `data-slot="relative-time-staleness"` carries those words. It is a muted typographic change, never a status tint. |

## Content guidelines

Always name the event. *Measured 3 days ago* is a fact; *3 days ago* is a fragment, and the
reader completes it with whichever assumption is most convenient.

The five words are bare past participles, and each one names a different actor. Choose the
one that is true, and pick *recorded* over *measured* only when nobody took a reading and
somebody instead wrote down a figure they believe to be true.

| Word | Who did it | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Measured | A device or a clinician took the reading. | *Measured 3 hours ago* |
| Recorded | The person or the app wrote the figure down, which may be someone typing what they believe a figure was. | *Recorded 3 days ago* |
| Received | The app got the figure from a source, which is not the person reading the screen. | *Received 13 days ago* |
| Synced | The app spoke to a server, which says nothing about how old any reading is. | *Synced 2 minutes ago* |
| Issued | A clinician or a laboratory released the result. | *Issued on 2 November 2025 at 09:00 UTC* |

*Received* names the app and never the reader, so a surface that shows it must make the
subject explicit in the words around it. A bare *Received 13 days ago* invites a layperson to
read it as themselves receiving something, which is the reassuring misreading the event word
exists to prevent. On a result, *received* belongs beside *issued* rather than instead of it:
the gap between when a laboratory released a result and when the app fetched it is the
reader's question, and only both words together answer it.

Never write *just now* for anything the product cannot vouch for to the minute, and never
write *up to date*. That is a judgement about the data, not a statement about the clock.
Neither string exists in the component, and the smallest phrase it will produce is *1 minute
ago*.

The component does not say *this morning*, *yesterday* or *last Tuesday*, and the omission is
deliberate rather than pending. Those are calendar facts in the reader's own time zone, and a
component that does not run in the reader's browser does not have one: *yesterday* offered
for anything between 24 and 48 hours old is a calendar claim made from a stopwatch, and it is
wrong about a reading taken on Monday evening and read on Wednesday morning. Where a product
knows the reader's zone and wants those words, it writes them itself and keeps the exact
instant beside them.

The absolute time names its offset by default, as *UTC* or *UTC+1*, because the component
cannot see the reader's own zone and a reading taken abroad shown as if it were local is the
error the accessibility bullet below is about. Where the product knows the instant came from
the reader's own device the zone cannot differ from theirs, and *08:12 UTC+1* then reads as a
different hour to a layperson who does not decode the label. Pass `showOffset={false}` for
that case, and leave it on wherever the zone can differ, such as a result issued by a clinic.

## Motion

RelativeTime declares no transition, no animation and no timer. It mounts once with
the phrase it was rendered with and it never changes on its own, so there is
nothing for `prefers-reduced-motion` to reduce. This is the same decision that
keeps it out of a live region, because nothing about it moves after it is rendered.
A timestamp is a health-adjacent fact, and a health value is never animated onto
the screen on first paint: there is no count-up from a lower number and no sweep of
the phrase, so the age is present the moment the element renders. A product that
re-renders the component on an interval with a fresh `now` inherits that stillness,
and it should not add a transition to soften the change, because a timestamp that
animates as it updates draws the eye to the clock rather than to the reading.

## Accessibility

Some of these are held by a gate on every pull request. The rest are arguments about the
implementation, and they are labelled as such: `beta` promises that accessibility is claimed
only where it has been checked, with the gaps listed rather than left out.

**Checked, by `node scripts/check-a11y.mts` on every pull request:**

- **No type size in px**, anywhere in the source. A reader who has raised their text size
  gets a larger timestamp, because every size the component uses is relative.
- **No raw colour and no ramp step.** The muted stale treatment is `text-muted-foreground`, a
  role the theme layer remaps for dark mode; a hex value, a Tailwind palette colour or a
  numbered step fails the build. So does any role that exists only in the documentation
  chrome and would resolve to nothing in the product.
- **No banned word**, in reader-facing copy or in an identifier. That covers the five event
  words and the staleness words, which are the only copy this component owns; the rest of what
  it emits is punctuation, the connective *on*, and an offset label.
- **A status colour could not be added quietly.** The same gate fails any file that paints
  from the status axis without stamping `data-status` and reading both the status vocabulary
  and a lucide glyph. That is what stops the stale treatment from drifting into an amber tint
  in some later commit. What it cannot prove is that the word and the glyph reach the
  surface: it reads source rather than a rendered page, and its own header says so.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **The absolute date and time are always in the accessibility tree**, not only on hover or
  focus, and not behind an affordance. A sighted reader sees them on screen once the reading
  is older than a day, and within a day sees the phrase alone; the date stays in the
  accessibility tree and in print regardless. `showAbsolute={false}` keeps the date off screen
  at every age for a dense list whose date is already visible in the row, and even then it
  stays in the accessibility tree and in print; the prop never decides whether it exists.
- **The announcement is one phrase** because the connective is the word *on* after a comma
  rather than a separator character, and the one comma reads on screen and to assistive
  technology alike. The phrase is *Measured 3 days ago, on 11 March 2026 at 08:12 UTC*.
- **Staleness is stated in words**, never by the muted tone alone, so it survives greyscale,
  increased contrast and print. The words themselves are left at the surrounding foreground
  while the phrase and the date recede, so on a surface whose text is already muted the
  staleness words sit above that low tone rather than at it, which keeps them visible without
  reading the caption. This is argued from the source and from nothing else: no preview on this
  page passes a threshold, so none of them renders the state.
- **It never announces itself.** The component mounts no live region and owns no timer:
  nothing about it changes after it is rendered, so there is nothing to interrupt a reader
  with. A product that re-renders it on an interval inherits that promise and should not
  wrap it in a live region to compensate.
- **At 200% text it wraps rather than truncates.** The root is inline, has no fixed height
  and no width, and nothing in it is shortened to an ellipsis or hidden by an overflow rule.
  The nightly truncation rig cannot read this claim, because it collects `data-opsinjs-value`
  elements and this component deliberately stamps none: that attribute marks a measurement a
  person reads as their own, and an instant is not one, so the claim rests on the source above
  and has not been read off a rendered page in this run.
- **Where the locale is resolved, hydration is safe by determinism rather than by suppression.**
  Rendering on the server and correcting in the browser is what produces a mismatch, so this
  component does neither: it has no `"use client"`, no effect, no `typeof window` branch and
  no clock read at all, which is why `now` is required rather than defaulted.
  `suppressHydrationWarning` was the alternative and is worse: it keeps the server's text and
  discards the browser's more correct one. The boundary is `locale`, and it is a real one.
  The prop is optional; an omitted one reaches `Intl` as `undefined`, where it resolves to
  whichever locale the *runtime* defaults to, so the same props render *11 March 2026* on a
  server set to en-GB and *March 11, 2026* in a browser that is not. Pass an explicit tag and
  the characters match. Every snippet and every example on this page passes one. A runtime
  built without full ICU data could still disagree with a browser about a tag they both
  accept; that has not been tested here. See
  [Server and client components](../handbook/server-and-client-components.mdx).
- **Time zones are explicit.** By default the absolute form names the offset the timestamp
  was written in, because whether it differs from the reader's own is a fact about the
  reader's browser, and of the answers available to a component that does not run there the
  offset with its name is the one that is never wrong. A product that knows the reader's zone
  supplies it as an IANA `timeZone`, and the reading is then on the reader's own wall clock
  with no label. A product that knows the instant came from the reader's own device passes
  `showOffset={false}` to drop the label. A product that knows neither leaves both off and
  gets the honest offset form.
- **It is not focusable and has no keyboard behaviour**, because it has nothing to operate.
  The absolute date is not behind a disclosure precisely so that there is no control to
  reach.

**Not yet checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- The muted stale treatment sits on `text-muted-foreground`, which the theme aliases to the
  generated chrome token `--opsin-chrome-muted-foreground` rather than to a raw value authored
  in `app/product.css`; that token resolves to neutral-600 in light and neutral-300 in dark.
  Both of those colours are measured under the neutral scope this component declares, so the
  `<ContrastReport>` below prints their rows against a neutral ground rather than an empty
  state. What stays unmeasured is the same role against the muted fill and the tinted grounds
  the treatment may also share, for which the generator emits no pair, and the treatment under
  increased contrast.
- The print behaviour is asserted from the source and has not been checked in a real print
  preview or a PDF export. In that source the absolute date is revealed by a print variant

These are the hooks a surface styles or tests against, transcribed from the
component source rather than derived. RelativeTime stamps no `data-status` and no
`data-category`, because it paints from neither colour axis, and the accessibility
gate refuses any file that paints a status colour without the matching
`data-status`, which is what stops the stale treatment from drifting into an amber
tint in a later commit. It stamps no `data-opsinjs-value` either, because that
attribute marks a measurement a person reads as their own and an instant is not
one. The exact instant travels instead on the `datetime` attribute of the root
`time` element, which carries the caller's `at` string unmodified so the instant
survives copy, export and print even when the visible text is a phrase.

RelativeTime declares no CSS custom property of its own. It sets no inline `style`,

treatment restyles the `data-slot` parts rather than reaching for a custom property

## API reference

`at`, `event` and `now` are required, and none of them has a default: an instant with no
offset is not an instant, a phrase with no event is not a fact, and a component that read the
clock itself would be impure and would read it once per timestamp rather than once per
screen. `staleAfterHours` is the one prop that changes what the component claims, and leaving
it out is a legitimate answer rather than a missing configuration. Omit it and no staleness
treatment is rendered, because nothing here knows what old means for a measurement it cannot
see. `TimeEvent` is exported alongside the props interface, so a product can type its own
data against the five events.

Three inputs are refused rather than approximated, and the refusals are not all the same
shape. An `at` that is not an ISO 8601 timestamp with an offset renders nothing at all, and
so does an `event` outside the five: there is no instant, or no fact to attach one to, and a
plausible-looking timestamp drawn from either would be the component asserting something the
product never said. A `now` that cannot be parsed renders the exact date with no phrase,
because a dated timestamp is never wrong where a computed age would be a guess. All three
complain on the console in development, none of them throws, and a reader sees no difference
between a timestamp this component refused and one that was never passed. That is a cost of
the first two refusals and is stated here rather than argued away. `absoluteAfterDays={0}` is
a meaning rather than a mistake: it says the exact date is shown beside the phrase at every
age, including a reading only minutes old.

`showAbsolute` defaults to true, so the exact date comes onto the screen beside the phrase
once the reading is older than a day with no configuration, which is the pairing the content
doctrine asks for beyond about a day; within a day the phrase stands alone on screen. Pass
`showAbsolute={false}` to keep the date off screen at every age, for a dense list whose date
is already visible in the
surrounding row; the date stays in the accessibility tree and in print even then.
