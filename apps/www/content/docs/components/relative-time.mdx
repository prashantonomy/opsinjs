Relative time is a small kindness that turns into a hazard at the edges. *Two days ago* is
easier to act on than a date; *fourteen months ago* is not, and a phrase attached to the
wrong event — the app's last sync rather than the reading's own timestamp — is false in the
most reassuring possible direction.

## Preview

Five rows across the four rungs of the ladder: minutes, hours, days, days again nearer the
boundary, and the date that replaces the phrase once it stops helping. No row carries a
staleness threshold, so no row says anything about staleness: opsinjs has no number to put
there, and in a preview the number is the part that gets copied. The muted stale treatment is
therefore described on this page and demonstrated by none of its previews.

The third exclusion is a threshold the product chooses. `absoluteAfterDays` is where it goes,
and it defaults to a fortnight because past that point *437 days ago* is arithmetic nobody
should be asked to do. It is a legibility boundary and not a clinical one: moving it changes
which words are used for an instant the component is equally certain about either way, and it
can neither add nor remove the staleness note.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** That the named event happened at that instant, and — where the product
has supplied a threshold and it has been passed — that the product no longer considers the
associated value current. Nothing else. It does not know what was measured, and it has no
view about whether the reading is still true.

**What it must never be read as.** Freshness of the data as a whole. *Synced 5 minutes ago*
on a dashboard means the app spoke to a server five minutes ago; it does not mean the
readings are five minutes old. Conflating the two is one of the most common and most
consequential errors in health dashboards, which is why `event` is required, has no default,
and is never inferred: the component would have to pick one, and the reassuring pick is the
wrong one.

**What it asserts about *now*.** Less than it looks, and it does not find out for itself.
`now` is a required prop: the component never calls the clock, so the phrase is a statement
about two instants the caller named, computed once, and it owns no timer to revise it. A
surface left open for an hour is still showing the phrase it was rendered with. Three things
keep that a limitation rather than a lie. The `time` element's machine-readable timestamp is
exact and never moves. The absolute date and time are always in the accessibility tree and
always in print, whether or not they are on screen. And the phrase rounds *down* at every
rung except the minute floor, which rounds up to *1 minute ago*, while the staleness verdict
is computed from the exact elapsed time rather than from the phrase — so rounding can never
make a stale reading look fresh. A product whose surface stays open across its own boundary
re-renders with a fresh `now`.

Reading the clock once per screen rather than once per timestamp is worth having for its own
sake: twenty rows that each ask the runtime what time it is can straddle a minute boundary
halfway down the list and disagree with one another about a reading they were all rendered
beside.

**Which colour axis it may use.** Neither, in any state. The stale treatment is a muted,
non-status typographic change carried by explicit words — never an amber tint, which would
put a clinical status onto a fact about the clock and leave the reader unable to tell that
amber from the one that means *needs attention*.

**Staleness is clinical and belongs to the product.** What counts as old differs completely
between one measurement and the next, and this component cannot see which one it has.
RelativeTime ships no default threshold, no per-event default and nothing derived from
`event`; no such number appears in its source, in its demo or in either of its examples. Omit
`staleAfterHours` and there is no stale treatment at all, which is the honest output when
nobody has said what old means here. Supply it and the timestamp always says something: past
the boundary it carries the words, and where the age could not be checked at all — a
threshold that did not arrive as a number, a `now` that could not be parsed — it carries the
same hedged words rather than falling silent, because a stale reading rendered in exactly the
tone of a fresh one is the first failure
[Uncertainty, staleness and missing data](../health/uncertainty-and-staleness.mdx) names.

**It de-emphasises the timestamp, and only the timestamp.** The muted treatment reaches the
`time` element and nothing around it. The doctrine asks for more than that: in the stale
state the *value* is de-emphasised too and there is a route to refresh it, and neither is
something a formatter that cannot see the value is able to deliver. A surface that embeds
this component still owes both. The staleness words are one carrier of that state and not the
whole of it.

**Precision follows recency.** Minutes for the last hour, hours for the last day, days up to
the boundary, then the date. Announcing *2 hours and 14 minutes ago* implies a precision the
reader neither needs nor believes. The smallest thing the component will say is *1 minute
ago*: *just now* is forbidden for anything the product cannot vouch for to the minute, and a
phrase fixed at render time can vouch for nothing finer.

<SafetyCallout severity="attention" evidence="opinion">
Never put a sync time where a measurement time belongs. *Synced 2 minutes ago* above a

The parts are `data-slot` names, not exports. There is no `RelativeTime.Absolute` to render
on its own, and that is the point: every one of them is a way of saying *when*, and a caller
who could take the absolute date out of the tree while keeping the phrase would have removed
the only part that is still true tomorrow.

There is no `status` prop, no `color` prop and no variant. The one visual state the component
has is the muted stale treatment, and it is reached by passing a threshold and exceeding it —
never by asking for it directly. Nothing opsinjs ships passes one, so no preview on this page
renders that state. It is `text-muted-foreground` on the parts rather than on the root, which
is a merge-order defence and not a style: `cn()` files an opsin type-size class in the same
conflict group as a colour, so a caller's `className` on the root would otherwise delete the
muting and leave a stale reading looking fresh.

## Examples

### A column of entries

Every row names its event, and an age is read far faster down a column than four dates side
by side would be. No row passes a staleness threshold. The version that showed one row past a
boundary taught the idea better and shipped a number opsinjs does not own, and in a worked
example a number is read as a recommendation whatever the caption beside it says.

## Content guidelines

Always name the event. *Measured 3 days ago* is a fact; *3 days ago* is a fragment, and the
reader completes it with whichever assumption is most convenient.

Never write *just now* for anything the product cannot vouch for to the minute, and never
write *up to date* — that is a judgement about the data, not a statement about the clock.
Neither string exists in the component, and the smallest phrase it will produce is *1 minute
ago*.

The component does not say *this morning*, *yesterday* or *last Tuesday*, and the omission is
deliberate rather than pending. Those are calendar facts in the reader's own time zone, and a
component that does not run in the reader's browser does not have one: *yesterday* offered
for anything between 24 and 48 hours old is a calendar claim made from a stopwatch, and it is
wrong about a reading taken on Monday evening and read on Wednesday morning. Where a product
knows the reader's zone and wants those words, it writes them itself and keeps the exact
instant beside them.

## Accessibility

Some of these are held by a gate on every pull request. The rest are arguments about the
implementation, and they are labelled as such: `alpha` promises that accessibility is claimed
only where it has been checked, with the gaps listed rather than left out.

**Checked, by `node scripts/check-a11y.mts` on every pull request:**

- **No type size in px**, anywhere in the source. A reader who has raised their text size
  gets a larger timestamp, because every size the component uses is relative.
- **No raw colour and no ramp step.** The muted stale treatment is `text-muted-foreground`, a
  role the theme layer remaps for dark mode; a hex value, a Tailwind palette colour or a
  numbered step fails the build. So does any role that exists only in the documentation
  chrome and would resolve to nothing in the product.
- **No banned word**, in reader-facing copy or in an identifier. That covers the five event
  words and the staleness words, which are the only copy this component owns; the rest of what
  it emits is punctuation, the connective *on*, and an offset label.
- **A status colour could not be added quietly.** The same gate fails any file that paints
  from the status axis without stamping `data-status` and reading both the status vocabulary
  and a lucide glyph — which is what stops the stale treatment from drifting into an amber
  tint in some later commit. What it cannot prove is that the word and the glyph reach the
  surface: it reads source rather than a rendered page, and its own header says so.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **The absolute date and time are always in the accessibility tree**, not only on hover or
  focus, and not behind an affordance. `showAbsolute` decides whether a sighted reader sees
  them without asking; it does not decide whether they exist.
- **The announcement is one phrase** — *Measured 3 days ago, on 11 March 2026 at 08:12 UTC* —
  because the connective is a word rather than the separator character, and the separator is
  hidden from assistive technology.
- **Staleness is stated in words**, never by the muted tone alone, so it survives greyscale,
  increased contrast and print. On a surface whose text is already muted the words are doing
  all of the work, which is the honest description of that treatment rather than a defect
  in it. This is argued from the source and from nothing else: no preview on this page passes
  a threshold, so none of them renders the state.
- **It never announces itself.** The component mounts no live region and owns no timer:
  nothing about it changes after it is rendered, so there is nothing to interrupt a reader
  with. A product that re-renders it on an interval inherits that promise and should not
  wrap it in a live region to compensate.
- **Hydration is safe by determinism rather than by suppression — given a resolved locale.**
  Rendering on the server and correcting in the browser is what produces a mismatch, so this
  component does neither: it has no `"use client"`, no effect, no `typeof window` branch and
  no clock read at all, which is why `now` is required rather than defaulted.
  `suppressHydrationWarning` was the alternative and is worse: it keeps the server's text and
  discards the browser's more correct one. The boundary is `locale`, and it is a real one.
  The prop is optional; an omitted one reaches `Intl` as `undefined`, where it resolves to
  whichever locale the *runtime* defaults to, so the same props render *11 March 2026* on a
  server set to en-GB and *March 11, 2026* in a browser that is not. Pass an explicit tag —
  every snippet and every example on this page does — and the characters match. A runtime
  built without full ICU data could still disagree with a browser about a tag they both
  accept; that has not been tested here. See
  [Server and client components](../handbook/server-and-client-components.mdx).
- **Time zones are explicit.** The absolute form always names the offset the timestamp was
  written in, because whether it differs from the reader's own is a fact about the reader's
  browser. Of the two answers available to a component that does not run there, this is the
  one that is never wrong.
- **It is not focusable and has no keyboard behaviour**, because it has nothing to operate.
  The absolute date is not behind a disclosure precisely so that there is no control to
  reach.
- **At 200% text it should wrap rather than truncate.** The root is inline, has no fixed
  height and no width, and nothing in it is shortened to an ellipsis or hidden by an
  overflow rule. That is read from the source, not from a screen — and no rig will read it
  from one either: the nightly truncation check collects `data-opsinjs-value` elements, and
  this component deliberately stamps none, because that attribute marks a measurement a
  person reads as their own and an instant is not one. See the last item below.

**Not yet checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- Contrast for the muted stale treatment against every surface it may sit on, in both themes.
  `prefers-contrast` and `forced-colors` are not implemented anywhere in opsinjs yet, so the
  increased-contrast claim cannot currently be made by anyone.
- The print behaviour is asserted from the source — the absolute date is revealed by a print
  variant rather than being removed from the accessibility tree — and has not been checked in
  a real print preview or a PDF export.
- Screen-reader behaviour in any language other than English, which matters more here than
  elsewhere: `locale` moves the date and the phrase and leaves the five event words in
  English.
- **This component has not yet been seen rendered.** The previews above resolve once the
  registry index is regenerated; until then every visual claim on this page — the wrapping at
  200%, the muted treatment, the print behaviour — is read from the source rather than from a
  screen, and this line is here to be deleted by whoever looks first.

## API reference

`at`, `event` and `now` are required, and none of them has a default: an instant with no
offset is not an instant, a phrase with no event is not a fact, and a component that read the
clock itself would be impure and would read it once per timestamp rather than once per
screen. `staleAfterHours` is the one prop that changes what the component claims, and leaving
it out is a legitimate answer rather than a missing configuration — omit it and no staleness
treatment is rendered, because nothing here knows what old means for a measurement it cannot
see. `TimeEvent` is exported alongside the props interface, so a product can type its own
data against the five events.

Three inputs are refused rather than approximated, and the refusals are not all the same
shape. An `at` that is not an ISO 8601 timestamp with an offset renders nothing at all, and
so does an `event` outside the five: there is no instant, or no fact to attach one to, and a
plausible-looking timestamp drawn from either would be the component asserting something the
product never said. A `now` that cannot be parsed renders the exact date with no phrase,
because a dated timestamp is never wrong where a computed age would be a guess. All three
complain on the console in development, none of them throws, and a reader sees no difference
between a timestamp this component refused and one that was never passed — which is a cost of
the first two refusals and is stated here rather than argued away. `absoluteAfterDays={0}` is
a meaning rather than a mistake: it says the phrase is never used and the date is rendered on
its own at every age.
