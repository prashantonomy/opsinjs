Popover is a small panel anchored to the control that opened it, floated in a portal so it escapes whatever card or scroll area the trigger sits in. Its home is a short aside a reader can open, read or fill and then dismiss without leaving the page: a note beside a value, a rename field on a list, a couple of options that are not worth a whole screen.

The decision that shapes the whole component is that it is a thin themed wrapper and nothing more. Base UI's Popover is already complete and unopinionated, so opsinjs adds only tokens: the material of the panel, the neutral chrome of the trigger, a 44pt target floor and a focus ring. Every behaviour, the anchoring, the portal, the dismissal, the focus return and the keyboard, is the primitive's, which is why this page names Base UI parts rather than opsinjs inventions wherever a behaviour is described.

The panel is built on Base UI's Popover primitive, which is where the anchoring, the portal, the dismissal and the keyboard come from, so installing it pulls in `@base-ui/react`. It composes no other opsinjs component and renders no icon, because a themed wrapper over a complete primitive needs neither.

## Usage

```tsx
import { Popover } from "@/components/ui/popover"
```

```tsx
<Popover title="How readings are grouped" trigger="Why two lists?">
  <p>
    Entries you add yourself sit in one list, and entries from a connected
    device sit in another, so you can always tell which is which.
  </p>
</Popover>
```

## When to use it

  use={[

  ]}

The exclusion that matters is the first, and it is a question about whether going away is a valid answer. A popover is non-modal: it floats over a page that stays live, and a reader can ignore it and carry on, which is exactly what makes it wrong for a decision. When there is no state of the world in which dismissing the surface is a valid outcome, the surface is a [Dialog](./dialog.mdx), which blocks the page and holds the reader until they answer. If the reader can always leave without answering, it is a popover; if leaving would leave the product guessing, it is a dialog.

The second exclusion is the honest limit of the shape. A popover floats and is dismissed by leaving it, so it has to stay small: once the content is a long form, or needs its own scroll, or has to sit under a thumb on a phone, a [Sheet](./sheet.mdx) is the surface that still works, because it docks to an edge and is built for touch. The third names the one thing a popover is often mistaken for. A [Tooltip](./tooltip.mdx) is a passive hover label, and it is on the considered roster and unbuilt on purpose, because this is a phone-first system and there is no hover on a phone. When all you want is to attach the everyday meaning of a clinical word to a sentence, that is a [Term](./term.mdx), which shows inline without anything to open.

The parts above are identified by `data-slot` and are styleable from outside; none of them is an export, and there is no way to render the panel without the root. The trigger is a button this component supplies rather than a control the caller passes, so `trigger` is the button's visible content and not a control of its own: a whole button handed to it would nest one button inside another. The panel content is `children`, and a small form fragment or a control that closes the panel goes there, closing through `onOpenChange` rather than through a part of its own.

## Content guidelines

Keep a popover small and give it a title. The panel is a `role="dialog"`, so its title is the accessible name a screen-reader user hears when it opens, and a panel without one announces that a dialog has opened and nothing about what it is. Name the panel for what it holds rather than for the trigger that opened it, so "How readings are grouped" rather than "More", and keep the body to a sentence or two or a single small fragment. When the content grows past what floats comfortably, that is the signal to move to a sheet rather than to make the popover scroll.

Never put a clinical reading, a reference range or a verdict loose inside a popover. A popover formats nothing and asserts nothing, so a value dropped into it arrives without the range and the words that make it readable, and it sits on a surface a reader dismisses by looking away. A measurement that matters belongs in the component built to carry it with its range and its status, on a surface that stays.

## Accessibility

Some of this is enforced by a gate on every pull request and every push to `main`, and the rest is argued and labelled as such. At `alpha`, accessibility is claimed only where it has been checked, with the gaps listed rather than left out. Nothing here has been through a review with a disabled user.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y`:**

- **No status colour, no colour literal, no ramp step.** The panel, the trigger and the arrow draw only the neutral chrome roles and the `overlay` material's own shadow token, so the component carries no `data-status`, reads no clinical vocabulary, and cannot mix the two axes. The gate reads every element and would fail the build on a colour drawn from either axis or on a raw colour value, and there is none here.
- **No px type size.** The panel's text is the `body` step, the title is the `headline` step, and the trigger is the `body` step, all semantic rather than pixel sizes, so a reader who has turned their text size up gets a larger popover instead of a pinned one.
- **A 44pt target floor on the trigger.** The trigger floors its hit area at `--opsin-target-minimum` in rem, so the pressable region survives and grows with the reader's text size rather than pinning at a device pixel.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **The panel is a named dialog.** Base UI renders `role="dialog"` on the panel and wires its `aria-labelledby` to the title, so a screen-reader user hears what the surface is when it opens. A missing title raises a development warning rather than shipping a nameless dialog.
- **Focus returns to the trigger.** Opening the panel moves focus into it, and closing it, by Escape, by a press outside, or by a control inside, returns focus to the trigger, which is Base UI's own behaviour rather than something this wrapper adds.
- **The arrow is decorative.** It is `aria-hidden`, so it adds nothing for a screen reader to read, and the panel's role and name carry the meaning instead.
- **The entrance respects reduced motion.** Under `prefers-reduced-motion` the panel's scale is pinned and only its opacity crossfades, so a reader who asked for less motion gets an instant, equally informative state rather than nothing.

**Not checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- Every contrast pair: the card panel against the host page, the hairline against both, the arrow's fill and border, and the trigger's ink and border against the background. `scripts/check-contrast.mts` has not been run for this component, which is why the report below is empty rather than reassuring.
- What the component draws under `forced-colors: active`, where the shadow is dropped and the panel leans on its hairline, and where the arrow's fill and border are replaced by the platform's own pair.
- Whether the arrow's two bordered edges still face outward after the panel flips to the opposite side on a collision, and under a right-to-left layout. This is argued from `data-side` rather than seen at every side.

## API reference

<PropsTable name="PopoverProps" />

`trigger` is the visible content of the button this component supplies, not a control of your own, because the wrapper owns the button so it can carry the target floor, the focus ring and the `aria-expanded` state. `open` and `onOpenChange` are both optional: left off, the popover is uncontrolled and owns its own open state, and passed together they hand the state to the product, which is the path the form example takes so it can close the panel from a control inside it. `title` is effectively required, because the panel is a `role="dialog"` that owes an accessible name, and its absence raises a development warning. `side` is a preference the primitive overrides on a collision, and `className` merges onto the panel and is the one route by which colour can reach it, which the two-colour-axes rule governs in full.

## Related

<RelatedComponents name="popover" />

**Easily confused with**

- [Dialog](./dialog.mdx) blocks the page and holds the reader until they answer, for the one decision where dismissing is not a valid outcome. A popover is non-modal and the page stays live, so if the reader can always leave without answering, it is a popover.
- [Sheet](./sheet.mdx) docks to an edge and is built for touch, for a larger form or a mobile-first surface. A popover floats and stays small, so once the content needs its own scroll it is a sheet.
- [Tooltip](./tooltip.mdx) is a passive hover label, and it is unbuilt on the considered roster because this is a phone-first system with no hover. A popover is opened and dismissed rather than shown on hover.

**Used by**

- No pattern, recipe or screen composes it yet. It is a neutral overlay a product anchors to its own controls, and wiring it onto a surface is a product's to make rather than something the corpus ships today.
