A sparkline is a claim that a pattern exists. Four points and a slope look exactly like
forty points and a slope, and the reader cannot tell them apart. Most of this component is
about refusing to draw a line the data does not support, and about making sure the sentence
beside the line carries everything the line carries.

## Preview

Two states, because they are the two that decide whether this component is doing its job: a
series long enough to draw, with a gap in it and one reading marked, and a series that is
not long enough, which draws nothing at all and says so. Neither passes a `changeThreshold`,
so neither caption names a direction. That is the most this component can say honestly
without being told how much movement counts as movement, and it is what a demo shipped by a
design system should show. Open it under the product theme, turn the text size up, and look at
it in greyscale. The caption should still tell you everything, because that is the whole
promise.

The third exclusion is the one products get wrong. A sparkline is a glyph, not a chart: no
axes, no gridlines, no hover, no tooltip, and this implementation offers none of them. The
moment a reader needs to know *which* day the spike was, this is the wrong component and a
real chart belongs there instead. See
[Chart anatomy](../foundations/data-visualisation/chart-anatomy.mdx).

The first exclusion is handled inside the component rather than by you remembering it.
`minimumPoints` is required, and below it TrendSparkline draws nothing and says why, so the
`empty-state` alternative is only needed when the whole surface has nothing on it.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** That these readings, of this measurement, in this unit, were recorded
at these times, and that the line joins them. It asserts the shape of a series and nothing
about its cause.

**What it must never be read as.** A prediction, an explanation, or a verdict. A line
sloping upward does not mean the reader is getting worse, and a line sloping downward does
not mean they are getting better. The valence depends entirely on the measurement, and for
several common measurements it depends on the person. The component therefore states
**direction** and leaves valence to whatever the product chooses to say beside it, as
[Trends and change](../health/trends-and-change.mdx) requires.

**There is no direction word until you say how much movement counts.** Doctrine gives the
metric the job of declaring the change threshold below which a difference is presented as
unchanged, and `changeThreshold` is that number: required in the reading's own unit before
the composed caption will name a direction, with no default. Pass it and the caption reads
*"Up, from 12 steps to 20 steps, over the last 14 days"*. Omit it and the caption prints both
endpoints and stops, and the plot's accessible name says *"with no clear direction"*. That
wording is what [Alt text and descriptions](../content/alt-text-and-descriptions.mdx)
gives for exactly this case. The alternative was calling any difference at all up or down,
which is a change threshold of zero chosen by opsinjs and applied to somebody else's
metric: it puts the word *"Up"* on a fortnight of readings that moved less than the
instrument they were taken with can resolve.

**Direction, when there is one, is first-to-last arithmetic and nothing cleverer.** The
caption compares the first real reading with the last one and prints both. It is not a fitted
slope, because a reader cannot check a regression line against the picture and can check two
endpoints against it in a second.

**A band needs both of its ends.** `range` shades an interval behind the line only when it
carries a lower bound, an upper bound and a `source`, with the lower below the upper. A
one-sided range is a real range. It is rendered as words in the caption and as no
rectangle at all. The caption reads *"The range from your laboratory is up to 20 steps. It
is not shaded, because a band needs both of its ends."* A rectangle has four edges, so
drawing one would mean taking the missing edge from the readings or from zero and then
telling the reader, in the same sentence, that the whole band came from their laboratory.
The caption also states the band's two numbers, because a reader told a shaded interval
exists and who owns it, and never what it says, has been shown a picture with no twin.

**Every caption says when the last reading was taken.** `TrendPoint.at` is already required,
so the date is printed rather than inferred: *"The last reading was taken on 6 February
2026."* `window` on its own is a display string this component can never check, and a series
whose last reading is three months old otherwise renders identically to one taken this
morning. No staleness boundary is invented here. How old is too old belongs to the metric,
and a product that wants a verdict about age puts a [RelativeTime](./relative-time.mdx)
beside the plot.

**Nothing is derived.** There is no reference range in this component, no smoothing, no
default window, no default minimum point count, no default change threshold and no staleness
rule. `TrendPoint.status` is an input: the product assigned it, and the component only
decides where to put the words. A status outside the four levels marks nothing and says
nothing, because emphasis in the plot with no word in the caption is a status carried by
colour alone. The likeliest such status is `unknown`.

**A gap and a failure are two different sentences.** An entry with `value: null` is a reading
nobody took and is counted as a gap. An entry whose value is a number but not a finite one is
a reading that was taken and did not survive the trip; it is counted and described separately,
because telling somebody they recorded nothing on a day they did record something is untrue
about their own record. That separate clause reads *"and 2 readings that could not be read"*.

**Which colour axis it may use.** The **category** axis, on the line itself, because a
sparkline's job in a dashboard is to be findable among five others. The **status** axis is
not drawn at all. A marked reading gets an emphasised marker in the line's own tint, and its
verdict is rendered as a [StatusPill](./status-pill.mdx) inside the caption. The
specification permits status colour on the emphasised point; this implementation declines it,
because a status is carried by colour *and* a glyph *and* the word *and* `data-status`
together, and a dot the size of a full stop can carry one of the four. See
[The two colour axes](../health/two-colour-axes.mdx).

**Who owns the window, the minimum and the change threshold.** The product. `minimumPoints`
is required, has no default, and a value that is not a whole number above zero is treated the
same as too few readings: no line, and a caption saying which of the two happened.
`changeThreshold` is optional and has no default either, and without it there is no direction
word. Both fail towards saying less. A component with no rule about when a trend exists has
no basis for drawing one, and one with no rule about how much movement is noise has no basis
for naming a direction.

**Gaps are data.** A missing reading is an explicit `{ value: null }` entry, drawn as a real
break in the path rather than a lighter stroke, and counted in the caption. A run of exactly
one reading between two gaps is drawn as a dot, because that reading did happen. Nothing is
ever interpolated. See
[Uncertainty, staleness and missing data](../health/uncertainty-and-staleness.mdx).

<SafetyCallout severity="attention" evidence="opinion">
The y-axis always includes zero, and there is no prop to change it. Scaling to the data's own
smallest and largest reading is what every sparkline library does, and it converts ordinary
variation into a full-height event: a reader has no way to tell a two-per-cent wobble from
something that happened. The cost is real and is accepted. Many honest series draw as a
nearly flat line. That is the honest picture, and it is why the caption carries the magnitude
in words rather than leaving it to the slope.
</SafetyCallout>

The parts above are identified by `data-slot` and are styleable from outside. None of them is
an export: there is no `TrendSparkline.Caption` component, no `showCaption` prop and no
`sr-only` variant of the sentence.

**`className` is the exception, and it is a real one.** It is merged onto the root with
`tailwind-merge`, so `className="hidden"` takes the whole component out of the accessibility
tree, `className="sr-only"` leaves the plot's `aria-label` in place while removing the
sentence from sighted readers, and a variant targeting the caption's `data-slot` hides the
sentence alone. The `sr-only` case is the inversion this component exists to prevent. Nothing
in the implementation prevents any of the three and no gate checks for them. The honest
statement is
that the caption is mandatory *by construction of the API* and not against a caller who
reaches past it.

Two departures from the specification's part tree, both deliberate and both visible in the
DOM above. `TrendSparkline.Window` renders once rather than twice. The composed caption
also names the period, so the standalone element is hidden from assistive technology in that
case only. And the range's interval and attribution are a clause of the caption rather than a
part of its own, because a part could be styled away and an unowned band must not be
renderable at all.

One clause is conditional in a way the earlier wording did not admit: the range clause exists
only when a `range` with a usable `source` was supplied, and the marked-reading clause only
when a reading carries one of the four levels. Both survive a caller's `caption` and both
survive the too-few-readings branch. A verdict the product assigned to a reading is not
withdrawn because no line was drawn, but neither is conjured when there is nothing to say.

## Examples

### A band behind the line, and one marked reading

The two optional parts together, and then the same range with one end taken away. The first
band is neutral and dashed, never tinted by status, and the caption gives both its numbers
and whose interval it is. The second range has an upper bound and no lower one: it is a
real range, it is stated in the caption as *"up to 20 steps"*, and it is drawn as nothing,
because the missing edge would have to come from the readings or from zero and would then
be attributed to a source that never gave it. The marked reading gets a heavier dot in the
line's own colour and a StatusPill in the caption; the plot never turns red.

## Content guidelines

Captions state direction where a `changeThreshold` makes one available, then magnitude,
window, coverage and when the last reading was taken, in that order, and stop. Leave the
interpretation to the product's own copy in the surrounding
[CareCard](./care-card.mdx) or paragraph, where it can be attributed to somebody.

Avoid *improving*, *worsening*, *better*, *worse*, *on track* and *off track* inside the
component. They are all valence, and valence in a caption is a verdict nobody signed.

Pass your own `caption` when you have something the component cannot know: a cadence, a
comparison, a reader's own language. It replaces the direction-and-magnitude sentence and
nothing else. The coverage clause, the date of the last reading, the clause naming a marked
reading and the clause naming a range's interval and source are appended either way. A count
of absent measurements, a date, a status and an attribution are each part of somebody's own
record rather than decoration, and a caller who replaces the sentence has not been given the
right to withdraw them.

## Accessibility

Several of these are held by a gate on every commit. The rest are arguments, and they are
labelled as such: `alpha` promises that accessibility is claimed only where it has been
checked, with the gaps listed rather than left out.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y` on every commit:**

- **No clinical number ships as a default.** A11Y014 fails the build on any name matching
  `minimum`, `threshold`, `stale`, `limit` or `band` that is given a numeric literal anywhere
  under `registry/**`, and A11Y015 fails on any object literal carrying two numeric bounds
  with no `source` beside them. Between them they refuse `minimumPoints = 7`, a literal
  `changeThreshold`, and a band written as `{ low: 10, high: 20 }` with nobody's name on it.
  **What A11Y014 does not refuse is a threshold-shaped number under a name its pattern cannot
  see**, and the demo in this file has one: `minimumPoints` is required with no default, so
  the zero-prop demo `shadcn add` copies into a consumer's project must pass a number, and it
  passes `DEMO_READINGS_A_TREND_NEEDS = 4` under a name the regex does not match. The three
  examples do the same. What holds that line is the API rather than the gate, because a caller
  cannot inherit the number by forgetting to pass one. The tension between "a required prop
  must be demonstrable" and "never ship a threshold, not even in an example" is recorded in
  the status notice above rather than resolved here.
- **No raw colour and no ramp step.** A11Y005 and A11Y006 fail on a hex value, an `oklch()`,
  a Tailwind palette colour or a numbered step. Every colour here is a role on one of the two
  axes or one of the eleven bridged surface roles.
- **No element takes colour from both axes.** A11Y008 reads every JSX element and every
  `cn()` body for a category utility and a status utility on the same one, and for
  `data-status` beside `data-category`. This file stamps only `data-category`, on the line.
- **No class that resolves to nothing in the product.** A11Y011 fails on `bg-secondary`,
  `rounded-2xl` and the rest of the docs-only palette, which render correctly in this page's
  preview and unstyled in a real product.
- **No banned word**, in copy, in an identifier or in a comment that ships (A11Y009, A11Y010,
  A11Y013).
- **No `px` type size** (A11Y003, A11Y004). The plot's height is on the space scale, so it
  grows with the reader's root font size.

**Held by the API rather than by a gate, which is a weaker promise honestly stated:**

- **The caption cannot be turned off by a prop.** There is no `showCaption`, no `sr-only`
  variant and no value of `caption` that renders the component without a sentence. **It can be
  turned off by `className`**, and that is worth stating plainly rather than claiming away:
  `className` is merged onto the root with `tailwind-merge`, so `hidden` removes the whole
  component from the accessibility tree, `sr-only` removes the visible twin while leaving the
  plot's `aria-label` in place, and a variant targeting `[data-slot=trend-sparkline-caption]`
  hides the sentence alone. Nothing in the implementation prevents any of the three and no gate
  checks for them. A gate asserting a visible text twin on all four graphics would be worth
  having and does not exist.
- **The y-axis cannot be truncated.** There is no `yAxisMin` prop, so OPSIN-0013 describes a
  mistake this API cannot express.
- **A range with no source is not drawn**, and neither is a one-sided one. The type requires
  `source`; a JavaScript caller who omits it raises OPSIN-0004 in development and gets no band.
  A range with only one bound is stated in the caption in words and drawn as nothing, so no
  edge this component supplied is ever attributed to the caller's source.
- **A status outside the four levels marks nothing.** A reading carrying `unknown`, or any
  value the vocabulary does not hold, leaves the plot unmarked and the caption unbroken, and
  raises OPSIN-0011 or OPSIN-0021 in development. Emphasis in the plot with no word in the
  caption is a status carried by colour alone, which is the failure this component's whole
  marker design is written against.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **The plot is `role="img"` with a composed name**, `focusable="false"`, and never `slider`,
  `meter` or `progressbar`. All three of those announce a value the reader can inspect or
  change, and this is a picture of readings somebody else took. It is not announced point by
  point: a fourteen-point series read aloud one point at a time is worse than silence.
- **The name follows the doctrine's pattern, and it overlaps the caption.** It gives the
  measurement, the period, the reading count, the extent, the direction and what is missing,
  which is the `TrendSparkline` pattern in
  [Alt text and descriptions](../content/alt-text-and-descriptions.mdx). It leaves out only
  that pattern's final element, a pointer to the table twin, because there is no table twin
  to point at. The
  earlier claim here was that the name says something the caption does not; in a series whose
  first and last readings are also its lowest and highest, which is every series this page
  ships, the extent and the endpoints are the same two numbers and a listener hears the window,
  the count and both numbers twice. That is a real cost of `role="img"` with a visible twin,
  and it is not resolved. Hiding the plot from assistive technology entirely, which
  [Accessible charts](../foundations/data-visualisation/accessible-charts.mdx) permits when the
  twin sits beside it, is the alternative and would need a decision this page has not made.
- **The summary comes after the chart, not before it.** Accessible charts states the order as
  load-bearing: summary, then chart, then table. The DOM order here is the period label, the
  plot, then the caption. The departure is deliberate: a sparkline is read as a glyph
  beside a number, and a paragraph above a 3 rem picture reads as the surface's own copy rather
  than as the picture's twin. It is a departure all the same, and a caller who wants doctrine's
  order can style the flex column to reverse it. Recorded here so it is visibly a choice rather
  than an oversight.
- **Category tint never encodes meaning**, so greyscale loses findability and nothing else.
  The break pattern across a gap in the series is drawn as a shape rather than a colour, so
  it should survive greyscale too. Should: it has not been put through the generated
  colour-vision audit, which measures token pairs and not rendered marks, and an earlier
  draft of this page stated the greyscale survival without that qualification.
- **At 200% text the caption reflows** to as many lines as it needs; nothing here sets a
  height, a `truncate` or an ellipsis. The plot keeps its height in `rem`, so it grows with
  the text rather than shrinking away from it. **Growing has a cost the earlier wording did not
  state.** `preserveAspectRatio="none"` stretches a fixed 100×32 viewBox into a `rem` height
  and a fluid width, so the apparent slope of the line is a function of the column's width and
  of the reader's text size rather than of the data: the same series draws about twice as steep
  at 200% text in the same column, and about three times as steep in a third-width tile. Two
  sparklines are comparable only at the same width and the same text size, which is the same
  class of caveat as `window`. And the strokes do not grow with the type: the line, the
  marker and the
  halo carry `vector-effect="non-scaling-stroke"`, so they stay at their device-pixel widths
  while the plot doubles, which thins the graphic relative to everything around it at exactly
  the setting a reader chose because thin things are hard to see. Checked by eye, not by a
  machine.
- **Nothing animates.** No path-drawing, no entrance transition, no morph on a value change,
  on first paint or after it. There is no reduced-motion fallback because there is nothing to
  fall back from.

**Known to be missing, which is a different thing from untested:**

- **The data-table twin.**
  [Accessible charts](../foundations/data-visualisation/accessible-charts.mdx) states it as a
  requirement rather than a recommendation: "a chart component that does not provide a table
  twin does not meet the definition of done". This component does not provide one. There
  is no slot, prop or part for it, so the twin is the caller's to place beside the sparkline
  and nothing checks that they did. The consequence is concrete: a reader who wants Tuesday's
  number cannot get it from this component in any modality, and the plot's accessible name
  cannot carry the doctrine's closing "full readings in the table below" because there is no
  table to point at. This is a stated gap in the definition of done, not an item awaiting a
  test.

**Not yet checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- Contrast for the line against the band, the band against the page, and the caption text in
  both themes. `scripts/check-contrast.mts` has no pairs for this component yet, which is why
  the report below is empty rather than green. **Whether the band is visible at all is the open
  question, not whether it can be told from the line.** It is drawn `fill-muted` with a
  `stroke-border` dashed edge. The nearest thing the generated audit does measure is a
  hairline neutral boundary on the page, and that boundary fails the non-text floor in both
  themes: APCA Lc 22.42 against a floor of 45, WCAG 1.47 against 3, in light; APCA Lc −8.21
  and WCAG 1.9 in dark. That pair is not this component's and the numbers are not a
  measurement of this band,
  which is exactly why the pairs need adding to `scripts/check-contrast.mts` before anything
  here is called distinguishable. Picking a darker neutral by hand instead would be replacing
  an unmeasured choice with another one.
- Print behaviour. The line should print as a solid stroke, the band as an outline and the
  gaps as visible breaks; the dashed edge and the `stroke-background` halo behind the marker
  have not been tested on paper.
- Right-to-left. The plot draws left to right in time order and does not flip, because the
  component cannot read the document's direction at render time and a chart that mirrored
  only sometimes would be worse than one that never does.

## API reference

<PropsTable name="TrendSparklineProps" />

Two props are required and neither has a default: `series` and `minimumPoints`. Two more decide
how much the component is allowed to say: `changeThreshold`, without which no direction word is
printed, and `range.source`, without which no band is drawn. Those four are the whole of the
safety design, and if the table below is empty the generated props data has not been rebuilt.
Run the generate step and check that `minimumPoints` appears as required with no default.

`TrendPoint` and `ReferenceRange` are declared once, in the substrate, and imported from
`@/lib/opsinjs` rather than from this component, because a second copy of either is a second
copy that can drift. `TrendDirection` is exported from this file as the vocabulary a product
may use in its own caption; the component itself only ever produces `up`, `down` and `level`,
only when a `changeThreshold` was supplied, and never `unsettled`, because deciding a series
is unsettled needs a noise measure that no prop carries.
