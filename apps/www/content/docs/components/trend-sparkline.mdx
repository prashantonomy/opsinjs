A sparkline is a claim that a pattern exists. Four points and a slope look exactly like
forty points and a slope, and the reader cannot tell them apart. Most of this component is
about refusing to draw a line the data does not support, and about making sure the sentence
beside the line carries everything the line carries.

## Preview

Two states, because they are the two that decide whether this component is doing its job: a
series long enough to draw, with a gap in it and one reading marked, and a series that is
not long enough, which draws nothing at all and says so. Open it under the product theme,
turn the text size up, and look at it in greyscale — the caption should still tell you
everything, because that is the whole promise.

The third exclusion is the one products get wrong. A sparkline is a glyph, not a chart: no
axes, no gridlines, no hover, no tooltip, and this implementation offers none of them. The
moment a reader needs to know *which* day the spike was, the component has failed and a real
chart belongs there instead — see
[Chart anatomy](../foundations/data-visualisation/chart-anatomy.mdx).

The first exclusion is handled inside the component rather than by you remembering it.
`minimumPoints` is required, and below it TrendSparkline draws nothing and says why, so the
`empty-state` alternative is only needed when the whole surface has nothing on it.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** That these readings, of this measurement, in this unit, were recorded
at these times, and that the line joins them. It asserts the shape of a series and nothing
about its cause.

**What it must never be read as.** A prediction, an explanation, or a verdict. A line
sloping upward does not mean the reader is getting worse, and a line sloping downward does
not mean they are getting better — the valence depends entirely on the measurement, and for
several common measurements it depends on the person. The component therefore states
**direction** and leaves valence to whatever the product chooses to say beside it, as
[Trends and change](../health/trends-and-change.mdx) requires.

**Direction is first-to-last arithmetic, and nothing cleverer.** The composed caption
compares the first real reading with the last one, prints both, and calls the difference up
or down. It is not a fitted slope, because a reader cannot check a regression line against
the picture and can check two endpoints against it in a second. It says *unchanged* only
when the two are exactly equal: doctrine asks for a change threshold below which a
difference is presented as unchanged, this API has no prop to carry one, and inventing the
threshold would be worse than printing both numbers and letting the reader see how small the
difference is. A product with a real threshold passes its own `caption`.

**Nothing is derived.** There is no reference range in this component, no smoothing, no
default window, no default minimum point count and no rule about how much movement counts as
movement. `TrendPoint.status` is an input: the product assigned it, and the component only
decides where to put the words.

**Which colour axis it may use.** The **category** axis, on the line itself, because a
sparkline's job in a dashboard is to be findable among five others. The **status** axis is
not drawn at all — a marked reading gets an emphasised marker in the line's own tint, and its
verdict is rendered as a [StatusPill](./status-pill.mdx) inside the caption. The
specification permits status colour on the emphasised point; this implementation declines it,
because a status is carried by colour *and* a glyph *and* the word *and* `data-status`
together, and a dot the size of a full stop can carry one of the four. See
[The two colour axes](../health/two-colour-axes.mdx).

**Who owns the window and the minimum.** The product. `minimumPoints` is required, has no
default, and a value that is not a whole number above zero is treated the same as too few
readings: no line. That is the failure direction on purpose — a component with no rule about
when a trend exists has no basis for drawing one.

**Gaps are data.** A missing reading is an explicit `{ value: null }` entry, drawn as a real
break in the path rather than a lighter stroke, and counted in the caption. A run of exactly
one reading between two gaps is drawn as a dot, because that reading did happen. Nothing is
ever interpolated — see
[Uncertainty, staleness and missing data](../health/uncertainty-and-staleness.mdx).

<SafetyCallout severity="attention" evidence="opinion">
The y-axis always includes zero, and there is no prop to change it. Scaling to the data's own
smallest and largest reading is what every sparkline library does, and it converts ordinary
variation into a full-height event: a reader has no way to tell a two-per-cent wobble from
something that happened. The cost is real and is accepted — many honest series draw as a
nearly flat line. That is the honest picture, and it is why the caption carries the magnitude
in words rather than leaving it to the slope.
</SafetyCallout>

The parts above are identified by `data-slot` and are styleable from outside. None of them is
an export: there is no `TrendSparkline.Caption` component, no `showCaption` prop and no way
to render the plot without the sentence. The caption is the component, and every way of
removing a mandatory text twin that has ever been offered has been used.

Two departures from the specification's part tree, both deliberate and both visible in the
DOM above. `TrendSparkline.Window` renders once rather than twice — the composed caption
also names the period, so the standalone element is hidden from assistive technology in that
case only. And the band's attribution is a clause of the caption rather than a part of its
own, because a part could be styled away and an unowned band must not be renderable at all.

## Examples

### A band behind the line, and one marked reading

The two optional parts together. The band is neutral and dashed, never tinted by status, and
the caption names whose interval it is. The marked reading gets a heavier dot in the line's
own colour and a StatusPill in the caption; the plot never turns red.

## Content guidelines

Captions state direction, magnitude, window and gaps, in that order, and stop. Leave the
interpretation to the product's own copy in the surrounding
[CareCard](./care-card.mdx) or paragraph, where it can be attributed to somebody.

Avoid *improving*, *worsening*, *better*, *worse*, *on track* and *off track* inside the
component. They are all valence, and valence in a caption is a verdict nobody signed.

Pass your own `caption` when you have something the component cannot know: a change
threshold, a cadence, a reader's own language. It replaces the direction-and-magnitude
sentence and nothing else — the clause naming a marked reading and the clause naming the
band's source are appended either way, because one is a status that owes a word and the other
is an attribution that cannot be dropped.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"Down, from 20 steps to 16 steps, over the last 14 days. 11 readings, and 3 gaps where
    nothing was recorded."** Direction, magnitude, window, gaps. A reader can check every word
    of it against the picture.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **"Trending in the right direction. Keep it up."** Valence with no author, praise for
    something the reader may not have caused, and no way to tell how much changed or over how
    long.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"Not enough readings to draw a trend over the last 14 days: 3 of the 7 this needs."**
    Names the rule, names the gap, and the number is the product's rather than ours.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **A line through three points.** It looks exactly like a trend and it is not one, and
    nothing on screen distinguishes them.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

## Accessibility

Several of these are held by a gate on every commit. The rest are arguments, and they are
labelled as such: `alpha` promises that accessibility is claimed only where it has been
checked, with the gaps listed rather than left out.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y` on every commit:**

- **No clinical number ships as a default.** A11Y014 fails the build on any name matching
  `minimum`, `threshold`, `stale`, `limit` or `band` that is given a numeric literal anywhere
  under `registry/**`. That is what keeps `minimumPoints` required: the gate would refuse
  `minimumPoints = 7` in this file, in the demo, or in an example, and it is right to.
- **No raw colour and no ramp step.** A11Y005 and A11Y006 fail on a hex value, an `oklch()`,
  a Tailwind palette colour or a numbered step. Every colour here is a role on one of the two
  axes or one of the eleven bridged surface roles.
- **No element takes colour from both axes.** A11Y008 reads every JSX element and every
  `cn()` body for a category utility and a status utility on the same one, and for
  `data-status` beside `data-category`. This file stamps only `data-category`, on the line.
- **No class that resolves to nothing in the product.** A11Y011 fails on `bg-secondary`,
  `rounded-2xl` and the rest of the docs-only palette, which render correctly in this page's
  preview and unstyled in a real product.
- **No banned word**, in copy, in an identifier or in a comment that ships (A11Y009, A11Y010,
  A11Y013).
- **No `px` type size** (A11Y003, A11Y004). The plot's height is on the space scale, so it
  grows with the reader's root font size.

**Held by the API rather than by a gate, which is a weaker promise honestly stated:**

- **The caption cannot be turned off.** There is no prop for it, no `sr-only` variant and no
  class that hides it — but nothing checks that a future edit keeps it that way. A gate that
  asserted a visible text twin on all four graphics would be worth having and does not exist.
- **The y-axis cannot be truncated.** There is no `yAxisMin` prop, so OPSIN-0013 describes a
  mistake this API cannot express.
- **A range with no source is not drawn.** The type requires `source`; a JavaScript caller
  who omits it raises OPSIN-0004 in development and gets no band.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **The plot is `role="img"` with a composed name**, `focusable="false"`, and never `slider`,
  `meter` or `progressbar`. All three of those announce a value the reader can inspect or
  change, and this is a picture of readings somebody else took. It is not announced point by
  point: a fourteen-point series read aloud one point at a time is worse than silence.
- **The name says something the caption does not.** It names the measurement, the period, how
  many readings there are and what they range between; the caption says the direction, the
  endpoints and the gaps. A name identical to the sentence below it would be the same words
  read out twice, and there is currently no way to have the caption *be* the name without a
  `useId` hook and therefore a client boundary this component does not need.
- **The line is distinguishable from the band without colour**, by weight and by the dashed
  edge on the band, and the break pattern across gaps survives greyscale. Not yet checked
  against the generated colour-vision audit, which has no entry for this component.
- **Category tint never encodes meaning**, so greyscale loses findability and nothing else.
- **At 200% text the caption reflows** to as many lines as it needs; nothing here sets a
  height, a `truncate` or an ellipsis. The plot keeps its height in `rem`, so it grows with
  the text rather than shrinking away from it — which is the opposite of what the
  specification asked for, and is the honest trade in a component that does not own its
  container. Checked by eye, not by a machine.
- **Nothing animates.** No path-drawing, no entrance transition, no morph on a value change,
  on first paint or after it. There is no reduced-motion fallback because there is nothing to
  fall back from.

**Not yet checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- Contrast for the line against the band, the band against the page, and the caption text in
  both themes. `scripts/check-contrast.mts` has no pairs for this component yet, which is why
  the report below is empty rather than green.
- Print behaviour. The line should print as a solid stroke, the band as an outline and the
  gaps as visible breaks; the dashed edge and the `stroke-background` halo behind the marker
  have not been tested on paper.
- Right-to-left. The plot draws left to right in time order and does not flip, because the
  component cannot read the document's direction at render time and a chart that mirrored
  only sometimes would be worse than one that never does.
- The data-table twin that [Accessible charts](../foundations/data-visualisation/accessible-charts.mdx)
  requires for every chart. This component has no slot for one; it is the caller's to place
  beside the sparkline, and nothing checks that they did.

## API reference

<PropsTable name="TrendSparklineProps" />

`TrendPoint` and `ReferenceRange` are declared once, in the substrate, and imported from
`@/lib/opsinjs` rather than from this component — a second copy of either is a second copy
that can drift. `TrendDirection` is exported from this file as the vocabulary a product may
use in its own caption; the component itself only ever produces `up`, `down` and `level`, and
never `unsettled`, because deciding a series is unsettled needs a noise measure that no prop
carries.
