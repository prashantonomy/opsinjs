StatusPill is where the status axis becomes visible. It is a small component with an
outsized job: it is the only place in the system where a clinical level is stated directly,
and every other status-bearing component either embeds it or copies its vocabulary.

## Preview

All four levels. Open it under the product theme and turn the text
size up: the claim that this component survives without colour is meant to be checked
rather than believed, and the fastest check is to look at it in greyscale and see whether
you can still tell the four apart. The sizes example further down shows all three sizes.

The exclusion that matters is `badge`. A pill and a badge look identical and mean opposite
things: a badge is a label, a pill is a judgement. Once a product uses the same component
for *sleep* and for *needs attention*, the status axis has no visual identity left and every
argument in [The two colour axes](../health/two-colour-axes.mdx) has already been lost.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** That the product has assigned one of exactly four levels of attention
to the thing the pill labels. Nothing more. It does not assert what is wrong, why, how
urgent it is in hours, or what happens next.

**The four levels are fixed** and their meanings are defined once, in
[Clinical status semantics](../health/clinical-status-semantics.mdx):

| Level | What it means | What it must never mean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **steady** | Nothing here needs attention right now | That the reader is healthy, or that anything has been ruled out |
| **watch** | Worth knowing about; keep an eye on it | That something is wrong |
| **attention** | Something needs a decision or an action, not necessarily today | An emergency |
| **urgent** | Act now, and the surface says exactly how | A diagnosis, or a substitute for calling emergency services |

**What it must never be read as.** A diagnosis, a severity score, or a triage category.
These levels describe what the interface is asking of the reader, not the state of their
body. `urgent` on a screen is not the same claim as *urgent* in a clinical handover, and
copy must never blur the two.

**Which colour axis it may use.** The **status** axis, exclusively and by definition. A
StatusPill never carries a category colour: a pill tinted for `heart` sitting beside a pill
tinted for `attention` is precisely the collision the two-axis rule exists to prevent. If
you need to show which category something belongs to, that is a `badge`, and it belongs
somewhere else on the surface.

**An ancestor may set a text colour freely.** The pill declares its own ink as a direct
`color:` property and inherits nothing, so a `text-*` class on a list row or a card never
reaches the word inside the pill. What is still forbidden is stripping the pill's surface or
its boundary through `className`. Remove the fill or the line and the word and the glyph are
left carrying the level on their own. The two-axis rule survives here as a concern about the
ancestor rather than a defect in the pill. A row tinted from the category namespace is a
category-coloured row that happens to contain a status-coloured pill, and the pill's own
element never reads from both axes. In [ResultCard](./result-card.mdx) the pill is the
category-tinted heading's sibling rather than its descendant, which keeps the two axes on two
elements by construction.

**Colour is never alone.** Every pill renders a word and an icon alongside its colour, and
the three are redundant by design. The word is the primary carrier; the colour is the
scanning aid. See [Colour independence](../accessibility/colour-independence.mdx).

**Who assigns the level.** The consuming product, from its own rules or from a clinician.
StatusPill derives nothing. It has no thresholds, no ranges and no opinions.

**A fifth level is refused rather than approximated.** `status` is typed to the four, and a
JavaScript caller who passes something else gets a development warning and no pill at all.
Rendering a plausible-looking fallback would be the component inventing a verdict, which is
the one thing it exists not to do.

<SafetyCallout severity="urgent" evidence="opinion">
At most one `urgent` pill may be visible on a screen. If the product's rules produce two,
the screen is wrong and needs an [AlertBanner](./alert-banner.mdx) that says what to do
about the most serious one. A screen of red pills teaches the reader that red means nothing,
and that lesson is permanent. See [Alarm fatigue](../health/alarm-fatigue.mdx).
</SafetyCallout>

The pill is deliberately not composable. Icon, word and colour are one unit, and the API
offers no way to remove the word, because every way of removing it that has ever been
offered has been used. The parts above are identified by `data-slot` and are styleable from
outside; none of them is an export, and there is no way to render one without the others.

There is no `StatusPill.Category`, no `color` prop and no `variant` prop. The four levels
are the whole surface.

### Every size keeps the word

`size` is visual weight and nothing else. There is no size at which a pill becomes a
coloured dot. That is the request this prop exists to refuse. `sm` and `md` label a status
in prose and in a list; `lg` sets the word at the headline step, so a pill placed inside a
heading reads as loudly as the sentence it qualifies rather than a step below it.

## States

StatusPill has no interaction states. It is not focusable, it takes no hover or pressed
treatment, and it never becomes disabled, because it is a label rather than a control. If a
product turns it into a filter it has built a [Button](./button.mdx), and the states that
matter then are Button's, not this component's.

It has no data states of its own either. The five data states the system defines live in the
reading rather than in the pill: a result that has not arrived shows a
[Skeleton](./skeleton.mdx), one that could not be loaded or is genuinely absent shows an
[EmptyState](./empty-state.mdx), and a reading old enough to doubt is wrapped in
[RelativeTime](./relative-time.mdx). The pill is handed a level only once the surface around it
has a level to state.

| Data state | What StatusPill renders |
| --- | --- |
| Error | Nothing. The surface reports the error; a pill would assert a level the reading does not have. |
| Loading | Nothing. The surface shows a Skeleton until a level exists. |
| Empty | Nothing. There is no reading to grade, so there is no pill. |
| Partial | The level for the part that exists, where the product has assigned one, while the surface says which part is missing. |
| Stale | The level unchanged, with freshness carried beside it by RelativeTime rather than by the pill. |

The one value that looks like a state and is not is `unknown`. It is the absence of an
assertion, and the pill refuses it rather than drawing it as a fifth level, because a reader
who sees it rendered reads it as "probably fine". See
[Clinical status semantics](../health/clinical-status-semantics.mdx).

## Content guidelines

Use the level's own word. Do not substitute synonyms per screen: a reader who has learnt
that `attention` renders as "Needs attention" should not meet "action needed", "review" and
"flagged" meaning the same thing in three places.

Never write a level as a sentence about the person. "Needs attention" labels a result; *your
health needs attention* labels a human being, and the pill has no basis for that.

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **A pill reading "Needs attention" beside a result, with a
    [CareCard](./care-card.mdx) underneath saying what to do.** The pill scans, the card
    explains, and neither pretends to be the other.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **A red dot with no word, explained in a legend at the bottom of the page.** Legends are
    not read, colour is not universally perceived, and a dot is not a message.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    **"Steady"** for a result that needs nothing right now.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    **"Normal"**, **"Healthy"**, **"Good"**, **"All clear"**. Every one of these is a claim
    about the person rather than about the interface, and the first is banned outright.
    [Reference ranges and normal](../health/reference-ranges.mdx) is where that ban is

  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

## Motion

StatusPill uses no motion. It does not animate when it mounts, and it does not animate when its
level changes while it is on screen. That is deliberate: motion is never a carrier of status in
this system, so a pill that moved to announce a change would be leaving to movement a meaning
the word and the colour are required to carry. No part of it declares a transition token.

Because it declares no transition, there is nothing for `prefers-reduced-motion` to reduce. A
surface that wants a level change to be noticed does that through a live region it owns, and an
[AlertBanner](./alert-banner.mdx) where the change is urgent, rather than by animating the pill.
What a screen reader should announce when a pill's level changes is one of the open questions at
the top of this page.

## Accessibility

Two of these are checked by a gate on every commit. The rest are arguments, and they are
labelled as such: `beta` promises that accessibility is claimed only where it has been
checked, with the gaps listed rather than left out.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y` on every commit:**

- **Colour is never the only carrier.** The static rig fails the build on any file that
  paints a status colour without also reading `CLINICAL_STATUS_META` and a glyph, and on any
  status surface with no `data-status`. A11Y017 also fails the build when two levels name the
  same glyph, whether in the `CLINICAL_STATUS_META` vocabulary or in a component's own
  four-level icon map, so the four levels are guaranteed four distinct glyph *names*: circle,
  ringed dot, diamond and octagon. They are not one glyph in four colours, which is what the
  measured CVD audit in `tokens/color.json` requires: `steady` and `attention` are identical
  under deuteranopia and in greyscale, and `steady` and `urgent` are identical under
  tritanopia. What no gate can settle is whether four distinct names draw four distinct
  *shapes* at the size the pill renders them. That judgement stays a human one, checked by
  eye, which is why the Preview above invites you to read it in greyscale. See
  [Iconography](../foundations/iconography/health-glyphs.mdx).
- **The status words are never restated.** They are read from
  `CLINICAL_STATUS_META[level].word`, and a copy of one written as a literal fails the same
  gate.

**Measured, by `pnpm run contrast` into `lib/generated/contrast.json`:**

- **Label against fill and boundary against fill, for all four levels in both themes.** The
  pill draws only role tokens (`-surface`, `-line` and `-ink`), and a component that uses only
  role tokens inherits those token measurements. The status ink clears the body-text floor on
  its surface in both themes, and the level's line clears the non-text floor. The table at the
  foot of this section is that measured data rather than a number anybody typed. What
  `contrast.json` does not carry is fill against page: there is no `surface on the page` pair,
  so that one comparison is still unmeasured.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **The word is in the accessible name, along with its subject.** With `describes`, the pill
  announces as *Needs attention, Second example measurement*, giving the level and then its
  subject, which is what a pill reached on its own needs: a table cell, a card corner or a
  standalone pill. Without it a screen-reader user hears a level floating free. Do not pass
  it when the subject is already visible text in the same reading unit, because the subject
  is then announced twice. That is why the list example on this page does not pass it, and the
  sizes example, whose pills float free of any adjacent subject, does.
- **The icon is decorative** and `aria-hidden`; the word carries the meaning. It is sized in
  `em`, so it scales with the text rather than staying put while the word grows.
- **The pill is not interactive** and is not in the tab order. If a product makes it a filter
  control it becomes a button, and at that point it owes a 44×44 target and a name that says
  what activating it does. This component provides neither.
- **At 200% text the pill grows and wraps**; the word is never truncated to an ellipsis and
  never replaced by the icon alone. Checked by eye at 200% in both themes; not yet checked by
  a machine.

**Not yet checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- Contrast under increased contrast, in both themes. Neither response lives in the token
  layer. [Surface](./surface.mdx#accessibility) answers `prefers-contrast: more` in its own
  utilities and draws its edge as an outline that forced colours keeps, and several other
  components (Field, Button, RangeBar, ScoreDial, Sheet and TrendSparkline) carry their own
  `forced-colors:` rules. This pill is neither of those. It renders no Surface and carries no
  rule of either kind, so it inherits whatever the material it is laid on does and nothing
  more. Nobody has yet opened the site under a real Windows high-contrast theme, so the
  increased-contrast claim cannot currently be made by anyone. Fill against page is unmeasured
  for a different reason: `contrast.json` carries no `surface on the page` pair for the status
  scope.
- Behaviour when a pill's level changes while it is on screen. The component mounts no live
  region, and that is deliberate, because it is not entitled to speak on the caller's
  behalf. What the caller should do instead is an open question on this page.

## Data attributes

The pill carries neither `data-category` nor `data-opsinjs-value`. It states a level, not a
category and not a measurement, so the two attributes that would tie it to the category axis or
to a reader's own number are absent by design. `data-status` is the hook a product styles or
scrapes a level by, and the three `data-slot` values are how a rule of your own reaches the
word, the glyph or the whole pill without an export.

## CSS variables

<CssVariablesTable name="status-pill" />

StatusPill exposes no custom property of its own for a consumer to set. Its colours are the
semantic status roles listed under Tokens below, read straight from the token layer rather than
through a component-scoped variable, so there is nothing of its own to override here. The one
custom property it reads directly is its ink, `--opsin-status-steady-ink` and its three level
siblings, and the Anatomy note above says why that is written as a bracketed `color:` property
rather than a `text-*` utility. To recolour any part, write a rule against its `data-slot`.

## Tokens

<TokenTable component="status-pill" />

These are the tokens the pill actually reads, generated from its source by
`scripts/build-tokens.mts` rather than listed by hand. They are the status surface, line and ink
roles for the four levels, together with the type step and spacing each size sets. The pill
never reads a category token or a raw ramp step, and a row here that named one would be the bug
