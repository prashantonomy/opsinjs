GoalRing draws how far a reading has come towards a goal the product set, as a ring, with the same fact stated in words beside it. It counts steps against a step goal, water against a water goal, whatever the product supplies, and it asserts one thing only: this value is this far towards that goal.

The design decision that shapes the whole component is what it leaves out. The roster declined goal rings for years because the pattern they come from turns progress into a game, with a streak that must not break and a celebration when the ring closes, and that is the wrong incentive for a reader managing a condition rather than training for one. So this ring closes and stops. It tracks no streak, fires nothing when it fills, reproaches nothing when it does not, and animates nothing on first paint. It is a picture of a position against a goal, and the words carry the rest.

## Preview

Check that the ring draws the fill in the category tint while the track stays neutral, so the one colour on the surface says what the ring counts rather than how urgent it is. Read the words beneath it: the label, the value against the goal, and a plain progress sentence that neither congratulates nor scolds. Turn the text size up and the ring holds its size while the words wrap, and nothing is truncated.

GoalRing has no dependency beyond the shared substrate every component installs. It draws its own SVG ring, composes no other component and is built on no Base UI primitive, because a static picture with a text twin needs none of them. It renders no icon, so it does not pull in `lucide-react` either.

The distinction that matters most is the first one. A ring and a dial look alike and mean different things: a dial places a number the product calculated between two bounds and names the band it falls in, while a ring shows how much of a goal is done. The moment there is a scale with named ranges and a verdict attached, the component is a [ScoreDial](./score-dial.mdx), because a ring has no band vocabulary and drawing a score as a ring invites a reader to read it as a mark out of the top.

The second and third exclusions keep the ring from standing in for a plain reading. A reading with no goal is a [MetricTile](./metric-tile.mdx), and a reading whose meaning is which reference band it lands in is a [RangeBar](./range-bar.mdx). A ring is only right when there is a goal, the product owns it, and being part-way there is the thing worth showing.

## Clinical meaning

**What it asserts.** GoalRing asserts exactly one thing: this value is this far towards a goal the product set, in these words. The fraction it draws is arithmetic over two numbers the product supplied, the value and the goal, and nothing more. It says nothing about what the reading means for the reader, nothing about a cause, and nothing about what to do next.

**What it must never be read as.** It is not a diagnosis, not a verdict on the reader and not a target opsinjs chose. A full ring means the product's goal was met, never that the reader is well, and an open ring means the goal is part-done, never that anything is wrong. It rewards no streak and stages no celebration, because a person managing a condition is owed information about their day rather than praise for a good one or blame for a missed one. The words "normal" and "abnormal" appear in no state it draws, as they appear nowhere in opsinjs, and [Reference ranges and normal](../health/reference-ranges.mdx) carries the argument.

**Which colour axis it may use.** The category axis, and only the category axis. The fill may take the identity tint of the kind of thing the ring counts, an activity ring in the activity colour, so a reader with several rings can tell them apart, and that colour answers what the ring is about and never how urgent it is. It may never use the status axis: progress towards a goal is not a clinical status, so no element carries `data-status` and the fill and the track are never both coloured with a verdict. Putting an identity colour and an urgency colour on one surface is the collision [The two colour axes](../health/two-colour-axes.mdx) exists to prevent, and a ring given no category draws neutral rather than borrowing a colour.

**Who owns the goal and the words.** The consuming product, always. GoalRing ships no default goal, no default label and no clinical vocabulary of any kind. The goal arrives as a prop because a target is a thing somebody chose for somebody, and shipping a goal inside a component library would be shipping a clinical decision to every product that installed it. The only thing this component supplies is the shape of the picture and the category colour read from the token layer.

## Content guidelines

Write the label as the reader's own name for what the ring counts, "Steps today" rather than an internal metric code, and keep the unit in the reader's words. Let the goal be the product's, and never phrase the surrounding copy as a challenge: the ring shows progress towards a target somebody set, not a test the reader is passing or failing. When the goal is met, say so plainly and stop; when it is not, name the progress and stop.

Never turn the ring into a reward. Do not add a streak counter beside it, do not fire a celebration when it fills, and do not imply that a missed goal is a lapse. A reader managing a condition is owed the number and nothing else, and the moment the ring starts keeping score of good days it has become the pattern the roster declined for years.

## Accessibility

Some of this is enforced by a gate on every pull request and every push to `main`, and the rest is argued and labelled as such. At `alpha`, accessibility is claimed only where it has been checked, with the gaps listed rather than left out. Nothing here has been through a review with a disabled user.

**Checked, by `pnpm run check:a11y`:**

- **No status colour, and no element on both axes.** The ring carries no `data-status` and reads no clinical vocabulary, and the fill carries `data-category` alone. The gate reads every element and fails the build on one that carries both axes or on a colour drawn outside the two shared axes and the neutral roles, and there is neither here.
- **No raw colour literal and no ramp step.** The track is the neutral border role, the fill is a category `-accent` role, and every text colour is a semantic role. No hex, no palette utility and no numbered step appears in the file.
- **No px type size.** The label, the readout and the sentence are the `subheadline`, `title2` and `footnote` semantic steps rather than pixel sizes, so a reader who has turned their text size up gets a larger ring and larger words.

**Argued, and true of the implementation, but not yet measured:**

- **Every fact is in words as well as in the ring.** The graphic carries `role="img"` with an accessible name that names the label, the readout and the progress, and the same three lines render visibly beside it. A reader who cannot parse the arc loses the picture and keeps every number.
- **The ring is not interactive.** Nothing takes focus, answers a key or a pointer, or animates on first paint. It is a static picture, so there is no keyboard contract to honour, and `motion-in-health-ui` is satisfied by drawing the fill where the reading sits rather than sweeping to it.
- **The three states stay three.** A finite value draws a fill, null draws an empty ring that says "no reading yet", and a broken value says "not available", so an absent reading is never rendered as a zero.

**Not checked at all, and listed rather than omitted:**

- Every contrast pair: the fill against the track, the track against the host ground, and each line of text against it. `scripts/check-contrast.mts` has not been run for this component, which is why the report below is empty rather than reassuring. The fill is a `-accent` role and the track a `-line` role, and this component sets no surface of its own, so both are painted on whatever ground the host provides rather than the matching `-surface` the token contract tunes against.
- How the ring and its repeated text twin play for a screen reader user, and whether repeating the label and the three lines reads as thorough or as a stammer. It has been reasoned about and not listened to.
- What the ring draws under `forced-colors: active`, where the fill maps to Highlight and the track to CanvasText. None of this has been put in front of a machine or a high contrast user.

## API reference

<PropsTable name="GoalRingProps" />

The two load-bearing props are `value` and `goal`. `value` is the reading, and it is typed `number | null` on purpose: null is the no-reading state, an empty ring with words that say so, and it is never drawn as a value of zero. A value that is not a finite number is a third state again, a reading that arrived broken, and it is announced rather than drawn. `goal` is the product's target, and opsinjs ships none and never invents one, because a goal is a decision somebody made for somebody. A goal of zero or less is not a target a fraction can be taken against, so the ring is drawn empty and the words say the progress cannot be shown.

`category` is the only colour the component takes, and it is the category axis alone: it tints the fill with the identity colour of the kind of thing the ring counts, so a reader with several rings can tell them apart, and it never says how urgent anything is. There is deliberately no `status` prop, because progress towards a goal is not a clinical verdict. `unit` is printed after the numbers in the readout and is never abbreviated by this component, and `label` names what the ring counts in the reader's own words.
