## Why a documentation site needs an expiry date

Guidance rots more quietly than code. A component that no longer compiles is
found in minutes; a paragraph that stopped being true two releases ago reads
exactly as authoritative as it did when it was written, and in a health system it
is read by somebody making a decision about how to present a number to a patient.

So every page here carries four fields in its frontmatter, and a script checks
them:

| Field | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| `reviewed` | The ISO date somebody last **re-read** the page against the system |
| `reviewer` | Which discipline reviewed it: `design`, `engineering`, `clinical` or `content` |
| `reviewEvery` | How long that review is good for: `3m`, `6m`, `12m` or `never` |
| `owner` | The team accountable for the next review |
| `a11yDate` | Where a page makes an accessibility claim, when that claim was last checked |

`<Reviewed />` prints the first three at the foot of the page, so a reader can see
the age of what they are reading without opening a file.

## The cadence, by page kind

`reviewEvery` is optional in frontmatter. When it is absent, the default for the
page's `kind` applies:

| Kind | Default cadence | Usual reviewer | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `health` | 6 months | `clinical` | Doctrine that shapes what a patient is told |
| `accessibility` | 6 months | `engineering` | Claims that are tested and can regress |
| `component` | 6 months | `design` | Tracks an implementation that moves |
| `foundation` | 6 months | `design` | Tracks tokens that move |
| `recipe`, `screen` | 6 months | `design` | Compose components, so they rot when components change |
| `guide` | 6 months | `engineering` | Installation instructions age fastest of all |
| `content` | 12 months | `content` | Language guidance changes slowly |
| `pattern` | 12 months | `design` | Patterns outlive the components implementing them |
| `handbook` | 12 months | `engineering` | Mechanics, tied to releases rather than the calendar |
| `reference` | 12 months | `engineering` | Bodies are generated; only the header block is reviewed |
| `project` | 12 months | `engineering` | Policy pages, changed deliberately |

**Pages that override the default downwards to 3 months** are the ones where
being wrong is most expensive: the emergency and crisis health pages, the
measured [contrast](../reference/generated/contrast.mdx) table, the
[catalogue](../reference/generated/catalogue.mdx), the
[roadmap](roadmap.mdx) and [state of the system](state-of-the-system.mdx).

`reviewEvery: never` exists for pages whose content is fixed by history — a dated
changelog entry, a decision record that has been superseded. Use it rarely; it is
the one setting that cannot be reported as stale.

## What the check actually does

`scripts/check-freshness.mts` reads every page, adds `reviewEvery` to `reviewed`,
and reports the pages whose review has expired, grouped by owner. It runs in the
nightly job rather than on every pull request, because a page expiring is not a
reason to block an unrelated change — it is a reason for somebody to be told.

A page with `kind: health` and no `reviewed` date is a different matter: that
fails `assert-ia.mts` on every build. Health doctrine without a review date is
not a stale page, it is an unsourced claim.

## What a review is, and is not

**A review is re-reading the page against the system as it is today** and
changing what has stopped being true. If a page needed no change, the reviewer
still updates `reviewed` — that is a real signal, and it is the whole point of
separating "when it was written" from "when it was last checked".

**Bumping the date without re-reading the page is the one prohibited operation
here.** It converts the only honest freshness signal on the site into decoration.
A page that is out of date and says so is more useful than a page that is out of
date and claims to have been checked last week.

## Owners

`owner` is a team slug, not a person: people leave and pages do not. The
disciplines are the four in the schema — `design`, `engineering`, `clinical`,
`content` — plus `core` for the project pages, which the maintainers own
collectively.

## If you are reading a stale page

The review date at the foot of the page is the truth. Cross-check against the
[changelog](changelog/index.mdx): if a release landed after the review date and
touched what you are reading, believe the changelog. Then tell us — the
report-a-problem control at the foot of every page is prefilled with the page
path and the docs version, and a stale page is exactly what it is for.
