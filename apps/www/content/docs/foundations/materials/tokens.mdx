## Overview

Six rungs, four properties each, two themes. That is twenty-four declarations in
the base layer plus the preference overrides, and every one of them is generated
from `tokens/material.json` by `scripts/build-tokens.mts`. Nothing here is typed
by a person and `pnpm check:generated` fails the build if the committed output
and a fresh regeneration disagree.

For what the rungs *mean*, start at [The ladder](./the-ladder.mdx). For which
one to use, [Choosing a layer](./choosing-a-layer.mdx). For why the alphas are
what they are, [The contrast floor](./the-contrast-floor.mdx). This page is the
list.

## How it works

### The naming grammar

```text
--opsin-material-<n>-bg        backing colour, including alpha
--opsin-material-<n>-blur      backdrop-filter blur radius
--opsin-material-<n>-border    hairline colour, or transparent
--opsin-material-<n>-shadow    drop shadow, or none

<n> ∈ 0..5   →   ground · panel · raised · bar · overlay · scrim
```

The numeric index is what appears in CSS; the name is what appears in
conversation, in review and in component props. They are the same thing and the
mapping never changes — a rung's number is part of its identity, because the
ordering has to be machine-readable for the compositing budget and the contrast
job to reason about it.

There is no fifth property and no per-rung variant. A rung is those four
declarations applied together.

### What the generator emits

1. A `:root` block with all six rungs for the light theme.
2. A `.dark` block overriding the rungs that differ — in dark, the translucent
   rungs' tints are near-black rather than near-white, and the borders flip from
   dark-at-low-alpha to white-at-low-alpha, because a dark border on a dark
   surface is invisible.
3. A `@media (prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce)` block collapsing rungs 3, 4
   and 5 to their opaque fallbacks
   ([Reduced transparency](./reduced-transparency.mdx)).

The print stylesheet removes `backdrop-filter` and `box-shadow` outright rather
than overriding the tokens, because a printed page has no backdrop to filter.

### Rungs 0, 1 and 2 reference other tokens

The opaque rungs do not carry their own colours. `ground` takes `--background`,
`panel` and `raised` take `--card`, and their borders take `--border`. This is
deliberate: an opaque material is a *composition* of the neutral palette, not a
parallel palette, and a theme change should move the ladder with it.
Translucent rungs are the exception — their tint carries an alpha the neutral
palette does not have, so it is authored on the rung.

## Using it

**Apply all four properties together.** A rung's blur without its backing tint
is the exact failure the contrast floor exists to prevent, and a rung's tint
without its border is a surface with no edge under reduced transparency. When
`Surface` exists, it will apply them as a unit; until then, applying them as a
unit is your job.

<NotBuiltYet name="surface" />

**Reference the token, never the resolved value.** A copied `oklch(… / 0.82)` is
a translucent surface outside the ladder: it will not respond to reduced
transparency, it will not respond to the theme, and it is in no measured pair.

**Do not build a rung out of Tailwind's opacity utilities.**
`bg-white/70 backdrop-blur-xl` looks like a material and is not one, for the same
three reasons. If a utility class is genuinely more convenient, expose the token
as one rather than approximating it.

**Do not add a per-component variant.** "The sheet on this one screen needs a
slightly stronger blur" is how a six-rung ladder becomes a fourteen-value
parameter space. If a screen genuinely needs something the ladder does not have,
that is intake, not CSS — see
[Contributing tokens](../../handbook/contributing/contributing-tokens.mdx).

## Accessibility impact

Every translucent rung above is measured against both of its compositing
extremes, in both themes, on every commit. The published results:

<ContrastReport scope="materials" />

Three properties of the table that matter and are easy to take for granted:

- **The alpha column is a floor, not a style.** It is the smallest value at which
  the rung's ink clears the contrast floor against a pure-black and a pure-white
  backdrop. Lowering it in a fork is a safety change, not a visual one.
- **Borders are measured as non-text pairs.** A hairline that disappears over a
  particular backdrop is an SC 1.4.11 failure, and it is the failure most often
  missed because it looks correct in the one mock everybody reviewed.
- **The reduced-transparency fallbacks are not separately measured, because they
  do not need to be** — they are the same opaque pairs rungs 1 and 2 already
  use, which is precisely why the fallback is the card colour rather than a new
  value.
