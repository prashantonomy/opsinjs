## What this means

Two readings a week apart. The second is higher than the first. What may the
interface say?

It may say that the second is higher than the first. That is the whole of what it
knows. It may not say the metric is rising, because two points do not establish a
direction; it may not say the change is meaningful, because it does not know the
measurement error; and it may not say the change is bad, because whether "higher"
is good depends on the metric, the person, and what they are trying to do.

Trend surfaces get all three of these wrong routinely, and they get them wrong in
the same direction: towards a confident narrative. A little green arrow is
enormously satisfying to build and to look at. It is also four separate
assertions — that the change is real, that it is a direction, that the direction
is good, and that it is about you — stacked in one glyph, none of them stated,
none of them checkable.

There is a second, quieter failure. Weight, blood pressure, resting heart rate
and sleep all fluctuate day to day for reasons that have nothing to do with
health: hydration, the time of the measurement, the cuff position, a warm room.
An interface that responds to that noise with a status change teaches people to
respond to noise too, which is how a health app produces anxiety as a feature.

## The rule

**Separate direction from valence, require a minimum window before drawing a
trend at all, and never let noise cross into status.**

1. **Direction and valence are two different pieces of information.** Direction —
   up, down, unchanged — is arithmetic. Valence — better, worse, neutral — is a
   clinical judgement about a specific metric for a specific person, and it is
   frequently unavailable.
2. **Where valence is unknown, show direction only.** An arrow with no colour, no
   status and no adjective is honest. An arrow that is green because the number
   went down is not.
3. **A metric declares its minimum window**, and no trend is drawn below it. Two
   readings is never enough for anything.
4. **A metric declares a change threshold** below which a difference is presented
   as unchanged. Below the noise floor, "about the same as last week" is the true
   statement.
5. **Say what is being compared.** "Compared with your average over the last 4
   weeks" — never a bare delta against an unnamed baseline.
6. **The time axis is honest.** Irregular readings are plotted at their real
   positions, gaps are visible as gaps, and the axis is never truncated to
   exaggerate a slope. See
   [Chart anatomy](../foundations/data-visualisation/chart-anatomy.mdx).
7. **A trend never sets a status on its own.** A direction may raise the salience
   of a value; the status still comes from the value against its range. Rising
   inside the usual range is not `attention`.
8. **Never extrapolate.** No projected lines, no "at this rate", no predicted
   values. The interface does not know what happens next and neither does anyone
   else.
9. **Every chart ships a text twin.** The trend must be readable as a sentence,
   for screen readers and for the many readers who do not read charts. See
   [Accessible charts](../foundations/data-visualisation/accessible-charts.mdx).
10. **Absence is not a flat line.** Missing readings are drawn as missing, never
    interpolated, and never as zero.

## Why (evidence)

<ResearchNote evidence="opinion" date="2026-09-02">
  Opinion throughout, and grounded in measurement rather than in a study.

  The defensible core is rules 3 and 4, and they are really one idea: any
  measurement has a variability, and a difference smaller than that variability
  is not a difference. This is not a design opinion — it is why a clinician
  measures blood pressure more than once and why weight is compared over weeks
  rather than days. What is a design opinion is the insistence that the interface
  must know the threshold and refuse to draw below it, rather than delegating the
  judgement to a reader who has no way to make it.

  The separation of direction from valence is our framing, and we think it is the
  most useful idea on this page precisely because it is so easy to check: for any
  arrow on any screen, ask which of the two it is claiming, and whether the app
  is entitled to claim it.

  We deliberately publish no minimum windows or thresholds. They are
  metric-specific, population-specific and clinical, and a plausible-looking
  default table here would be copied into products by people who assumed we knew.

  What would change our mind: little. Rule 8's absolute ban on extrapolation is
  the one worth arguing about — a well-calibrated projection with visible
  uncertainty is defensible in principle. We ban it because the uncertainty is
  the first thing to be dropped in a redesign.
</ResearchNote>

## Applying it

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    "About the same as your average over the last 4 weeks." Named baseline, named
    window, honest about a difference that is below the noise floor.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    "▲ 2%" with no baseline and no window, in green because up is good for this
    metric and nobody checked whether it is good for this person.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    "You have 2 readings. Log a few more over the next couple of weeks and we can
    show you how this is changing." An honest empty state for a trend.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    Draw a line between two points and label it "Trending up". The line is a
    drawing of the fact that there are two numbers.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    Keep the status pill driven by the latest value against its range, and let
    the sparkline carry the direction separately.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    Escalate to `attention` because three readings went up while all three sat
    comfortably inside the usual range. You have alarmed someone about noise.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    Show the gap: "No readings between 3 and 17 March." Missing data is
    information about the data.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    Connect across the gap with a straight line. The interface has drawn two
    weeks of readings that were never taken.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

## Components that implement this

`TrendSparkline` is specified to take a minimum window and a change threshold as
required inputs rather than optional ones, so a caller cannot get a trend by
omission, and to render an explicit not-enough-data state instead of a two-point
line.

## What this does not cover

- **Minimum windows and change thresholds** for any metric. Clinical content,
  and yours.
- **Statistical smoothing, seasonality and time-series modelling.** If you smooth
  a series, the reader must be able to see the raw points.
- **Chart construction, axes and colour**, which are
  [Data visualisation](../foundations/data-visualisation/index.mdx).
- **The pattern for a whole trend review screen**, which is
  [Trend review](../patterns/trend-review.mdx).
- **Goals and targets.** A target is a valence the reader chose, which changes
  the analysis and is not yet specified here.
