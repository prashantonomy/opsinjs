## What this means

Most component libraries ship four semantic states borrowed from server logs:
`info`, `success`, `warning`, `error`. They are a perfectly good vocabulary for
describing what happened to a request. They are a terrible vocabulary for
describing a person.

`error` is wrong because nothing has failed — a high reading is a fact about a
body, not a bug. `success` is wrong because it congratulates someone on a number
they may have very little control over, and its absence then reads as failure.
`warning` is wrong because it is a single level, so every non-ideal reading lands
in the same bucket as the one that genuinely needs same-day attention. And
`info`, in a health context, is where teams quietly put the things they have not
decided how to say.

opsinjs replaces them with four ordinal levels that describe the relationship
between a value and what is expected for this person, and nothing else.

## The rule

**There are exactly four clinical status levels. They are ordinal, they describe
a value's relationship to what is expected, and their meanings are fixed.**

### `steady`

**Meaning:** this sits where it is expected to sit. Nothing here needs the
reader's attention today.

**Tone:** neutral and unexcited. Not congratulatory. "In your usual range."

**What it must never mean:** healthy, safe, normal, good, or "you are fine".
`steady` is a statement about one number against one range, not a verdict on a
person. A reading can be `steady` in someone who is seriously unwell.

### `watch`

**Meaning:** slightly outside what is expected, or moving in a direction that is
worth noticing. No action is required now; the reader should not be surprised if
it comes up next time.

**Tone:** informative, not anxious. "A little higher than your usual range."

**What it must never mean:** a mild version of a diagnosis, or a hint that the
reader should do something without saying what. `watch` with no next step is the
level most likely to generate an unnecessary phone call.

### `attention`

**Meaning:** outside the expected range far enough, or for long enough, that a
person should do something specific and reasonably soon. There is always a named
action.

**Tone:** direct and calm, action first. "Book an appointment to have this
checked."

**What it must never mean:** an emergency, a diagnosis, or "you have X".
`attention` says the number needs looking at by someone qualified. It never says
what the answer will be.

### `urgent`

**Meaning:** this may need help today, and the interface should say so and get
out of the way.

**Tone:** short, unambiguous, no hedging, no jargon, action in the first line.

**What it must never mean:** anything that is merely important to your product.
`urgent` is reserved for clinical urgency. It is not for expiring trials,
incomplete profiles, or unsynced devices. See
[Emergency and escalation](./emergency-and-escalation.mdx) for what an interface
must actually do at this level, and
[Alarm fatigue](./alarm-fatigue.mdx) for the budget that keeps it meaningful.

### Who assigns a status

**You do — or rather, whoever is clinically accountable in your organisation
does.** opsinjs ships the vocabulary, the tokens, the tone and the presentation
rules. It ships no thresholds, for any metric, ever.

A status assignment must be traceable to a rule someone owns and can defend:

- The rule is written down outside the component, with its source and its owner.
- The rule states the population it applies to. A threshold that is right for a
  40-year-old is often wrong in pregnancy, in childhood, or with a long-term
  condition.
- Where a device or a laboratory supplies its own flag, prefer that flag over
  deriving one, and say whose flag it is.
- A status that comes out of a model or an estimate is labelled as such; see
  [Data provenance and device accuracy](./data-provenance-and-device-accuracy.mdx).

### Rules that hold at every level

1. **Status is never conveyed by colour alone** — colour, icon and word together.
2. **Status is never conveyed by motion, sound or haptics alone.**
3. **The word is not optional and is not an abbreviation.** The visible label is
   the level's meaning in plain words, not the token name.
4. **One level per element.** An element does not carry two statuses; a container
   does not inherit the highest status of its children by default.
5. **Escalation is stated, not implied.** Moving a reader from `watch` to
   `attention` between sessions requires saying that it changed and why.
6. **A status change is announced to assistive technology** using the contract on
   [Screen readers](../accessibility/screen-readers.mdx) — politely for `steady`
   and `watch`, assertively only at `urgent`.

## Why (evidence)

<ResearchNote evidence="mixed" date="2026-09-02">
  **Cited.** Rules 1 and 2 rest on WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.1 (Use of Colour) and on the
  broader requirement that information is not conveyed through a single sensory
  characteristic. Rule 6 follows the ARIA live-region model rather than our
  preference: an assertive announcement interrupts, so it is reserved for the
  level where interruption is warranted.

  **Opinion.** Four levels, and these four names, are our judgement. Three levels
  collapse "worth noticing" and "do something", which is the distinction users
  most need and the one teams most often blur. Five levels cannot be assigned
  consistently by different people looking at the same number — we consider
  reliable assignment more valuable than resolution.

  The names were chosen to be verbs-adjacent and non-diagnostic. `steady` rather
  than `normal` because [normal is banned](./reference-ranges.mdx); `watch`
  rather than `caution` because it names what to do; `attention` rather than
  `warning` because a warning implies a threat rather than a task; `urgent`
  because there is no gentler word that survives translation without losing
  force.

  What would change our mind: field evidence that users cannot reliably
  distinguish `watch` from `attention` in context, or that a two-level system
  (fine / not fine, plus an emergency path) produces better outcomes because it
  is harder to misuse. The second is a real possibility and we would take it
  seriously.
</ResearchNote>

## Applying it

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    `attention` — "Blood pressure 158/96 mmHg. Higher than your usual range.
    Book an appointment in the next week to have this checked."
    The value, the relationship, the action, in that order.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    `attention` — "Warning: abnormal blood pressure detected." Three problems:
    "abnormal" is a clinical judgement the interface has not earned, "detected"
    sounds like a machine has diagnosed something, and there is no action.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    `steady` — "72 bpm. In your usual range." Then stop. A steady reading does
    not need a paragraph.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    `steady` — "Great work! Your heart rate is perfect." It congratulates
    someone for something they may not control, and it sets up the next reading
    to feel like a personal failure.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    Reserve `urgent` for clinical urgency and let the product's own problems live
    at `Callout` or lower. Your sync failure is not the same kind of thing as a
    dangerously high reading.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    Style a subscription prompt with `--opsin-status-urgent-surface` because it
    converts better. It does, once. Then every genuine alert converts worse
    forever.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    Keep the status word in the accessible name: "Attention: blood pressure
    158 over 96". A screen reader user gets the level and the value in one
    utterance.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    Rely on an amber dot with `aria-hidden` on the icon and no text. The status
    is then visible only to sighted users with unimpaired colour vision.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

## Components that implement this

{/* Generated from `implements`. Do not restate the list by hand. */}

Every component that accepts a status takes it from the same four-value union —
exported as `ClinicalStatus` and documented at
[Types](../reference/generated/types.mdx) — so an invalid level is a type error
rather than an unstyled element. The status vocabulary is also published as JSON
at [Machine-readable schemas](../agents/machine-readable-schemas.mdx) so an agent
generating a screen can enumerate the legal values instead of guessing.

## What this does not cover

- **Thresholds.** Which number is `watch` and which is `attention`, for any
  metric, in any population. This is the boundary and it is absolute.
- **Triage.** These four levels are not a triage scale and must not be mapped
  onto one. Clinical triage systems are validated instruments; this is a
  presentation vocabulary.
- **Diagnosis.** No level asserts a condition. If your product makes diagnostic
  claims, read [Regulatory context](./regulatory-context.mdx) — you are probably
  building a medical device.
- **The exact sentences.** Wording per level, including the words banned at each,
  is [Writing status and alerts](../content/writing-status-and-alerts.mdx).
- **Colour values.** The palettes, their derivation and their measured contrast
  are [Colour](../foundations/colour/index.mdx).
