opsinjs publishes no minimum window, change threshold, freshness window or accuracy figure for any metric. The metric's clinical owner supplies each one.

## Trends

1. Direction (up, down, unchanged) is arithmetic. Valence (better, worse) is a clinical judgement about one metric for one person, often unavailable. Keep them apart.
2. Where no valence is defined, show direction only: no colour, status or adjective on the arrow.
3. Draw no trend below the metric's declared minimum window. Two readings give a difference, not a direction.
4. Below the metric's declared change threshold, say no change: "About the same as your average over the last 4 weeks."
5. Name the baseline and window on screen, never a bare delta.
6. Plot readings at their real times, show gaps, and never truncate the axis to steepen a slope ([Data visualisation](../foundations/data-visualisation.mdx)).
7. A trend never sets a status. Status comes from the latest value against its range, so rising inside the usual range is not `attention`.
8. Never extrapolate: no projected line, no "at this rate", no predicted value.
9. Every chart has a text twin stating the same baseline, window and direction.
10. Draw missing readings as missing, never interpolated or plotted as zero: "No readings between 3 and 17 March."
11. A smoothed series keeps its raw points visible.

The product owns the baseline and the sentence. The screen is [Trend review](../patterns/trend-review.mdx).

## Staleness and missing data

A value is fresh, stale, partial, estimated or absent ([States](../foundations/states.mdx)), and the reader must be able to tell which.

12. Every value has a timestamp: relative for recency, absolute on demand.
13. Each metric declares its own freshness window; none is defaulted.
14. Staleness is shown by de-emphasising the value itself, not only in a caption.
15. A stale value shows its age and a way to refresh, neither at full weight nor withheld.
16. An estimate is labelled at the number with its source and rounded more coarsely ([Numbers and units](./numbers.mdx)): "Estimated from your watch. Roughly 7 hours 30 minutes."
17. A failed fetch and an empty history get different sentences and next actions. Neither is ever rendered as a number.
18. A loading placeholder implies no value: no plausible number, chart shape or status colour.
19. Status degrades with certainty. Check it first: a value of unestablished recency, completeness or provenance never drives `attention` or `urgent`.
20. State a partial result; never average over the gap: "3 of your 4 devices have reported today."
21. Offline is a state of its own: say the device is offline rather than showing last values as current ([Offline and stale data](../patterns/offline-and-stale-data.mdx)).

Your data layer supplies each reading's time, marks estimates, separates failure from absence and names the reporting sources.

## Provenance

Each class bounds what the interface may assert:

- **Clinically measured** (a laboratory result): compare against an attributed range and carry a status.
- **Device measured** (a validated home cuff, a scale): compare against a range where the device's method supports it, and carry a status where a clinical rule says so.
- **Device estimated** (optical heart rate in motion, sleep stages): show as an estimate and as trends, never with a clinical status on its own.
- **Self-reported** (a typed weight, a remembered dose): record and display as reported, never as measured.

22. Record provenance at capture; it travels with the value, never reconstructed or defaulted.
23. Show it in plain words at the value and in its accessible name: "from your cuff", "estimated by your watch", "you entered this".
24. Status is bounded by class. An estimate never escalates on its own, so a wrist reading taken mid-run raises no cardiac alert, and may prompt a cuff reading instead.
25. Never combine classes in one figure, such as a weekly average, without saying so.
26. Name the device ("your Model X cuff"), never marketing ("clinically validated technology").
27. Attribute and link a manufacturer's accuracy claim. Never restate it as the product's own, strengthen it, or invent a figure.
28. State once, at the number, what the device does not do: a wrist sensor does not measure blood pressure.
29. Label a model output as one, with its inputs.
30. Never silently correct self-reported data. Query an implausible entry with the reader.
31. Exports carry provenance ([Sharing with a clinician](../patterns/sharing-with-a-clinician.mdx)).

## Components

[TrendSparkline](../components/trend-sparkline.mdx) requires `minimumPoints`, and below it draws nothing and says so. `changeThreshold` has no default, and without it the caption names no direction. A gap is an explicit `value: null`. [RelativeTime](../components/relative-time.mdx) has no staleness default; `staleAfterHours` adds the stale words and muted treatment. [Value](../components/value.mdx) renders `null` as an absence, never `0`.

No component carries a provenance class, so rules 22 to 31 are the caller's. [ResultCard](../components/result-card.mdx) takes provenance as free text, so never pass it a `status` derived from an estimate or a self-report. [MetricTile](../components/metric-tile.mdx)'s `event` defaults to `measured`, unchecked against how the figure was obtained. [SourceCitation](../components/source-citation.mdx) renders provenance beside a value, not in its accessible name, and checks nothing.

## Evidence

Every rule here is opinion; no study is cited. Rules 3 and 4 follow from measurement variability. The four classes are our model, coarse so a non-expert can assign them.

Take any accuracy claim from the manufacturer's current documentation or a study you have read. Differences in optical sensing across skin tones are discussed in research and by regulators. We give no figure or citation: ask your supplier, and apply rule 28.
