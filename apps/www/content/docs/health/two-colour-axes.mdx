## What this means

Open almost any consumer health app and count the meanings of red.

Red is the heart section. Red is also "high". Red is also the brand accent on the
primary button. Blue is sleep, and blue is also the informational tooltip, and
blue is also the disabled state on the same screen. By the fourth screen the
palette has stopped meaning anything, and the user has learnt — correctly — that
colour here is decoration. That learning is very hard to reverse, and it is
learnt long before you need it: the one day the app genuinely needs to say "this
is not like your other readings", it has nothing left to say it with.

The reason this happens is not carelessness. It happens because two entirely
different jobs are being done with one palette. One job is **identity**: helping
someone recognise, at a glance, that this tile is about sleep and that one is
about their heart. The other job is **status**: telling them whether the number
in front of them is fine, worth watching, or needs attention. Both are legitimate
uses of colour. They just cannot share a wavelength.

opsinjs separates them into two axes with two palettes, two token namespaces, and
a rule against mixing them that is enforced by a lint rule rather than by good
intentions.

## The rule

**Colour in opsinjs runs on exactly two independent axes. A single colour value
may express identity or status, never both, and no element may be tinted by both
axes at once.**

**Axis 1 — category identity.** Six families, each with a stable hue that means
"this is what the metric is about" and nothing else. Tokens live under
`--opsin-category-*`:

| Category | Token stem | What it identifies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heart | `--opsin-category-heart-*` | Blood pressure, heart rate, rhythm, circulation |
| Activity | `--opsin-category-activity-*` | Movement, steps, exercise, energy expenditure |
| Sleep | `--opsin-category-sleep-*` | Duration, timing, quality, disturbance |
| Nutrition | `--opsin-category-nutrition-*` | Intake, hydration, glucose, weight |
| Mind | `--opsin-category-mind-*` | Mood, stress, cognition, wellbeing |
| Cycle | `--opsin-category-cycle-*` | Menstrual and reproductive tracking |

Each category exposes three roles — `-accent`, `-surface`, `-ink` — and nothing
else. There is no `--opsin-category-heart-danger`, and there never will be.

**Axis 2 — clinical status.** Four ordinal levels that mean "how does this sit
against what is expected for you" and nothing about which body system produced
it. Tokens live under `--opsin-status-*`, with roles `-surface`, `-line`, `-ink`:

`--opsin-status-steady-*` · `--opsin-status-watch-*` ·
`--opsin-status-attention-*` · `--opsin-status-urgent-*`

The meanings, the tone and the assignment rules are on
[Clinical status semantics](./clinical-status-semantics.mdx).

**The never-mix rule, stated so it can be tested:**

1. An element painted from the category axis may not change colour when its
   status changes.
2. An element painted from the status axis may not change hue when its category
   changes.
3. No element resolves a colour from both namespaces for the same property. A
   card may have a category-tinted header and a status-tinted badge; the header
   may not be a blend of the two.
4. Status is never expressed by colour alone. Colour, an icon and a word,
   together, every time — which also means a status is legible when both axes are
   rendered in greyscale.

## Why (evidence)

<ResearchNote evidence="mixed" date="2026-09-02">
  **The cited part.** Rule 4 is not ours. WCAG 2.2 success criterion 1.4.1 (Use
  of Colour) requires that colour is never the only visual means of conveying
  information, indicating an action or distinguishing a visual element. In a
  health interface the consequence is unusually concrete: a red-only "high"
  marker is simply absent for a reader with a common form of colour vision
  deficiency, and colour vision deficiency is more prevalent among men, who are
  already less likely to engage with health apps. We test this and publish the
  result at
  [Colour independence](../accessibility/colour-independence.mdx).

  **The opinion part.** The separation into exactly two axes, and the choice of
  six categories, is our design judgement. We are not aware of a study comparing
  a two-axis palette against a merged one in a consumer health product, and we do
  not cite one. The argument is structural rather than empirical: a palette where
  a hue has one meaning can be learnt; a palette where a hue has two meanings can
  only be memorised case by case, and a user who cannot predict the meaning of a
  colour will stop attending to colour.

  What would change our mind: evidence that users read a merged palette
  accurately in context, or that six identity hues is too many to distinguish
  reliably at tile size on a low-quality display. The second is the risk we
  consider most real, which is why category colour is never load-bearing — losing
  it costs recognition speed, never meaning.
</ResearchNote>

The four status levels above must remain distinguishable under protanopia,
deuteranopia, tritanopia and full greyscale. If they do not, the icon and the
word are doing the work, which is exactly the design intent — colour is the
fastest channel, never the only one.

## Applying it

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    Paint identity on the container and status on a discrete indicator. A sleep
    tile keeps its sleep hue in every state; the `StatusPill` inside it changes
    from `steady` to `watch` without the tile changing colour.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    Turn the sleep tile red when sleep is short. You have destroyed the reader's
    ability to find the sleep tile *and* spent the strongest signal in the system
    on a mildly interesting number.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    Use `--opsin-status-attention-surface` for the background of an alert about a
    heart reading. Status owns the alert; the heart category is named in the
    words.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    Derive an "urgent heart" colour by blending `--opsin-category-heart-accent`
    with `--opsin-status-urgent-line`. There are twenty-four such blends, none of
    them are tested for contrast, and each one is a new meaning nobody has been
    taught.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    Let a chart use the chart ramps from
    [Chart colour](../foundations/data-visualisation/chart-colour.mdx). Series
    colour is a third, separate problem with its own constraints.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    Colour a chart's series by category token. Category hues are chosen to be
    distinguishable as identity marks at tile size, not as adjacent lines in a
    sequential ramp.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    Reach for `Callout` when the message is informational — a tip, a note about
    how the app works. `Callout` deliberately sits outside the status axis.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    Use `AlertBanner` for a non-clinical message because it is the component that
    looks important. Spending `attention` on a marketing prompt is how the
    vocabulary dies.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

## Components that implement this

{/* Generated from `implements`. Do not restate the list by hand. */}

The axis separation is enforced three ways: the token namespaces are disjoint,
components that accept a `status` do not accept a `category` for the same
surface, and a lint rule flags any declaration that resolves both namespaces onto
one property. The lint rule is the one that survives a deadline.

## What this does not cover

- **Which category a metric belongs to.** Some metrics genuinely sit in two —
  weight is nutrition and it is also cardiovascular. Pick one per surface and be
  consistent; opsinjs does not adjudicate.
- **Adding a seventh category.** Possible, and covered mechanically at
  [Category palettes](../theming/category-palettes.mdx). It is a system decision
  with a cost, not a theme tweak.
- **Redefining the status palette.** Strongly discouraged; if you must, read
  [Status palettes](../theming/status-palettes.mdx) first.
- **Brand colour.** Your brand accent is a third namespace again, and it may
  never be a status colour. See [Colour roles](../foundations/colour/colour-roles.mdx).
- **The engine.** How the ramps are derived, clamped to gamut and validated
  against a contrast floor is [Colour](../foundations/colour/index.mdx), not this
  page. This page is what the colours are allowed to *mean*.
