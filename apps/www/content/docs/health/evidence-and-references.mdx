## What this means

A health design system is unusually easy to make look authoritative. Add a
citation to each page, cite a study for each rule, and the whole thing acquires
the register of clinical guidance. Almost nobody checks references, and in a
domain where readers are inclined to trust, an unchecked reference is worse than
none — it converts an opinion into an apparent finding.

So this pillar operates a rule that is stricter than it looks: **`evidence:
cited` means a source we can hand you, that we have opened, that says what we say
it says.** Everything else is `opinion`, and marked as such in the page's own
frontmatter where a machine can read it.

The consequence is uncomfortable and we publish it rather than hide it: most of
this pillar is `opinion`. It is careful, argued, falsifiable opinion, and every
page says what would change our mind. It is not evidence, and we will not dress
it as evidence to look more serious.

## The rule

**Never invent a citation, a statistic, a date or a study. Prefer an honest
opinion to a plausible reference. Every `cited` claim names a source you can
open.**

Four rules, applied to every page in this section:

1. **`evidence` is declared in frontmatter** on every `kind: health` page. It is
   machine-readable and is emitted into the corpus a machine reads.
2. **`cited` requires an openable source.** A DOI, a URL, or a named published
   standard. Not "research shows", not "studies have found", not a reference
   reconstructed from memory.
3. **`mixed` is honest and common.** Most pages have one or two grounded claims
   and several judgements. The page says which are which, in the body.
4. **Never copy.** Sources are cited and linked, never pasted. The NHS Digital
   Service Manual and NHS content are Crown copyright and are not reusable; where
   this documentation covers similar ground it does so in original prose and
   points you at the original. Where we name a guideline we do not paraphrase its
   substance, because a decayed paraphrase is a liability.

## Why (evidence)

<ResearchNote evidence="mixed" date="2026-09-02">
  The rule itself is our policy, so this page's `mixed` marking refers to the
  sources listed above rather than to the discipline.

  The reasoning for the discipline is about failure modes. A design system is
  copied. A rule stated here appears, unattributed, in a product's internal
  guidelines within a year, and from there in a clinical safety case. If the
  original was an opinion presented as a finding, the error is now three levels
  deep and unrecoverable. Marking opinion as opinion is the only version of this
  that degrades safely: a downstream reader can weigh it.

  There is a cost and it is worth stating. Pages that say "this is our judgement"
  are less persuasive than pages that say "research shows", and some readers will
  discount them. We accept that. The alternative is to be more persuasive than we
  are entitled to be, in a domain where the people relying on us cannot check.

  What would change our mind: nothing.
</ResearchNote>

## Applying it

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    "This is our design judgement. Here is the reasoning, and here is what would
    change our mind." Falsifiable, weighable, honest.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    "Research shows that users prefer…" with no reference. The construction
    borrows authority it has not earned, and it is unfalsifiable.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    Cite the standard by name and number and let the reader open the current
    version: "WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.1".
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    Paraphrase the standard's requirement into your own sentence and present the
    sentence as the requirement. Paraphrases decay; requirements do not.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    Set `evidence: opinion` and say so in the body when you are stating a design
    position. It is machine-readable, so an agent reading this corpus can weigh
    the claim.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    Set `evidence: cited` and link a search results page, a blog summary, or a
    reference you have not opened.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

## What this does not cover

  on, not a survey of the field.

  anywhere in opsinjs.

  [Docs freshness](../project/docs-freshness.mdx).
