## What this means

Someone opens a dashboard with nine tiles on it. They are looking for the sleep
one. They are not reading the labels — nobody reads nine labels — they are
looking for the colour and the shape they remember from last time.

That is the entire job of the category axis: to make a metric findable in under a
second, in peripheral vision, without reading. It is a wayfinding system, and
like every good wayfinding system it works only because the colours are boring,
stable and mean exactly one thing. The moment the sleep tile turns amber because
last night was short, the reader has lost their landmark, and they have lost it
on precisely the day they were most anxious to find it.

Category colour is therefore the *least* semantically loaded colour in opsinjs.
It carries recognition and nothing else. This is a demotion, and it is
deliberate.

## The rule

**A category colour identifies which family a metric belongs to. It may never
express value, quality, severity, direction, urgency, or brand.**

The six categories, and the boundary of each:

| Category | Covers | Does not cover |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Heart** | Blood pressure, heart rate, resting heart rate, rhythm, variability, circulation | Fitness performance — that is Activity |
| **Activity** | Steps, workouts, active minutes, movement, energy expenditure | Sleep-derived recovery scores — that is Sleep |
| **Sleep** | Duration, timing, consistency, disturbance, sleep-derived recovery | Mood on waking — that is Mind |
| **Nutrition** | Intake, hydration, glucose, cholesterol, weight and body composition | Digestive symptoms — those are a symptom surface, not a metric family |
| **Mind** | Mood, stress, cognition, wellbeing check-ins | Sleep quality — that is Sleep |
| **Cycle** | Menstrual cycle, ovulation, pregnancy tracking | General hormonal results — those are Nutrition or a clinical result surface |

Each category exposes exactly three roles and no others:

- `--opsin-category-<name>-accent` — the identity mark. A rule, a dot, an icon
  tint, a chart accent. Never a large fill behind body text.
- `--opsin-category-<name>-surface` — a low-chroma background tint for a
  container. Always passes the contrast floor with `-ink` on top.
- `--opsin-category-<name>-ink` — a foreground that is legible on `-surface` and
  on the page background in both themes.

The prohibitions, each of which is testable:

1. **A category colour never changes with a value.** The sleep tile is the same
   colour when sleep was four hours and when it was nine.
2. **A category colour never encodes quality.** There is no "good" green
   category and no "bad" red category; the palette is chosen for mutual
   distinguishability, not for connotation.
3. **A category colour is never the only cue.** Every category-coloured element
   also carries its name in text, because six hues at tile size on a poor display
   in bright sun is not a reliable channel.
4. **A category namespace never gains a fourth role.** If you find yourself
   wanting `--opsin-category-heart-alert`, you want the status axis.
5. **Category colour is not chart colour.** See
   [Chart colour](../foundations/data-visualisation/chart-colour.mdx).
6. **Category colour is not brand colour.** Your accent lives in the brand
   namespace and never appears as a seventh category.

## Why (evidence)

<ResearchNote evidence="opinion" date="2026-09-02">
  This page is opinion throughout, and the honest summary is that we chose six
  categories because six is what a general consumer health product tends to need,
  and because six hues can be spaced far enough apart in OKLCH to stay
  distinguishable while all clearing the same contrast floor. That second
  constraint is real and measurable; the choice of six is not.

  The demotion of category colour to a pure wayfinding channel *is* argued, and
  the argument is the same one made on
  [The two colour axes](./two-colour-axes.mdx): a channel that sometimes carries
  meaning and sometimes does not is a channel that must be checked every time,
  and users do not check.

  We are aware of one real risk with this design and state it openly: because
  category colour is decorative by construction, a team under pressure will
  eventually ask why it exists at all, and the answer — recognition speed — is
  hard to demonstrate in a usability session that lasts twenty minutes with a
  screen the participant has never seen. The value accrues to the user who has
  opened the app two hundred times. We have not measured it.

  What would change our mind: evidence that consistent iconography and position
  alone deliver the same recognition speed, in which case the category palette
  could be retired and the system would get simpler.
</ResearchNote>

## Applying it

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    Give the tile a persistent category identity — a `-accent` rule at the top
    edge or a tinted icon — and put the status on a separate indicator inside it.
    Two channels, two jobs, both readable at a glance.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    Fill the whole tile with `-surface` and then also tint it toward
    `--opsin-status-attention-surface` when the reading is high. That blend is
    outside the tested palette and it means nothing to anyone.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    Keep one metric in one category everywhere in the product. Weight is
    Nutrition on the dashboard, in search, in the trend screen and in the export.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    Let weight be Nutrition on one screen and Activity on another because it fit
    the layout. Inconsistent identity is worse than no identity: the reader now
    has to read the labels *and* has a false landmark.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    Use `-accent` for marks and `-surface` for containers, and check the pair
    against [The contrast floor](../foundations/materials/the-contrast-floor.mdx)
    before shipping a new category.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    Set body text in `-accent`. Accents are chosen for chroma and separation, not
    for reading, and several of them fail the text floor by design.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

## Components that implement this

{/* Generated from `implements`. Do not restate the list by hand. */}

Components that accept a `category` expose it as a `data-category` attribute so
the identity is available to CSS and to tests without a prop drill, and they
refuse to accept a `status` for the same painted surface. The one-metric-one-
category rule cannot be enforced by a component — it is a product decision — so
it belongs on the review checklist instead.

## What this does not cover

- **Adding, removing or renaming a category.** Mechanically possible and
  described at [Category palettes](../theming/category-palettes.mdx). It is a
  breaking change to a shared vocabulary, not a theme setting.
- **Which category a borderline metric belongs to.** Blood glucose is defensibly
  Nutrition and defensibly a clinical result. Choose once, write it down.
- **Symptoms, medications and appointments.** They are not metric families and
  they do not take a category colour. They are their own surfaces.
- **The hue values.** Derivation, gamut clamping and the P3 escalation path are
  [Colour](../foundations/colour/index.mdx).
- **Icons.** A category icon is a stronger recognition cue than its colour, and
  it is governed separately.
