## What this means

A pulsing red dot is the most tempting element in health interface design. It
costs eight lines of CSS, it reads as urgent to everyone in the design review,
and it makes the screenshot look alive.

It is also invisible to a reader who has reduced motion turned on, invisible to a
screen reader, invisible in a screenshot the user sends to their partner,
invisible to anyone who arrives at the screen after the animation has finished,
and actively distressing to some readers with vestibular disorders or anxiety.
The information content of a pulse is zero for a large fraction of the audience,
and the fraction is enriched for exactly the people most likely to be using a
health app.

Motion in a health product has a second problem that other products do not have.
An animated number counting up to a result is a small delightful flourish when
the number is a step count. When the number is a biopsy-adjacent lab value and
the reader is holding their breath, the same flourish is a slot machine.

## The rule

**Motion may support a message. It may never be the message.**

Concretely:

1. **Urgency is never conveyed by motion.** No pulsing, flashing, shaking or
   bouncing to indicate status. Status is colour, icon and word — see
   [Clinical status semantics](./clinical-status-semantics.mdx).
2. **A value is never revealed by animation.** Health numbers appear at their
   final value. No count-up, no dial sweep on first paint, no progressive
   reveal of a result.
3. **Nothing that carries clinical meaning may auto-dismiss.** A message that
   times out is a message the reader may never have read.
4. **Nothing flashes.** More than three flashes per second is a seizure risk and
   is prohibited outright; below that threshold it is still forbidden here
   because it fails rules 1 and 3.
5. **Every animation has a defined reduced-motion behaviour, stated per token.**
   The fallback is not "no animation" by default — it is an instant, complete,
   equally informative state. See
   [Reduced motion](../foundations/motion/reduced-motion.mdx).
6. **Motion never delays access to a result.** If an entrance transition means a
   reader waits 400 ms to read their result, the transition is too expensive.
7. **Transitions preserve continuity.** Morph a surface rather than swapping it,
   so a reader who looks away and back does not lose their place. See
   [Choreography](../foundations/motion/choreography.mdx).

What motion is *for*, in this system: confirming that an input was received,
showing where a surface came from and where it went, keeping a scroll position
comprehensible, and making a loading state feel bounded. All four are orientation
jobs. None of them is a meaning job.

## Why (evidence)

<ResearchNote evidence="mixed" date="2026-09-02">
  **Cited.** Three WCAG 2.2 success criteria bear directly on this page and we
  treat them as normative rather than advisory: 2.2.2 (Pause, Stop, Hide) for
  moving content, 2.3.1 (Three Flashes or Below Threshold) for flashing, and
  2.3.3 (Animation from Interactions) for motion triggered by interaction. Rule 1
  additionally follows from 1.4.1 (Use of Colour) read together with the general
  principle that information must not depend on a single sensory characteristic —
  motion is a sensory characteristic. `prefers-reduced-motion` exists because
  vestibular disorders are real and common enough that operating systems ship a
  system-level control for them.

  **Opinion.** Rule 2 — no animated reveal of a health value — is ours, and it is
  the one we would defend hardest. The argument is about the emotional frame a
  count-up creates: a number that arrives gradually invites the reader to
  anticipate, and anticipation is the wrong state to put someone in when the
  content may be bad news. See
  [Delivering difficult results](./delivering-difficult-results.mdx).

  Rule 6's 400 ms is illustrative, not measured. The principle is that no
  transition may sit between a reader and a result they came for.

  What would change our mind on rule 2: evidence that a brief reveal improves
  comprehension or recall of a result. We would still want it off by default and
  off entirely for anything outside `steady`.
</ResearchNote>

## Applying it

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    Render an `urgent` banner at its final position and appearance, with an
    assertive announcement to assistive technology. Its weight comes from
    contrast, size, position and words.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    Pulse the banner border to make it feel more urgent. Under reduced motion it
    is a static banner; you have added risk for a subset of readers and
    information for nobody.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    Show a result at its value the moment the screen paints. If the data is not
    there yet, show a `Skeleton` that reserves the exact layout and never implies
    a number.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    Animate `0 → 142` over 800 ms. For 800 ms the interface displays values that
    are not this person's result, one of which may be a value they have been
    dreading.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    Under reduced motion, show the sheet in place with a crossfade of opacity
    only, retaining every affordance. The reader gets the same information in the
    same structure.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    Under reduced motion, remove the transition and let a modal appear with no
    change of context at all. Reduced motion means less movement, not less
    orientation.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

<DoDont>
  <DoDont.Do>
    Use a skeleton or a progress indicator whose motion is bounded and
    non-urgent, and stop it when loading ends.
  </DoDont.Do>
  <DoDont.Dont>
    Leave an indeterminate spinner on a screen indefinitely when a request has
    failed. Perpetual motion reads as "still working" and is a lie about state.
  </DoDont.Dont>
</DoDont>

## Components that implement this

The motion tokens themselves are spring-derived `linear()` easings with a stated
reduced-motion behaviour per token rather than one global kill switch, which is
what makes rule 5 checkable rather than aspirational.

## What this does not cover

- **How the easings are built.** Spring parameters, the `linear()` generator and
  the token set are [Motion](../foundations/motion/index.mdx).
- **Writing transitions in code**, which is
  [Motion in practice](../handbook/motion-in-practice.mdx).
- **Sound and haptics**, which are a separate channel with a separate budget:
  [Sound and haptics](../foundations/sound-and-haptics.mdx).
- **Data visualisation transitions.** Animating between two chart states has its
  own honesty problems and belongs to
  [Data visualisation](../foundations/data-visualisation/index.mdx).
- **Games, rewards and streak animation.** Outside the clinical surface, your
  product may do what it likes — but not in the same visual channel as clinical
  status.
