The export answers to the clinician, who wants the fewest facts that change a decision. The screen before it answers to the reader.

## When to use it

Use it when the reader takes their logs to an appointment, a clinic asks for readings between visits, or a referral needs their history.

Do not use it for:

- Continuous clinical monitoring, a regulated surface with alerting obligations that opsinjs is not built for. See [Safety review](../health/safety-review.mdx).
- Sharing with a family member or carer, which needs its own design.
- Raw data portability, which is a different artefact. Offer both.
- Granting and revoking access. That is [Consent and permissions](./consent-and-permissions.mdx).

## How it works

1. The reader picks a window and the measures to include. With nothing to share, say what would be needed rather than producing an empty document.
2. The preview is the artefact itself, and the reader can still change the dates or remove a measure.
3. In person, the product prints a one-page summary that depends on no colour.
4. Sending needs consent naming the recipient, scope and duration, and a record of what was sent, to whom and when.
5. The reader sees what was shared, to whom and until when. Expiry is visible, and revoking is immediate and says what the recipient may already hold.

The export itself:

- Fits one page, and the first third is the answer: identity, window, measures included and a summary sentence.
- States values, ranges and dates, never the product's status words. Those are written for a layperson and would read as a clinical assessment.
- Declares completeness: "42 of a possible 60 mornings, none between 12 and 19 August." Gaps are declared, never filled.
- Carries provenance: which device, which method, and whether each value was measured or self-entered.
- Marks each stale value, as in [Offline and stale data](./offline-and-stale-data.mdx).

## Components

opsinjs ships no export or summary component. The product builds the document from these:

- [Table](../components/table.mdx) carries each chart's table twin into the export.
- [Value](../components/value.mdx) and [RelativeTime](../components/relative-time.mdx) format every number and date.
- [ConsentSheet](../components/consent-sheet.mdx) asks before anything is sent.
- [DisclaimerNote](../components/disclaimer-note.mdx) says what the document is, because it will outlive the app.

## Content

- Write the export for the clinician: "Home blood pressure, 3 May to 2 June 2026. 42 morning readings of a possible 60. Self-measured, upper-arm cuff." Never "your amazing progress".
- Write the screen for the reader: "This is what Dr Ellis will see. You can change the dates or remove a metric before sending."

## Accessibility

- The export is a document, not a screenshot: real text, real headings, real tables.
- Nothing in it depends on colour, because it will be printed in monochrome and photocopied.
- The share control names the recipient: "Send summary to Dr Ellis's clinic", not "Send".
- Test the print styles: page breaks between sections, and URLs printed where link text is not self-describing.
